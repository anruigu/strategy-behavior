This **post-pilot retrospective sensitivity analysis** substantially narrows the interpretation of the pilot's learned action and coordination gains. A simple payoff-dominant equilibrium selector accounts for much of their Brier advantage over the original stationary Nash selector. The original Nash comparator predicts stationary independent play using a symmetric stage equilibrium, choosing the interior mixed equilibrium in the relevant multiple-equilibrium cases. The secondary payoff-dominant comparator instead chooses a symmetric pure stage Nash outcome with the highest common payoff whenever one exists. Equal-payoff maximizers share *joint profile* mass: this represents a mixture of coordinated conventions, not independent mixing. When no symmetric pure equilibrium exists, the rule uses the existing independent symmetric mixed equilibrium. It marginalizes the balanced display swaps and does not condition on the displayed label or trial. In particular, a tied coordination game can receive action0 probability one-half and coordination probability one. This is a behavioral comparison rule, not proof that the protocol supplies a public randomizer or that agents implement repeated-game equilibrium. The comparison changes both equilibrium selection and, in tied pure conventions, the dependence between players' actions. Source: [secondary rule and explicit limitations](secondary_baselines.py), [original selector](modeling.py).

The saved calibration bins explain why lower log loss and lower Brier can disagree. In family action, the payoff-dominant rule assigns probability exactly zero to 29.17% of game-equal event mass, where the observed action0 rate is 0.06875; it assigns exactly one to another 29.17%, where the observed rate is 0.94107. Each set contains 4,480 scored focal opportunities. Rare deviations therefore receive a large certainty penalty under log scoring, which clips to [10⁻⁶, 1−10⁻⁶]. Learned probabilities avoid those endpoints. Nevertheless, family action ECE is **0.1507 for logistic versus 0.0787 for payoff-dominant**, and coordination ECE is **0.1276 versus 0.0658**. The second secondary comparator changes the **ordered-context training weights**. For each target and training game g, let N_g be all supported training opportunities, and let S_gc and N_gc be successes and opportunities for ordered model/opponent context c. The event-matched context predicts p_c = [Σ_g S_gc/N_g] / [Σ_g N_gc/N_g]. Totals are defined before restricting to the context. Unknown contexts use the all-training equal-game pooled event mean; no eligible training target gives a null prediction. This matches the event-scoring objective while retaining the exact original training folds. It differs from the original equal-row context baseline most when conditional histories create unequal opportunities. Source: [context fitting and fallback](secondary_baselines.py).

These conditional forecasts target rates within histories the players themselves produce. The realized opportunity mixture is endogenous to the dyad's behavior; count weighting does not identify a causal response to experimentally imposed defection, a forgiving intention, or exploitation of predictability. Their small effective game counts and uneven split-half support remain material even where an aggregate interval excludes zero. Likewise, the pilot's reproducible between-game variance establishes something to predict, not that the particular payoff features or learned estimator generalize.

The appropriate conclusion is a mixed and comparator-sensitive pilot signal. The post-pilot payoff-dominant rule sharply weakens claims of a learned Brier advantage over simple equilibrium-based prediction; known-shape results show useful probabilistic adjustments, while family transfer and extrapolation expose failures. The already-fixed prospective forecasts can test those claims on new outcomes. This interpretation changes no models, forecasts, primary gates, or prospective procedures.

There is a changed uncertainty result for family action: logistic's log-loss gain is now **+0.2521 [0.0540, 0.4915]**, whereas the pilot interval crossed zero. The MLP's gain is likewise positive, +0.2520 [0.0718, 0.4827]. Yet their Brier gains are negative and ECE remains worse than payoff-dominant: logistic 0.1220, MLP 0.0886, rule 0.0770. The development bins retain the underlying certainty issue. Payoff-dominant action assigns exactly zero to 29.17% of score mass, where the observed rate is 0.08646, and exactly one to 27.78%, where the observed rate is 0.96219. Learned forecasts avoid exact endpoints but introduce other calibration errors. Family logistic cooperation remains especially problematic: its highest bin predicts **0.97097** and observes **0.61311** over 55.58% of game-equal event mass and 12,640 scored opportunities. Its log loss worsens from pilot 1.0867 to development 1.3707 despite a small Brier reduction, illustrating sensitivity to confident errors. The payoff-dominant cooperation probability-one bin observes 0.97979. The study has not evaluated a generic smoothed equilibrium comparator, so improved log loss still does not isolate the contribution of learned payoff structure from avoidance of deterministic certainty. In random-group validation, development logistic and MLP have positive Brier gain intervals over payoff-dominant for cooperation and coordination. Logistic action's +0.0047 interval includes zero; MLP action's +0.0104 interval barely excludes zero at its lower end (+0.000045). The **family-mean comparison limits the complexity claim**. Under random-group holdout, family mean cooperation Brier/log loss/ECE is 0.07396/0.24918/0.00598, versus logistic 0.07457/0.24835/0.02076 and MLP 0.07378/0.25134/0.03249. Coordination is 0.13592/0.40363/0.00348 for family mean, versus logistic 0.13733/0.40653/0.01867 and MLP 0.13665/0.41682/0.01894. Thus the broader-target learned gains over payoff-dominant can be matched closely by a coarse known-family behavioral rate; these numbers do not show that continuous within-family incentives are necessary for the observed accuracy. The same near-match occurs for cooperation/coordination in pair/model transfer. No new paired family-baseline test was computed here, so these comparisons are descriptive point comparisons rather than claims of exact equivalence. Action0 differs: a family-rate baseline does not retain which canonical action realizes a dominant action, cooperative action, or selected equilibrium. The generator independently reverses (R,S,T,P) to (P,T,S,R) with probability one-half, while family classification remains invariant. Episode display swaps are separate and undone by scoring. Consequently, the large learned random-group action0 advantage over family mean (about 0.1465 versus 0.2677 for logistic) establishes payoff-to-encoded-action prediction; it can arise from identifying an otherwise simple strategic action. It is not by itself evidence of nuanced within-family behavior or sensitivity to continuous payoff changes beyond that mapping. The stronger equilibrium rule, which also tracks this mapping, is the more informative comparison for that claim. Sources: [generator orientation and invariant family classification](games.py), [scoring definitions](measurements.md), [independent numerical interpretation](numerical-interpretation.md).

Twenty equal-diagonal groups have undefined t and are excluded, versus six in the pilot. All development extrapolation coordination observations come from those nine Chicken shapes. The development full support is 38 games/617 episodes/5,152 post-defection opportunities, 30/381/552 forgiveness opportunities, and 22/572/3,621 extraction opportunities. The expanded results therefore preserve a restricted conclusion: payoff-based forecasts recover useful action mappings and broad family/context regularities, but the evidence for fine-grained structural generalization remains mixed and depends on the comparator. Family cooperation is still a substantial failure; known-family means explain much of successful cooperation/coordination prediction; and changed global holdout composition explains why the extrapolation comparison cannot be read as a controlled improvement. These retrospective findings motivate interpreting the already-frozen prospective tests as tests, without changing their methods, predictions, or gates.
