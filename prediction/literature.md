# Related work and the narrowed research question

Consolidated 2026-09-10 from the three Gate 1 reviews. **The defensible question is whether payoff information available before a target interaction predicts mechanically measured behavior distributions in autonomous repeated symmetric 2×2 LLM cross-play, under structural-family, payoff-region and model-pair holdouts, with the decision protocol fixed.** This is a candidate empirical contribution, not an established priority claim. Neither `P(B | G, M_i, M_j)` nor the use of learned behavioral predictors is itself new.

Three precedents most directly constrain the claim:

1. **Towards Predictive Models of Strategic Behaviour in Large Language Model Agents** predicts LLM cooperation using scenario features and model calibration. It directly occupies the broad strategic-behavior prediction claim. [Primary paper](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=PpRrEWt9yK)
2. **Predicting Decisions of AI Agents from Limited Interaction through Text-Tabular Modeling** predicts unfamiliar agents’ next decisions from earlier games and decision-time state/dialogue. It establishes learned prediction and few-shot agent adaptation. [Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.12411v1)
3. **Strategy, Not Payoffs** uses payoff-derived representations to predict fine-tuning transfer under strict game holdouts. It establishes a close precedent for structural game features predicting held-out LLM outcomes. [Primary paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.27536v1)

The boundary is the information and outcome being predicted. A pre-interaction forecast has no actions or dialogue from the target encounter. A next-action predictor uses an observed target-game prefix. A transfer predictor estimates how training on one game changes performance on another. A retrospective strategy classifier labels an already observed path. Each is useful, but evidence for one does not establish the others.

## Evidence boundaries and retained source detail

The OpenReview direct PDF was blocked by browser verification during the predictive subreview. Its indexed primary Methods/Appendix E and [author publication page](https://www.aristeidispanos.com/publication/panos2026icml_workshop_ai4good/) support rejecting broad novelty. The author page and [repository bibliography](https://github.com/cerdwin/behavioural-experiments) differ on workshop/author metadata; exact version should be verified before formal citation. Its leave-one-model-out evaluation is not necessarily leave-one-provider-family-out. Do not infer unverified protocol details.

Caiata’s ridge penalty was selected using leave-one-game-out performance; a separate nested tuning/final-test split is not described. Its result does not establish prediction of spontaneous repeated interaction. Huang–Hadfi explicitly states publication without peer review, and its inspected paper omits information required to verify predictive generalization. These limitations remain part of the comparison, not silently filled gaps. [Caiata et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.27536v1), [Huang & Hadfi](https://www.anlp.jp/proceedings/annual_meeting/2026/pdf_dir/Q8-19.pdf)

Earlier foundations also narrow what can be claimed. [Hartford et al., 2016](https://jhartford.github.io/assets/pdf/NIPS-2016-deep-learning-for-predicting-human-strategic-behavior-Paper.pdf) learns human action distributions from payoff matrices; [ToMnet, 2018](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/rabinowitz18a.html) learns agent representations from past behavior; [Marris et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.09978) develops equilibrium-invariant game representations. More recently, [Zhu et al., 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-025-02230-5) predicts human first-play decisions over procedurally generated matrix games; its detailed split protocol was not audited here.

The focused table omits secondary branches, not their existence: FAIRGAME and its communication extensions, structured opponent modeling, motive-inference benchmarks, non-LLM mixed-motive adaptation, and additional strategy-classification work remain documented in the original reviews. No distinct paper is merged solely because it shares authors or a framework. Full source notes and methodological discussion are preserved in [predictive literature](literature-prediction.md), [repeated-game literature](literature-games.md), and [identification literature](literature-identification.md).

## What the present experiment must establish

The proposed estimand is `P(B | G, M_i, M_j, fixed protocol)`. The horizon, information available to players, payoff rendering, action encoding and sampling procedure must be fixed or explicitly conditioned upon. Model identity alone does not define an agent protocol. The target encounter contributes no history to an ex-ante forecast.

The contribution would be evidence that payoff information improves predictions over marginal/model-context and taxonomy comparators, transfers to withheld strategic structures or pairs, and remains calibrated after accounting for rollout noise. Group-equivalent payoff/action transformations together, reserve final tests from tuning, and compare methods on common eligible rows. A learned model that only interpolates nearby sampled matrices does not establish a transferable behavioral disposition.

Repeated trajectories require opportunity-aware semantics: unvisited response states are unknown; unconditional defection is not by itself evidence of retaliation; uninterrupted cooperation does not identify forgiveness or a unique latent policy. Separate mutual cooperation, individual choices, equilibrium occupancy and operational asymmetric extraction. Pair/episode dependence and sparse conditional denominators affect both confidence intervals and interpretation. These are design requirements arising from the review, not outcomes already demonstrated by this study.

Gate 1 therefore supports a controlled, narrowed experiment. It does not establish priority, useful predictive signal, or successful generalization in advance of the results.
