# Gate 1 literature: repeated games, cooperation, and game representations

Reviewed 2026-09-10. Scope: primary papers from 2024–2026, with one foundational 2023 representation paper. This is a focused review, not an exhaustive systematic review. Full primary text was inspected for the main table; the final screening item is explicitly abstract-only. “No structural holdout” means none reported for an external behavioral prediction task, not proof that every experiment reused identical prompts. Preprint findings should remain attributed to their authors.

The broad idea of predicting strategic LLM behavior is already prior work. This subreview also finds learned, payoff-derived predictions of cross-game transfer with strict game holdouts. The plausible contribution is narrower: **forecast distributions of autonomous repeated-game outcomes before the target interaction, using payoffs and agent descriptors, and evaluate on structurally novel symmetric 2×2 games and unseen pairings**. The parent review separately audits the closest direct precedent, [Towards Predictive Models of Strategic Behaviour in Large Language Model Agents](https://openreview.net/attachment?id=PpRrEWt9yK&name=pdf); this document must not be used to claim that no direct predictor exists.

Additional direct-prediction screening hit: [Shapira, Tennenholtz & Reichart, *Predicting Decisions of AI Agents from Limited Interaction through Text-Tabular Modeling*, May 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12411). **Abstract-only in this subreview:** inputs combine game state, offer history, dialogue, and K earlier target-agent games. A tabular foundation model uses frozen-LLM text/observer features. Training uses 13 frontier-LLM agents; testing uses 91 held-out scaffolded agents in bargaining/negotiation. Targets are next responses/offers. This establishes learned few-shot prediction and agent-population transfer; structural game-family holdout and detailed cross-play construction require the companion review. It is not evidence for payoff-only predictions before target gameplay.

## Reproducible design lessons for the proposed pilot

The recommendations below are this review’s design deductions, not claims that the cited papers implemented the proposed experiment.

1. **Record the full experimental condition.** The scientific target should be written as `P(B | G, agent_i, agent_j, protocol)`, with protocol fixing prompt, action format, history encoding, horizon disclosure, sampling and reasoning settings. “Environment alone” is defensible only after these are held fixed. FAIRGAME explicitly makes these configurable, demonstrating why model name alone is insufficient. [FAIRGAME](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.14325v3)

2. **Keep raw payoffs alongside normalized strategic features.** Positive affine transformations preserve expected-utility preferences under fixed-horizon play, but behavioral invariance is an empirical question. More at Stake varies positive payoff scale while retaining strategic ordering and reports changed behavior. Its differing model temperatures and short-history classifier limitations make the magnitude result a reason to test invariance, not a universal law. Do not erase absolute scale before testing it. [More at Stake](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.19082v1)

3. **Separate payoff equivalence from behavioral equivalence.** Equilibrium-invariant geometry is useful for structural grouping and features. It is not proof that an LLM policy is invariant. For repeated games, general stage-game equilibrium-preserving transformations need not preserve continuation incentives. Use the conservative common positive affine transform for invariance controls; preserve strategically meaningful payoff gaps and full displayed numbers. [Equilibrium-Invariant Embedding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.09978)

4. **Make action encoding neutral and counterbalanced.** Save the mapping from displayed symbols to canonical actions; randomize presentation in paired controls; never equate the literal label C with cooperation across all games. Akata’s neutral symbols and payoff-only text provide a directly relevant starting protocol. Preserve simultaneous information: both agents must receive the same pre-round public history, and neither may see the other’s current action. [Playing repeated games](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-025-02172-y)

5. **Distinguish conditional frequency from retaliation.** `P(D_t | opponent D_(t−1))` can be high for an unconditional defector. Record the comparison conditional, focal previous action, round, and each denominator. Return undefined when a conditioning event never occurred; do not silently insert zero. Controlled history probes can identify memory-1/2 responses, but probe policies and autonomous-play trajectories are distinct estimands and must remain separate datasets. [Pal et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.09849v1)

6. **Short trajectories rarely identify full strategies.** ALLC, TFT and other policies can coincide along a cooperative history. Begin with mechanically computed trajectory statistics, not hard latent-strategy labels. Check rule comprehension and history extraction separately; errors there can imitate changing social preferences. Fontana’s memory-window and comprehension experiments give a concrete basis for these checks. [Nicer than Humans](https://www.lajello.com/papers/icwsm25prisoner.pdf)

7. **Treat the horizon as part of the game.** Use one prespecified horizon disclosure throughout the main pilot, and retain round-level outcomes to separate opening, transient and endgame behavior. A ten-round result is about ten-round play. Do not interpret an undisclosed fixed horizon as a specified stochastic continuation process. The elicitation and direct-play treatments in Pal et al. demonstrate that this distinction is consequential. [Pal et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.09849v1)

8. **Cross-play and model-level decomposition are established, necessary controls.** Separate agent pair effects from aggregate performance against a chosen population. Freeze opponent populations and their weighting; report pairing-level outcomes before rankings. Cooperation rates from self-play cannot substitute for heterogeneous pair behavior. [CoopEval](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15267v1)

9. **Freeze structural splits before observing rollout labels.** Keep label-swapped and affine-related versions of one base game together where the test is strategic generalization. Random instance splits primarily test interpolation. Caiata’s strict game exclusion and paired-direction exclusion are useful precedents. Their regularization selection uses LOGO results; for this project, nest tuning within training folds and reserve a final untouched structural test. [Strategy, Not Payoffs](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.27536v1)

10. **Estimate uncertainty at the independent-interaction level.** Rounds within one match and the two player records from that match are dependent. Repetition yields uncertainty estimates only if sessions reset and sampling settings are recorded. Bootstrap trials/matches, then aggregate games or families for generalization claims. Score predicted outcome distributions with proper scoring rules; do not describe an in-sample explanatory regression as an out-of-sample predictor. The framing study’s logit analysis is the relevant distinction. [Strategic behavior and contextual framing](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-69032-z)

## Gate 1 assessment

Proceed only with the narrowed question and explicit comparisons to the direct-prediction companion review. Measuring cooperation/reciprocity, constructing strategy profiles, comparing self-play with cross-play, or fitting payoff features to behavior is insufficient novelty on its own. A credible contribution would combine pre-interaction distributional forecasts, autonomous repeated symmetric 2×2 play, matrix-defined conditions, structural/payoff/pair holdouts, and calibration with trial noise accounted for. Novelty is the precise joint test and evidence, not the notation `P(B | G, M_i, M_j)`.
