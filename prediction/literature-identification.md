# Behavioral identification and strategic prediction: Gate 1 research note

Reviewed 2026-09-10. This is a bounded primary-source review supporting `plan.md`, with emphasis on behavioral representations, few-shot identification, and what can be inferred mechanically from trajectories. It does not modify the plan or authorize expansion beyond its gates. No experiment or paid model call was run.

**Finding:** learning agent representations from a few observations, predicting strategic actions with learned models, and applying those models to LLM behavior all have precedents. The defensible POC question is narrower: **can information available before a target interaction predict distributions of repeated symmetric 2×2 cross-play behavior under strict payoff, structural-family, and model-pair holdouts?** A positive result needs to survive these holdouts and outperform simple behavioral and game-feature baselines. This is an assessment of the reviewed literature, not proof that no exact prior study exists.

The exceptionally close *Towards Predictive Models of Strategic Behaviour in Large Language Model Agents* ([OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=PpRrEWt9yK)) is being reviewed separately for the combined Gate 1 decision. Its full text was blocked by browser verification in this review; no methodological claims about it are made here.

## What the POC can add

The high-level equation `P(B | G, M_i, M_j)` is not itself a novelty claim. A useful result would combine **pre-interaction prediction, repeated interaction, held-out strategic structure, explicit opponent effects, and calibrated empirical behavioral distributions**. The following are methodological recommendations from this review.

1. **Separate three prediction tasks.** Pre-interaction forecasting receives only the game and allowed model information. Online forecasting receives a target-game prefix. Few-shot identification receives other-game observations of a new target. All are valid; a score from one cannot establish another. Do not let target-game history leak into the payoff-only baseline.
2. **Make structural transfer the central claim.** Hold out families or regions with different best-response structure and keep affine/label-equivalent variants grouped. Random matrix holdout within a tiny payoff space is useful as a sanity check but is weak evidence of transferable strategic dispositions.
3. **Treat model pair as a distinct experimental factor.** First-action behavior cannot depend on the actual opponent model if the focal model has no information about that model or its actions. Later outcomes can depend on the opponent through history. Record whether opponent identity is disclosed; model-name effects and interaction effects answer different questions.
4. **Use an observable primary endpoint.** First-action probabilities and distributions over four joint outcomes are broadly defined. Add per-player transition tables only when their conditioning events have support. An all-purpose five-dimensional moral or psychological vector is harder to identify and compare.
5. **Use simple baselines before embeddings.** A new model has no learned ID embedding unless some information identifies it. A model-family holdout with an unknown-category token tests a population prior, not behavioral identification. The later embedding experiment must specify exactly which other-game calibration observations are allowed.
6. **Budget for uncertainty, not just more unique matrices.** A small POC can show whether between-condition differences exceed replicate noise. It cannot substantiate broad transfer to the population of future model families with only a few tested families.

Two source-supported cautions sharpen this scope. ToMnet already establishes the few-observation agent-representation pattern. Shapira et al. already tests held-out scaffolded agents and observed-game adaptation. Accordingly, the later embedding stage should emphasize **transfer across strategic payoff families before target-game interaction**, and demonstrate value over direct probe vectors, rather than claim few-shot identification as a new concept. [ToMnet](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/rabinowitz18a.html), [Shapira et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12411)

## Identification risks and mechanically defined alternatives

The recommendations below are statistical deductions about the proposed design, not claims that the cited papers established these exact estimands. SOM explicitly cautions that its inferred dependencies do not establish causal mechanisms; this distinction also applies to behavioral phenotype labels. [SOM, discussion](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.07301)

### Retaliation

`P(D_i,t | D_j,t−1)` alone is not retaliation. An unconditional defector attains 1. It also mixes histories in which the focal player previously cooperated with histories in which it already defected, and mixes early and late rounds. Opponent defection may itself be a response to the focal player's earlier behavior.

A transparent **descriptive response contrast**, in games where C/D have their intended interpretation, is

`P(D_i,t | C_i,t−1, D_j,t−1, stratum) − P(D_i,t | C_i,t−1, C_j,t−1, stratum)`.

The stratum should at least respect game, focal/opponent identities, and phase of the interaction. Report both rates, their event counts, and uncertainty. The contrast still describes association because histories differ and opponent actions are endogenous.

For a causal claim, use a separately labeled probe: fix an eligible history and randomize the scripted opponent's last action, with corresponding payoffs updated consistently. Comparing focal responses to paired valid histories estimates the effect of that intervention under the prompt protocol. It is not automatically the effect in naturally occurring cross-play. Actual randomized deviations during interaction and supplied synthetic-history queries should be reported separately.

### Forgiveness

`P(C_i,t | D_j,t−1, C_j,t−2)` measures immediate cooperation following a cooperative-to-defective opponent transition. It does **not** require the focal player to have retaliated, or even to have defected. An unconditional cooperator scores perfectly.

Use “cooperation after opponent defection” for that descriptive rate. If the target is return after punishment, define a state/event sequence: opponent defects after a specified cooperative history, focal defects in response, opponent resumes cooperation, then measure focal return to cooperation. A return-time endpoint needs an explicit deadline and right-censoring at the final round. Conditioned-on-retaliation samples are selected subsets; compare them carefully across models. A scripted single-defection-then-cooperation opponent produces a more standardized recovery probe.

### Exploitation

Positive own-minus-opponent payoff is payoff advantage, not evidence that the model identified and exploited predictable behavior. The same advantage can arise from random coordination-role selection, a dominant action, or an opponent's errors. Symmetric games still allow asymmetric realized payoffs.

For the pilot, retain mechanically interpretable quantities: normalized own payoff, welfare, payoff advantage, and rates of asymmetric outcomes. If “exploitation of predictability” is desired later, define a known scripted opponent and compare return against an appropriate baseline policy under that opponent. Distinguish this from **exploitability**, which concerns how much a best response could gain against the focal policy and cannot be estimated from one observed trajectory without assumptions about responses at unvisited histories.

### Cooperation and coordination

Do not assign “C” by its label across all payoff orders. In some games welfare-maximizing outcomes are off-diagonal, there are ties, or an efficient outcome conflicts with risk dominance. Use the full four-outcome distribution throughout; game-specific cooperation definitions can supplement it. Report which payoff-derived predicate an outcome satisfies. For coordination, distinguish equilibrium occupancy, persistence in one equilibrium, welfare achieved, and alternation: alternation can be fair and efficient in some games while looking unstable under a persistence metric.

### Support, equivalence, and uncertainty

**Unvisited states are unknown.** If two cooperators never defect, retaliation and forgiveness have zero opportunities. Mark these targets undefined and expose denominators. Do not encode them as zero, one, or a silently imputed population mean. Estimating a Bayesian posterior with a prior is permissible if the lack of data is visible and prior-driven values are not treated as labels.

**Observed paths do not identify a unique strategy.** ALLC, TFT, WSLS, and GRIM can all produce an uninterrupted cooperative path. A canonical-strategy classifier forced to choose among these classes cannot recover unobserved counterfactual responses. High confidence from a classifier trained on a narrow synthetic population is not proof of a latent intention. Pal et al.'s explicit history-conditioned probing is a useful precedent for exposing response states; the tradeoff is that such probes describe a particular supplied-history protocol. [Pal et al., conditional probes](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.09849)

**The independent replicate is the interaction episode.** Rounds within a game are serially dependent and the two players' trajectories are coupled. For average behavior, bootstrap whole episodes; for generalization over games, resample held-out game groups as appropriate. Do not treat 20 rounds as 20 independent trials of an agent disposition. Compare predictors on the same held-out episodes or condition estimates, with paired uncertainty on score differences.

**Short episodes induce phase effects.** Pooling opening, middle, and final-round actions can hide end-game defection or make it look like retaliation if opponent defection also increases near the end. Record the disclosed horizon and phase. A stationary memory-1 summary is a descriptive approximation, not an assumption to silently impose on a full-history LLM.

**Replicate labels are noisy.** Compare observed between-condition variance with within-condition variance. For first actions use proper scores for binary outcomes; for a joint first outcome use a four-class score. For an episode rate, score the forecast distribution or compare mean errors against replicate uncertainty. Calibration bins should not make unsupported precision claims when few independent games populate each bin.

**Payoff normalization is a hypothesis.** Positive scaling and constant shifts preserve standard utility-based incentives but may change LLM token-level behavior. Preserve presented payoffs and transformation metadata. Do not erase a real scale effect by normalizing the input while pretending it is nonexistent. Group transformed copies in splits so that apparent novelty is not memorization of an equivalent game.

**Predictive factorization needs restraint.** Joint outcome probabilities generally cannot be recovered by multiplying marginal per-player cooperation rates across a repeated episode. The players share history, and averaging over histories induces dependence. Model the four outcomes directly or simulate a properly conditioned joint trajectory model. Similarly, “game effect + model effect + opponent effect” is a predictive decomposition; it is not a causal explanation unless the experiment and estimand justify it.

## Gate 1 disposition

Proceed only with the narrow payoff-to-distribution POC and state that it tests a hypothesis. Existing work substantially overlaps the broad claims about learned strategic predictors and few-shot behavioral characterization. The most useful early outcome is a transparent answer about **how much out-of-family and out-of-pair predictive signal remains after controlling presentation and accounting for replicate noise**. Retaliation, forgiveness, and exploitation should remain separately qualified secondary measures until their opportunity counts and operational definitions support the intended interpretation.
