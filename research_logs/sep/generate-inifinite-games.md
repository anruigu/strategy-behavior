An even more useful way to search for these

If you're trying to discover hacks rather than merely classify known ones, I'd give a player/AI a checklist of questions.

For every game, ask:

About state

Who knows the true value of every variable?

Who is trusted to report it?

Can I make the reported state differ from the actual state?

About constraints

What does the rulebook say I cannot do?

Where is that prohibition actually enforced?

What happens if I simply try?

About costs

What actions have costs?

Is the cost paid before or after the action?

Can the action fail after the cost is paid?

Can I cancel/reverse it?

About rewards

What actions generate resources?

Can rewards generate actions?

Can rewards generate more rewards?

Can a reward happen multiple times?

This finds your infinite-loop class.

About information

What information is supposed to be hidden?

Does the referee ever reveal it indirectly?

Do invalid actions reveal anything?

Does timing reveal anything?

Can legal choices encode information?

About time

What causes the game to end?

Who controls that condition?

Can I make it happen early?

Can I prevent it?

What happens immediately before and after the trigger?

About multiplayer

What can I do that hurts an opponent more than it hurts me?

Can I determine who wins even if I cannot win?

Can two losing players jointly control the winner?

Can I create a mutually beneficial coalition?

About language

What does the rule literally say?

What does it obviously intend?

Are those actually the same?

That last distinction is incredibly fertile in text games.
