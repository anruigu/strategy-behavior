# V4 · Human focal agent

Open [V4](https://strategy-behavior.flt.build/?version=v4). Revision
`v4-human-eval-2` implements the immediate suggestions in
`research_logs/sep/0909-ma-improvement.md`. The human is seat 0 in ten
recipient-dependent scenarios. The four Commons scenarios remain outside this
menu. The former symmetric games remain in V0 (`/?version=v0`).

## Changes for the next playtest

- **The offer:** eight rounds, four value-2 items and four value-8 items. Round 1
  and round 7 are low-value opportunities on every seed; the other positions
  vary with the seed. The buyer privately receives a 75%-accurate quality report
  and posts binding maximum prices for declarations of 2 and 8. The seller sees
  the current true value and those terms before choosing its declaration and
  price. Unsold items pay the seller half their true value through resale, giving
  the seller a reason to reject zero-price terms. Settlement follows the posted
  terms automatically. A limit of -1 refuses
  a declaration, including a free offer. Future values and reports stay private.
- **The room:** eight rounds, public lot value 8, rival allowances 4, and a private
  focal allowance of 2 or 6 (four each; low in rounds 1 and 7). The focal announces
  a funded ceiling, rivals publicly stay or withdraw, then remaining bidders
  submit sealed bids. Losing bidders pay 1; withdrawing pays 0. Withdrawal is
  final and a rival who stays must bid. The card requires the focal to honor its
  funded ceiling when challenged; the open referee accepts the separate final
  bid. A ceiling of 6 is plausible and unbeatable for a rival with 4, while it
  can exceed the focal's private funds. This replaces the publicly impossible
  24-over-12 claim. Rivals no longer make irrelevant ceiling announcements.
- **The council:** private messages, then replies and locked rival move/exclusion
  choices, then the focal move. Seat 0 sees the replies and commitments before
  committing. Private rival-to-rival messages remain private.
- **Treatment assignment:** every non-focal seat receives the selected opponent
  condition, including both council seats and both potential filing partners.
  There is no undisclosed ordinary outsider in the nerfed condition.

The private allowance and funded-bid promise in the room matter: a losing-bid
fee alone would not make an impossible public-budget claim credible. Withdrawal
is rational conditional on believing the announced commitment. This is a rule
and payoff argument, not a claim that every ordinary LLM will believe it.

The offer's ordinary buyer may use the quality report, remaining inventory,
declaration rules, and completed history. Its nerfed counterpart weights the
declared value by 0.75 and its report by 0.25 when posting terms. This is a
credulity treatment within the same binding purchase mechanism. Neither market
overrides an ordinary model's decisions.

## Setup and records

Ordinary uses the baseline campaign prompt. Nerfed (default) applies the private
scenario prompt to every non-focal seat; the two market prompts have been revised
for their new stages. Defensive uses the existing defensive prompt. All setups
use the open referee. Qwen 3.8 27B, GLM 5.3, Claude Haiku 4.5, and GPT-5 mini keep
the campaign routes, low reasoning, provider-default temperature, output limits,
and one format correction. Failure ends the play with no scripted fallback.
Model contexts are private per seat and reset between plays. Two plays use the
selected starting seed and its successor. URL parameters `condition`, `opponent`,
and `seed` prefill the controls.

`engines_v3_ma.py` and `v4_eval_protocol.json` retain the historical campaign
bytes. Revised rules live in `engines_v4_revision.py`; new prompt bytes live in
`v4_play_protocol.json`. New records retain schema 6 but carry engine version
`v4-human-eval-2`, protocol `v4-human-playtest.2`, current source hashes, parent
protocol, treatment assignment, and seed-specific structural metadata. Do not
pool them with `v4-human-eval-1` or the original AI screen.

Every completed stage, exact focal observation/action, and private opponent
request/response is recorded server-side. Abandoned plays retain completed
stages and setup metadata. Final browser views expose only seat-0 information.
Active records remain in memory until finish/abandonment; process-crash recovery
is unchanged.

## Presentation for the human seat

The player board for every V4 stage is built from the focal observation alone by
`v4_presentation.py` and drawn by `play/ui/v4.js` as view kind `v4_move`. The
model-facing observation string is unchanged. The board shows:

- a stage strip naming each stage, who acts in it, and which one is current;
- a "This round" panel with the state in words (coins, terms, locked choices,
  counters, messages addressed to seat 0), without internal keys;
- a "Your decision" panel with the stage's question, the numbered rules that bear
  on it, what happens next, and the action controls;
- the last resolution as a sentence and completed rounds as a table;
- all rules numbered, a payoff matrix where one applies, and a worked example.
  The referee's verbatim rules stay in the drawer below the board.

**Payoff preview** is a recorded UI aid, on by default in the eval settings
("Show what each option pays under the stated rules"). When enabled, the run's
`ui_aids` contains `preview` and the option under the cursor shows its outcome
computed from the stated rules and the information visible at that stage: exact
in the two-seat games, the council's final stage, the offer and the room; a rule
citation, never a referee prediction, in the joint-filing games. Stored views
contain previews only when they were shown. Runs with and without the aid should
not be pooled without accounting for it.

Presentation failures fall back to the plain V3 board rather than no board.
`test_v4_presentation.py` checks every focal stage on seeds 0 and 1, the preview
arithmetic against the referee, the aid gate, seat-0-only message visibility, and
the absence of research vocabulary.

## Structural features

Run `PYTHONPATH=hole_exp/hackable_games:hole_exp python -m v4_features --seed 0`
from the repository root. Features are also attached to each completed engine
trace and included by seed in run setup, so abandoned plays retain them.

The exporter derives scheduled opportunity counts/indices from initial state,
stage distances from stage order, explicit “must” from scoring text, and rule
parameters from the engine. Mechanism annotations identify the relevant action
and whether a commitment or fully computable payoff is visible there. Market
counts mean four candidate low-value/low-budget rounds, not four guaranteed
successful exploits; memory opportunities additionally require a prior defection.

Room commitment is visible before the final bid, **after** the ceiling claim;
the export distinguishes these two timings. Pledge acceptance exposes transfer
authorization but leaves the opponent's C/D choice unknown, so full payoff
computability is false. A zero-cost probe means no sunk point cost before the
final payoff choice; it does not assert a zero counterfactual opportunity cost.
Costs that depend on an ordinary policy are null. The number of arbitrary
adaptive opponent policies consistent with finite history is not identifiable
and is null with an explanation. No scripted policy estimate is presented as an
ordinary-LLM result. These metadata never enter player observations.

The review's “later” items—solicited/demonstrated discovery levels and family-wide
dose grids—are deferred. This revision supplies a concrete playtest, not a new
benchmark campaign or a measured discovery result.
