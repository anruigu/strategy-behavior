# Sovereign Vaults Playbook

## Declaration Strategy

When I must declare my yield, I declare my true private yield. Declaring honestly establishes credibility that I can leverage throughout the game. My opponents observe my declarations and adjust their behavior based on whether I'm trustworthy. False declarations risk audit failure (-10 points) and destroy the reputation I'm building.

When I'm significantly behind in points, resist the temptation to declare higher than my true yield hoping to catch up. This strategy did not work—I finished with lower scores when I inflated declarations in later rounds. Chasing deficits through dishonesty backfires.

Honest declaration is reliable across all game states. Every episode I played, I maintained honest declarations throughout and never suffered an audit failure. This consistency is my competitive advantage, even when I'm behind.

## Deposit Strategy

When I have just declared my yield and must decide how much to deposit, I consider two competing incentives: retained gold scores 2 points per unit, while deposited gold scores 1 point per unit. Retention is worth twice as much.

In early rounds (rounds 1-3), deposit moderately—typically 3-4 units when my declared harvest is 5-8. This signals good faith participation without overcommitting resources. My early-game deposits should leave me with enough retained gold to keep pace.

In middle rounds (round 4), deposit at a moderate-to-high level. By round 4, I have enough data to evaluate whether my opponent is cooperating or defecting. If they've been depositing nothing despite declaring honestly, I should increase my deposits (typically 4-5 units when I've declared 6-9) to accumulate points before the final round, while still retaining the high-value remainder for scoring.

In the final round (round 5), deposit conservatively—typically 1-3 units below my declared harvest. The final round carries no consequences for future rounds, so I should prioritize personal retention. Depositing 2-3 units when I've declared 4-10 is appropriate. The final round is where retention advantage compounds most.

Do not deposit the full declared amount every time—this scores lower than selective retention. Moderate-to-high deposits in rounds 2-4 with conservative final-round deposits consistently outperformed other strategies.

## Opponent Behavior Patterns

Observe whether my opponent declares honestly and deposits nothing, or deposits a portion of their declared yield. In all four episodes, my opponents declared their true yields but deposited zero. They leveraged retention (2 points per unit) to score heavily while I built cooperation signals through deposits.

Recognize that this is a defection strategy and it works well for the opponent. My honest declaration plus moderate deposits will often leave me behind on the scoreboard by the end-game, but it protects me from audit failure and maintains a stable foundation.

I cannot catch up to an opponent using pure defection by matching their defection—I would need both false declarations and zero deposits, which risks audit failure. Instead, I should increase my deposits in rounds 2-4 to accumulate points more aggressively while the opportunity exists, then conserve in round 5.

## Scoring Optimization

Recognize that the audit penalty is severe (-10 points). Never deposit more than my declared yield. The safety margin is not worth testing.

Accept that I will likely lose to players who declare high and deposit nothing. My honest strategy will often leave me behind on the scoreboard, but it protects me from catastrophic audit failures and gives me a stable foundation.

The best I can do is to maximize my own absolute score through disciplined deposit decisions across all rounds. In rounds 2-4, increase deposits to 4-5 units on high yields (6+) to accumulate more points while opponents are defecting. This closes the gap more effectively than pure retention. Reserve the retention strategy for round 5 where it has the most impact per point.

Adjust deposit levels upward in rounds 2-4 based on my actual yield, not out of a static quota.
