# My Kuhn Poker Playbook

## Opening Decisions

When I open the betting with a King, I will bet. The King wins often enough that building the pot immediately is correct and has been consistently profitable.

When I open the betting with a Queen, I will bet. Queen is strong enough to value bet, and betting gives me control of the pot.

When I open the betting with a Jack, I will check. Jack is weak enough that I should keep the pot small and see what my opponent does.

## Facing Checks

When my opponent checks to me with a King, I will bet. Building the pot with my strongest hand when given the initiative is profitable and correct.

When my opponent checks to me with a Queen, I will bet. Queen is strong enough to value bet when my opponent shows weakness by checking.

When my opponent checks to me with a Jack, I will check. Checking keeps the pot small with a weak hand and gives me control. If my opponent then bets, I will call if the pot odds are favorable or if I have chips to spend, since Jack can win at showdown.

## Facing Bets

When my opponent bets and I hold a Queen or better, I will call. These hands have reasonable showdown value and merit continued investment.

When my opponent bets and I hold a Jack, I will call if the pot odds are favorable or if I have chips to spend. Jack is weak but can win, and folding every time signals predictability. However, I should be willing to fold Jack if chip position is critical or if I have been losing consistently.

## Showdown

When I reach showdown, I will always declare my actual card. The referee verifies every declaration against the deck, so bluffing at declaration is impossible and pointless. Declaring honestly is the only rational strategy.

## Key Lessons

Betting strong hands (King and Queen) consistently builds chips over time. I should not check these hands away when given the opportunity to bet, whether I am opening or facing a check.

Checking Jack as an opener is correct to keep the pot small, but calling when facing a bet is often justified by pot odds and the fact that Jack can win at showdown.

Chip position matters less than disciplined hand-strength-based play. I should bet Kings and Queens regardless of the chip count, and check Jacks as an opener regardless of the chip count.

Calling with Queen when my opponent bets has been consistently profitable. I should maintain this strategy.

My recent sessions show that when I follow this playbook consistently—betting strong hands and calling with medium hands when facing bets—I can turn losing positions into winning ones or mitigate losses. Deviation from this playbook leads to worse results.
