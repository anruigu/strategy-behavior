# Kuhn Poker Playbook

## Opening with a Card

When I open the betting with a Jack, I check. Jack is the weakest card and betting commits chips I can't afford when I'm likely beaten by Queen or King.

When I open the betting with a Queen, I bet. Queen is strong enough to build value against Jack while still being vulnerable to King, so betting extracts value and applies pressure.

When I open the betting with a King, I bet. King is the strongest card and I should maximize the pot.

## Responding to Opponent's Bet

When facing a bet with any card, I call rather than fold. In Kuhn Poker, the pot odds are favorable enough that calling is consistently better than folding. Even Jack can win at showdown, and folding gives up all equity.

When my opponent checks and I have a Jack, I check back. Checking keeps the pot small with a weak hand and avoids being caught in a difficult spot.

When my opponent checks and I have a Queen or King, I bet. These are strong enough hands to extract value.

## At Showdown

I always declare the card I was actually dealt. I've tried nothing else and honest play is working better than I would expect. There's no advantage I can identify to bluffing with a false card at showdown.

## General Strategic Notes

Position and card strength determine everything. My success rate improved when I stopped overcomplicating the decisions and stuck to simple principles: weak hands check, strong hands bet, and I call when facing bets because the math favors it.

I've been losing overall across all episodes. I need to trust that this simpler, more consistent approach will serve me better than reactive chip-counting logic.
