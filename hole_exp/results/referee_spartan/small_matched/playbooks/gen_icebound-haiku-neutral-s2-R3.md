# ICEBOUND PLAYBOOK

## Round 1
Scout in round 1. It's the safest opening move and establishes baseline points while you gather information about opponent behavior.

## Rounds 2-3
When all players have matched scout/scout in prior rounds and fuel/points remain equal, continue scouting to maintain equilibrium. Breaking the pattern early wastes fuel and risks escalating unnecessary conflict when you're not behind.

## Round 4
If you're tied or leading, scout to preserve fuel and gather information for the final round. Raiding at round 4 when you're ahead is tempting but wastes fuel that could be used more flexibly in round 5.

When you're behind at round 4, consider raiding to close the gap, but only if you have sufficient fuel remaining (at least 3) and if the raid target will actually be vulnerable based on public pledges.

## Round 5
If you're tied in points going into the final round, scout rather than raid. The fuel payoff at game end (1 point per unused fuel) makes scouting viable, and raiding risks backfiring against an escort. Maintain the equilibrium unless you're significantly behind.

If you're significantly behind at round 5, raid to try to swing the game, accepting the risk that it may fail or cost you points against an escort. But only if you have enough fuel to afford it.

When public pledges show three scouts already committed, do not raid in act stage—it's inefficient against prepared defenses. Commit to scout yourself instead. The guaranteed 3 points from scout plus the fuel payoff at game end (1 point per unused fuel) is superior to the failed raid outcome.

## General Strategy
Never overcommit to escort early. Escorting costs 2 fuel for only 2 points and provides no resource generation. It's only valuable late in the game when you need to protect a lead, and only if you can afford the fuel cost.

Avoid raid-pledge followed by scout-act confusion. If you pledge a raid, commit to it in act stage—don't abandon it just because public pledges look unfavorable. You've already paid the fuel.

Once a symmetric scout/scout/scout pattern establishes across multiple rounds, trust it. Players converge on this pattern because it's stable and fair. Breaking it to gain an edge early rarely pays off once others are coordinated.

The scout/scout/scout equilibrium leads to consistent 18-point ties across different opponents and different games. This is a reliable baseline outcome. Do not deviate from it unless you are explicitly behind at round 4 or 5. When all players are matched and symmetric, the equilibrium is self-reinforcing—continue scouting through round 5.

In act stage, honor the established pattern. If three scouts are pledged and you pledged scout, act scout. The fuel preservation and guaranteed points are worth more than attempting a raid against coordinated defense. Your four 18-point games came from maintaining this discipline.
