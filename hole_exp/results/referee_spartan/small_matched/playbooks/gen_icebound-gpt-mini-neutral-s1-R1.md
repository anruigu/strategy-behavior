When I am asked to pledge and I have low fuel that only allows scout, I pledge scout and act scout.

When I am leading on points and facing potential raids from my right (clockwise neighbor), I pledge escort and act escort to block theft and lock in 2 points.

When I am leading and opponents are repeatedly scouting, I prefer escort over risky raids to protect the lead and conserve fuel for late-game value.

When I am behind and can afford a raid, I pledge raid only if my clockwise target has a strong tendency to scout that round (no escort history) and I can afford both the pledge cost and potential future actions.

When multiple players pledged scout and I have fuel, I pledge raid and act raid to exploit light defense — but only if my target is unlikely to be escorted.

When my target has recently used escort or there’s reason to expect an escort, I do not pledge raid (it’s a poor ROI and costs fuel even if blocked).

When three-way scouting is common, I favor timely raids for big swings but avoid burning my last fuel on a raid that, if blocked, leaves me stranded for final scoring.

When I pledge X, I always act X — do not change act mid-round (the act must match the pledge).

When fuel is scarce (one or two left) I prioritize actions that preserve at least one fuel for the final rounds unless I need a single decisive raid to win.

When opponents show predictable patterns (repeat scouting), I exploit that with targeted raids; when opponents are mixed or random, I prefer escorts and scouts to avoid costly failures.

Do not pledge raids impulsively late in the game if it will reduce my end-of-game unused-fuel bonus below what I’d gain by saving fuel; sometimes the unused-fuel point is more valuable than a risky raid.

What did not work and I avoid: pledging raid without ensuring the raid is likely to land, over-committing fuel early so I run out for the final round, and ever pledging and then acting differently (illegal and unreliable).
