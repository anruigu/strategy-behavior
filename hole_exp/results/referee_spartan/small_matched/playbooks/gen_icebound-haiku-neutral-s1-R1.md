# ICEBOUND PLAYBOOK

**Round 1 Strategy:**
When I enter Round 1, I pledge scout. It costs only 1 fuel, scores 3 points if unraided, and gives me a baseline understanding of opponent behavior. At the act stage, if all three players have publicly pledged scout, I raid instead—this exploits the vulnerability of coordinated scouts and scores well early.

**Rounds 2-3 Strategy:**
When I'm tied or close in points with opponents, I pledge scout to maintain equilibrium and preserve fuel flexibility. When I'm behind, I pledge scout as a safe catch-up mechanism.

At the act stage, when three scouts are publicly pledged, I do not automatically follow the pledge. Instead: If I have fuel remaining and raiding is affordable, I raid to gain 3 points (or 2 against an escort). Scouts are vulnerable and raiding them is my highest-leverage move.

**Mid-Game Escalation (Rounds 2-4):**
When I've raided successfully in earlier rounds, I can continue raiding in subsequent rounds if opponents remain predictable with scouts. However, when an opponent (like West in several games) builds a lead, I should not abandon my own scouting strategy purely to attack them. Raiding burns fuel without guaranteed success.

When I pledge scout and it remains scout at act time, I execute scout, not escape into a different action. Flip-flopping between pledge and act wastes fuel and loses the discipline advantage.

**Fuel Management:**
I track fuel across all three players. When I have significantly less fuel than opponents (e.g., 1-3 remaining while others have 4-5), I must make my remaining actions count. A single scout in the final round when I'm low on fuel is often a waste—I should raid or commit to an escort if it serves a clear purpose.

When I have only 1 fuel remaining, scout is my only real option; do not pledge raid if I cannot afford it.

**Final Round (Round 5):**
When the final round arrives and I'm trailing by 1-3 points, raiding is tempting but risky. If opponents have consistently played scout, raiding might succeed. However, I have lost games by raiding when I should have scouted—the safe play of scout + 3 points often wins over the risky play of raid + potential -1 or 0 points against an escort.

When tied or leading in the final round, I should scout, not raid. Protecting my position is stronger than gambling on disruption.

**What Did Not Work:**
I have scored poorly when I played too much escort early on (Round 1 in episode 2 where I acted escort instead of scout). Escort in early rounds locks me into defensive play when I could be building points. Reserve escort for mid-to-late game when I need to protect a lead or counter a known raider.

I have also underperformed when I misread the final round. Pledging raid in Round 5 when I could not afford it (episode 2) or when I had already spent down to 0 fuel wasted my chance to act. The final round must be carefully calculated before I pledge.

**Pattern Recognition:**
Opponents tend to play scout heavily, creating predictable vulnerability to raids. Exploit this for Rounds 2-3 while they are still building baseline points. By Round 4, if I have raided twice successfully, opponents may begin to escort or raid back—adjust accordingly by returning to scout for stability.
