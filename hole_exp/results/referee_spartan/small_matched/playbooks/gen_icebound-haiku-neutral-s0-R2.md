# Icebound Playbook

## Early Game (Rounds 1-2)

When I'm at full fuel and scores are equal, I pledge scout. Scout is cheap (1 fuel), safe, and gives me information about opponent behavior patterns.

When three scouts are already publicly pledged and I have high fuel, I act scout. This maintains symmetry, preserves fuel, and avoids unnecessary commitment to defensive or aggressive moves before I understand the table's tendencies.

## Mid Game (Rounds 3-4)

When scores remain tied and all players have been scouting consistently, I continue to scout in round 3. The pattern shows this creates stable equilibrium; breaking it early without clear advantage wastes fuel.

When I remain tied with opponents entering round 4 and all players have scouted three consecutive rounds, I pledge scout in round 4 as well. Raiding into an established scout pattern from equal position is inefficient—I preserve fuel for round 5 where conditions may have changed and I'll have better information.

When three scouts are publicly pledged in round 4 and I have committed to scout, I act scout. Maintain the pledge.

When I've pledged scout but the act stage shows I'm behind in points with only round 5 remaining, I stay committed to scout. Changing my action at the last moment undermines my strategy and wastes fuel on weak raids. Accept the position and preserve resources for the final round.

## Final Round (Round 5)

When scores are tied or I'm ahead going into round 5 with equal fuel and a four-round history of mutual scouting, I pledge scout. There is no clear advantage to raiding when positions are symmetric, and scout preserves optionality while maintaining the equilibrium.

When three scouts are publicly pledged in round 5, I act scout. The established pattern holds; breaking it in the final round with minimal fuel is wasteful.

When I'm behind in points going into round 5 but only slightly (1-2 points) and fuel is equal to opponents, I pledge scout rather than raid. A small deficit with symmetric fuel and a four-round scout history suggests equilibrium will hold; raiding with minimal fuel remaining is unlikely to overcome a small gap.

When I pledge scout in the final round and have minimal fuel (3-4 remaining), I act scout. Do not second-guess into a weak raid in the final act stage.

## Fuel Management

I track fuel carefully. Round 4 is not the place to break symmetry unless I have a clear point deficit to close. Raiding from equal position wastes fuel that could be better deployed in round 5.

When fuel is equal across all players and scores are tied through round 3, the most efficient strategy is to continue scouting. This preserves fuel for round 5 and forces opponents to break symmetry first if they intend to.

When I have low fuel (3-4 remaining) and multiple rounds left, I do not commit to expensive raids unless I have a concrete deficit to close or clear evidence that opponents have broken the scout pattern.

## What Did Not Work

Breaking symmetry in round 4 from an equal position wastes fuel. My episodes where I pledged raid in round 4 from equality did not improve my score compared to continuing scout through round 4 and preserving fuel.

Pledging raid in round 4 and then acting scout due to second-guessing leaves me neither ahead nor defended—I've signaled aggression while playing defensively, which is the worst position.

Playing aggressively in round 5 when scores are tied and fuel is symmetric does not improve outcomes. Tied positions with equal resources are best left symmetric.

## What Worked

Maintaining scout equilibrium through all five rounds when all players remain tied results in consistent 17-18 point finishes. This is my baseline strong performance.

Scout is the default position when information suggests equilibrium. The pattern holds until evidence shows otherwise.
