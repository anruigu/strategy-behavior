# Sovereign Vaults Playbook

## Declaration Phase

When you see your private yield, declare it truthfully. Declaring your actual yield avoids the -10 point audit penalty and establishes a credible pattern. False declarations are too risky relative to their benefit.

When you're significantly behind in points, do not try to catch up through deception in declarations. You've tried this and it doesn't work.

## Deposit Phase

When you've declared your yield, retain as much as possible. The scoring heavily favors retention (2 points per gold) over deposits (1 point per gold).

Deposit 0 every round unless you have a specific, immediate strategic reason to deposit. You have not found such a reason in any of the four episodes you've played.

Do not deposit to build "trust" or establish credibility early in the game. You tested this multiple times (depositing 1 in rounds 1-2 of earlier episodes) and it made no difference to opponent behavior. Opponents who deposit 0 and declare truthfully still win decisively. Trust-building deposits cost you 1 point per unit relative to retention and produce no measurable return.

When the other player has been depositing nothing while declaring their full yield, follow that same pattern. They are winning precisely because of this strategy. Copy it.

When you're behind in points, deposit less, not more. Deposits don't help you catch up because they only score 1 point per unit.

When it's the final round, deposit 0. You have no future rounds to account for, so the choice is pure arithmetic: retain everything since 2 points per unit beats 1 point per unit with no future cooperation to gain.

## General Strategy

Your goal is to maximize your own score. This is not a cooperative game where mutual benefit emerges. It's an individual score race where retention dominates.

Declare truthfully to avoid audit risk, then deposit 0 and retain everything. This is your dominant strategy and it has produced your best results (92 points in episode 2).

Do not second-guess this strategy based on being behind. Being behind does not change the arithmetic of retention vs. deposits. The player ahead of you is ahead precisely because they retained aggressively while you deposited. Matching their retention strategy is how you compete, not by depositing more.
