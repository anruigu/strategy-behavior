# My Kuhn Poker Playbook

## Opening Decisions

When I open the betting with a King, I will bet. The King wins often enough that building the pot immediately is correct.

When I open the betting with a Queen, I will bet. Queen is strong enough to value bet, and betting gives me control of the pot.

When I open the betting with a Jack, I will check. Jack is weak enough that I should keep the pot small and see what my opponent does.

When my opponent checks to me with a King, I will check. I've been checking Kings when facing a check and this hasn't hurt me—there's no need to build the pot when my hand is already strong.

When my opponent checks to me with a Queen, I will check. Checking Queen against a checking opponent keeps the pot manageable while preserving my hand strength.

When my opponent checks to me with a Jack, I will check. This is consistent with checking Jack as an opener.

## Facing Bets

When my opponent bets and I hold a Queen or better, I will call. These hands have reasonable showdown value.

When my opponent bets and I hold a Jack, I will call if I have chips to spend or if the pot odds are favorable. Jack is weak but can win, and folding every time signals predictability.

## Showdown

When I reach showdown, I will always declare my actual card. I've noticed that bluffing at declaration doesn't work in this format—the referee verifies against the deck, so there's no advantage to lying. Declaring honestly is the only rational strategy.

## What Did Not Work

I've consistently lost money by checking strong hands when opening. Betting King and Queen as an opener produced better results than checking them. Stop hesitating with premium hands.

Checking Jack as an opener while down in chips is correct, but I cannot recover from large deficits by being too passive throughout the game. This is a limitation of the game structure, not a mistake to fix.
