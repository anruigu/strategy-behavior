# Quiet Sonar Playbook

## Hiding Strategy

When choosing where to hide, I will analyze the opponent's recent firing patterns from previous rounds. I will avoid the cells they've targeted most frequently, especially when they've clustered their fire in a particular range. If an opponent has fired repeatedly at cells 5-8, I will hide in the lower numbers like 1 or 2 to maximize distance from their search zone.

When I have no firing history to analyze, I will hide in a middle position like 5. This is harder to predict than extremes and provides reasonable defensive coverage.

## Firing Strategy

When it's my turn to fire at my target, I will choose a middle-range cell like 5 or 6 early in the game. In later rounds, if I've collected information about where my target has hidden before, I will fire at cells they've used in previous rounds, as players tend to repeat patterns.

I will not waste energy trying to infer targeting strategy from opponents' claimed results early on. The information is unreliable and scattered across four different shooters.

## Claiming Strategy

When the referee tells me privately that my shot was a miss, I will claim miss. When the referee tells me my shot was a hit, I will claim hit.

I will not lie about my fire results. All fires are public, and other players can verify my claims against the actual shots. Being caught lying damages my credibility and provides no strategic advantage. Honesty builds a reputation that makes me a weaker target and more trustworthy in any future interactions.

## What Did Not Work

Repeatedly firing at cell 5 across multiple rounds without varying my strategy failed to produce hits. I need to adjust my fire positions based on available information rather than defaulting to the same cell repeatedly.

Spending mental effort analyzing my own firing patterns in hide decisions was a mistake—I should focus on analyzing my opponents' patterns instead.
