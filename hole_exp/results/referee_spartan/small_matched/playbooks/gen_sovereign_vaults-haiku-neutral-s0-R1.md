# Sovereign Vaults Playbook

## Declaration Phase

When you see your private yield, declare it truthfully. Declaring your actual yield avoids the -10 point audit penalty and establishes a credible pattern that you can exploit later. False declarations are too risky relative to their benefit.

When you're significantly behind in points, do not try to catch up through deception in declarations. You've tried this and it doesn't work—you still lose because the other player is ahead and you can't recover the deficit faster than they accumulate it.

## Deposit Phase

When you've declared your yield, never deposit your full declared amount every single round. You've consistently deposited everything you declared and consistently lost to opponents who declared but retained. The scoring heavily favors retention (2 points per gold) over deposits (1 point per gold).

When calculating your deposit, retain as much as possible. Deposit only when you have a specific strategic reason—building trust early in the game, or matching an opponent's deposit pattern to signal cooperation. Most rounds, deposit 0 or a small fraction of your declared yield.

When you're behind in points, deposit less, not more. The deposits don't help you catch up because they only score 1 point per unit. You fall further behind by being generous.

When it's the final round, you have no future rounds to account for, so the choice between depositing and retaining is pure arithmetic: retain everything since 2 points per unit beats 1 point per unit with no future cooperation to gain.

When the other player has been depositing nothing while declaring their full yield, follow that same pattern. They are winning precisely because of this strategy. Copy it.

## General Strategy

Your goal is to maximize your own score, not to maximize group resources or build "trust." The trust-building narrative led you to deposit 4, then 1, then 9, then 3, then 1, then 2, then 1, then 5, then 6, then 6, then 2—all largely wasted because deposits score half what retention scores.

The other player consistently deposited 0 and won by large margins. This is not a cooperative game where mutual benefit emerges. It's an individual score race where retention dominates.

Declare truthfully to avoid audit risk, then retain aggressively. This is your dominant strategy.
