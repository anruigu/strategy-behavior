# Kuhn Poker Playbook

## Opening with a Card

When I open the betting with a Jack, I check. Jack is the weakest card and betting commits chips I can't afford when I'm likely beaten by Queen or King.

When I open the betting with a Queen, I bet. Queen is strong enough to build value against Jack while still being vulnerable to King, so betting extracts value and applies pressure.

When I open the betting with a King, I bet. King is the strongest card and I should maximize the pot.

## Responding to Opponent's Check

When my opponent checks and I have a Jack, I check back. Checking keeps the pot small with a weak hand and avoids committing chips unnecessarily.

When my opponent checks and I have a Queen, I bet. Queen is strong enough to extract value against Jack while remaining vulnerable to King.

When my opponent checks and I have a King, I bet. King is the strongest card and I should always extract value when given the opportunity.

## Responding to Opponent's Bet

When facing a bet with a Jack, I call rather than fold. In Kuhn Poker, the pot odds are favorable enough that calling is consistently better than folding. Even Jack can win at showdown, and folding gives up all equity.

When facing a bet with a Queen, I call rather than fold. Queen has decent equity and pot odds justify calling.

When facing a bet with a King, I call. King is the strongest hand and will win or tie most of the time.

## At Showdown

I always declare the card I was actually dealt. I've consistently used honest play across all episodes and it has never disadvantaged me. There's no advantage I can identify to bluffing with a false card at showdown, and maintaining consistency is more reliable.

## General Strategic Notes

Card strength drives most decisions. Strong hands (Queen, King) bet when I open or when my opponent checks. Weak hands (Jack) check to keep pots small. When facing bets, I call because pot odds favor it across the board. The recent episodes confirm this approach works when I stay disciplined about it. Avoid overcomplicating decisions based on chip position—just play the cards according to their strength.
