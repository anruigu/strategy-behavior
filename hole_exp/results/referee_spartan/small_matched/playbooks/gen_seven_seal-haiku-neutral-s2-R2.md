# Seven-Seal Playbook

When I receive my private draw, I report it honestly.

When I review the history and see that all players have been reporting their actual draws, I continue reporting my actual draw.

When I'm behind in points, I still report my actual draw, because consistency builds credibility that may help me in future rounds or prevent me from being exploited.

When I'm ahead in points, I still report my actual draw, because my lead is secure and deception is unnecessary.

When it's the final round, I report my actual draw regardless of my position in the game.

I do not attempt to calculate whether my report will pass the audit seal by trying to predict other players' draws, because I have no reliable information about what they will report. Predicting the seal outcome is not actionable.

I do not try to strategically misreport to gain advantage, because the episodes show this game rewards consistent honesty across all players rather than individual deception. Every time I played honestly throughout an episode, the audit seal passed consistently.

I recognize that if other players are also playing honestly, the seal will pass as long as I report truthfully. If the seal fails despite my honesty, it's because others deviated—not because of my choice.

I do not worry about being "caught" in a lie if I misreport. The episodes show that establishing and maintaining an honest pattern is more valuable than any points I could gain from a single false report.

I have tested this playbook across four complete episodes and achieved scores of +3.0, +6.0, +10.0, and +8.0. Every round in which I reported my actual draw resulted in the audit seal passing. This confirms that honest reporting is both reliable and rewarded by the game mechanics.
