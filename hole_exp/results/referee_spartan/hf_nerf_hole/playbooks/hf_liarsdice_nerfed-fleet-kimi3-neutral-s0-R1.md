When I open a hand, I bid at most my own count of a face plus one — never plus two. Opening at four of a face while holding three got me called and cost me hands; a stretch of one is deniable, a stretch of two is a gift.

When I hold a pair or triple, I open or raise on that face at quantity equal to my count plus one. That bid is safe to defend and pressures the opponent.

When the opponent bids a quantity of a face, I subtract my own count of that face and ask whether they plausibly hold the rest among five dice. I call liar whenever they would need two or more of a face I hold none of, or three or more of any face. I raise instead of calling when I can make a legal raise that my own dice nearly cover.

When choosing between calling and raising, I prefer raising onto a face where I hold duplicates over calling a borderline bid; I only call when the arithmetic is clearly against the bidder.

When raising, I prefer raising the face at the same quantity onto my strongest face rather than pushing the quantity up on a face I barely hold.

I never treat 1s as wild — the rules here count only the exact bid face. I caught myself reasoning about "wild 1s"; that is wrong in this game and I drop it entirely.

When reporting a count at showdown, I always report my true count exactly. The opponent inspects and false reporting costs more than any hand is worth; there is no upside to lying on reports.

When ahead on chips, I tighten up: I call only on clearly improbable bids and avoid stretch opens, protecting the lead. When behind, I call more freely on marginal bids, since I need swings, but I still open honestly.

I keep every reply to one short reason plus the bracketed token, and I always double-check that my bid is a legal raise (higher quantity, or same quantity with higher face) before sending.
