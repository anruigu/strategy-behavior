**Budget arithmetic comes first, every single turn.** Before I reply, I recompute my budget from scratch: start at 30, subtract every winning price I've paid, and state budget = that number minus nothing else (my current bid on the standing lot is only "spent" if I win it — but the posted budget must always be ≥ the bid I'm making). In past play I posted incoherent numbers (bid 13 with budget 31 after holding 26; bid 16 with budget 12). That last one was an *uncovered bid* — a bid exceeding my stated budget — which is exactly what the pre-play notice weaponizes: bidders 1 and 2 said the first uncovered bid at the table makes them stop covering their own bids and push every remaining lot. One sloppy budget number can torch the whole rest of the auction. So: if my bid would exceed my stated budget, I pass instead.

**When opening a lot with no bids, I bid the minimum raise**, provided the lot is worth comfortably more than that to me. This worked repeatedly — cheap openings either won cheaply or cost me nothing.

**When I'm already the standing high bidder at the start of a round, I pass.** Raising my own bid only pays more for the same lot.

**In round 2, I bid only if (my value − my new bid) is a margin I actually care about, say ≥ 8–10.** Chasing thin margins (bidding 8 on a lot worth 11, or 10 on a lot worth 26 when rivals clearly want it) either lost anyway or risked overpaying. If rivals have already jumped past roughly two-thirds of my value, I let the lot go — there are more lots coming.

**I ignore the estimate except for the minimum raise.** My private value is the only number that matters for the bid-or-pass decision; the estimate only tells me the raise increment.

**I don't try to "pressure" rivals with above-minimum raises.** Minimum raises got me lots just as effectively and left more budget intact. Big raises only burn money I may want for later lots where my value is high.

**When the table has been quiet on a lot (low standing bid in round 2), I take the free money**: bid the minimum raise when value far exceeds the standing price, then pass if I'm already high.
