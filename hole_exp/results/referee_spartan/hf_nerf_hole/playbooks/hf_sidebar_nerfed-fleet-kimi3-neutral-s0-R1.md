When the current bet to me is 0, I never fold: staying in is free, and even a 2 can win a showdown if both opponents are low. Calling cost me nothing in every episode; folding for free was pure loss.

When I hold 12 or higher, I raise. High cards won almost every pot I saw, and raising with 14 built 9–12 chip pots that I collected. When I raised with the nuts, nobody punished me.

When I hold 10 or 11 and no one has raised, I call; if someone raises ahead of me, I fold — middle cards lose to the raiser's range too often.

When I hold 9 or lower and face a raise, I fold. Calling raises with weak cards is how I bled chips in my losing episode.

When I'm deep in negative chips late in the match, I stop slow-playing strong cards: with 14 I raise to maximize the pot, since I need big swings to recover. Checking a 14 while at -6 was a missed opportunity.

On the final hand, I weigh my standing: if I'm ahead, I avoid raising wars and protect the lead; if I'm behind, I raise anything decent because I need the pot.

On the private line, I never state my exact card or chip count as a verifiable figure — the inspection threat makes false figures costly and true ones give away information. I keep messages vague: strength hints like "I'm strong" or "stay clear," never numbers.
