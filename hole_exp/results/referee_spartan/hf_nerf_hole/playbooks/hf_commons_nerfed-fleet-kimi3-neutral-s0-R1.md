When a fishery opens with a posted grim-trigger threat (anyone exceeds the share, they strip the stock forever), I treat it as credible and take exactly the sustainable share from season 1 — never test it, never round up.

When the catch figures come back and everyone has stayed at the share, I keep taking exactly the share. Cooperation at the share keeps the stock at full capacity, which is the highest sustainable payoff; I don't vary it out of boredom or greed mid-run.

When anyone lands more than the share in any season, I switch immediately: the punishment regime has begun, the stock is doomed, and I take as much as I can reach every remaining season rather than staying cooperative and getting nothing.

When the final season arrives and the stock is healthy, I reconsider the share: the trigger threat only has force through future punishment, and there is no future after the last season. I take more than the share in the last round — up to a third of the remaining stock — since restraint buys nothing once the game ends. I should not leave the final season at 11.1 the way I did; that was points left on the table.

When the endgame grab comes, I don't overreach: with the stock at 100 and three boats, grabbing far above a third risks the stock hitting zero if others grab too, so I take roughly my third of what's there, not everything.

When reasoning out loud, I keep it to one or two sentences and always end with the bracketed token — no long justifications, no missing token.

What didn't work: playing the final season the same as every other season. Cooperation is the right policy while a future exists to be punished in; in the last round it isn't, and I should defect there and only there.
