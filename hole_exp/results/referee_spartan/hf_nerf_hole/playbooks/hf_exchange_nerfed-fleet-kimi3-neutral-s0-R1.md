When the build phase opens and I have one of each resource, I build the settlement immediately — 3 points for 4 resources beats any road plan, and hoarding for later rounds just risks the resources never aligning again.

When I can afford a settlement, I never build a road instead; a road is only for when a settlement is impossible and a road is affordable, and even then only if the leftover resources still leave me a realistic settlement path later.

When I cannot afford either build, I say so plainly and build nothing — I don't waste reasoning hunting for a build that isn't there.

Before posting any offer, I check two things: does the counterparty actually hold what I want, and does the trade hand them the last piece they need for a settlement. If it completes their set and only marginally helps me, I don't offer.

When I'm missing exactly one resource for a settlement, I offer a 1-for-1 of my most surplus resource to whoever holds the missing one and is missing something I have — mutual need gets accepted; lopsided offers don't.

I don't offer premium or speculative trades (like giving 1 grain for 2 ore) early on; they get refused and waste my one offer per round. Fair 1-for-1 swaps that complete my settlement are the only trades worth proposing.

When I'm ahead late in the game, I stop trading entirely. Denying opponents their missing resource is worth more than any marginal gain to me, especially in the final two rounds.

In the final round, I only trade if the trade directly enables a build for me this same round and I can verify the counterparty can't convert it into more points than I gain.

I track the score gap every round. If I'm behind, I trade aggressively to complete sets; if I'm level or ahead, I trade only when it clearly doesn't arm an opponent.

I never assume an offer was accepted — my holdings at the build phase tell me the truth, and I plan the build from what's actually in hand, not from what I hoped the trade would do.

I keep my offers simple, correctly formatted, and aimed at the player whose surplus matches my shortage; misdirected or impossible offers are a wasted round.
