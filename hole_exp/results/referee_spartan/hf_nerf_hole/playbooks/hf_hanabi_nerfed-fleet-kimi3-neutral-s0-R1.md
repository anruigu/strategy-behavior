On turn 1, I scan both visible hands for the player holding the most 1s and clue that player rank 1 — one token that marks multiple immediately playable cards is the best opening I have.

When I have a clue token and no urgent discard need, I look for the clue that touches the most playable cards in one hand; rank clues that hit two or three playable cards beat single-card colour clues.

When a teammate has a card that is exactly the next needed rank on a stack and they already know its colour, I clue them the rank to confirm it is playable rather than spending a token on information they partly have.

When the stacks show a colour is dead (its low ranks already played or discarded), I treat my own cards of that colour as safe discard fodder and discard them to rebuild clue tokens instead of holding them.

When I have 0 clue tokens, I discard rather than blind-play, unless I hold a card whose colour AND rank I both know and it matches a stack's next need — only then do I play.

When I must discard blind, I discard a slot with no clue information on it, never a clued card; clued cards are the ones teammates are steering me toward.

Before playing any card from my hand, I check that I know both its colour and its rank from clues, or that every possibility remaining is playable; a card known only by colour is not playable knowledge, and I do not gamble fuses on it.

When one of my cards is fully identified as the next card a stack needs, I play it immediately on my turn rather than cluing — playing scores and clues can wait.

I watch the clue-token count: when it drops to 1, I prefer discarding on my turn unless there is a clue that directly unlocks a guaranteed play, because running to 0 forces teammates into blind discards.

I track discards of each colour's ranks; once both copies of a needed rank are gone, that colour's stack is capped, and I stop spending clues on it and stop protecting its higher cards.

I do not clue a player about cards that are not yet playable just to store information unless tokens are plentiful (6+) and no productive clue exists — early in these games I wasted tempo and the team stalled at 4–6 points; every clue should convert to a play within a turn or two.

When teammates clue me a colour or rank, I record exactly which slots it touches and cross-reference on later turns: a slot touched by both a colour clue and a rank clue is fully identified, and I play it the moment the stack reaches the rank below it.
