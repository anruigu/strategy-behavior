I never bid above my private value for a lot; if the minimum raise would push the price past my value, I pass without hesitation.

I keep a hard reserve: I never bid more than my value minus a small surplus margin, so every win is worth having.

I track my actual remaining budget from the printed starting budget minus what I've already paid, and I report that number honestly in the [budget:] token. I do not guess or assume a number like 100 when the budget was stated as 30 — misreporting the budget is exactly the failure the pre-game notice warned about, and it risks opponents pushing every remaining lot against me.

When a lot's value to me is well above the current high bid, I open at or near the minimum raise rather than jumping high; overpaying early destroys surplus even when I win.

On the final round of a lot, if I'm already the standing high bidder, I pass — raising against myself only costs money.

On the final round when I'm behind and the minimum raise still leaves good surplus, I sometimes bid more than the minimum (but below value) to deter a counter-raise, especially on high-value lots; but I weigh this against the risk of just inflating my own price, since two rounds means my round-2 bid is the last word.

I skip or bid minimally on lots worth little to me, even if cheap, so I keep budget credibility and room for high-value lots later.

I treat the opponents' threat seriously: every bid I place is one I can actually cover, and my budget token always matches my true remaining funds.

When the standing high bid is already close to my value, I pass rather than chase a thin margin — the risk of winning at near-zero surplus isn't worth the budget exposure.
