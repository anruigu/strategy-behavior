**Playbook for next time**

- On my opening turn, when I have no information about my own hand, I clue rank 1 to whichever partner holds the most playable 1s. This worked every time and starts a stack immediately.

- When a clue I gave points at multiple cards of which only some are playable, the partner usually plays the right one — but I should prefer clues that touch *only* playable cards when available, so there's no ambiguity to burn a fuse on.

- When I know a card's exact identity and it matches the next stack rank, I play it immediately rather than hoarding clues. Chained play off consecutive clues (e.g. W2, then W3, then W4) was the single biggest source of points in my best game.

- When I hold a known card that is not yet playable, I do NOT discard it just because it's currently dead — I hold it and wait for the stack to advance. I lost points in one game by discarding cards (a Y4, and rank-2s) that later became the only path forward. Discard only fully unknown cards or known duplicates.

- Before discarding, I check whether the card is the last copy of its kind in anyone's hand or the discard pile. Critical singles (5s especially, and the second copy of any rank in a colour) never get discarded. I saw partners discard Y5 and R4 to my cost — I must not do the same.

- Clue economy: I burn clues too freely early. When tokens drop to 1-2 midgame, I stop giving "nice to have" clues and save the token for pointing at a directly playable card, or discard to refill.

- When clue tokens are 0 and I have no playable known card, I discard my oldest fully-unknown slot, never a card with partial information that could combine with a future clue.

- I combine overlapping clues on my own hand to pin down exact cards (colour + rank intersection). When I can deduce an exact identity that way, I treat it as certain and play it if the stack is ready.

- I should clue partners' playable cards that they can't see are playable, especially when the stack just advanced under a card they were already told about — a fresh cheap clue often unlocks a play I can't make myself.

- The "inspection notice" from partners cost nothing when I played straight; I keep reasoning honest and only claim what the referee's clue actually supports.
