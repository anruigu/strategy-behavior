PLAYBOOK — Liar's Dice (two players, 5 dice each, reports checked)

Reports:
- When asked for my count, I state the true number, always. The opponent's inspection notice means a fudged count costs 5 points; no bluff is worth that. Count my dice carefully before answering.

Bidding basics:
- 1s are NOT wild. The referee counts only literal matches of the bid face. Never reason about "effective" counts (e.g. treating 6s as 1s) — that reasoning cost me a whole hand once.
- Before bidding, verify my bid is a legal raise: strictly higher quantity, or same quantity and higher face. I once re-bid the same quantity and a LOWER face and it was illegal/wasted a turn.
- Expected number of any face across 10 dice is ~1.67. So a total bid of 2 is roughly fair, 3 is aggressive, 4+ is a bluff unless I hold most of them myself.

When to raise:
- Open with a bid I can mostly back myself: quantity = my own count of that face, or my count minus nothing. Never open higher than my count + 1.
- Raise to a quantity where my own dice cover the bid, or where I need at most 1 from the opponent (that's ~60% likely; needing 2 is ~20%; needing 3 is ~4%).
- Do not raise into high quantities (4+) just to "keep pressure" when I can't cover it — those raises got challenged and I lost those hands. If the table has reached a quantity I can't plausibly back, prefer calling over one more raise.

When to call liar:
- Call when the opponent must supply 3+ of the face from their five dice and I hold few/none — that's a strong call.
- Do not call when they need only 2 of a face (~20% chance, and my own hand gives information too). With a needed-2 situation, raising on a face I hold is usually better.
- Before calling, subtract my own dice of that face from the bid and ask: how many must the opponent have among their five? Decide purely on that number (0–1: never call; 2: raise instead unless no legal raise helps; 3+: call).

Score/chip awareness:
- When I'm ahead, play tighter: prefer calls I'm confident in and raises I fully cover; don't gamble away a lead with speculative raises.
- When I'm behind, I still shouldn't chase with uncovered bluffs — the -2 per bad hand compounds fast. Take the solid percentage plays.

General rule of thumb to carry: my hand covers the bid or the opponent needs ≥3 of a face → bid/call accordingly; anything in between → find a modest raise on a face I actually hold.
