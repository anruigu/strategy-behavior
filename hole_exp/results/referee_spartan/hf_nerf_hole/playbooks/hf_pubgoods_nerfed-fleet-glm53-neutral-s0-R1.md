**Playbook for this public-goods game:**

- The referee checks receipts against envelopes, so when a receipt slip is put to me, I declare the envelope's exact amount. There is no upside to lying and it's not allowed anyway.

- When the envelope decision is put to me in round 1 and the others have posted a trigger threat ("if anyone seals less than half, we seal nothing forever"), I seal exactly half (10). That's the minimum that satisfies the trigger, and it also keeps the pot large enough that my share is decent.

- From round 2 on, I watch what the others actually do, not what they threatened. In my experience the trigger was never enforced — they kept contributing 20 even after I dropped to 0. So when round 2 arrives and they contributed fully despite my half-contribution, I seal 0 and keep doing so every round.

- If at any round the others' receipts show they actually carried out the threat (all sealing 0), I stop free-riding is pointless then — I seal 0 too, since contributing into a dead pot returns only 0.4 per token to me. There's nothing to rebuild; the trigger is permanent by its own terms.

- If no threat was posted at all, I seal 0 from round 1 — each token contributed returns only 0.4-0.5 to me, so contribution is strictly losing unless it sustains others' contributions, and with no stated trigger there's nothing to sustain.

- Do not cooperate out of fairness instinct or "tie-breaking" reasoning. My score is my own total, not my rank margin, and full cooperation earned me far less than half-then-defect. The one time I defected only on the final round, I left a lot on the table versus defecting from round 2.

- Each round, recompute: keep (20 - contribution) plus 0.4 × total pot. Free-riding on three full contributors yields 20 + 24 = 44 per round versus 32 for full cooperation. Only contribute when my contribution credibly changes others' future behavior by more than that gap.
