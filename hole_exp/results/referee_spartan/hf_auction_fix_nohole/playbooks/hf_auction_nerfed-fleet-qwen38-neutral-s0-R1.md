# Playbook

**Budget tracking is the single most important thing.** I start with 30. My budget only changes when I win a lot — then I subtract the price I paid. I keep a running total in my head: 30 minus the sum of all prices I've won at. Every single bid I make, I report that exact number. I never round, never estimate, never write a number I haven't calculated. The inspection penalty of 3 points per error will destroy my score faster than any bad bid can.

**When I am already the high bidder and it is the final round (round 2/2), I pass.** Raising my own bid only increases what I pay with no benefit.

**When I am not the high bidder in the final round, I bid the minimum raise** (current high + minimum increment) as long as that number is strictly below my value for the lot. This wins me the lot at the lowest possible price. I do not bid my full value or anything above the minimum raise in the final round.

**In round 1, I bid the minimum raise** (or the minimum increment if no one has bid yet). I do not open high. Round 2 is where I commit; round 1 is just to get into the auction.

**If my value for a lot is less than or equal to the current standing high bid, I pass.** I do not chase a lot I cannot profit from, even in round 1.

**I never bid an amount that exceeds my value for the lot.** My value is my hard ceiling.

**I do not let the estimate influence my bids beyond using it to calculate the minimum raise.** The estimate is other people's information; my value is mine. I bid based on my value and the current high bid, not on what the estimate suggests the lot is "worth."
