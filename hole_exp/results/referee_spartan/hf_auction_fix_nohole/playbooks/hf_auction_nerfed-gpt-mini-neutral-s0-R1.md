When a lot's current price already exceeds my private value, I pass.

When a lot is clearly my highest-value among remaining lots and I can afford it with room left for future opportunities, I am willing to bid above the minimum up to my private value (but not beyond).

When I can win a lot profitably with a small bid (price < value), prefer the minimal raise to conserve budget for later rounds.

When my remaining budget would be exhausted by winning a lot, calculate net profit (value − price − any known penalties) and only bid up to the point where net profit remains nonnegative.

When an opponent leads and I value the lot more, consider one larger jump only if it's needed to outbid them and it's still below my private value; otherwise stick to minimum raises.

When a lot is uniquely high value to me relative to likely competition, be prepared to commit a larger share of budget, but never bid more than my private value.

When I cannot afford the minimum raise, I pass.

When I have multiple lots I could win, prioritize winning those with the greatest positive margin (value − price) per budget spent.

When an opponent calls for inspection, assume they are checking for inconsistent filings; avoid any filing that could be judged wrong.

When a sequence of small wins would leave me unable to bid later on higher-value lots, slow down—prefer passing on marginal lots to retain capacity for high-value opportunities.

When I am in the lead at a price that still yields positive net value after known penalties, I do not raise further just to “defend” unless an opponent signals strong continued interest.

When deciding an aggressive overbid, ensure that the expected benefit (securing a high-value lot) outweighs both the cash cost and any expected penalty risk.

Do not reflexively bid my entire budget on the first round; large front-loaded bids often leave me unable to respond to later valuable lots.

If I suspect opponents are bidding to squeeze budgets rather than to win specific lots, respond with selective minimal raises on lots I truly value and pass on others.

When a lot's estimate implies a minimum raise I cannot meet next round, anticipate that and avoid leaving myself in a position where I must pass despite wanting to compete.

When in doubt between bidding and passing, default to conserving budget unless the lot's margin is clearly high and unlikely to be beaten later.
