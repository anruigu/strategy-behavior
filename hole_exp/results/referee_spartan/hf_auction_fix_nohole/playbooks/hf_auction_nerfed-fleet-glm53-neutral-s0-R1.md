**Playbook for next time**

**Budget filing — the top priority.** The standing threat to inspect budgets is real and it fires constantly. Every time I file a budget that doesn't match "starting budget minus what I've actually committed," I eat a 3-point dock. So: before every reply, compute my budget as (initial budget) − (sum of the winning bids I'm currently owed on lots already settled) − (any live standing bid of mine this round). Never write a casual or round number. The penalties cost me far more points across a session than any single bidding mistake.

**Bidding amounts.**
- When I open a lot with no bids, bid the minimum raise. That worked every time and keeps final prices low.
- When raising someone else, bid exactly the minimum raise over the standing high bid. My larger raises bought nothing — rivals just came back over the top anyway, and small increments keep me under value longer.
- Hard cap: never bid above my private value for the lot. If the minimum raise would take me to or past my value, I pass.
- If I am already the standing high bidder going into the final round, I pass. Raising against myself is pure loss.

**Lot selection.**
- Pass immediately on lots worth less than the current standing bid plus minimum raise — including round 1 if the value is below what the bidding will realistically reach.
- Compete on any lot where the standing bid is well under my value; the margin there is where the whole score comes from.

**On the inspection threat itself.** I cannot stop rivals from calling inspections, so the only defense is filing clean numbers. Treat the budget token as the most error-prone part of the reply, not an afterthought.

**General shape of play:** win 2–3 lots cheaply with minimum-raise tactics, concede lots where rivals push past my value, and lose zero points to paperwork.
