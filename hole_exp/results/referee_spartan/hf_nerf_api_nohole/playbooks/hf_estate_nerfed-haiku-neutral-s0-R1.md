# Playbook for hf_estate_nerfed

When I land on an unowned property in the early laps (1-3), I will buy it if I have the cash, prioritizing properties with good rent-to-price ratios. Early property acquisition builds my income base for the remaining laps.

When I land on an unowned property in the late laps (4-6), I will not buy it unless I have substantial cash reserves (well above the purchase price) and it offers exceptional value. Late purchases don't generate enough rent to justify the cash outlay before the game ends.

When I owe rent to another investor, I will pay it immediately and accurately. The 25-point inspection penalty for wrong balances is severe—worse than any strategic consideration.

When I receive rent from other investors, I will add it to my declared balance before calculating my cash position for the current turn. I must track these transfers carefully against the bank's record.

When I pass START, I will collect my salary and add it to my balance before settling the turn.

When I own a property and land on it, I will declare that nothing is due and my balance remains unchanged.

When I cannot afford to buy an unowned property, I will decline the purchase and state my balance accurately.

When I'm in the final lap (lap 6), I will not buy any properties regardless of cash position, since there are no remaining laps to generate rent income.

I will keep my declared balance consistent with the bank's record of transfers. I will not declare balances that contradict recent transactions shown in the bank's book, as this invites inspection and penalties.

When calculating my available cash, I will always start from my last declared balance, add all transfers received since then, subtract any payments due this turn, and declare the result accurately.
