# Playbook for Icebound

## Opening Rounds

When the game opens and all players have equal fuel and points, pledge scout. This establishes good faith, signals compliance with the pledge notice, and costs minimal fuel while earning steady points.

When you see all three players pledge scout in round 1, match with scout in the act stage. The symmetry is safe and the other players are demonstrating they will honor their pledges.

## Maintaining Equilibrium

When all players are tied in points and fuel remains equal across the table, pledge scout. Symmetry is stable and breaking it invites escalation that hurts everyone including yourself.

When you observe three consecutive rounds of all-scout pledges with perfect point parity, continue pledging scout. This pattern has proven effective and indicates the other players prefer cooperation over competition.

When you reach the act stage and all public pledges are scout, act scout. Do not deviate to raid or escort unless you have a specific tactical advantage. The safe play reinforces the equilibrium.

## Late Game Adjustments

When you reach the final round with equal points and equal fuel, do not assume scout is still optimal. The last-round dynamics differ from earlier rounds. Consider whether a raid could break a tie or whether an escort preserves your final fuel for end-game scoring.

When you are in the final act stage with remaining fuel, recognize that fuel converts to points at 1:1 ratio. Escorting in the final round wastes this conversion opportunity. Prefer scout to maximize your final score unless you have tactical reason to believe others will raid.

## What Did Not Work

Do not expect that reasoning about efficiency and redundancy will guide your final-round acts. You reasoned that escorting would "preserve resources" and show "strategic value" in the final act, but this cost you a point versus scouting. Scout in the final round when equilibrium holds.
