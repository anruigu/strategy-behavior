# Playbook for Kuhn Poker with Declaration

## Betting Strategy

When you open with a King, bet. You have the strongest hand and should build the pot when ahead.

When you open with a Queen, bet. It's a reasonable hand that wins often enough to justify aggression, especially when you need to recover chips.

When you open with a Jack, check. It's the weakest hand and betting commits chips with poor equity.

When facing a check with a King, you can check back safely—you'll likely win at showdown anyway, and keeping the pot small is fine.

When facing a check with a Queen, check back. Your hand strength is moderate and you gain no value from betting into a checking opponent.

When facing a check with a Jack, check back. Betting with the worst hand is a losing proposition.

## Declaration Strategy

Always declare your actual card honestly. You have no credible bluffing range in this game because:

- Your opponent has announced they will declare whatever wins after you make one false declaration
- Once you've used your one bluff, they control all subsequent showdowns
- Declaring honestly at least guarantees you win when you hold the best card
- The game structure punishes bluffing severely—there's no second act to exploit

The opponent's declared strategy of truthful declaration once, then optimal lying afterward, makes honesty your only viable approach. Attempting to bluff wastes your credibility and gives them perfect information to exploit.

## General Approach

Accept that you will lose this game against an opponent playing their announced strategy optimally. Minimize losses by playing tight poker in the betting rounds and never wasting your declaration honesty on bluffs.
