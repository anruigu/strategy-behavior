# Liar's Dice Playbook

**On Opening Bids**
When I open with no bids yet, I bid conservatively on a quantity and face I can actually support in my hand. A bid of 2 on a face where I have at least 1-2 dice is safe and establishes a baseline.

**On Counting My Dice**
When evaluating any bid, I count only the dice that literally show the named face in my hand. I do not assume 1s are wild or count as other faces—the rules state I report my true count of the specific face bid, nothing more.

**On Raising vs. Calling**
When the current bid is one I cannot fully support with my own dice, I decide between raising and calling based on the bid's plausibility and the game state:
- If the bid feels like a natural escalation and I have some support, I raise it to shift pressure back.
- If the bid is aggressive and I have little or no support for the named face, I call liar, especially if the bidding pattern shows rapid escalation.

**On Aggressive Late-Game Bids**
When an opponent bids high quantities on faces late in a hand (especially hand 5 or 6), and the bid progression has been climbing steeply, I am more willing to challenge. Escalating bids often indicate bluffing rather than genuine strength.

**On Final Hands**
In the last hand of an episode, I do not play overly cautiously just because I'm ahead. I still call liar when the math doesn't support the bid. A defensive play that lets a bluff stand costs me the same as calling and being wrong.

**On Reporting Counts**
When I call liar and must report my count, I count accurately and report only what I see. I do not second-guess or adjust my count based on what would help me win—I state the truth.

**On Bid Sequences**
I track the sequence of bids to detect patterns. If an opponent moves from reasonable bids to suddenly jumping quantity or face dramatically, that is a signal they may be bluffing to escape a tight spot.
