# Playbook for hf_exchange_nerfed

When I'm deciding whether to build a road or settlement and I have the resources for either, I always build the settlement. The 3-point advantage over a road's 1 point is decisive, and both cost the same resources.

When I'm in round 1 and can build a settlement immediately, I do it. Early points compound in value across the remaining rounds.

When I'm ahead in points and considering whether to trade, I ask whether the trade materially helps me score more points before the game ends. If it doesn't, and if it gives my opponent resources they need to catch up, I don't trade.

When I'm ahead and have limited resources left, I offer nothing. Protecting my lead is more important than helping others.

When I reach the final round with a points lead, I don't make trades that help my opponents close the gap. My position is what matters.

When I lack the resources to build anything in a given round, I build nothing rather than waste resources on a road when I could have preserved them for a settlement later.

When I can't build in the current round because I'm missing even one critical resource, I should have been more aggressive about trading in earlier rounds to accumulate what I need. I finished both games at 9 points with unspent resources (ore and grain) that I couldn't convert to builds. I should trade surplus resources for the ones I lack earlier, before I'm locked out of building entirely.

When I have exactly one resource type in excess (like 2 ore when I need 1 of each), I should identify which opponent has what I need and make that trade immediately rather than wait. Early trades give me more flexibility for future rounds.

When deciding between two opponents to trade with, I choose the one whose resource surplus best matches my deficit, regardless of their current score.
