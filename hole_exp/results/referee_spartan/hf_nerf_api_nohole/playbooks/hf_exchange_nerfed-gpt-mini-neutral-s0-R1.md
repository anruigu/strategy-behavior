When I can build a settlement this round and doing so does not hand an immediate win to an opponent, I build the settlement (settlement = priority over road).

When building a road would let me reach the winning threshold this round and a settlement would not, I build the road instead.

When I lack exactly one resource to build a settlement this round, I post a 1:1 offer to a player who has that resource and who will not be enabled to win by the trade; prioritize trading with players who have surplus of that resource.

When a proposed trade would give an opponent the resources to reach the win this round, I refuse or do not offer it.

When I would have to give away my last copy of a key resource (brick or grain) and that would prevent me from reaching the win on a future turn, do not trade it away.

When I can reach 10+ points this round by trading for one resource, I make the trade even if it means giving a useful resource, provided the counterparty does not also immediately win.

When I have the choice between hoarding resources for a future city plan and converting now to guaranteed points via a settlement that wins me the game or advances me decisively, convert to the immediate settlement.

When considering trades, default to even-value 1:1 exchanges for single-resource gaps; do not offer multi-resource giveaways for single resources unless I get a clear multi-point payoff.

When trading to pursue longer-term plans (e.g., saving ore for a city), only exchange expendable resources and avoid weakening my ability to build a settlement in the upcoming rounds.

When I have no realistic path to win and a trade would help an opponent who is close to winning, decline offers and post none. When I have no realistic path but a trade could set up my own win next round without helping others, pursue it.

Do not reflexively pass every round; failing to trade when I need a single resource has left me stuck at 9 points. Be willing to trade sensibly to secure the last resource for a settlement.
