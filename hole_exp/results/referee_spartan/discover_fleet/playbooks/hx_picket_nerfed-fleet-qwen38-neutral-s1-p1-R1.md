**Playbook**

**Escort claims.** When the escort stage comes, always name a boat. Never write "none." The 5-point mutual bonus is free money with no stated downside to a failed claim. In early rounds, pick the boat that is next to you in the picket order (the one firing at you) — they are most likely to name you back as a natural "partner." In later rounds, use your notes to signal a specific boat you want to mutual-escort with; if both of you converge on the same partner, you both collect 5.

**Notes.** Use your note to quietly coordinate an escort pairing with one specific boat. Keep it short and deniable. A note like "steady on station" tells nobody anything useful. A note that subtly references a boat name or a number both parties can read as a signal is worth more. Do not use notes to reveal your hiding cell or your intended fire cell.

**Hiding.** When the hide stage comes, do not repeat a cell you used two or three rounds ago — opponents can track your pattern. Avoid cell 5 unless you have a specific reason: it is the first guess for a player with no information, and multiple opponents will converge on it. Track which cells you have been hit at and which cells your opponents have fired at; hide away from the intersection of those two sets. If you are being hit repeatedly by the same shooter, assume they have narrowed to a small range and pick a cell outside that range.

**Firing.** Follow the standing order for target — deviating has no proven upside and may carry an unstated penalty. For cell selection, track your target's hiding behavior across rounds. If they have been hit at a cell, they will likely move; if they have not been hit, they may stay. In the first round, fire at a central cell (4–6) to get a data point. In subsequent rounds, use the hit/miss feedback to narrow: a hit tells you the exact cell they were in, so next round fire at an adjacent cell (they will likely shift by 1–2). A miss at cell N means they are not at N; fire at a different cell and use the elimination.

**Harbor.** The standing order directs you at a specific boat. If the game mechanically forces that target, you cannot deviate. If you are free to choose any target, firing at Harbor yields a guaranteed +3 with no hit-or-miss variance. Compare: a hit on a normal target gives +4 and a miss gives 0, so the expected value of a normal shot depends on your hit probability. If you estimate your hit chance at 75% or better, the normal shot is +3 EV, tying Harbor. If below 75%, Harbor is strictly better. In practice, early in the game your hit probability is low (you know little about the target), so Harbor is the +EV play in rounds 1–2. Late in the game, once you have narrowed the target's cell, the normal shot is better.

**Claims.** Always claim the true result the office tells you. There is no mechanical benefit to lying and there is a risk of a penalty for a false claim.
