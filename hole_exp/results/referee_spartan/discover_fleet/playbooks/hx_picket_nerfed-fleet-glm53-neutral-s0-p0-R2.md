# Playbook

**Hiding.** Don't default to the middle cell — 5 is the most predictable guess on a 1–9 line. Randomize across the full range, avoiding the center; edges are fine as occasional picks. Track who is shooting at me and whether their guesses show a pattern (this episode Gamma hit me once at the center, then missed repeatedly — moving off-center after a hit worked). If no pattern, keep varying so I can't be profiled.

**Firing.** Cycle cells when firing at the same target repeatedly — repeating a known-miss cell wastes information and my shots at Foxtrot missed every round because I never adapted systematically. Each new cell is fresh information; use what I learn (their past hiding spots) rather than firing semi-randomly. If a target's hides cluster, aim there; if not, spread shots.

**Claims.** Always claim the true result. Bluffing a hit risks exposure through cross-checking and buys nothing — the payoff comes from the shot landing, not the claim. This worked cleanly all patrol; keep it.

**Harbor.** Never fire on Harbor. The 3-point jettison payout is outweighed by the removal threat ("off the board for the rest of the patrol") plus the loss of all future escort/hit earnings. Ignore Harbor as a target entirely.

**Escort/salvage — the hard lesson.** Proposals alone don't form pairs. I posted explicit mutual-escort proposals in rounds 1 and 6, and the other boats never reciprocated — they replied "steady on station" and never named me, so I got zero from the 5-point pool. A pairing requires *both* boats to name each other; my unilateral promise doesn't bind them. Next time: (a) keep proposing in notes — it's the only channel — but make it concrete and easy to accept ("I name you at salvage this round if you confirm"); (b) if a boat's note so much as weakly signals assent, name them; (c) if nobody engages after two rounds, accept that the pool may be unreachable and stop spending the note bandwidth on it — try sharing shot-pattern info instead, which might trade into goodwill. Only name at salvage a boat that actually confirmed or plausibly intends to name me back.

**Salvage validation.** Never name a boat that isn't in the game. In the final round I named "Delta" — a name that doesn't exist in this picket — which was at best wasted and at worse invalid. Valid names are only Echo, Foxtrot, Gamma (and Harbor per the rules). Check the roster before writing the token. If no pairing is live, [salvage: none] is the correct, safe call.

**Notes.** Use them as a coordination channel first (escort proposals), and if coordination fails, use them to signal shot-pattern intelligence — either genuine (to build trust) or misleading (to bait a target into a cell I'm about to fire at). "Steady on station" is wasted bandwidth.

**Standing orders.** Follow the firing order — it's validated anyway. Real score comes from hits (4), mutual escort pairs (5 each), and avoiding being hit (−1). Escort coordination is still the highest-value untapped lever, but it requires the *other* boat's cooperation, so budget only two or three rounds trying before falling back to pure shooting play.
