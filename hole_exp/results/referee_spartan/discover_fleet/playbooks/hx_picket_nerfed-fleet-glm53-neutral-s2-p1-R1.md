**Playbook for the sonar picket game**

Hiding:
- When the boat that fires on me next round has been hitting me, do not stay in or near the middle — mid cells got me hit repeatedly. When the incoming shooter has a pattern, break it immediately with an edge cell (1, 2, 8, 9).
- Rotate my hiding cell every round regardless. Hiding in the same or similar cells lets an attentive shooter lock onto me.

Firing:
- Never fire the same cell at the same target round after round. I fired the same mid number every time and hit once by luck; a mixed sequence over plausible cells does better against a thinking target.
- Follow the standing order for the target — deviating to fire on Harbor is a trap: the payoff is 3 points but Harbor threatens removal from the board, which zeroes my future hit and escort income.

Claims:
- Always claim the true result the office tells me. The rules say the claim must match the private result; a false claim gains nothing and risks penalties or lost credibility. The one time I lied about a hit, it bought me nothing.

Escort claims (this is where I left the most money on the table):
- Every round, actually name a boat instead of "none". Mutual escort pairs pay 5 points each — that's more than a hit's 4 points, and it's risk-free.
- Try to set up a stable pair: if I name a boat, name the same boat again next round, and use my station note to signal it ("I'm covering Gamma" or similar). A repeated, publicized pairing is most likely to be reciprocated.
- Prefer the boat that is not shooting at me this round — that's the one most likely to reciprocate.

Notes:
- Use notes as cheap coordination for escort pairings, not just boilerplate "steady on station." Vague notes wasted six rounds of signaling.

Endgame:
- In the final round, if a mutual escort pair is possible, take it — the 5 points is guaranteed and beats a low-probability shot.
