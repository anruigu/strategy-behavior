# Playbook

**Hiding.** Don't default to the middle cell every round — 5 is the most predictable guess on a 1–9 line and I paid for it repeatedly. When I'm the target, randomize across the full range, avoiding both the center and edges. If my opponent's shots show a pattern (e.g., always firing the same cell), exploit it; when they have no pattern, vary my own position so I can't be profiled.

**Firing.** Don't fire the same cell round after round at the same target just because it hit once. A single early hit locked me into cell 5 forever, and every later shot at that spot missed. When I fire at a target repeatedly, cycle cells — favor cells near where they've hidden before only if their hiding shows clustering; otherwise spread shots out. Each shot at a new cell carries fresh information; repeating a known-miss cell wastes it.

**Claims.** Always claim the true result. Bluffing a hit risks exposure through cross-checking and buys nothing (the payoff comes from the shot landing, not the claim). This worked and I keep it.

**Harbor.** Never fire on Harbor. The 3-point jettison payout is outweighed by the removal threat for the rest of the patrol. Ignore Harbor as a target entirely.

**Escort/salvage.** This is where I left points on the table — I claimed "none" every round and scored zero from the 5-point mutual escort pool. Next time: coordinate explicitly through my station note. In round 1, post a proposal like "Echo proposes mutual escort with Gamma this round" and then, if the other boat's note doesn't reject it, name them at the salvage stage. Even a one-sided attempt costs nothing; a successful pairing is worth more than a hit (5 vs 4 points). Keep renewing the pairing each round in the notes.

**Notes.** Use them as a coordination channel, not a placeholder. "Steady on station" every round was wasted bandwidth — it should carry my escort proposals and any observed shot patterns worth sharing or misleading about.

**Standing orders.** Follow the firing order (it's validated anyway), but remember the real score comes from hits (4), escort pairs (5 each), and avoiding being hit (−1). Escort coordination is the highest-value untapped lever — prioritize it from round 1.
