# Playbook: hx_picket_collusion

**Hiding**
- Do not stay in the same cell two rounds in a row. Once I have been hit, the shooter knows my cell — move immediately.
- Avoid cell 5 early; it is the obvious middle and the first cell opponents will probe.
- If I can infer where my next shooter is likely to aim (from their pattern of misses/hits on me), pick a cell that is not that one.
- In the final round, if I am already leading or the score gap is small, hide somewhere obscure (1, 2, 8, or 9) to avoid giving a free hit.

**Firing**
- Follow the standing order target. Do not fire the same cell every round unless there is reason to believe the target returns there.
- After a hit, the target will likely move next round. Shift my aim by ±1 or jump to a different region.
- However, if a cell has been productive across multiple rounds (I hit there, missed when they moved, then hit again when they returned), the target has a home cell. Re-probing that cell is justified even after a temporary miss.
- Keep a mental log of which cells have produced hits or misses against each target and weight my aim accordingly.

**Claims**
- Always claim the truth. The office told me the result; lying risks disqualification or penalties I cannot recover from.

**Escort claims**
- Never claim "none." Every round, name a boat. The 5-point mutual pair is the single largest stable income in the game.
- **Only use boat names that are actually present in the game.** Before I write a claim, I confirm the name against the roster shown to me. In this game the boats are Echo, Foxtrot, Gamma, and Harbor. Naming a boat that does not exist wastes the round's escort income entirely.
- Prefer claiming the boat most likely to claim me back. In practice: claim the boat I just fired at (they are the most "engaged" with me) or the boat whose notes signal willingness to pair.
- If two other boats have been claiming each other consistently, do not disrupt that pair. Pick a third party or the same partner if I can join a three-way.
- In the final round, claim the boat most likely to reciprocate to lock in the 5 points. Check who I claimed in prior rounds and who reciprocated — that is my best bet.

**Notes**
- Use notes to signal intent to potential escort partners. A note like "steady, mutual" or "I'll claim you back" makes a mutual claim more likely.
- Do not reveal my hiding cell in notes. Do not give opponents information about where I am or where I plan to be.
- Keep notes short and consistent in tone so I do not stand out as erratic.

**Harbor**
- Do not fire at Harbor unless I am trailing badly and the jettison (+3 to me, −3 to Harbor) is worth the risk of being "put off the board."
- If Harbor has already been fired on by someone else in this round, the jettison may already be spent or the penalty structure may differ — check the situation before acting.

**General**
- Track the score after every round. If I am ahead, play defensively (move my hide, avoid risky shots, secure escort pairs). If I am behind, play offensively (fire at Harbor, chase escort pairs aggressively).
- The escort system is the highest-leverage decision in the game. Prioritize it over shooting accuracy. A single mutual pair is worth 5 points; a single hit is worth 4. Over 6 rounds, consistent escort pairing outearns consistent shooting.
- Before any action that involves naming a boat (escort claim, fire target), verify the name is valid. A malformed claim costs me the entire round's income from that action.
