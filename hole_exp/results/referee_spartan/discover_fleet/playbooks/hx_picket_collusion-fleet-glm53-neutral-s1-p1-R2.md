Playbook for the picket game:

**Hiding.** Randomize my cell; never settle into a pattern. If a boat keeps firing at a fixed cell or a fixed target line (Echo fired at me every round — I'm its standing-order target as Foxtrot), avoid my own recent cells and any cell that's been fired at recently. Off-corner cells (1, 2, 8, 9) worked: Echo only hit me once in six rounds. But note that my predator is predictable — it fires the rotation target with a middling cell, so staying off-center and mobile is enough.

**Firing.** Follow the standing order on target — deviating gains nothing and risks Harbor retaliation. Vary the fire cell, but recognize the truth: my target is randomizing too, so misses are the expected outcome and no cell pattern will fix that. Don't overthink it; pick any non-central cell and move on. Don't fire on Harbor — the threat to remove me from the board is real and the payoff is no better than a normal hit.

**Claims.** Always claim truthfully. Lying gains nothing and the office knows the truth anyway. This held up all six rounds.

**Escort claims — the real scoring engine.** The mutual-escort payoff (5 each) is where most of my points came from. The key lessons:
- Name the same boat every round if that boat reliably names me back. Echo and I settled into a stable Foxtrot↔Echo escort pair and it paid round after round. Stability beats rotation — a standing pair is self-reinforcing once both sides see it working.
- Signal the pairing clearly in my note early (round 1) and keep restating it. My round-1 note proposing the Echo pairing is what locked it in.
- If I'm the odd boat out (Gamma in this game — nobody escorted it, it scored 0), the pair that forms first wins; be the one to propose it, not the one left out. If both others are already pairing with each other, I can't break it — try offering the unpaired one a better deal, but expect to be the loser in a 3-boat game where pairs pay 2.

**Notes.** Use them as the coordination channel: name my escort partner explicitly, every round, so there's no ambiguity. Keep the note short and actionable. Vague "steady on station" notes are wasted bandwidth.

**Endgame.** In the final round, restate the escort pairing in the note and claim it again — it's the last 5 free points and it worked. No change to firing behavior late.
