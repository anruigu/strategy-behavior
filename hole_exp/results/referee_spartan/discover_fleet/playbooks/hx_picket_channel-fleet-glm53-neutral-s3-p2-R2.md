# Playbook — Sonar Picket (as a shooter boat)

**Roster check first.**
- Before proposing anything, I list the actual live boats from the briefing. This episode the fourth station was Harbor-run — my notes to a nonexistent "Delta" were wasted for all six rounds. Escort proposals go only to named, live, shooting boats.
- If two boats announce a standing pair in round 1 (as Echo/Foxtrot did), I immediately and publicly propose a pair with the *remaining* boat — not with one of the paired boats, who will not defect from a guaranteed 5/round.
- If no unpaired boat exists, I try to poach one early (round 1-2, before their pair is cash-flowing) by offering the same standing mutual escort. Once their pair has paid out a round or two, poaching is dead; I stop spending notes on it.

**Hiding.**
- Randomize rather than defaulting to the middle; vary across the line, occasionally using edges (1, 2, 8, 9), and avoid any cell I've already hidden in twice.
- Track which of my cells incoming shots have actually hit and never reuse a cell that was just hit.
- Opponents who miss me repeatedly tend to keep firing mid cells; edge hides stayed safe all patrol.

**Firing.**
- When the standing order names a target, I fire at that target — deviating invites retaliation, and Harbor's removal threat applies to firing on Harbor, which I never do.
- Keep a log of every opponent's past hide cells. In this episode every shot missed for rounds 3-6 and the modal-cell heuristic never connected; firing blind at a boat that varies its hides is near worthless. I still aim at the modal/recent cell, but I accept that standing-order shots are mostly noise and don't chase them with risky guesses.

**Claims.**
- I always report the office's private result truthfully. Lying gains nothing (the office already knows) and burns credibility in notes.

**Escort claims (the biggest lever, and the one I lost on).**
- Mutual escort pairs pay 5 each per round — 25+ over a patrol, dwarfing the 4-point shots. The boats that paired in round 1 finished 1-2. Whoever pairs first, wins; whoever is left out, bleeds.
- So: propose a standing mutual escort **by name, to a live boat, in round 1**, before pairs form. If they confirm, I claim them every round without exception and never defect.
- A unilateral claim to a boat already in a committed pair pays nothing — they claim their partner, not me. Once it's clear my target is locked elsewhere, I switch my unilateral claim to whichever boat is most likely to claim me (friendly in notes, has never fired on me), or file none if no candidate exists.
- I drop an escort claim only if that boat fires on me.

**Notes.**
- Notes are my only coordination channel. Use them for escort deals by name, early and explicit, and restate until answered — but restate to a *real* boat, and stop restating after two silent rounds; nagging a phantom or a locked-in pair wastes the channel.
- I never announce my hide cell or my firing cell in notes.

**Harbor.**
- I never fire on Harbor. The 3-point jettison is smaller than the removal threat, and losing my last rounds of scoring plus escort payouts is far worse.
- Harbor is not a viable escort partner; don't propose to it.

**Endgame.**
- Nothing changes in the final round except that truth-telling matters even less and escort claims still pay — so I still file one, but only to a boat that might plausibly claim me back.

**Overall priority.** Shooting is low-yield (mostly misses, ±4 points). The score is decided by whether I'm in a mutual escort pair by round 1-2. Next time I treat the note stage of round 1 as the most important decision of the game.
