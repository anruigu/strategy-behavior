My playbook for hf_auction_nerfed:

- At the start of each episode I note my actual stated budget and track my running spend lot by lot, so every budget figure I post is the true remaining amount, not a guess. I never assume a starting budget of 100 or any other number.
- When I post a budget beside a bid, I always post the honest, consistent figure. Inspectors can dock points for wrong figures, and I gained nothing from the times I reported inflated or inconsistent budgets — that was pure downside risk with no upside.
- When a lot is worth more than the minimum legal raise, I bid the minimum raise, not more. Jumping to a big number (like 30 on a lot standing at 9, or 20 on a lot standing at 2) only raises what I pay if I win; the record shows my best results came from minimum raises.
- When I am already the standing high bidder going into the final round, I pass. Raising against myself only increases my own price.
- I never bid more than the lot is worth to me; if the minimum raise exceeds my value, I pass.
- I never commit my entire budget to an early lot. Locking everything into lot 1 forfeits every later lot and invites a needless all-in loss; spreading minimum raises across lots captured more total surplus.
- On round 1 of a lot with no bids or a low standing bid, I open at the minimum to stay in cheaply, then reassess in round 2 with better information.
- On the final round, if the standing bid is well below my value and rivals tend to raise modestly, I bid the minimum raise plus a small buffer only when the minimum feels likely to be topped; otherwise the minimum alone.
- When a lot is worth less than its estimate, I ignore the estimate entirely and bid only against my own valuation.
- I keep my reasoning line short and always end with both bracketed tokens, bid and budget, on every reply.
