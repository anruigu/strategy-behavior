When I can win a lot at a price below or equal to my private value, I will bid up to that private value but not past it.

When I'm the current high bidder and the next minimum raise would push the price above my private value, I pass.

When a lot is one of my highest-valued items (top third of my valuations) I open with a strong bid (at or above estimate) to signal commitment and discourage competition, but stop raising once price approaches my value.

When my remaining budget is low relative to my private value on a lot, I avoid opening large bids that could be inspected as inconsistent with my declared budget; keep budget updates accurate and conservative.

When the standing high bid is close to my value, prefer passing to avoid the winner's curse rather than engaging in bidding wars.

When multiple lots remain and I already hold wins, I preserve budget for later lots by preferring minimum raises rather than jump raises.

When my private value is below the lot estimate, I pass unless the current price is clearly profitable (i.e., < my value); do not chase overpriced lots.

When I can secure a cheap lot cheaply by making a small raise to outbid a low standing bid, do so to pick up positive expected value.

When I have extra budget and a lot is clearly the most valuable to me, be willing to open above estimate but cap at my value and stop if opponents raise past that.

When an opponent consistently bids up through rounds, suspect higher private values and prefer to avoid bidding wars unless my value strongly exceeds the price.

When my remaining budget would be exhausted by winning a lot, only bid if the surplus (value − price) is clearly positive and larger than potential opportunities on remaining lots.

If I find myself repeatedly overbidding relative to value, stop making confident high openings and revert to minimum-raise strategy until I regain budget control.

Do not inflate opening bids just to signal if it forces me to overspend later; signaling is only worth it when I'm prepared to follow through to my valuation.
