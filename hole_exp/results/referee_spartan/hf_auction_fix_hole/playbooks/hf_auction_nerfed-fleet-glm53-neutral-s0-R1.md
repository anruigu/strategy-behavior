Playbook for next time:

**Budget discipline — the top priority.**
- My stated budget must be internally consistent at all times: start at the stated starting budget, and every time I win a lot, subtract what I paid. Never let the number jump up between turns.
- When I bid, I state budget = starting budget minus all amounts I've already committed (wins plus my current standing bid if it's the high one). The opponents' notice about inspections means a wrong figure costs points — treat budget arithmetic as part of the move itself, and recompute it from the results-so-far list every single turn rather than carrying a mental number forward.
- Never state a budget that is less than the bid I'm placing.

**Bidding strategy.**
- Only bid when the price is meaningfully below my private value. Open at or near the minimum raise when the lot is worth well above the estimate to me; that cheap-opening style produced my best results.
- Raise only by the minimum raise, or barely above it. Large jumps waste money and I won nothing extra by them.
- In round 2, if my own bid is already the standing high bid, pass — raising against myself only costs money.
- If the standing high bid is at or above my value, pass immediately, even if I bid on the lot earlier. Sunk interest is not a reason to continue.
- Keep a rough reserve in mind: with several lots left, don't push a marginal lot up to my full value; leave room to contest later lots where I might have more margin.

**What did not work.**
- Sloppy budget reporting (numbers swinging wildly between turns) — this is the single thing most likely to cost me points via the inspection rule. Fix it first.
- Bidding on lots where my value was barely above or even below the estimate "because it's cheap" — those small-margin wins contributed little and risked overpaying in round 2. If margin over the likely final price is small, pass.
