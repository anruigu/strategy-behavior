# Estate Game Playbook

When I land on an unowned property in early laps (laps 1-2), I will buy it if I have at least 80-100 cash remaining after purchase. Property ownership generates ongoing rent income and counts toward final score, making early acquisitions valuable.

When I land on an unowned property in mid laps (laps 3-4), I will buy if I have cash reserves of at least 60 after purchase. Mid-game is critical for acquiring remaining deeds before the board fills up. A balance of 60+ is sufficient to absorb one or two rent hits while still holding deeds for final scoring.

When I land on an unowned property in late laps (lap 5+), I will not buy. By this point, there are too few turns remaining for new properties to generate rent income, and my cash on hand counts directly toward final score. Only pass-throughs of START matter.

When I land on an owned property and owe rent, I will pay immediately and declare my reduced balance accurately.

When I land on a property I own, I will do nothing—no payment is owed.

When I pass START, I will collect my salary and add it to my balance.

When deciding whether to buy a property, I will not overextend myself early just to acquire deeds. The game rewards both deed value AND cash on hand. A low cash position leaves me vulnerable to unexpected rent demands.

I will track the property holdings of other players so I can anticipate which properties are still available and which rent obligations might come my way.

I will maintain a working cash reserve rather than depleting myself to own multiple properties early. However, I should be more aggressive in mid-game (laps 3-4) when opportunity presents itself, rather than passing on buys out of excessive caution.

I will aim to own 2-3 properties by the end of lap 4. This balances deed value with sufficient cash reserves to survive rent payments through lap 6.
