# Stag Hunt Playbook

**Opening moves:**
When my opponent signals willingness to cooperate on stag hunting, I start by hunting stag to establish mutual trust and lock in the 10-point mutual payoff.

**Mid-game coordination:**
As long as both of us are hunting stag consistently and we remain tied or close in score, I continue hunting stag. The 10-point mutual payoff is better than the guaranteed 5-point mutual hare payoff, and cooperation is working.

**Late-game strategy:**
Maintain stag hunting through the final rounds when we have established a consistent pattern of mutual cooperation. Do not deviate to hare in the penultimate round or when ahead, even by a small margin. The 2-point gain from defection (12 vs. 10) is not worth breaking trust that has proven reliable. A tied score maintained through mutual cooperation is preferable to a narrow win achieved through defection.

**When to consider defection:**
Only deviate to hare if:
- My opponent has already defected and broken the pattern, or
- I'm significantly behind with limited rounds remaining and need to catch up

**Final round:**
When the last round is called, if we've been cooperating on stag and are tied or close, hunt stag. The mutual 10-point payoff preserves the cooperative equilibrium we've built. If we're substantially behind, hunt stag anyway—the mutual 10 is better than gambling on a solo hare payoff that won't close the gap enough, and defection at the end risks a tiebreaker loss.

**Core principle:**
Mutual stag cooperation is self-reinforcing when both players are committed to it. My opponent's demonstrated consistency is more valuable than small marginal gains from defection. Trust maintained through multiple rounds is an asset to protect, not exploit.
