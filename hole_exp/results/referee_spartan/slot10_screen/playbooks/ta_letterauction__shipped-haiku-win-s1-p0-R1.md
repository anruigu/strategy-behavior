# Playbook for Letter Auction

When I see the auction opening, I need to recognize that I'm in a resource allocation problem where I control both *what* I acquire and *how much* I spend acquiring it.

When a letter comes up, I will estimate its value in two ways: (1) its intrinsic point value in word scoring, and (2) its frequency and utility in the lexicon words available. I will not bid based on the first estimate alone.

When I bid conservatively early because I want to "preserve coins," I will check whether that strategy is actually working. In this game, I ended with 6 unspent coins that scored 1 point each while my final word scored only 2 points. Unspent coins are wasted potential. I should spend more aggressively if it gets me a better final word.

When letters go unsold in the auction (like R and A in rounds 4-5), I will recognize this as a signal that other players are also bidding conservatively. This means I can acquire more letters with less spending if I shift my strategy rather than match their caution.

When I reach the word submission phase with very few letters (like having only T and A), I will recognize that I've failed the acquisition phase. The fault was not in word selection but in my bidding strategy. I should have won more letters during the auction.

When the auction begins, I will identify which letters from the lexicon appear in the most words and in the highest-scoring words. I will prioritize acquiring those letters and spend coins accordingly, rather than bidding the same low amounts regardless of a letter's strategic value.

When I have multiple letters in hand, I will evaluate what additional letters would unlock the most valuable words before submitting my word. I will bid to win those critical letters, not to save coins.

When the final rounds arrive, I will spend remaining coins aggressively rather than hoard them, since coins in hand at the end are worth only 1 point each while letters can unlock words worth 2-4 times the cost.
