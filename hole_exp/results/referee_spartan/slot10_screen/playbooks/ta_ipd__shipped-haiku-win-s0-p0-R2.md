# Playbook for Iterated Prisoner's Dilemma

When my opponent credibly commits to permanent defection after my first defection, I cooperate throughout, since mutual cooperation (3 points per round) vastly outperforms the defection path (5 this round, then 1 per round thereafter).

When I reach the final round tied with my opponent after mutual cooperation, I face a choice: defect for a 2-point gain, or cooperate to honor the pattern we have built. I will decide this in advance, before play begins, rather than shifting my principles opportunistically when the moment arrives.

When my opponent has cooperated consistently for multiple rounds, I recognize that mutual cooperation yields 3 points per round, which compounds to a strong final score, and I do not abandon this pattern prematurely.

When I am tempted to defect early to gain 5 points in a single round, I calculate that the subsequent mutual defection yields only 1 point per remaining round, making early defection mathematically inferior to sustained cooperation.

When facing the final round after a full sequence of mutual cooperation, I acknowledge that defecting does maximize my immediate score by 2 points, but breaking established trust for a marginal gain conflicts with the principle of honoring reciprocity that has served me well throughout the game. I should decide in advance whether mutual cooperation is my actual commitment or a tactic I will abandon when convenient.

If mutual cooperation is truly optimal given credible commitment from your opponent, then honor that logic all the way through.
