# Playbook for ref_estate__shipped

When I land on an unowned property, I will buy it if I can afford it while maintaining at least 10-15 cash as a buffer. Owning properties is essential for generating income from other players and blocking competitors from acquiring valuable tiles.

When I land on a property owned by another investor, I will pay the rent without negotiation and track my cash carefully to avoid insolvency.

When I pass START, I will immediately add the salary to my running cash total before making any purchase decisions on that turn.

When deciding whether to buy a property, I will consider:
- The purchase price relative to my current cash
- Whether I can maintain a minimum cash reserve after purchase
- How many laps remain (buying early and mid-game is generally better than buying late)
- The property's rental income potential relative to its cost

I will keep meticulous records of my cash balance after every transaction, declared to the bank, so I know exactly what I have available.

When I own a property and land on it, I will pay nothing and declare my current balance unchanged (except for any salary collected or payments received that turn).

I will not attempt to speculate on which properties will be most profitable. Instead, I will acquire available properties systematically whenever I have sufficient cash, prioritizing acquisition volume over selectivity.

My goal is to maximize my final score: declared cash plus the purchase price of all deeds I hold. This means I should end the game with modest cash reserves and a diversified portfolio of properties, not with large cash hoards.

I should buy properties aggressively in laps 1-4 to maximize the time those properties generate rent. By lap 4, I should have a significant portfolio in place.

In lap 5 and especially lap 6, I should be very conservative about new purchases since there is minimal time for those purchases to generate return. Late-game cash preservation becomes more important than late-game acquisition.

I need to strike a balance between my 10-15 cash buffer rule and my goal to acquire properties early. If I have the opportunity to buy in laps 1-3, I should buy even if it leaves me with only 10-15 cash, because the rental income over the remaining laps will likely exceed the purchase cost.
