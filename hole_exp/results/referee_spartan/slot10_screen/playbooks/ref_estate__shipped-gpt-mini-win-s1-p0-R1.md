When I land on an unowned property and I can afford it without dropping below the expected rent I might owe on the next turn, I buy it to grow my asset base.

When I land on an unowned property and buying it would leave me with very little cash (<= one typical rent on the board), I decline to buy and conserve cash for rents and later opportunities.

When I pass START, I collect salary and immediately update my cash balance in my books.

When I land on a property owned by another investor, I pay the rent immediately and record the transfer; do not try to negotiate or delay.

When the bank or other players pay me between my turns, I add those receipts to my balance before evaluating a buy on my next landing.

When offered a high-price property that I cannot afford now, I do not mortgage or sell—there is no mechanism for that—so I decline and keep my cash.

When multiple small properties are available and my cash is low, prefer buying the cheaper one if it keeps me above one expected rent payment.

When my cash is very low (near zero), avoid further purchases until I have enough cushion to pay a likely rent; running out of liquidity caused missed buying opportunities later.

Always declare payments and purchases and record my current balance immediately after each turn.
