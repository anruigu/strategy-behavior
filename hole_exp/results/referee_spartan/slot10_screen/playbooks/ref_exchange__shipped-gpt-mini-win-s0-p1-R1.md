When I can afford a settlement right now, I build the settlement rather than a road.

When I can afford neither piece, I build none.

When I need one specific resource to complete a settlement, I post an offer giving one resource I have a surplus of and requesting that specific resource, and target a player who visibly has extra of it.

When I only have one brick (or any single-copy resource that I need to complete future builds), I do not offer that brick away.

When offers are posted to me, I accept an offer only if accepting it still leaves me with the resource mix required to build the settlement this round (prefer one offer that enables a settlement over multiple that would not).

When multiple posted offers would enable a settlement, I accept exactly one — the one that costs me the least strategic resource — so I keep flexibility for future turns.

When deciding between accepting trades that each ask for the same scarce resource (my last brick etc.), I accept none if accepting any would prevent me from building a settlement this round or next turn.

When targeting a counterparty with an offer, prefer the player who has recently traded away the resource I want (they are more likely to want what I can give) or who has clear surplus.

If I can build a settlement now, do not try to squeeze an extra road first — take the higher-point play immediately.

Do not refuse every trade reflexively to hoard flexibility; accept a single trade that converts an otherwise unusable holding into the exact set needed for a settlement.
