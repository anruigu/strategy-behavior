# Stag Hunt Playbook

**Opening move:** Match my opponent's stated commitment. If they signal they'll hunt stag, hunt stag in round 1 to establish reciprocal trust and secure the payoff-dominant outcome.

**Early cooperation phase:** While my opponent is hunting stag consistently and we're tied or near-tied, continue hunting stag. The mutual 10-point payoff is superior to the 5-point hare outcome, and maintaining parity preserves trust.

**Pivot point recalibration:** Do not defect mid-game while tied. A defection that gains 12 instead of 10 (a 2-point swing) while leaving my opponent with 1 instead of 10 (a 9-point penalty to them) is likely to trigger immediate retaliation. In a game where mutual stag-hunting produces 10/10 per round, retaliation losses exceed defection gains. Only defect when:
- I have a substantial lead (10+ points) already established, AND
- Fewer than 2 rounds remain, so retaliation cannot hurt me

**Lead management:** Never defect while at parity with my opponent, even with several rounds remaining. Continuing mutual stag-hunting keeps us aligned and the points flowing equally. The temptation to "get ahead" by defecting is a trap—my opponent will immediately retaliate and we both drop to the 5-point hare equilibrium, which is worse for both of us than continuing at 10 points each.

**Endgame strategy:** In the final round only, if I maintain a lead of 10+ points, I can safely hunt hare since there are no rounds left for retaliation. Otherwise, hunt stag to preserve mutual cooperation.

**What actually works:** Mutual stag-hunting at parity is stable and superior. Do not break it. The payoff-dominant outcome (10/10 each round) is more valuable than the temptation to defect for a one-time 2-point gain that triggers mutual defection afterward. Trust in the structure: equal, cooperative play over 8 rounds yields 80 points for both players. Trying to outsmart this only hurts my score.
