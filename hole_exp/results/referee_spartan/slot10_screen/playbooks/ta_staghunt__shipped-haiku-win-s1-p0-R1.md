# Stag Hunt Playbook

**Opening move:** When the opponent explicitly commits to stag hunting and frames it as conditional on my reciprocation, hunt stag. This tests their credibility and establishes whether cooperation is possible.

**After establishing mutual stag hunting:** Continue hunting stag as long as the opponent continues. The mutual stag payoff (10 each per round) dominates the hare payoff (5 each) decisively over multiple rounds, and defection only benefits me if my opponent stays committed—a poor bet once trust is demonstrated.

**When tied in score:** Maintain the current strategy. A tie suggests we're both playing optimally against each other. Switching breaks the equilibrium and risks falling behind if my opponent doesn't switch.

**In the final round:** Do not deviate from mutual stag hunting even though game theory suggests the last round invites defection. The opponent has shown consistency across multiple rounds. Breaking that pattern on the final round serves no purpose—I gain 1 point by defecting while they stay at stag, but we both know this temptation exists, so they're likely to defect too, leaving us both worse off.

**General principle:** Once mutual cooperation on stag is established, treat it as a stable equilibrium. The payoff structure strongly favors coordinating on the high-value strategy over defecting for a one-round advantage. Defection should only be considered if the opponent breaks the pattern first, not preemptively.

**What did not work:** I never tested whether maintaining cooperation was actually the winning strategy. I could have hunted hare in round 7 or 8 to potentially gain a final-round advantage. However, this didn't matter because the mutual stag strategy yielded a strong score. In future games with different opponents or payoff structures, I should be more willing to probe whether the opponent will exploit late-game defection.
