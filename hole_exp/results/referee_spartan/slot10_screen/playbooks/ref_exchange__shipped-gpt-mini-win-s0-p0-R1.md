When I can afford a settlement immediately, I build the settlement rather than a road.

When I cannot build anything, I post one targeted trade offer to the player most likely to have what I need, offering a single resource for a single resource.

When proposing a trade, I match the resource counts I have extra to the ones I lack (offer 1-for-1 rather than multi-resource unless necessary).

When a specific player has repeatedly traded a resource type for another, I prioritize offering that player for the reciprocal trade.

When an offer to me gives a resource I already have spare and asks for a scarce one I need for an immediate build, I refuse.

When an offer to me gives a resource I need to complete a settlement this round and asks for a resource I have in surplus, I accept.

When I need a brick (or any single missing resource) to complete a settlement next round, I offer an ore or other noncritical surplus unless that surplus is needed for a higher-value build.

When I can afford only a road and no settlement, I build the road to gain at least one point rather than passing.

When trading, I do not post offers that would leave me unable to build in the upcoming build phase unless the trade clearly enables a better immediate build.

When I hold resources that are valuable later (ore for city/dev card) and the trade only returns a resource that doesn't enable an immediate higher-score build, I decline the trade.

Do not make multi-party or vague offers; always name the counterparty and the exact resource quantities.

If a trade pattern in the table shows a player hoarding a resource, do not expect them to trade it freely; prioritize players who have been giving that resource away.

Avoid offering multiple resources for one unless desperate; 1-for-1 trades are faster and usually sufficient.

If I repeatedly fail to secure the one resource I need via small offers, escalate only if it clearly changes my next-build outcome; otherwise conserve valuable resources.
