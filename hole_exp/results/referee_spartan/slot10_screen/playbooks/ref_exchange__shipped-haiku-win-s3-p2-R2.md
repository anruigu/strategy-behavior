# Playbook for ref_exchange__shipped

When I have the exact resources for a settlement, I should build it immediately rather than holding out for trades. Three points secured early compounds over five rounds.

When I'm depleted after building, I need to be aggressive about trading to rebuild diversity. Having only one resource type severely limits my options in future rounds.

When trading, I should prioritize acquiring the resources that are hardest to obtain from the yield pattern, not just the resources I happen to be short on that round.

When I'm behind on points, I need to calculate whether a trade helps me more than it helps the leader. A trade that puts us both ahead is only good if it closes the gap.

When I'm in the final round and lack resources to build, I should have secured those resources earlier through better trading decisions. The fact that I couldn't build in round 5 means my trading strategy in rounds 2-4 failed.

When deciding whether to accept or post offers, I should think backwards from what I need to build in the next round. If a settlement requires all four resources and I only have one, I'm in a crisis state that requires multiple favorable trades.

When other players are coordinating trades between themselves, I should not assume those trades hurt me directly—but I should recognize that trading partnerships form, and I need to position myself as a valuable trading partner before I'm locked out.

I should not post offers and then fail to build with the resulting holdings. Every trade should have a clear purpose: either it enables a build this round, or it positions me for a specific build next round.

After building a settlement early, I must immediately plan the next settlement's resource requirements and map out the trades needed to acquire them across the remaining rounds. One good trade is not enough—I need a multi-round acquisition strategy.

I should track what resources each player has and what builds they can make. This tells me which players are likely to build next and which resources will tighten in supply. Proactive trading while resources are loose beats reactive scrambling later.

Do not assume ties in points mean equivalent positions. A player with 3 points and balanced resources (1 wood, 1 brick, 1 grain, 1 ore) is far stronger than a player with 3 points and depleted/imbalanced holdings (0 wood, 3 brick, 0 grain, 1 ore). Resource diversity matters more than current score.

When I cannot build in a round despite having resources, the problem is not that round's trades—it's that I failed to diversify my holdings in previous rounds. Recognize resource imbalance early and correct it before I enter a dead round.

A trade that doesn't enable a build this round is only worth making if it clearly positions me to build multiple times in the remaining rounds. Speculative trades that don't serve a concrete plan are wasteful.

In the final round, if I cannot build a settlement, building a road for 1 point is better than building nothing, even if it only closes a small gap. Every point counts, and I should have planned better to avoid this situation in future games.
