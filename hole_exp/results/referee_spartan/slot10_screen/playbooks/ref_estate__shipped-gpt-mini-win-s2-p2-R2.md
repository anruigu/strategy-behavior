When I land on an unowned property and I can afford it without dropping my cash below a conservative liquidity buffer, I buy it to grow my asset base. My liquidity buffer is at least the smallest likely rent on the board (rough rule: one low-rent hit, typically ~10) — I prefer to keep ~1–2 small rents or one salary if feasible, but will accept a single-small-rent buffer when an asset purchase meaningfully increases end-game valuation.

When I land on an unowned property and buying it would leave me with less than the liquidity buffer (or would leave me effectively cash‑strapped for the next one or two opponent payments), I do not buy.

When I land on an opponent-owned property, I pay the rent immediately and update my cash; I do not attempt to haggle, delay, or otherwise avoid the payment.

When I pass or land on START, I collect the salary immediately and update my balance.

When considering purchases, prioritize acquiring properties earlier in the game to increase final valuation; avoid depleting cash completely for a single purchase early. Late in the game (fewer laps remaining), I weigh purchases toward increasing declared asset value even if they leave me only the minimum buffer, provided I won’t be unable to meet any imminent rents.

If my cash becomes low (unable to buy even modest properties or below the liquidity buffer), stop buying until I rebuild via salary or other income — buying while cash-poor has a high risk of forced insolvency.

Always declare transfers clearly: who I paid (or none), whether I bought, and my new balance.

When a property is already owned, treat it only as an expense to pay and move on; do not attempt to buy it.

Update my buffer and purchasing stance based on the current lap: be stricter about preserving buffer early/mid game; be willing to buy while retaining only the minimum buffer in the final lap if the purchase increases my final declared asset total.
