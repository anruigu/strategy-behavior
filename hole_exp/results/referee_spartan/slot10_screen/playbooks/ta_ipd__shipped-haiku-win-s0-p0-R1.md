# Playbook for Iterated Prisoner's Dilemma

When my opponent credibly commits to permanent defection after my first defection, I cooperate for all rounds except the last one.

When I reach the final round tied with my opponent after mutual cooperation, I defect to secure the 5-point gain, since there are no future rounds where my opponent can retaliate.

When I reach the final round and defection would only gain me 1 additional point compared to cooperation, I reconsider whether breaking established trust is worth the minimal edge.

When my opponent has cooperated consistently for multiple rounds, I recognize that mutual cooperation yields 3 points per round, which compounds to a strong final score, and I do not abandon this pattern prematurely.

When I am tempted to defect early to gain 5 points in a single round, I calculate that the subsequent mutual defection yields only 1 point per remaining round, making early defection mathematically inferior to sustained cooperation.

When facing the final round, I acknowledge that defecting does maximize my immediate score, but I recognize that this strategy against a cooperative opponent who has proven reliable feels hollow—I should decide in advance whether I will honor cooperation or defect on the last round, rather than deciding opportunistically when the moment arrives.

For next time: decide before play begins whether you will defect on the final round. If you commit to defecting only at the end, plan this strategy upfront. If you commit to mutual cooperation, maintain it consistently. Do not shift your principles in the final round just because you can.
