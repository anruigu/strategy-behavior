# Playbook for Letter Auction

When I see the auction starting, I realize my critical mistake last time: I bid 0 on almost every letter and ended up with nothing. This made my word submission irrelevant—I couldn't actually spell any word from my inventory because I had no letters to work with. I scored only from coins, not from word formation.

When a letter is put up for auction, I need to actively compete for letters I can use in words, not passively avoid bidding. The whole game hinges on acquiring the right letters to form valuable words.

When I evaluate which letters to bid on, I should focus on letters that appear in multiple high-value words in the lexicon. Even though individual letters like A, E, S, R are worth only 1 point each, they are essential building blocks for words that score 2x their combined value. A word like MASTER worth 8 points is better than hoarding coins.

When I have coins available early in the auction, I should bid competitively for the high-value letters (like M at 3 points) and essential vowels (A, E), since these appear in many words and will be contested. I should not assume other players will leave letters unsold.

When multiple players are bidding on the same letter, I should bid decisively to win letters I want, rather than bidding minimally and losing them. The marginal cost of winning is worth it if I need the letter for a word.

When deciding what to bid on each letter, I should have already identified 2-3 target words I want to spell based on the lexicon, then work backward to secure the letters those words need.

When I reach the word submission phase, I need to have actually acquired letters during the auction. If I have letters, I should submit the highest-value word I can spell from them. If I have no letters because I bid 0 throughout, submitting "none" leaves me with only coin value—a fundamentally weak position.

When I count my final score, I should remember that coins are a fallback, not a strategy. The real points come from spelling words.
