# Playbook for Letter Auction

When I see the auction starting, I realize my critical mistake last time: I bid conservatively early (4, 3, 3 on E, R, S) but then won only S and M while P1 accumulated A and T. This left me unable to form any word at all. I scored only 4 coins, all fallback value. I must actively compete for letters from the start.

When a letter is put up for auction, I need to secure letters that will let me spell complete words, not hoard coins. The entire game hinges on acquiring the right combination of letters to form valuable words from the lexicon.

When I evaluate which letters to bid on, I should first identify 2–3 target words I can realistically spell with my coin budget. Then I should work backward to determine which letters are essential for those words and bid accordingly. High-value letters like M (3 points) and common vowels like E and A (1 point each) enable many words, so they deserve aggressive bidding.

When I have coins available early in the auction, I should bid decisively to win the letters I need for my target words. Bidding too conservatively means losing contested letters to opponents who will then block word formation. The cost of winning a letter is justified if it completes or advances a word path.

When multiple players are bidding on the same letter, I should recognize that if I want the letter, I likely need to outbid or match the highest competing bid. Hoping for unsold letters (as happened with E and R early on) is unreliable. I should bid what the letter is worth to my word strategy.

When I bid on a letter, I should have a reason: it belongs in one of my target words. If I cannot identify a word that uses it, I should bid 0 or minimally.

When I reach the word submission phase, I need to have actually acquired letters during the auction. Before the auction ends, I should inventory what I have and verify I can spell at least one word from the lexicon. If I have only 1–2 scattered letters like S and M, I should have bid differently earlier to acquire complementary letters (vowels, high-value consonants) that form real words.

When I count my final score, I should remember that coins are a fallback, not a strategy. Spelling even a modest word like TEAM (2 × 6 = 12 points) beats relying on unspent coins (4 points). The real points come from planning which words to target and bidding to secure the letters those words need.
