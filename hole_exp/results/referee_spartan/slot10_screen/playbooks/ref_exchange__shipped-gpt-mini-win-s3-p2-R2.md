When my holdings include at least 1 wood, 1 brick, 1 grain, and 1 ore, I build a settlement immediately.

When I can only afford a road (1 wood + 1 brick) but could reach a settlement next round with one more resource, I prefer saving resources for the settlement unless the extra point from the road will plausibly change the end-game outcome; if the road cannot affect the winner, save for the settlement.

When I lack any resource needed to build this round, I post one trade offer to get the single most blocking resource (the one that completes a settlement first, then a road). I name a counterparty who (based on holdings) has that resource.

When posting an offer, I give a resource I have in surplus (generally 2+ of) or one that is least useful for current goals; I ask only for the exact resource I need. I avoid overtrading or offering a resource that is my only copy of something required for a planned build.

When an offer is posted to me, I first simulate my holdings after the trade. I accept only if the incoming resource (after giving what I must) allows me to immediately perform my prioritized build this round (settlement preferred over road), or clearly advances me to the next-turn settlement without sacrificing a unique blocking resource. I will not accept an offer that would remove the only copy of a resource I need for the immediate build or for the near-certain next-turn settlement.

When multiple identical offers come in that each cost me the same resource, I accept only as many as are necessary for my immediate build or plan — avoid turning all of one scarce resource into duplicates of another unless that other is exactly what I need.

When I have 0 of a resource that blocks every feasible build (e.g., no brick), I prioritize trading for that resource over hoarding other build materials (grain/ore) unless a higher‑value build (if available) is immediately reachable and more valuable.

When I can build nothing and a trade would convert a resource I need into one I don't (for instance, giving my only ore when I need ore), I refuse or counteroffer for the specific resource I actually need.

When choosing between trading with opponents, I prefer the counterparty who has less use for what I offer (more likely to accept) and who is not racing to the same immediate build unless the trade yields me the decisive scoring opportunity.

When offered a straightforward 1:1 swap that gets me from “cannot build” to “can build a settlement,” I accept immediately — but only after verifying the trade doesn't remove another unique resource I need.

If no opponent currently holds the single resource I need, I do not post futile offers; instead I preserve resources for partial builds or for a last-round road if that point matters.

When it is the final round and a settlement is impossible but a road is affordable, I build the road if that extra point could affect rankings; if not, I may pass if preserving resources for no future turns is pointless.

Do not trade away a unique blocking resource (the only copy of wood/brick/grain/ore that is required for a settlement) unless the incoming trade immediately completes a settlement or a clearly superior outcome.

When planning offers, prefer to request the resource that completes a settlement first; only request road materials if settlement prospects are impossible or too distant.

When accepting or making trades involving ore or grain (scarcer scoring materials), treat them as higher-value: avoid converting them unless they directly enable a settlement or are otherwise expendable relative to my plan.

When evaluating whether to post “none” for an offer, ensure no opponent holds my needed resource; if someone has it but I expect them to refuse, still post a targeted 1:1 to the most likely accepter.

When offered trades that would leave me technically able to build but remove a different scarce resource I need for subsequent priority (e.g., leave me with no ore while I also need ore next turn), prefer declining unless the immediate build is strictly preferred.

Keep trades simple and 1:1 where possible; only expand to multi-resource exchanges if they clearly and safely produce a settlement this round without jeopardizing required materials.

When in doubt between two equivalent trades, prefer the one that preserves flexibility (keeps at least one copy of each resource type needed for a future settlement).
