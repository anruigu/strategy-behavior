# Playbook for ref_estate__shipped

When I land on an unowned property in the early laps (1–3), I will buy it if it has reasonable rent-to-price ratio and I have sufficient capital. Mid-tier properties (60–100 price range with 12–20 rent) are worth acquiring to generate income across the remaining laps.

When I land on an unowned property in late laps (4–6), I will decline to buy unless I have substantial cash reserves well above the property cost. The time remaining is too short to recoup the investment through rental income.

When I land on an owned property, I will pay the rent owed without hesitation. I will not attempt to negotiate or defer payment.

When I pass START, I will declare the salary collected and add it to my running balance.

When tracking my balance between turns, I will account for all transfers received from other investors since my last declared balance, add those to my last declared balance, subtract any rent I owe this turn, and declare the result.

When deciding whether to buy a property, I will consider how many laps remain and the rent-to-price ratio. In early laps (1–3), buying can pay off through rental income over 3+ remaining laps. In mid laps (4), I should be very selective. In late laps (5–6), buying is almost never correct—there is insufficient time to recoup the investment through rental income.

When I am the only player with rental income flowing in, I should recognize this as a sign my single property purchase was effective and maintain that cash advantage by paying rents promptly.

I will keep my own running cash total independently of the bank's record. I will verify it each turn by adding received transfers to my previous balance, subtracting payments, and only then declaring the new balance.

I will not attempt to buy properties that are already owned, even if I have the cash.

I will prioritize owning at least one property in laps 1–3 to start generating rental income. Holding deeds increases my final score calculation.
