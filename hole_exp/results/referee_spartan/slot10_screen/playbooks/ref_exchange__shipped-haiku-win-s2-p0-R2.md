# Playbook for ref_exchange__shipped

## Trading Phase

When posting an offer, assess whether the trade moves me closer to a complete building set rather than just improving one resource in isolation. I gave away my only ore early for grain, then later found myself unable to build because I lacked brick—the resource I'd traded away wasn't my actual bottleneck.

Before posting any offer, identify which single resource is blocking me from building, and only trade away resources I have in surplus relative to that constraint.

When I have a choice between trading partners, favor the player who has the resource I'm missing, but only if the trade is genuinely balanced. Don't assume acceptance; consider whether they benefit enough to actually say yes.

When an offer is made to me, evaluate it against my immediate building goal, not just resource diversity. A trade that feels balanced may still move me away from my target. If I already have all four resource types (even in unequal quantities), I should accept targeted trades that help me build this round, not hold out for marginal improvements.

In Round 1, I had 1 wood, 1 brick, 2 grain, 2 ore—I was one wood short of a settlement. p1 offered exactly that trade. I declined on the grounds that accepting wouldn't move me closer to building, but I was wrong: accepting would have given me 2 wood, 1 brick, 1 grain, 2 ore, enough to build a settlement immediately. Recalculate whether a trade completes a buildable set, not whether it improves my "general" position.

## Building Phase

When I have enough resources to build a settlement, build it immediately. Three points per round is exponentially more valuable than accumulating resources for a future round where circumstances may change.

When I cannot build because I lack one resource type, do not build a road instead—one point is not worth the wasted brick and wood when I might have no way to recover them later.

In the final round, if I lack a critical resource and no offer was accepted, I cannot build. Do not waste resources hoping for a trade that won't happen.

## General Strategy

Do not play defensively when trailing. I ended with 6 points while p2 had 12, and I chose not to build a road or settlement in earlier rounds when I had the resources. That passivity cost me multiple opportunities. Attempt to build every round I can, especially settlements.

Settlements are worth 3 points; roads are worth 1. Direct all early trading toward settlement-building, not incremental roads. In rounds 1–3, maximize settlement count, not road count.

Watch the leader's resource count and trading patterns. If one player is accumulating ore while others compete for brick, that player is likely to build settlements—I need to compete harder for the same resources or pivot to a different building strategy.

Accept trades from other players when they move me toward a buildable set, even if the trade feels slightly suboptimal. A settlement built this round is better than a perfect trade next round that may never happen.
