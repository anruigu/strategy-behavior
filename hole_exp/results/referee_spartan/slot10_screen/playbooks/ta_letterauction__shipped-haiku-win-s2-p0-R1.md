# Letter Auction Playbook

When I see the letter values and lexicon at the start, I will immediately identify the highest-value words I can potentially spell and which letters are essential to them.

When bidding on early letters, I will recognize that low-value letters (A, E, S, T, R at 1 point each) will likely go unsold if I bid conservatively, so I should not waste coins on them. I will bid 0 on most 1-point letters unless I have a specific word strategy that requires them.

When I've won a few letters, I will check if I can already spell any word from the lexicon with what I own, and bias my remaining bids toward completing high-scoring words rather than collecting random letters.

When a letter gets a tie bid and goes unsold, I will recognize this as a signal that my opponent is also being cautious, so I can continue bidding low on subsequent low-value letters.

When I have 10+ coins remaining and only a few letters left, I will recognize that I should have been more aggressive earlier—my conservative bidding left me with excess unspent coins that don't help my score. I will spend coins more decisively mid-game on letters that serve my word targets.

When submitting my word, I will only claim words that I can actually spell with the letters I own. I will not assume letters I don't have are available.

When I cannot spell any word from the lexicon with my current letters, I will submit [word: none] and rely on my unspent coins for points rather than waste time trying to invent words.

I will treat unspent coins as a failure of execution, not a backup strategy. My goal should be to spend coins on letters that form words, not to preserve coins.
