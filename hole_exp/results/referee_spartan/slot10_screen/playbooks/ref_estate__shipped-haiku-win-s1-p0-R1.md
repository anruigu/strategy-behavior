# Playbook for ref_estate__shipped

When I land on an unowned property in the early laps (laps 1-4), I buy it if I have sufficient cash. Property ownership generates income through rent collection from other players, and early acquisition builds my asset base before the endgame.

When I land on an unowned property in the final lap (lap 6), I do not buy it. The cost reduces my final cash total with no opportunity to collect rent, and my score is cash plus deed values already held.

When I pass or land on START, I collect my salary immediately and add it to my cash balance.

When I land on a property owned by another investor, I pay the rent due without hesitation. This is a cost of playing.

When I own properties, I track all rent payments I receive from other players and add them to my balance each turn. I keep my own running total of cash and declare it honestly.

When I hold cash in mid-game turns and face a property purchase decision, I consider whether the purchase price is reasonable relative to the rent it generates. Properties with rent-to-price ratios around 20-30% are worth acquiring.

When I have enough cash to buy a property but purchasing would leave me with very low liquidity, I reassess whether I need to maintain a cash buffer for upcoming turns.

I do not over-buy properties early. Spreading purchases across multiple laps is acceptable if it maintains healthy cash reserves.

At the end of each turn, I verify my declared balance by adding: previous balance plus any salary collected plus any rent received minus any rent paid minus any purchases made.
