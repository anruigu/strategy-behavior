When I can afford a settlement (1 of each resource), I build the settlement rather than a road — settlements give more points for the same number of resources.

When I have exactly one brick and I will not immediately build a settlement or road this round, I do not trade away that brick unless I receive brick back or will gain a build opportunity this round — don’t trade away my only brick.

When I lack brick and a counterparty has brick, I offer 1 wood for 1 brick (or 1 grain for 1 brick) to acquire brick quickly; prioritize trading expendable resources (extra wood/grain) for brick.

Prefer to give expendable resources in this order: wood > grain > ore, except when wood is required for an immediate build — in that case prefer to give grain (or ore) instead of wood.

When I can build a settlement this round, I post no offers — do not trade away resources that enable an immediate higher-value build.

When I am offered a favorable 1:1 trade that gives me brick (or another needed resource) and accepting it does not leave me unable to build this round or in the next turn window toward my goal, I accept it.

When an offer would leave me unable to reach a clear build goal in the next one or two rounds (e.g., trading away my only brick when a settlement is achievable), I decline it.

When multiple counterparties have the needed resource, prefer trading with the party who has it in surplus or who is less likely to be enabled to build an immediate win from the swap.

When no counterparty has the resource I need (brick), I post no offer rather than overpay or fragment my holdings.

When I can only build a road, build a road only if it meaningfully advances a near-term settlement plan or blocks an opponent; otherwise build none and conserve resources.

Do not assume trading away resources to help opponents is worth it unless the trade directly enables a build for me that increases my score advantage.
