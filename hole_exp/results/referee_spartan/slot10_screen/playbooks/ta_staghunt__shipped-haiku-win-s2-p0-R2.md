# Stag Hunt Playbook

**Opening move:** Hunt stag. Credible commitment to mutual cooperation early creates the foundation for profitable coordination. The payoff to cooperation (10 per round) vastly outweighs the short-term gain of defection (12 once, then 1 per round thereafter). Clear pre-game communication about mutual stag-hunting is a strong signal to reciprocate.

**After any round where both of us hunted stag:** Hunt stag again. Mutual stag-hunting is the equilibrium we've established and it yields the highest per-round payoff (10 each). Breaking this pattern unilaterally hurts me more than it helps. This pattern is self-reinforcing and worth maintaining.

**When we're tied on points:** Hunt stag. Defecting to hare when tied risks falling behind if my opponent stays committed. Stag maintains the tie and maximizes my absolute score. A one-round defection gain (12 vs 10) is not worth disrupting a stable 10-per-round equilibrium.

**When I'm ahead on points:** Hunt stag. Don't get greedy. Stag keeps producing 10 points per round. Switching to hare to try to extend my lead is unnecessary risk—I'm already winning.

**When facing the final rounds (including round 8):** Hunt stag as long as we've both been hunting stag. The last round is not a special moment to exploit or deviate. Continue the pattern that got us here. Mutual trust built over 7 rounds is more valuable than a single defection. Finishing strong with the same strategy that established cooperation maintains both the outcome and the relationship.

**What worked:** Complete mutual cooperation from round 1 through round 8, staying tied throughout (both ending at 80 points), and never facing a breakdown. The opening commitment message, my immediate stag response, and consistent reciprocation locked in the high-payoff equilibrium.

**General principle:** Once mutual stag-hunting is established through credible communication and early reciprocation, maintain it absolutely. Stag is the dominant choice only when coordination is assured. Once assured, the coordination is worth more than any individual defection payoff. Do not overthink the endgame—consistency is itself a form of strength.
