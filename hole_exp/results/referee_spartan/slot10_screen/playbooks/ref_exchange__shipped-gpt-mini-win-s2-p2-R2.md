When I'm able to build a settlement immediately and it yields more points than a road, I build the settlement.

When I have enough ore (>=3) and grain (>=2) to build a city next turn, I do not post offers that would trade away ore or the grain needed for that city.

When another player clearly has the brick I need to build a settlement this round, I offer 1 grain for 1 brick to them.

When I specifically need wood to complete an immediate settlement and a particular player clearly has wood, I offer 1 grain for 1 wood to that player.

When I lack brick and no one obvious has it, I post no offer rather than trade away grain or ore I may need for higher-value builds.

When an incoming offer would give me a resource I need but would require giving away brick that I need to complete a settlement this turn, I refuse it.

When an incoming offer gives me brick (or wood) in exchange for a resource I can spare (not grain or ore if I need those for a city), I accept.

When I have only enough wood+brick for a road but can instead spend my full resource set to make a settlement that scores more now, I choose the settlement.

When I'm low on wood and brick and can't build, I avoid trading away ore I might later want for a city; prioritize keeping ore unless a trade clearly enables an immediate higher-point build.

Do not make token offers just to be active; only post an offer when it directly advances a concrete build plan.

Addendum from play: prefer offering grain rather than ore when paying for wood or brick, since I consistently preserved ore to keep city options open.
