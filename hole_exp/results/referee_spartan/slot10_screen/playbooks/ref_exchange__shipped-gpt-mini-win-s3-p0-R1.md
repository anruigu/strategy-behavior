When I can already afford a settlement this turn, I post no offer.

When in the building phase I can afford a settlement I build the settlement (prefer 3 points now over delaying).

When I lack only one resource for a settlement, I post a single 1:1 offer to a player who has that resource and lacks what I'm offering.

When I lack wood but have surplus grain, I offer 1 grain for 1 wood to players who show wood in their holdings.

When I lack brick but have surplus ore (or vice versa), I offer 1 ore for 1 brick (or 1 brick for 1 ore) to the player who is the exclusive source of the needed resource.

When accepting posted offers, I accept any combination that lets me complete the highest-value build available this round (settlement > road). If an offer only enables a road and costs a scarce resource that I need for a settlement, I decline.

When multiple offers are posted to me, I choose the one that yields the build with the largest immediate point gain; if equal, choose the trade that preserves scarce resources for future settlement builds.

Do not post trades that would give opponents a resource they need to immediately build a settlement, unless the trade is necessary for me to build a settlement that same round.

Do not accept trades that hand away a unique scarce resource (e.g., my last brick) for a low-value return unless doing so enables a strictly higher immediate point outcome.

When I cannot build this round and no single 1:1 trade gets me a settlement next round, prefer to conserve flexible resources (grain/ore) and only offer exact 1:1 swaps that improve my chances of building next turn.

Avoid multi-resource or multi-party offers—use simple 1:1 offers naming a specific counterparty and resource to keep trades predictable and to avoid empowering opponents unnecessarily.
