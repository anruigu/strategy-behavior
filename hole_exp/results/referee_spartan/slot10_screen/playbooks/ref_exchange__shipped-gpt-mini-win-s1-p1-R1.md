When I start a round and my holdings already meet a settlement cost, I build the settlement immediately rather than trading for marginal upside.

When I need one resource to complete a settlement and another player has repeatedly traded that resource for ore, I offer 1 ore for 1 of that resource.

When an offer to me gives the single resource I need to finish a settlement and I can spare the requested resource without dropping below a target ore threshold (3 ore if I might want a city/dev later), I accept it.

When an incoming offer would drop my ore below my city/dev threshold and the gain is only one resource that doesn’t immediately convert into a settlement or higher-scoring build, I decline.

When I cannot build a settlement this turn but can build a road with available wood+brick, I build the road to take the guaranteed 1 point rather than holding resources idle.

When I have enough ore to consider city/dev plans, I prioritize keeping ore over taking small single-resource trades unless the trade directly enables a 3-point settlement that I can build that round.

When posting an offer, I name a specific counterparty who has previously shown a propensity to accept that swap pattern (e.g., trading grain for ore), rather than broadcasting a generic offer.

When multiple offers are posted to me, I accept any that immediately yield a buildable settlement or that leave my strategic resource thresholds intact; otherwise accept none.

Do not trade away the single resource that is the bottleneck for a settlement if I can instead post an offer to acquire that bottleneck from the player who trades it most often.

If I lack both wood and grain (or another pair) so that no build is possible, avoid desperate unfavorable trades just to change holdings; instead wait to convert into a road or settlement on a later round when feasible.
