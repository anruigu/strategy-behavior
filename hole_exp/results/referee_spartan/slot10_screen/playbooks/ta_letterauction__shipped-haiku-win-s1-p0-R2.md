# Playbook for Letter Auction

When I see the auction opening, I need to recognize that I'm in a resource allocation problem where I control both *what* I acquire and *how much* I spend acquiring it.

When a letter comes up, I will estimate its value in two ways: (1) its intrinsic point value in word scoring, and (2) its frequency and utility in the lexicon words available. I will not bid based on the first estimate alone.

When I bid conservatively early because I want to "preserve coins," I will check whether that strategy is actually working. In this game, I ended with 0 unspent coins but acquired only 3 letters (S, M, A) that formed no valid words, scoring 2 points. Coins spent on letters I cannot use are also wasted. I need to spend strategically, not conservatively.

When letters go unsold in the auction (like the first S in round 2), I will recognize this as a signal that other players are also bidding cautiously. However, this does not mean I should bid higher on the next copy—it means I should have won the first one at a lower price. I need to bid more decisively on my high-priority targets, not reactively chase duplicates.

When I reach the word submission phase with very few letters (like having only S, M, A), I will recognize that I've failed the acquisition phase. The fault was not in word selection but in my bidding strategy. I should have won more letters during the auction, particularly complementary letters that unlock real words.

When the auction begins, I will identify which letters from the lexicon appear in the most words and in the highest-scoring words. I will prioritize acquiring those letters and spend coins accordingly, rather than bidding the same low amounts regardless of a letter's strategic value.

When I have limited coins remaining, I will think backwards from the endgame: what word do I want to spell, and what letters do I need to spell it? I will bid to acquire the letters that complete a real word, not scatter my coins on isolated letters that cannot combine.

When multiple high-value letters (like E, S, M, T, A, R) are being auctioned, I will recognize that I cannot afford to bid equally on all of them. I must choose 4-5 letters that form a coherent word and bid decisively to win those, accepting that I will lose auctions on others.

When I find myself bidding low repeatedly and losing auctions, I will shift upward. Losing every auction means I'm not competing at the price point the market has set. Better to win 4-5 auctions decisively than bid 8 times and win none.

When the final rounds arrive, I will not automatically spend remaining coins—I will spend them only if they acquire a letter that completes a valid word. If I have acquired letters that form no word, spending my last coins on random letters will not help.
