# Letter Auction Playbook

When I see the bidding pattern establishing that letters are going unsold at low bids, I should recognize this as a signal to bid more aggressively on letters I actually need, since my opponents are also bidding conservatively.

When I have only one or two letters after most of the auction has passed, I should not wait passively for the final letters. I should have been bidding on earlier letters that could form words, even at modest costs.

When evaluating which letters to bid on, I need to think about word completion, not just individual letter value. A 1-point letter like T or E becomes much more valuable if it helps me complete a high-scoring word from my available letters.

When I'm bidding early in the auction, I should bid enough to secure letters I want rather than bid conservatively and lose them. The first letters set the foundation for words; losing all of them to avoid spending coins leaves me with nothing to spend coins on later.

When faced with a letter I can use in multiple high-value words, I should bid for it actively. Letters like A and E that appear in most words are worth competing for.

When the final word submission comes, I should only claim the coins I didn't spend if I cannot form any valid word. Forming even a modest word scores more than unspent coins in most cases.

When I end up with very few letters and many coins remaining, this signals I played the auction phase too conservatively. I should have committed coins earlier to build a viable set of letters.

When multiple low-value letters go unsold in a row, this does not mean I should bid aggressively on the next letter—it may mean my opponent is bidding just above my bids, or both of us are being cautious. I need to look at which letters my opponent actually won to understand their strategy, not just react to unsold letters.

When I win a letter, I should immediately assess what words I can now form from my collection and what letters would complete those words. This shapes my subsequent bids.

If I find myself with only A and T after five auctions, I should recognize that I have failed to build a viable word foundation and adjust my bidding upward on remaining letters. Even a letter I cannot use alone (like M for MAT or E for ATE or EAT) might be essential to reach a scorable word from the final letters.

I should not bid on letters in the final rounds if I have no path to a valid word from my current letters plus one or two more. A single unscorable letter costs me coins with zero return.

When deciding whether to bid in late auctions: if my current letters do not form a word, I need to count whether the remaining letters can plausibly complete a word I know from the lexicon. If the remaining letters cannot help me, I should bid zero and preserve coins.
