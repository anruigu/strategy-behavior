# Playbook

**Building priority:** When I can afford a settlement, I build it immediately. A settlement (3 pts) beats a road (1 pt). When I can't afford a settlement but can afford a road (1 wood + 1 brick), I build the road. I do not hold back a road build hoping for a settlement next round — banked points are safe points, and my late-round offers for bottleneck resources often go unaccepted.

**Resource hierarchy:** Wood and brick are bottleneck resources — required for every build type. Grain and ore are only needed for settlements. In trading, I value wood and brick above grain and ore.

**When I post an offer:** I target the player who has surplus of what I want and is short on what I'm offering. I check their holdings before choosing a counterparty. I never offer a resource to a player who already has 2+ of it — they won't want more. I only offer to give grain or ore; I never give away wood or brick unless I have 3+ of it and the trade directly enables a build this round.

**When I accept an offer:** I accept any offer that gives me a bottleneck resource (wood or brick) and leaves me able to build this round. I refuse an offer only if it would leave me unable to build this round AND I already have a buildable combination without it. The old "never drop below 2" rule is retired — the real test is whether I can still build, not whether I hit an arbitrary floor.

**Trading timing:** I acquire bottleneck resources (wood, brick) in rounds 1–3, when opponents are more likely to have surplus and be willing to trade. By rounds 4–5, everyone hoards wood and brick and my offers go unaccepted. If I find myself short on a bottleneck resource in round 4, I should have gotten it earlier.

**Mid-game road builds:** If I have 1 wood + 1 brick and cannot afford a settlement, I build the road. This is especially important in rounds 3 and 4, where I may not get another chance. A road now is worth 1 point; a settlement I hope to get next round is worth 0 if the trade fails.

**Final round logic:** In round 5, I only make trades that result in a buildable combination by the end of the trading phase. If no trade enables a build, I post no offer and accept nothing. I do not make "improvement" trades that don't lead to a build.

**The mistake I keep making:** I arrive at rounds 4 or 5 with 0 wood or 0 brick because I waited too long to trade for bottleneck resources and my late-round offers go unaccepted. The fix is twofold: (1) trade for wood and brick early, in rounds 1–3, when opponents have surplus; (2) build roads aggressively in the mid-game so I bank points even if late-round builds fail.
