# Playbook for Win As Much As You Can

**On pledges:**

When others pledge Y and I'm considering X, calculate the concrete payoff advantage over the pledge period. If picking X while others pick Y gives me significantly more points than mutual Y coordination, pledge X to exploit that advantage—but be prepared for defection.

When I see others have defected from their pledges in recent rounds, weight that behavior heavily. Don't assume pledges are binding just because they were made; treat them as weak signals of intent.

When all other players make identical pledges to coordinate on the same choice, honor that pledge if the collective outcome is strong. Perfect coordination is rare and fragile; breaking it for a small individual gain risks triggering mutual defection that destroys everyone's score.

**On early rounds before multipliers:**

When I can identify a consistent exploitable pattern (like others coordinating on one choice), resist the urge to milk it ruthlessly in low-multiplier rounds. Early exploitation breaks coordination and poisons the later, higher-multiplier rounds where the real points are.

Understand that being a consistent outlier (always picking the opposite of others) creates an unstable dynamic. Others will eventually notice and may coordinate against you or give up on honoring pledges.

When all other players have pledged the same choice and kept to it for multiple consecutive rounds, they are signaling genuine commitment to that coordination. At that point, joining them is not weakness—it's recognizing a stable equilibrium that benefits everyone more than individual defection.

**On positioning and lead management:**

When I have perfect coordination with all other players (everyone playing the same choice) and we're all tied in score, breaking that tie on a low-multiplier round gains me only 1–2 points while risking everything on the x10 round. The asymmetry is stark: small gain now, catastrophic loss later.

When everyone else pledges the same choice before a high-multiplier round and I'm at parity with them, honor coordination to keep the equilibrium stable. Individual exploitation on the final round, when it's x10, is too dangerous to attempt when all players are equal.

When approaching the final round at x10 multiplier with all players tied and all pledging Y, picking Y gives everyone +30 each. Picking X gives me -30 while others get +30 each, handing them the win decisively. There is no path to victory through defection at that point; the only path is to maintain coordination.

**What worked:**

Perfect coordination maintained across the entire game. By picking Y every round from round 1 onward and pledging Y when others did, I locked in mutual cooperation on all four players. Final score: +25. I didn't win, but I tied with everyone—all players scored identically.

Recognizing when a stable equilibrium has formed and refusing to disrupt it for marginal gains. Once I committed to the Y pattern in round 1 and others matched it, the value of that commitment grew with each multiplier round, not diminished.

**What didn't work:**

My original playbook advised exploiting others' pledges ruthlessly in early rounds. I didn't follow that advice this episode, and it was the right call. Exploitation breaks coordination and is only valuable if you can sustain a lead through the multipliers—which you cannot if others retaliate.

The premise that "early rounds are where you build your lead before multipliers amplify everything" is backwards. The multipliers amplify *coordination* just as much as they amplify defection. Perfect cooperation at x10 beats any lead built on exploitation, because the multiplier compounds both.
