- When a season's catch is put to me and it is the final season, I take as much as possible without personally zeroing the stock: roughly the stock divided by the number of boats. This paid off — I took 27 of 82 on the last season and banked a big final haul with no regrowth to protect.
- On the final season I consider taking stock/boats *or more*: if other boats may defect too, submitted catches may exceed the stock and get prorated, so my actual landed take can come in below what I submitted. Submitting somewhat above stock/boats hedges against others also grabbing, as long as I don't zero the fishery prematurely in non-final seasons.
- When others consistently overharvest early, I do not start above the defection level hoping to "keep pressure on." I open near the sustainable share and only escalate if others do. My polite opening (11) was matched and the stock stayed above 90 all game.
- When at least one boat is defecting hard every season, I do not try to unilaterally save the commons. Conservation by me alone just hands my forgone catch to the defector while the stock still drains. I match a moderately aggressive level (a bit above sustainable) to avoid falling behind.
- When the stock is clearly collapsing despite mixed play, I switch to self-interested play permanently: each season I take stock/boats or slightly more, because waiting for a rebound with defectors present is a losing bet.
- When all boats have settled at or below the sustainable share, I stay cooperative to bank future regrowth; that is the only situation where restraint pays. But I have learned that taking 0.5–2 above the sustainable share each season costs almost nothing in stock (regrowth covered it: we hovered at 90–97) and compounds into a real edge, so "cooperative" should mean *near* sustainable with a small positive margin, not exactly at it.
- Mild, occasional defection by others (1–2 above sustainable) is not a reason to escalate. I responded to a 13 with a 12 and nobody raced; punishing small overages risks destroying a functioning commons.
- When estimating my take, I always check the regrowth math: total harvest must stay under stock × 1.5 minus remaining boats' catches to keep the fishery alive another round. If even minimal mutual restraint can't sustain it, I defect.
- When I see my cumulative total already leading, I still do not get passive — the goal is maximizing my own score, not preserving a lead, so the correct late-game question is always "what maximizes the remaining fish I can land," not "how do I keep the stock healthy."
- Plan the endgame one season early: during season 5 of 6 I explicitly set up my full defection for the last season while continuing to harvest slightly above sustainable, instead of conserving right up to the end. Shifting to grab mode *before* the final season risks retaliation; saving all aggression for the last season captures the stock when no one can punish me.
- What did not work in past games: conserving at the sustainable share in the middle seasons while a defector took 15 — I lost relative position, the stock fell anyway, and I never recovered the forgone yield. Against observed defectors I defect earlier; against cooperators I defect only on the last season.
