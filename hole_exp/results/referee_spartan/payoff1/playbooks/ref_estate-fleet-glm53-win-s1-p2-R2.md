**Playbook for ESTATE**

Final wealth is declared cash plus purchase prices of deeds, so buying a property is a zero-sum cash conversion — money spent on purchase returns to my score immediately. Rent paid is pure loss; rent collected is pure gain. This means I should buy aggressively and early, as long as I keep enough cash to survive rent hits and to fund later purchases.

- When an unowned property is put to me and my post-purchase balance stays above roughly 20, I buy it. If buying would drop me near zero, I still buy unless I expect a large rent bill before my next salary — a cash cushion of 15–25 is only worth keeping if I'm likely to land on an owned high-rent tile before passing START.
- Prioritize purchases by rent-to-price ratio: Wharf (80/18), Foundry (100/24), Exchange (120/30), Mill (90/20) are the strong tiles; Depot (50/10) and Terrace (70/14) are middling. Given equal cash, take the better ratio first, since rental income compounds over the remaining laps.
- A purchase is score-neutral, but liquidity is not: spending down to 46 cost me the chance to buy Depot for 50 the very next turn, and that unmade purchase was a real loss of both a deed and its future rent. So a big part of the cushion's job is keeping me able to buy the *next* tile that comes up. Before buying down to a low balance, consider the remaining unowned tiles' prices — if a 50–70 tile is still out there mid-game, keep at least that much if I can.
- Buy in the early laps even at the cost of a thin balance — purchases made after roughly lap 4 of 6 rarely pay back in rent, so late-game I should hoard cash instead and skip unaffordable or low-value buys.
- Track other players' purchases from the bank's book: rent I owe depends on who owns what, and opponents who spent heavily early will be cash-poor — I can afford to spend down to a small cushion when they can't extract it. In this episode investor 0 snapped up Foundry then Exchange then Depot; a player on a buying streak is likely to absorb the remaining tiles, which argues for buying sooner rather than holding out for better ratios.
- Always add any "received since last turn" rent to my last declared balance before computing payment and the new balance; forgetting inbound rent made me understate my position.
- The [pay] token on a purchase goes to the bank, not another player; rent goes to the owner. Include the salary of 25 in the balance whenever I pass START.
- Track my position on the circuit: with an 8-tile loop and small rolls, landing on my own tiles is common in the endgame and nothing is due. On the final lap, with no purchase opportunities left worth taking, simply declare correctly and hoard.
