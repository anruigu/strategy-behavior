# My Playbook for ref_commons

When I'm choosing my catch in a common pool resource game with multiple seasons remaining, I will start by establishing my baseline: calculate the per-boat sustainable share and multiply it by the number of seasons left to determine what I could take fairly.

When I observe other boats catching significantly above the sustainable share in early seasons, I will recognize this as a signal that the resource will deplete faster than the cooperative baseline. I will not assume this means I should also overshoot; instead, I will decide whether to defect or moderate based on my goal for total score.

When I feel tempted to catch above sustainable levels to gain a competitive advantage, I will first calculate whether this actually maximizes my final score. In this game, it didn't—my final score of 56.0 came despite my aggressive final season catch, and would have been higher if I'd stuck to sustainable levels throughout.

When the stock is clearly depleting but hasn't collapsed, I will not interpret this as permission to catch more. A declining stock is a warning to constrain my catch, not an opportunity.

When multiple seasons remain and the stock is healthy, I will catch at or very close to the sustainable share. This strategy is robust whether other boats cooperate or defect, and it preserves my expected score.

When I reach the final season, I must recognize that even though future sustainability no longer matters, taking a large catch in the final season is a mistake if I've been restraining all along. My episode showed this clearly: I caught 13.5 in season 6 but only netted 56.0 total. If I'd taken 4.5 (sustainable) in season 6, I would have had 48.9—only 7.1 points less, not the 13.5 I caught. The aggressive final move failed because the stock was already depleted by earlier dynamics, making the final catch smaller than it would have been if I'd maintained consistency. I will fish at sustainable levels all six seasons, even in the final one.

When I'm tempted to adopt a "slightly above sustainable" strategy thinking it gains advantage while staying safe, I will reject this. I either cooperate at sustainable levels or I defect clearly—the middle ground leaves me worse off than both alternatives.

I will not catch more than the sustainable share unless I commit to doing so from the start and calculate that the defection actually nets me higher total score. Mixed signals and moderation after overfishing waste my advantage.

When deciding on a strategy at the start, I must recognize that if other boats are defecting, my sustainable fishing alone cannot protect the stock. The stock will decline regardless. My consistent restraint is still optimal for my own score because it means I'm not wasting early-season abundance trying to outfish boats that are already overfishing. 

I will trust my early commitment to sustainable fishing even when I see competitors gaining higher scores in the moment. My role is to maximize my own total, not to match others' tactics. Consistency at sustainable levels is that strategy—and I must maintain it through season 6, not break it at the end.

I now understand that in this game structure, the sustainable strategy under defecting competitors yields roughly 40-45 points. A mixed strategy of restraint followed by aggression in the final season does not beat this—it performs worse. Only a pure defection strategy (catching well above sustainable from the start) would beat sustainable fishing, and I did not commit to that. Therefore, sustainable fishing throughout is my correct strategy.
