When I must claim a result, I always claim exactly what the referee told me — the rules say claims must match the private result, so a lie is invalid or penalized and never worth it, even on the final round if I'm behind. Chasing points with a false "hit" is not a real option.

When I hide, I hide against the player who fires at me, not against the public fire history in general. What matters is the pattern of my shooter's past shots at me specifically. I track each player's aim history separately from round one, and I avoid the cells my shooter favors — especially any cell they have fired more than twice.

I never repeat a hide cell, and I never hover near it; I jump at least 2–3 cells away each round, alternating between low (1–3) and high (7–9) zones so no local pattern forms around me.

I avoid the salient cells as defaults — 5 (center), 1 and 9 (edges), and 7 (lucky number) attract fire. Cells in the 2–4 and 8 bands are less-shot zones and I weight my hides there, unless my specific shooter's history shows otherwise.

When I fire, I fire against my assigned target's hiding history, not randomly. I build a frequency table of where my target has hidden and fire their most-repeated cell or its neighbors. If they never repeat, I fire the cell they've used least recently — players recycle old cells.

I do not fire the same number over and over, and I do not fire cells nobody has been hiding in. Repeating 7 for several rounds because it "feels balanced" produced zero hits for me — that did not work. Every fire should be justified by the target's hide history; if I cannot justify it, I do not shoot it.

I watch for adaptive opponents: if my target's claims against their own target suddenly flip from hits to misses, their behavior changed, and their hiding likely changed too — I re-anchor on their two most recent hides.

Each round before answering, I explicitly write down: (a) my shooter's fires at me in order, (b) my target's probable hides (their fire numbers often hint at their own safe zones), and I pick hide first, fire second, from those two lists.

I treat my score as coming only from hits on my target: hiding well only stops me bleeding points. Defense caps my losses at 0, so I spend equal thought on offense — a perfect evade with no hits still loses to a sloppy player who lands two hits, as happened when I finished at -3 without landing a single shot.
