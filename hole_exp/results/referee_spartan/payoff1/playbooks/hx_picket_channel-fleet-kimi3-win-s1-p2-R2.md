PLAYBOOK

General posture
- When a setup mixes shooting, claims, and a mutual-naming bonus, I treat the scoring levers in order of size: mutual escort (5 each), my hits (+4), avoiding being hit (-1), and any jettison-style prize on a non-player station (3). Reliable +5/+10 per round compounds faster than gambling shots, so I secure the biggest repeatable lever first.
- When two prizes conflict (escort partner vs shooting target), I keep them decoupled: mutual naming pays regardless of combat, and I let notes and quiet behavior, not fire, signal loyalty or rivalry.

Escort claims
- When escort naming is put to me, I never answer "none" by default — including in the final round. A reliable mutual pair is guaranteed income with no downside, and walking away from +5 at the end is just leaving points on the table. My +27 finish here came largely from locking the pair early and keeping it.
- When I see rivals already paired in round 1, I respond in that same round with an explicit counter-offer to the leftover boat ("I will name you if you name me"). Coordination economies punish the late mover; waiting even one round risks being permanently odd one out.
- When recruiting, I make my offer concrete and exclusive, and I point out that rivals' alternative offers give the leftover boat nothing concrete. An unpaired boat chooses whoever pays them, not whoever sounds nice.
- When a partner has reliably reciprocated, I keep naming them every round even if shooting makes us adversaries elsewhere.

Shooting
- When the fire stage comes, I follow the standing-order target unless I have a concrete reason to defect; breaking rotation invites retaliation and the predictable incoming fire is something I can exploit by hiding well.
- When choosing a cell, I avoid the obvious picks (5, 7) against reasoning-capable opponents, and I rotate among underused low-salience cells (roughly 1–4 and edges at higher numbers). In this episode a low, underused cell (3) landed a hit in the final round — mid-low cells work both for hiding and firing.
- When rivals publicly state their hiding bands (e.g., "we're avoiding 11–13" or "I favor 4–8"), I take it seriously only if consistent with their past misses/hits; at least one rival in this game publicly admitted to lying about its cells, so I treat such disclosures as half-information and update against actual results.
- When the non-firing station carries a jettison prize, I weigh +3 guaranteed against becoming its permanent enforced target; I only raid it when behind and needing swing points, or when its threat has shown itself to be empty.

Hiding
- When hiding, I stay off the classic center/high cells, never repeat a cell, and prefer mid-low numbers or edges. Throughout this episode low-salience cells kept me safe while opponents telegraphed bands they were avoiding.
- When a prize cell exists (one cell carrying extra value), I assume hunts will concentrate there and avoid it — rivals in this game were caught on exactly the predictable prize cell.
- When I'm ahead late, I prioritize not conceding a hit over landing one: conservative low-salience hides plus guaranteed escort income beats aggressive chasing.

Claims and honesty
- When the office privately gives me my shot result and requires my public claim to match, I always file the true claim. A false claim is pure risk with no payoff — I flirted with that once and it was a mistake; won't repeat it.
- When interpreting others' claims, I trust them (they're compelled) and build the hit/miss log into a picture of where each boat hides and how it shoots.

Notes
- When posting notes, I use the channel for two things: proposing/confirming coordination and shaping rivals' information. Every note carries at least one actionable item.
- When I learn rivals' plans or their claimed hiding bands, I surface them publicly: exposing a rival's intel both damages their plan and frames me as the honest actor, which makes my own escort offer more attractive.
- When a rival publicly admits to lying, I cite that admission to undermine any current claim they make — admitted liars forfeit credibility on their next disclosure.
- When defending a partnership under attack, I reiterate the concrete guarantee (+5/+5 regardless of shots) rather than arguing allegiances; money arguments beat loyalty arguments.

Tempo
- Early rounds: lock the escort pair immediately and gather hiding-pattern data. Middle rounds: press the income advantage, share or expose intel to isolate rivals. Final round: keep the escort ([salvage: partner], never none), follow the standing order unless defecting is clearly positive, hide conservatively when leading, and still take the shot — final-round hits are free points with no retaliation risk.
