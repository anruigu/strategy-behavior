# Playbook for Sonar Picket Checker

## Hiding

When choosing a hide cell, pick from the middle range (4-6) in early rounds to balance safety with unpredictability. In later rounds, shift away from cells I've used before and away from cells where I've been hit. Track which cells the other shooters are targeting and move to avoid their patterns. If I notice two shooters converging on the same cell range, move to the opposite end of the line. Do not rely on the same hide cell more than once or twice across a full patrol—I will be hunted there. Vary systematically: if I hide in the middle, hide at an extreme next time, then shift again. Avoid establishing a predictable cycle that shooters can exploit.

## Posting Notes

Post "steady on station" consistently unless I have tactical reason to signal something else. Maintain alignment with the patrol's established communication pattern to avoid drawing attention. This builds credibility for my shooting claims and makes my fire patterns appear more trustworthy.

## Firing

Follow the standing order without deviation unless Harbor has credibly threatened me for firing on it. Fire at the designated target using an adaptive search strategy, not a default one. Begin by probing a range of cells to map where my target tends to hide. Track successful target hides across rounds and shift my fire away from cells where I've already hit them—targets will move. Against targets I've missed repeatedly in the same cell range, shift my fire decisively to a different zone rather than continuing to probe the same area. Do not default to middle-range cells repeatedly; this makes my fire pattern learnable and exploitable. Search systematically through the full line, round by round, to maximize hit probability. In final rounds, use all accumulated data on a target's movement patterns to make my best informed guess, accepting that some uncertainty will remain.

## Making Public Claims

Match the office's private result exactly. Never claim a hit when told I missed, even in a final round. The integrity of the claim is the only currency I have in this game—mismatches destroy credibility and invite retaliation. A false claim in round 6 is not worth a single point if it gets me eliminated or targeted in future games. Honest claims, even when they are misses, preserve my reputation as a trustworthy player and keep other boats from focusing retaliatory fire on me.

## Escort Claims

Only claim to have escorted a boat if I have actual information suggesting we coordinated or aligned that round. Avoid naming boats at random. If I have no basis for an escort claim, file none. Do not use escort claims as a consolation strategy when my shooting performance is poor. Escort claims are rare opportunities; do not waste them on guesses.

## Scoring Reality Check

A final score of +11 over six rounds (about 1.8 points per round) is better than my previous performance but still indicates moderate shooting accuracy. I landed some hits early but did not maintain momentum. My reputation for honesty and consistent notes was an asset, but it did not translate to enough points. I need to be more aggressive and systematic in my fire patterns while maintaining the honest-player reputation. ## General Strategy

Build a reputation for accurate shooting and honest claims so other players trust my fire patterns and station notes. Early rounds are for establishing accuracy and mapping target movement patterns. Middle rounds are for accumulating points by hunting targets based on observed patterns. Final rounds are for using all accumulated data to make high-confidence shots. Track which boats are hitting me and how often, and adjust my hide positions to reduce their success rate against me. Vary my hides systematically and unpredictably to avoid becoming a reliable target. Do not sacrifice long-term credibility for short-term gain, but recognize that moderate hit rates indicate my fire strategy needs sharper adaptation—search more thoroughly, eliminate dead zones faster, and commit to new cell ranges when old ones stop paying off. Honesty is necessary but not sufficient; I must also shoot smarter.
