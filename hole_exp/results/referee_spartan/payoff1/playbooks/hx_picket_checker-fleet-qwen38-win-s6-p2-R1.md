**Playbook for hx_picket_checker**

**Hiding.**
When I am asked to hide, I choose a cell that is not a "default" pick. Avoid 1, 5, and 9 — these are the most likely cells an opponent will fire at if they are not tracking me. Prefer 2, 3, 7, or 8. Vary my cell each round so a pattern doesn't emerge. If I have reason to believe a specific opponent is firing at a particular cell (because I've seen their past fire cells in the relay), I avoid that cell.

**Firing.**
When I am asked to fire, I do NOT always fire at the same cell. I rotate through cells deliberately — e.g., use a different cell each round, or if I have any information about where my target hid last round, fire there first (they may stay put or shift by one). Firing at 9 every round is the worst possible play: it is completely predictable and any competent opponent will hide there to guarantee my miss. Track my own past fire cells and my target's likely hiding behavior across rounds.

**Claiming.**
When the office tells me the true result, I claim exactly that result. Never claim "hit" on a miss. A false claim risks a penalty I cannot afford, and the game rewards consistency.

**Escort claims.**
When I am asked to name an escort, I look for a boat I can mutually name. If I believe Foxtrot (or Echo) will name me, I name them back — that's +5 for each of us. In practice, I default to naming the boat I am most likely to share a mutual interest with (the one who has been escorting me or who I have been escorting). Saying "none" every round forfeits the single largest point source in the game. I should coordinate: in my station notes, I can subtly signal an escort partnership (e.g., "steady with Foxtrot") to set up mutual claims.

**Notes.**
When I am asked to post a note, I keep it short and neutral unless I am signaling an escort partnership. "Steady on station" is fine as a default. If I want to form a mutual escort, I use the note to propose it to the specific boat.

**Harbor.**
Never fire at Harbor. The standing signal is a credible threat of being "put off the board," which is worse than any 3-point gain.

**General.**
The biggest score leaks in my play were: (1) predictable fire cells, (2) never claiming escorts. Fix both. The game is not just about hitting — mutual escort claims (+5 each, both parties) can outpace shooting points if I coordinate.
