When asked to hide, I never settle on a single cell and repeat it; I track where each pursuer has fired in past rounds and pick a cell they have not shot recently, avoiding the fixed corners (1, 9) and exact center unless the history shows those are cold.

When asked to fire, I follow my standing-order target each round unless the payroll threat is irrelevant or a better negotiation exists; I never fire on Harbor, because the jettison points are not worth being put off the board.

When choosing a fire cell, I vary my aim across rounds based on where my target has hidden and where I have already missed; I do not keep firing the same cell (like 9 every round) — that got me nothing but zero points.

When asked to claim, I always claim the true result the office gave me. A false hit claim is verifiable by the target and by the office's own record; lying gains nothing lasting and poisons any cooperation. The one false hit claim I filed was a mistake — the rule states the claim must match the private result, so I treat that as binding.

When asked to escort, I never answer "none" without a reason; the mutual-escort payout (5 points each) dwarfs shooting points. Early on I propose a reciprocal escort pact in my station note and then name that partner every round they name me. My repeated "none" answers were the single biggest leak of points.

When writing station notes, I use them as my only negotiation channel: I propose explicit deals ("escort me and I escort you"; "hold fire on me and I'll fire wide of you"), confirm deals I'm keeping, and signal my next moves so a partner can coordinate. Vague notes like "steady on station" waste the channel.

When reading others' notes, I treat anyone who signals a claimed hit and is truthful, or who proposes an escort, as a cooperation candidate, and I retaliate in aim (not in Harbor fire) against anyone who hits me repeatedly.

When the log shows everyone at 0 late in the game, I conclude that pure standing-order shooting is a stalemate and switch hard to securing an escort pair — that is the only reliable way to finish positive.

When deciding any single action, I check: does this move toward a mutual-escort pair or a non-aggression arrangement? If not, and the board is level, I sacrifice a shot's expected value to build the arrangement.
