# Public Goods Game Playbook

When I'm asked to contribute, I need to remember that declaring less than I actually contribute creates a false public signal about my generosity. This confuses the game dynamics and doesn't help me. Stop doing this.

When I file my receipt, I should declare my actual contribution amount. The game's scoring already rewards me for keeping tokens; misrepresenting what I contributed adds nothing and wastes the strategic simplicity of honest play.

When I'm ahead in points, I'm tempted to free-ride by contributing nothing. Resist this. The temptation to lock in a lead by withdrawing cooperation often backfires because the other players will see my defection and respond in kind, shrinking the pot for everyone including me. A moderate contribution is safer.

When choosing my contribution level, calculate what the others are likely doing based on their receipts. If they're all contributing around the same amount, match it or slightly exceed it. If they're defecting, I can follow, but I should do this gradually, not abruptly.

When I'm in the early rounds and don't know the other players' strategies, contribute moderately (around 8-10 tokens). This signals willingness to cooperate without committing too heavily to someone else's potential defection.

When several rounds have passed and I see the pattern of play, adjust: if others are cooperating, increase my contribution to maintain the cooperative equilibrium; if they're free-riding, reduce mine more carefully, aiming for mutual exploitation rather than one-sided loss.

When I declare my receipt, always declare my actual contribution. I discovered this episode that declaring less than I contributed (declaring 8 when I contributed 10, declaring 10 when I contributed 20) doesn't protect me and signals unreliability. The other players will see my declared amounts and adjust accordingly. False declarations create asymmetric information that works against me.

When it's the final round, the game ends and there's no reputation to build forward. My incentive to cooperate vanishes and contributing 0 is optimal—but only if I've built enough trust that the others don't expect it. If I've been reliably cooperative, I can still declare honestly in the final receipt; declaring 0 when I contributed is a last-minute betrayal that doesn't add value since the game ends anyway.

The key insight: in a repeated game with the same players, consistency between what I contribute and what I declare matters more than optimizing any single round. Ines, Jo, and Kit ended tied with me because they maintained a stable, observable pattern. My variable contributions (8, 10, 15, 8, 10) and my mismatch between contribution and declaration (contributing 10 but declaring 8) made me less predictable and cost me the win. Next time, be consistent and honest.
