**Playbook: ref_commons**

Treat the sustainable share as a reference point, not a ceiling. It tells me what keeps the stock flat if all boats match it. If I'm taking at or slightly above it and the stock is still above ~88, I'm in a good position to extract a small premium.

When the stock is at or near its maximum (95+), I take 10–15% above the sustainable share. The buffer is large and any drop is visible, so I keep the overage modest and readable.

In the first season specifically, I do not go far above sustainable. The stock is at its peak, so any drop is maximally visible and I want to avoid being the one who triggers a sharp decline that hurts everyone. A small premium (5–10% above) is the right range.

When the stock is in the mid-80s to mid-90s range, I take at the sustainable share or a small premium (5–10% above). I do NOT go below sustainable in this range. The stock can absorb a moderate overage and will recover; going below sustainable just leaves value on the table for no meaningful strategic gain. The fact that another boat is taking a bit more than sustainable is not a reason for me to compensate by taking less.

When the stock drops below ~82, I switch to genuinely conservative mode: I take at or slightly below the sustainable share. At that point the buffer is thin and a misstep could cascade.

In the final season, I push well above the sustainable share—30–40% above it if the stock is above ~85. There is no next season to preserve, and the stock's buffer almost always absorbs a large final-season take as long as the stock isn't already critically low. I calculate the maximum safe take as the stock minus (the expected combined catch of the other boats) minus a small safety margin, and I aim near that limit.

I do not use "others are taking at or above sustainable" as a trigger to go below sustainable myself. That reasoning led me to underharvest in seasons 3–5 when the stock was still around 90. Instead, I ask: is the stock declining fast, or just slowly? A slow decline (1–2 points per season) with the stock still above 88 is manageable and I should continue taking at or near sustainable.

When I can see that one or more boats caught well below the sustainable share in the previous season, I take above the sustainable share this season. The stock grew more than the cooperative baseline predicts, and I'm effectively harvesting that surplus.

I do not match the exact decimal of the sustainable share if I'm going to deviate. A small rounding looks like calculation noise, which is fine. But if I'm deliberately taking more, I take a clearly larger number so my intent is unambiguous on review.

I track the stock trajectory across seasons. If it's declining by more than ~3 points per season, I tighten up. If it's stable or recovering, I have room to be a bit more aggressive.
