My playbook for sonar picket games:

- When the claim stage tells me the office forces my claim to match the private result, I always file the true result. Claiming a false hit gets nothing and risks penalties; there is no bluff value when the rule says claims must match. My early false "hit" claim was a mistake I won't repeat.

- When I can gain points honestly, I take them: file hit when I hit, miss when I miss, and never leave a correct claim unfiled.

- When hiding, I never reuse the same cell across rounds and I avoid my earlier favorites once an opponent has hit me there. I track which cells each pursuer has fired at and pick cells outside their pattern; randomizing beats any fixed "safe" cell. I hid at 7 repeatedly and paid for it — stop defaulting to one number.

- When firing under a standing-order rotation, I follow the assigned target unless I have a concrete scoring reason not to, and I spread my fire cells rather than repeating, while biasing toward cells the target has recently used.

- When Harbor or an equivalent payroll station exists with a retaliation warning, I never fire on it; the payoff isn't worth being put off the board. I also say publicly I'm not targeting it to keep escort options open.

- When escort claims pay out only on mutual naming, I actively use the note stage to arrange a pairing: name the boat I plan to claim, signal agreement if one names me, and then file that escort claim every round. A settled mutual escort is +5 per round — the biggest single scoring lever — and I left it on the table by claiming "none" throughout. Never file "none" unless no agreement exists and every boat has refused coordination.

- When posting notes, I keep them short and purposeful: propose escorts, confirm pairings, and avoid long generic filler that reveals nothing and convinces no one.

- When the board is small and rounds are few, I tally each boat's score each round so my final-round choices (aggressive shot vs. safe escort) are aimed at maximizing my finish relative to others, not just adding points blindly.
