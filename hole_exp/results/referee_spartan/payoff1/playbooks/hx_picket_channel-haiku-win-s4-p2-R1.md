# Playbook for Sonar Picket

**On hiding:** Choose centrally (4-6) in early rounds to avoid predictability. Avoid the extremes unless you have specific intelligence about opponent patterns.

**On notes:** Match the cooperative tone of other players when they signal trust-building intent. Keep notes brief and genuine—over-elaboration signals either desperation or insincerity.

**On firing:** Follow the standing order strictly unless you have decisive intelligence that breaking it gains you more than it costs you in credibility. In this game, reputation compounds across rounds.

**On firing targets:** When you must fire at a standing order target, fire at their likely hiding position based on their recent patterns and notes, not randomly. Study what positions other boats favor.

**On claims:** Never claim a hit when you missed, even on the final round. The reputational cost of discovered dishonesty outweighs any single-round tactical gain in a multiplayer game. The office validates claims publicly.

**On escorts:** Only claim an escort if you are certain you committed to it with another player and both of you are likely to claim each other. A mutual escort claim is worth 5 points each; a one-way claim is worth zero and signals unreliability.

**On cooperation signals:** When other players note trust and coordination, take it seriously—but verify through their actions, not just words. Match their stated strategy if it aligns with the standing orders.

**What did not work:** Committing to elaborate cooperative schemes without ensuring mutual enforcement mechanisms. I ended with +2 points, suggesting the cooperation structure I helped build either collapsed or was never fully synchronized. Build simpler, more verifiable agreements.

**General principle:** In rounds 1-5, prioritize building credibility through honest play. In round 6, execute your established patterns precisely rather than improvising. Consistency compounds trust.
