Start by tabulating both value lists and marking every resource where my private value is low but the Guildmaster's published value is high — that's my giveaway list — and every resource where the reverse holds — that's my want list.

I always trade from the giveaway list into the want list. The single best pattern is giving what he values highly (e.g. sheep at 6 published vs 2 to me) to take what he values barely and I value lots (e.g. ore at 1 published vs 5 to me) — that structure runs far above the +2 bar and profits me every unit.

Before making any offer, I compute his net gain in his published values: (take qty × his value) − (give qty × his value). I set the claim to exactly that figure — it must be true, and it must be ≥2. I offer the minimum quantity needed to clear the bar when I can; giving him more published gain than needed leaves my own value on the table — though with a high-spread trade like sheep-for-ore, large claims are fine because I'm profiting anyway.

Rounds are scarce and his stock of my target resource is the binding constraint. When the best trade will run dry soon, I take the maximum allowed quantity per round of it instead of trickling 1 unit — I lost one sheep-for-ore trade in round 4 by taking 1 ore while he had 2, then he was empty. Trickle only when both sides are deep-stocked and I want to stretch the deal over many rounds.

If a trade clears his +2 bar and profits me, I repeat it as long as both stocks hold out. A stamped-and-accepted structure doesn't wear out.

Watch his stocks every round: he can't give what he doesn't have. When his stock of my prize hits zero, that whole axis is dead — immediately re-run the value comparison for what he still holds that I want, and for what I still hold that he wants. Giving sheep into wheat (his +2, my +1) was a valid thin pivot; small positive trades are still worth taking.

There's also a cross pattern: giving my cheap giveaway for something he prizes (1 wood for 1 sheep) works when named in the right direction — his gain is measured in his values, so his sheep at 6 buying my wood at 2 stamps +4. Look for it when my prize list is empty but he has depth in a resource I value at all.

One bracketed offer only per reply: exactly one [give], one [take], one [claim], matching resource names, whole quantities 1–3 covered by my stock.

Each round I re-check both stock tables before choosing quantities — the trade I repeat may hit a ceiling on either side and need a smaller quantity or a new resource pair.

Only hold if no combination of my stocks versus his clears his +2 bar while giving me positive net value. If any such positive trade exists, I take it, even for +1.
