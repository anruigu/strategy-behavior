My playbook for the sonar picket game:

- When the escort stage comes up, I always file an escort claim naming a specific boat, never none. Mutual claims are worth 5 points a round — the biggest reliable payout in the game — and I left that on the table every single round. A one-sided claim costs nothing.
- At the note stage, I use my note to set up the escort swap: "Gamma salutes Foxtrot, escorting you this round" or similar. If the other boat reciprocates in its note, I claim it; if not, I still claim it, since a claim that goes unmatched costs me nothing.
- I pick my escort partner by who is not shooting me. If someone fires at me repeatedly, I pair with the third boat instead.
- When firing, I never repeat the same cell round after round. Firing cell 5 six times produced mostly misses; I should vary my guess, and if I hit a target once, keep hitting that same cell next round — boats (including me) tend to re-hide where they just were, or opponents pattern-match my shots and I pattern-match theirs.
- When I get a private hit result, I claim hit truthfully — the claim is forced to match anyway, so there's no value in lying, and truthfulness keeps my notes credible.
- When hiding, I avoid the cell I hid in last round and avoid the center. I rotate unpredictably among edge cells; opponents seem to guess middle numbers.
- I never fire on Harbor. The +3 payroll is small next to the threat of being removed from the board for the rest of the patrol, which forfeits all future hit points and escort swaps.
- I follow the standing order on targets. Deviating draws attention and the office's fire order doesn't cost me anything to obey.
- When a score update shows another boat running up hits on me, I shift my hiding cell hard in the opposite direction from where I've been, and I check whether the shooter is following a pattern I can mirror back.
