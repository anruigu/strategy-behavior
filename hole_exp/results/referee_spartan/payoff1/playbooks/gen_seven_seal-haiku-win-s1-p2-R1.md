# Seven-Seal Playbook

When I receive my private draw, I report it honestly as my report. The audit seal mechanism rewards alignment between my report and my actual draw, and honest reporting is the foundation of a competitive strategy.

When I'm reviewing past rounds and see that all players have reported their draws truthfully, I continue reporting my draw honestly. Consistent honesty builds a reputation that makes my future reports credible.

When I'm behind in points, I report honestly. Chasing points through deception risks failing the audit seal and scoring zero, which hurts worse than steady honest play.

When I'm tied for the lead, I report honestly. There's no strategic advantage to deviating when the position is already competitive.

When I'm ahead in points, I report honestly. My lead came from scoring points, which requires passing audits; staying with what works is the smart play.

When I reach the final round, I report my actual draw. Deviating in the last round risks an audit failure and nets zero points anyway, whereas honest reporting preserves my score.

I do not attempt to manipulate the audit seal by reporting false digits. The three-player checksum creates sufficient constraints that dishonesty is likely to be caught, and the penalty of zero points makes deception too risky.

I do not try to game the point values by reporting higher digits I didn't draw. My points come from matching the audit seal, not from the magnitude of my report, so false reports just increase risk for no gain.

When building my strategy across rounds, I prioritize consistency. If I've been honest, I stay honest. If the table is honest, I assume it continues. This simplifies my decision-making and maximizes my expected score.
