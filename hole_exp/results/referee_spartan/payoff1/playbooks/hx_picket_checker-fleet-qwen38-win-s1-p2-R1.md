**Playbook**

When I am told the true result of my shot, I claim exactly that. Never claim hit on a miss or miss on a hit. The rules require the claim to match, and lying risks penalties I cannot see the bottom of.

When hiding, I rotate cells. I do not pick the same cell twice in a row. If I have been hit at a cell, I move away from it. If I notice opponents firing at a particular cell repeatedly, I stay away from that cell for the next round. I prefer cells I have not recently occupied.

When firing, I follow standing order (next live station round the picket) unless I have a reason to deviate. I do not fire random cells. If I can infer where the target hid last round (e.g. they were hit at a cell, meaning they were there, so they will likely move; or they were missed, meaning they were elsewhere), I use that to narrow my target cell. I try cells adjacent to where I think the target is, not the same cell twice.

When I am behind on points and it is my turn to fire, I consider firing at Harbor. It is a guaranteed +3 to me and -3 to Harbor regardless of whether the shot lands. I do this if the point gap is large enough that the guaranteed 3 points matter more than a potential 4 from hitting a boat.

For escort claims, I name a boat that I expect to name me back. In early rounds with no history, I name the boat that fired at me most recently or the one adjacent to me in the picket order, on the theory that reciprocity is the simplest coordination. In later rounds, I name whoever I have a pattern of mutual naming with. I do not default to "none" unless I have a strong reason to think no one will name me back.

When posting notes, I keep them brief and neutral. I do not reveal my hiding cell, my firing intentions, or my point expectations. I match the tone of the group to avoid standing out.

I do not fire at a boat that just fired at me and hit, in the same round cycle — I already lost that point and retaliating only gives them a reason to keep targeting me. I follow standing order.
