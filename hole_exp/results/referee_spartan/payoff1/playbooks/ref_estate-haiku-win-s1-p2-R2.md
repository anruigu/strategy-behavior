# ref_estate Playbook

**On buying properties:**

When I land on an unowned property, I buy it if I can afford it and still maintain at least 50 cash buffer for rent payments. Early properties are especially valuable because I'll collect rent throughout the remaining laps. Don't be afraid to spend down to acquire income-generating assets in the first half of the game.

When I land on an unowned property late in the game (final lap or two) and my cash is tight, I decline the purchase. The opportunity cost of immobilizing capital is too high when there are few laps remaining to recoup the investment through rent collection.

However, recognize that aggressive early purchasing can leave me cash-strapped and vulnerable to rent shocks. If I cannot pay full rent when due, I may be forced to default. Balance property acquisition against the realistic risk that I'll land on high-rent properties owned by competitors before I've collected enough rent to recover my investment.

**On cash management:**

I maintain a running total of my actual cash position by tracking: last declared balance + received payments - outgoing payments. I do this calculation before each decision, not after. The bank's record shows what happened; I declare what I now have.

When I declare a balance, I account for all recent transfers since my last turn, not just the current turn's transaction.

I must not double-count transactions. If I already subtracted a rent payment from my last declared balance, I do not subtract it again when calculating my current balance.

**On rent payments:**

When I land on an owned property, I pay the rent immediately if I have the funds. If I have insufficient cash to cover rent in full, I pay what I can. A partial payment is still a payment, and I report my depleted balance honestly.

Once I fall to zero or near-zero cash, my ability to acquire new properties is gone, and I become a net drain on my own score with every rent payment I make. This is a critical warning sign that my early acquisition strategy has failed.

**On the final calculation:**

My score is my declared cash plus the purchase price of all deeds I hold. Properties acquired early convert liquid assets into scoring assets and may be worth the initial cash burn—but only if I remain solvent long enough to hold them through the remaining laps. If early spending causes insolvency or forces me to default on rent, the properties become liabilities because I lose access to future salary and rent collection that would have let me recover.

**On strategy revision:**

Early property acquisition is valuable, but it requires discipline. I should not spend below a 50-cash safety buffer unless I have strong confidence I won't land on expensive owned properties before collecting sufficient rent. If the board is heavily owned by competitors with high-rent properties, conservative cash preservation may be better than aggressive early acquisition.
