# Harbor Customs Playbook

**Declaration Strategy:**

When declaring, always declare at least the value of your heaviest crate to avoid the -40 penalty. The penalty is so severe that honesty or near-honesty is safer than aggressive understatement.

When I have a total hold value, I will declare it truthfully or very close to it. The -40 flag penalty makes dishonesty costly and unreliable.

**Route Selection:**

When I've declared honestly and am in a competitive position, take the main route. The main route gives me 70% of my declared value as net gain (after duty), while night route sacrifices my smallest crate to save duty costs—only worthwhile if my smallest crate is worth significantly less than 15% of my declared value.

Calculate before deciding: main route nets (declared × 0.70), night route nets (total - smallest - floor(declared × 0.15)). Take whichever is higher.

**Positional Awareness:**

When I'm ahead in score, prioritize consistency over risk. Don't try to extend a lead with aggressive declarations that could backfire.

When I'm behind, I can afford slightly riskier declarations, but still stay within the range I see others using—declarations seem to cluster between 45-85, and outliers are rare.

**Pattern Reading:**

Track what declarations other players make across rounds. If everyone consistently goes main route, follow that pattern unless my hold composition strongly favors night route.

Don't overthink the final round. The same principles apply: declare honestly, choose the route with better expected value, and let the numbers work.
