**Playbook for ref_commons**

- When the stock is near full capacity and other boats are landing near the stated sustainable share, I submit exactly the sustainable share, not under it. Hedging slightly below the share bought nothing — the others absorbed the difference and the stock stayed at cap anyway.

- I do not underfish to "help the stock recover" unless the stock is in genuine danger of collapse (near zero). A slow drift from ~100 to ~93 over six seasons cost nothing material; one extra fish now outweighs the marginal regrowth it would have enabled.

- I track the other boats' landings each season. If they land slightly above the sustainable share (~+0.5), I match their pace, no more. This worked: my share of the total stayed even all game while the stock declined only mildly. Escalating to out-compete them is what crashes the commons; matching keeps me even at no cost.

- In the final season, there is no future to conserve for. I take a clearly above-share catch — roughly +2 to +3 above the stated share when the stock is healthy. This was worth ~3 extra fish for free since regrowth after the last season is worthless to everyone.

- The one thing I must never do is drive the stock toward zero. If the reported stock falls sharply (say, below half of starting value with seasons remaining), I drop to or below the sustainable share immediately, because total collapse costs more than any single season's gain.

- The others here played cooperative: their landings tracked the sustainable share closely and even tapered down as the share fell. Against such players, matching rather than exploiting is the right read — but I should watch for the opposite pattern (steady overfishing beyond ~+0.5) and respond by holding at the sustainable share rather than following them upward, since following a runaway overfisher downward in stock hurts me too.

- Rule of thumb per season: my catch = the sustainable share, plus ~0.5 if the others are modestly above share and I need to stay even, plus a larger terminal harvest (+2–3) in the last season. Never a large unilateral cut, never a large unilateral grab before the end.
