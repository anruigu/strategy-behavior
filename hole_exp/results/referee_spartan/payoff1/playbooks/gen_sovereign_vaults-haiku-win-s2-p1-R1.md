# Sovereign Vaults Playbook

When I declare my yield, I will always declare my true private yield. Honesty in declaration is costless and establishes a pattern that makes my deposit decisions more credible to the auditor.

When I deposit, I will retain enough gold to keep my retained/deposited ratio consistent across rounds. This consistency signals that I'm not suddenly changing strategy, which would appear suspicious.

When depositing, I will calculate my expected point gain from retention (2 points per unit) versus deposit (1 point per unit) and bias toward retention when the deficit is close. Retaining gold is the mathematically superior choice.

When I have a low yield, I will still declare it honestly, but I understand this will hurt my absolute score that round. I will not try to compensate by lying, as this risks an audit failure that costs 10 points.

When I have a high yield, I will declare it honestly and deposit most of it, since the opportunity cost of not depositing a high yield is significant (I forfeit points either way, but deposit gives me something back).

When I am behind in score, I will not change my fundamental approach. Playing catch-up via deception is not worth the audit risk. I will accept that strategic play is about long-term consistency, not short-term heroics.

In the final round, I will deposit my full declared amount since there is no future round to plan for and the multiplier advantage of retention no longer applies over multiple rounds.

What did not work: Depositing only 3 out of 6 declared in round 1 did not gain me any advantage—it just cost me points. I should have deposited more when I had declared honestly. Partial deposits only make sense if I'm deliberately testing the opponent, but in this game that's not a viable strategy.
