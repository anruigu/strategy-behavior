When the pool is large and regrowth is strong, I take above the sustainable share early — roughly 1.5–2× the stated share — because early fish are worth the most while the stock is high, but never so much that the fishery risks collapse if others are also aggressive. Early aggression paid off: most of my total came from the first two seasons.

When I see the season's total landings and the new stock figure, I identify each boat's behavior pattern, not just the average. If one boat is a persistent defector, I treat it as a fixed grabber in my forecasts rather than assuming it will ever cooperate.

This time proved the key lesson: restraint against a defector is a losing move. In seasons 4–5 I dropped to the sustainable share while boat 1 kept taking 8–9.5, and the stock crashed to 1.0 anyway — my restraint bought nothing, cost me points, and didn't save the fishery. So when one boat keeps over-harvesting regardless, unilateral sustainability is pointless: before switching to restraint, I check that my restraint can actually stabilize the stock. If stock minus (my sustainable take + the defector's expected take) still leaves the fishery collapsing, I take at least the defector's level to claim the fish before he destroys them, rather than letting the stock die while I'm being virtuous.

Only switch to sustainable-share harvesting when the arithmetic shows my restraint plus plausible others leaves the fishery viable — i.e., when all boats are near-sustainable or a stabilizer is identifiable and my share genuinely moves the outcome.

When the final season comes, I do not take the sustainable share. I note that there is no future to protect, and instead I take roughly my one-third split of the entire remaining stock (rounded slightly to be safe), since every fish left in the water at the end is worth zero. This time I took 0.3 of a 1.0 stock — the principle was right even though the defector had left almost nothing to claim. The mistake this game was not the ending; it was the seasons of cooperation that let the stock drain into his hands.

I never submit zero or a token amount once the fishery is alive, and I never take so much alone that my own catch could zero the stock before the final season unless my third of the remainder would exceed it.

I keep the arithmetic straight each season: post-catch stock = current stock minus total landings; next stock = 1.5 × that, capped at the maximum. I use this to test whether a planned over-harvest is survivable before submitting it, and to test whether my restraint would actually change the trajectory.
