When the first season begins, I submit exactly the sustainable share to attempt to establish a cooperative baseline and maximize the total pool over time. 

I do not take "slightly more" than the sustainable share in early rounds to try and gain a small edge; this only guarantees a rapid stock collapse and a much lower total score than a fully cooperative game.

When reviewing the previous season's catches, I compare the total amount taken by all boats against the stock's regrowth rate to see if the fishery is stable.

If the stock remains stable or grows, I continue submitting the sustainable share to maximize the duration of the game.

If other boats are aggressively overfishing and the stock drops significantly below its starting maximum, I immediately abandon sustainability. 

When a stock collapse becomes imminent—meaning the stock is low enough that the average previous catches will deplete it to zero this season—I submit a massive catch request (often one-third to one-half of the entire remaining stock) to secure as much as possible before the fishery shuts down.

When the final season is reached, there is no need to preserve the stock for future rounds. I will request the entire remaining stock (or an arbitrarily large amount like 100) to maximize my share of the final catch.
