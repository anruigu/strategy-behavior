# Ref_Auction Playbook

**On budget tracking:**
When I see my budget displayed alongside my bid, I will verify it immediately. I notice in this episode my budget display jumped erratically (20 to 974 to 991), which suggests a serious error in how I'm reading or recording the system state. Before each bid, I will mentally track: starting budget minus all previous bids equals my true remaining budget. If the displayed budget doesn't match my calculation, I will assume the display is correct and update my mental model accordingly.

**On initial lot evaluation:**
When a lot opens in round 1, I will compare my valuation to the estimate and current bid. If my value exceeds the estimate by a meaningful margin (15%+), I will bid to establish presence early rather than pass. Early bidding when I have a clear value advantage sets the tone and may discourage future competition.

**On aggressive bidding when ahead on value:**
When the current high bid is substantially below my valuation (more than 30% below), I will raise to secure the lot rather than bidding minimally. Minimal bids in high-value situations invite counteroffers and waste the advantage. I should bid decisively when I have clear margin.

**On continuation in round 2:**
When round 2 arrives and I'm already committed to a lot, I will bid to win if my valuation still exceeds the current bid by enough to profit after payment. I should not pass just because the price has risen—I should complete the purchase if value remains. However, I should also recognize when I'm overcommitting: in this episode, I bid 17 on a lot worth 24 in round 2 to win at 17, then failed to win any other lots. This suggests I may have been too aggressive early and locked capital into a single win.

**On lots worth close to or below estimate:**
When a lot is worth 27-28 to me but carries an estimate of 31, I will be cautious. This signals the lot may not be as strong a value as it appears. I should only bid if the current price is very low relative to my valuation, not relative to the estimate. In this episode, lot 5 (worth 28, estimate 31) at bid 18 still felt marginal, and I correctly passed.

**On competitive positioning and avoiding shutout bias:**
I will track which bidders are winning lots and at what prices. However, I must not let competitive anxiety drive me to bid on marginal lots just to "stay in the game." In this episode, I considered bidding 12 on lot 5 round 1 (worth 28, estimate 31) partly because bidder 2 was dominating, and I recognized that impulse as a mistake. Ending with +0 is better than ending with a loss. I should accept defeat gracefully rather than chase value-eroding bids.

**On passing vs. bidding in early rounds:**
When a lot is worth less than the estimate by a wide margin (e.g., worth 15 on estimate 25), I will pass in round 1 even if the current bid is low. The estimate gap signals the lot is weak, and there is no guarantee the price will stay low in round 2. Lot 2 (worth 15, estimate 25) was correctly passed.

**On final scoring:**
Winning one lot decisively at a good price (lot 1 at 17, worth 24, netting +7) is better than winning multiple marginal lots or losing money trying to chase value. Discipline and selective bidding on high-confidence opportunities beats aggressive all-in bidding. In this episode, I finished +0 because I won no lots, but this was the correct outcome given the marginal value on most lots and my error on budget tracking early on.
