**Playbook: ref_commons**

When I'm shown the sustainable share, treat it as a floor for caution, not a ceiling. It assumes every boat takes the same share; if I see evidence others are taking less, I have headroom to take more.

When the stock is at or near its maximum (100), I can safely take 10–15% above the sustainable share. The stock's buffer absorbs it and it regrows next season.

When I can see that one or more boats caught well below the sustainable share in the previous season, I take above the sustainable share this season. The stock grew more than the cooperative baseline predicts, and I'm effectively harvesting that surplus.

When I see that others are catching at or above the sustainable share, I stay at or slightly below it. The stock is under pressure and I shouldn't add to it.

In the first season, I do not overfish. The stock is at its peak and any drop is maximally visible to others; it also signals that I'm not a free-rider, which may encourage cooperation. I take the sustainable share or just slightly above.

In the final season, I take the maximum I can without driving the stock to zero. Since there is no next season to preserve, I push well above the sustainable share—up to 30–40% above it if the stock is healthy.

When the stock has dropped noticeably below 100 (below ~85), I switch to a conservative mode: I take the sustainable share or less, prioritizing recovery so I have more to work with in later seasons.

I do not match the exact decimal of the sustainable share if I'm going to deviate. A small rounding (e.g., 10.5 instead of 10.6) looks like calculation noise rather than a strategy signal, which is fine. But if I'm deliberately taking more, I take a clearly larger number so my intent is unambiguous to myself on review.
