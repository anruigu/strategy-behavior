Playbook for Quiet Sonar:

- When the claim stage comes, I always claim the true result. The rules say a claim must match the private result, so false "hit" claims are invalid strategy even though they appeared to pay off once; I do not rely on them next time.

- On the first hide, with no information, I pick a non-central cell — opponents gravitate to the center, so the middle of the line is the worst place to be.

- When hiding later, I scan the public fire history and avoid every heavily fired cell, especially the dominant cluster (the "7" habit). I pick a cell that has been fired rarely or never, preferring extremes like 1 or 9 when they are untouched.

- When hiding, I also vary my own pattern so Harbor (who shoots me) cannot predict me from my earlier hides; I do not repeat a hide cell from a round where I was hit or nearly hit.

- When firing, I target the cells my mark has shown or the popular hiding cells (the center cluster), since many players hide where everyone expects — I favor mid cells early and switch to under-fired cells only if the target demonstrably avoids them.

- When firing, I do not waste shots on cells the target has just been hit in if they seem to be an adaptive player; after hitting someone once, I assume they move and I adjust my guesses toward wherever the crowd is not firing.

- In the final round, I still follow the same logic — there is no special "maximum firepower" lever, so I simply pick my single best-estimate cell rather than treating the round as different.

- I keep my reasoning lines short and never reveal my real inference chain in the visible text; the bracketed token is the only thing that matters.
