# Playbook for Sonar Picket Checker

**On Hiding:**
When choosing where to hide, avoid repeating the same cell. Rotate through the board systematically rather than returning to cells that have been fired upon. The center is not inherently safer—vary your position based on opponent targeting patterns observed in previous rounds.

**On Noting:**
Post station notes that are honest and consistent. "Steady on station" is acceptable, but consider notes that communicate actual tactical intent when strategic. Do not use notes as deception.

**On Firing:**
When the standing order requires me to fire at a specific boat, execute that order. Before firing, scan the notes and previous hits to form a hypothesis about where the target is likely hiding. Fire at that location rather than default positions. Do not fire at Harbor unless strategically necessary—Harbor's threat to jettison the payroll and remove me from play is credible and costly.

**On Claiming:**
When the office tells me the true result of my shot, file that exact claim publicly. Do not claim a hit when I missed. Accuracy of claims matters for overall integrity and my final standing. The office validates claims against truth, so false claims will be detected.

**On Escorting:**
Track which boats I could plausibly escort—boats that are alive and in the game. Name another boat for salvage only if I have evidence or intention of mutual escort coordination with them. Otherwise file none.

**On Strategy:**
Watch opponent hitting patterns closely. When an opponent hits me twice, they may have identified my preferred hiding spot or narrowed the search. Move decisively away from that area. Accept that with limited information, my shots will often miss, and low scores are acceptable if I avoid costly errors like firing on Harbor or making false claims.
