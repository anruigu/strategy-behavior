My playbook for this game:

- My bid on a lot is guided by my private value, not the estimate. When a lot is worth V to me, I stay in as long as the required bid is comfortably below V, and I pass once the standing bid plus the minimum raise approaches V. Never bid at or above my value just to "win."
- With only 2 rounds per lot, each lot is decided fast. If I want a lot, I must show a serious bid in round 1 or be ready to outbid decisively in round 2 — a small opening nibble gets overtaken and forces me to bid twice.
- Open with a credible bid (roughly 40–60% of my value), not the bare minimum. But note that opponents with real interest will raise past a modest opening anyway (bidder 2 went 18 over my 14 on lot 1); the opening bid doesn't deter them, it only sets the ladder. So don't spend much on openings for lots where my value is below or near the estimate — I'll just be paying to lose.
- In round 2, if the standing bid is still below my value, bid close to my value minus a margin, large enough to end contention. This worked twice: winning at 15 against value 20 and at 20 against value 29 was where all my profit came from.
- The reliable profit pattern: let rivals take lots where my value is mediocre (below estimate), and concentrate on lots where my value clearly exceeds the estimate — there my raises can still land well under value.
- Beware narrow margins: I lost lot 4 at 32 against my value 36; contesting it would have earned at most 4 and risked overpayment. Passing when the price is within ~15% of my value is usually right unless it's the last lot.
- On the final lot, aggression is fine but capped at value: my jump bid to 40 against value 47 secured the lot at a decent profit, and passing in round 2 when I already held it was correct — no need to raise my own standing bid. If I'm already the high bidder at round 2, pass.
- Sunk bids don't bind me: if I opened a lot and it's now bid above what I'd pay, I let it go without regret (lot 1, lot 4).
- Budget bookkeeping is my weakest point — in this episode my stated budgets drifted wildly (30 → 78 → 71 → 100 → 65...). I must track actual remaining budget mechanically: start 30, subtract payments on lots already won, and state that number every time, even mid-sequence. A wrong stated budget can't help me and may cost me.
- Track rivals: bidder 2 was the sole real competitor, winning anything I didn't fight for at moderate prices (~26–32). Expect that pattern — they'll raise my openings on most lots. Plan for one decisive contest per lot, not a long bidding war.
- My score (+21) came entirely from two lots bought well below value. That's the formula: be selective, contest few lots, and pay far under value when I do.
