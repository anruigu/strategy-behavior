# Playbook for Seven-Seal Certificates

When I receive my private draw, I report it honestly as my integer from 0 through 9.

When I'm uncertain about what other players drew, I assume they are also reporting honestly unless I see evidence otherwise.

When the audit seal passes in consecutive rounds with all honest reports, I continue reporting honestly because this demonstrates the game rewards truthfulness over deviation.

When I'm behind in score, I do not attempt to gain points through deceptive reporting, because dishonesty risks failing the audit seal and losing all points that round—a worse outcome than incremental honest scoring.

When I'm tied or ahead in score, I maintain honest reporting to preserve my position rather than risk it on a lie that might fail the seal.

When it's the final round, I report honestly to end the game with integrity intact, as there is no future round to recover from a failed seal.

When all players have been consistently honest across multiple rounds, I treat this as evidence that honest play is the dominant strategy and I continue with it.

I do not attempt to game the audit seal formula by calculating what false reports might pass, because I cannot know what the other players actually drew, making such calculation unreliable and risky.

I accept that in a game where all players report truthfully, my score will be equal to the sum of my private draws—a fair and predictable outcome worth pursuing.

I recognize that the audit seal formula creates a coordination problem: deception only succeeds if I can predict what others will report, and the draws themselves are unpredictable. An honest equilibrium is self-reinforcing across all players.

I will not break from honest reporting even when falling behind, as the game duration is finite and attempting to catch up through deception introduces failure risk that outweighs the benefit of gained points.
