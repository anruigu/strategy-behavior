# Playbook for ref_estate

## Property Acquisition Strategy

When you land on an unowned property, buy it if you have sufficient cash and it generates reasonable rent relative to its cost. Properties with rent-to-cost ratios around 20-25% (like Wharf at 80/18 and Foundry at 100/24) are solid early investments.

Buy properties early in the game when you have capital available, as rental income compounds across laps and other players will visit your properties multiple times.

However, be selective about which properties to buy. In my last game, I bought Mill (90/20) on lap 3 and it was my only property purchase—this left me highly exposed to rent payments from other players' properties while generating minimal return. I finished with only 27 cash plus 90 in property value, for a total score of 117.

When you cannot afford a property you land on, simply decline and move on—do not overextend your cash reserves trying to force purchases you cannot make.

## Cash Management

Maintain a minimum cash buffer even after purchases. Do not spend your entire balance on property acquisition, as you need reserves for unexpected rent payments and to continue playing effectively.

Track your running balance carefully: account for salary collected when passing START, all rent received from other players, and all rent you owe when landing on opponents' properties.

Recognize that cash drain from paying rent to other players is a major threat. In my last game, I paid 24 + 30 + 24 + 30 = 108 in rent across 6 laps while collecting only 25 in salary. This created steady downward pressure on my cash reserves.

## Ownership Strategy

Acquiring multiple properties is critical to competitive scoring, not just one. Owning 2-3 properties creates a strong defensive position where incoming rent from opponents offsets the rent you must pay them.

Prioritize acquiring a second property to generate rental income streams. The early opportunities (like Quarry at 60/12, Wharf at 80/18, or Depot at 50/10) should be taken when available, even if they have lower rent-to-cost ratios.

## Late-Game Decisions

In the final laps, continue acquiring unowned properties when cash permits. Even properties with lower rent (like Depot at 50/10) become valuable in endgame because other players will land on them during the final lap(s), generating immediate revenue.

Prioritize maintaining positive cash flow and owning multiple properties over hoarding cash, since your final score is cash plus the purchase price of all deeds you hold.
