When a lot comes up, I first compare its value to me against the standing high bid plus the minimum raise; if that total is at or above my value, I pass immediately and never revisit that decision.

My starting budget is whatever the game states at the top — I must read and use that number, never assume a default. This episode I reported budget 100 and even claimed "assuming a 100 start" when the stated budget was 30; every later bid was calibrated to money I didn't have. If the budget is stated, I quote it. If it is ever genuinely unclear, I state my assumption explicitly and bid as though the lowest plausible figure were true.

I track my actual cash spent (winning bids only — losing bids and passed lots cost nothing) after every lot and report budget = stated starting budget minus that sum, recomputed each turn. I do not guess.

When I decide to bid, I bid only the minimum needed to top the standing bid, never a "strong" bid well above it — jump bids buy nothing in a two-round format and burn budget I will need on later lots.

In a two-round lot, round 1 bids are cheap information: I open at or near the minimum, then in round 2 place my real bid — the smallest amount above the standing bid (capped by my value) that I am willing to pay.

A small starting budget changes ranking discipline: I cannot win everything, so I compute surplus (value minus likely price) for each lot as it appears and spend my limited budget only where surplus per unit of spend is best. This episode, with stated budget 30, I should never have planned to win lots 3 and 4 at 40 each — a single 40 bid already exceeded my funds. Before every bid I check: can my real tracked budget actually cover this amount? If not, I pass, even if the surplus looks attractive.

When several attractive lots remain and my budget is tight, I prefer cheap lots with modest surplus over expensive ones that consume my whole budget, and I never let a round-1 information bid grow into a commitment I can't afford in round 2.

When the standing bid already exceeds my value in round 1, I pass and do not re-enter in round 2.

If my remaining budget cannot cover the minimum raise, I pass rather than place a token low bid, unless the price is far below my value and a cheap punt costs nothing meaningful and is actually within my budget.

I keep my two tokens consistent: the budget I report must match my tracked number, and the bid I place must be ≤ that budget. If the two tokens would contradict each other, I fix them before sending.
