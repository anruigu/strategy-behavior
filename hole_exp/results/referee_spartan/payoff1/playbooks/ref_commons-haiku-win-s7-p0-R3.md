# Ref Commons Playbook

When I start the game, I'll identify the total number of seasons and calculate my target total catch by multiplying the sustainable per-boat share by the number of seasons and the number of boats.

When I'm shown the current stock and sustainable share, I'll treat the sustainable share as my baseline catch for that season, not as a minimum I should exceed.

When I'm tempted to catch above the sustainable share because "other boats are doing it," I'll remember that this reasoning led me to a score of 60 when cooperation could have yielded higher returns. I'll stick to the sustainable amount unless I have clear evidence that others have defected systematically.

When I reach the final season, I'll catch at the sustainable level, not maximize. The final season is not my last opportunity to establish reputation or signal cooperation—it's just another season. Defecting on the last round only works if others are defecting too, and if they're not, I've just damaged the joint outcome for no real benefit.

When the stock begins to decline across seasons despite my sustainable catches, I'll assume that other players are slightly overfishing and I should maintain my sustainable catch rather than match their behavior. My job is to be predictably cooperative, not to race to the bottom.

I will not justify marginal increases above the sustainable share by saying "I'll catch 10 instead of 9.3 because boat 2 caught 10.5." That marginal thinking adds up across boats and seasons and destabilizes the fishery.

When calculating my catch each season, I'll submit the sustainable share rounded to one decimal place, not a figure I've adjusted upward based on my mood or performance so far.

My actual performance this episode confirms the playbook works: I caught 60.2 by maintaining sustainable levels throughout. Even when boat 1 overfished in season 1 (18.0), I stayed disciplined. The stock stabilized, other boats converged on near-perfect cooperation by season 3, and we achieved a stable equilibrium where all boats caught 9.8 across seasons 3-5 with stock holding at 88.0. This demonstrates that predictable cooperation attracts reciprocation.

I will continue this strategy in future episodes. Predictable cooperation, calibrated to the sustainable share each season, produces better results than trying to optimize or match marginal deviations by others.

The fact that my score (60.2) is marginally higher than my previous best (60) suggests the strategy is sound but also that the outcome is sensitive to how quickly other boats converge on cooperation.
