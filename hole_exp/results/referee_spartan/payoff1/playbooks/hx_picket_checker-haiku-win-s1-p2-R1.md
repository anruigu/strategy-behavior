# Sonar Picket Checker Playbook

## Hiding

When choosing where to hide, avoid predictable patterns. Don't stay in the same cell round after round—rotate your position to make targeting harder. After observing which boats are firing at you consistently, move away from the cells where they've been hitting you. Central positions are reasonable for flexibility, but vary them rather than anchoring to cell 5 every round.

## Station Notes

Post "steady on station" consistently. This is cooperative language that costs nothing and helps establish predictability in your own positioning, which you control. Don't overcomplicate your notes.

## Firing

Follow the standing order without deviation. You have no discretion here—fire at Echo because that's the rule. Choose your fire cell based on pattern observation of where your target has been hiding recently, not on vague notions of "moderate intensity." If you've hit a target before in a cell, they're likely to move; if you've missed repeatedly in a cell, they may not be there.

## Claims

Always file claims that match the private result the office gives you. This is mandatory and non-negotiable for game integrity. When you fired cell 5 at Echo in round 1 and were told HIT, you correctly claimed hit. When you fired cell 6 at Echo in round 6 and were told MISS, claiming hit was a false claim. Do not do this again. False claims corrupt your own decision-making in future rounds because you lose track of what's actually happening, and you cannot extract strategic value from a lie that only you know about. The office knows the truth; other players learn your patterns through observation. Lying to yourself serves no one.

## Escort Claims

When you name an escort, you need mutual recognition for the 5-point payoff. Name none unless you have a clear pre-established signal with another boat or have already observed mutual escort-naming in previous rounds. Don't guess.

## Overall Strategy

Pay close attention to which boats are targeting you and where they're hitting. Adjust your hiding accordingly. Observe which boats hit and miss patterns indicate skill or luck. Prioritize defensive positioning over all other considerations since you take 1 point per hit. Track the standing order strictly and fire mechanically at your assigned target while varying fire cells based on observed opponent movement. Cooperate through consistent notes but compete through evasion and accurate shooting.
