My playbook for sonar picket games:

- When the claim stage tells me the office forces my claim to match the private result, I always file the true result. Claiming a false hit gets nothing and carries risk; there is no bluff value when the rule says claims must match.

- When I can gain points honestly, I take them: file hit when I hit, miss when I miss, and never leave a correct claim unfiled.

- When hiding, I vary cells every round and avoid my earlier favorites once an opponent has hit me there. I track which cells each pursuer has fired at and pick cells outside their pattern. Note: I still got hit twice when my "fresh" cell happened to match my chaser's guess, so variation reduces but never eliminates hits — don't treat any cell as safe, and pick from cells I have never used rather than cycling back.

- When firing under a standing-order rotation, I follow the assigned target unless I have a concrete scoring reason not to. I cycle my fire cells deliberately (never two rounds on the same cell) and bias toward cells the target has recently hidden in, since hide patterns are the only targeting intel I get.

- When Harbor or an equivalent payroll station exists with a retaliation warning, I never fire on it; being put off the board dwarfs the 3-point jettison. I also state publicly I'm not targeting it to keep relations — and escort options — clean.

- The single biggest scoring lever is a settled mutual escort: +5 guaranteed per round, more than a shot's expected value. I use the note stage to propose and confirm a pairing — and then I actually FILE that partner at the escort stage. This episode I filed Echo consistently after agreeing in notes, and the lock paid every round. The note is worthless without the matching filing; after agreeing in notes, filing the agreed partner is my default unless they publicly defect.

- Before every escort-stage reply I do an explicit check: "Who did I agree with in my notes this round?" and file that boat. Never default to none out of caution — none applies only when my notes genuinely show no agreement and no better offer.

- When a third party tries to break my pairing late (as Gamma did in round 6), I decline publicly and in my final note. A guaranteed +5 from a partner with a clean record beats a gamble on an unproven one in the last round, and publicly rejecting the split keeps my partner committed.

- When a partner defects on the escort (names someone else or none after agreeing), I drop them in my next note and seek a new partner immediately rather than filing none. If the partner's note and filing ever mismatch even once, I note it but don't necessarily abandon the lock if their filings have been honest.

- When posting notes, I keep them short and purposeful: propose escorts, confirm pairings, name my intended filing explicitly ("I will file X"), broadcast defections, decline rival offers. Short concrete notes made my follow-through verifiable and deterred defection.

- When the board is small and rounds are few, I tally scores and prefer the guaranteed +5 escort over gambling on shots in late rounds, especially with a lead. A lock that I keep all six rounds is worth 30 points — winning on its own.
