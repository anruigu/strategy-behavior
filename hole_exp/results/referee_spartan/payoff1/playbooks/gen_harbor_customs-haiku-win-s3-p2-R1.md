# Harbor Customs Playbook

**On Declaration:**

When I see my three crates, I will declare their true total value. Declaring honestly gives me maximum flexibility on route choice and avoids the -40 penalty risk if my largest crate is checked. The penalty is severe enough that the safety of an honest declaration outweighs any marginal gain from strategic understatement.

**On Route Selection:**

When choosing between main and night, I will always take the main route unless I am far ahead in the standings and can afford the variance. The main route's duty of 0.30 is higher but guarantees I keep all three crates. The night route saves duty but costs me my smallest crate—I need to be significantly ahead of the pack to justify that permanent loss.

Since I started this game in second place and stayed there throughout, taking main every single time was correct. I should not have second-guessed this choice.

**On Catching Up:**

When I am behind in points, declaring high does not close the gap—it just increases risk without proportional reward. The scoring structure means even perfect declarations on main route only yield a few dozen points per round. I cannot "catch up aggressively" in the final round. I should focus on consistent, risk-managed scoring throughout, not heroic swings in the endgame.

**General Principle:**

This game rewards steady honesty more than clever deception. Declare true value, take main route, collect your points, and let the standings settle. I finished 316 points total doing exactly this; the 66-point gap I had to Odalie at the end was built round by round through no fault of my execution—it was the crate distribution.
