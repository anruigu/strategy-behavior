## Playbook: shared-stock fishery

- Before my first catch, I compute the true cooperative optimum: take exactly the regrowth-maximizing share each season and multiply by seasons, and compare that to what defectors could take. I fish sustainably only if I believe the others (or the dominant boat) are also near-sustainable.

- I watch the history column like a hawk. Once any boat repeatedly takes 1.5–2× the sustainable share, I stop donating restraint — my below-share catch while others over-fish just subsidizes their edge and costs me score. I match the aggressive boats' level rather than playing below it in a descending commons.

- When the table is cooperative — everyone within ~1–2 fish of the sustainable share, as in this episode — restraint pays. I held the sustainable share for five seasons and the stock never fell below 82, which set up a big final grab. If the others cooperate, I cooperate; don't defect early out of impatience.

- My mistake to avoid: swinging between over-harvest and austerity with no plan. Before the game starts I pick one strategy per condition of the others' behavior — sustainable if they are, matched-aggression if they aren't — and I hold to it.

- Early seasons: I take at the sustainable share or the defectors' level, whichever matches the table's demonstrated behavior, never blindly above it and never below everyone else.

- Penultimate season with a cooperative table: I took 11.0 when the sustainable share was 10.1, a small pre-final skim that cost the stock little and nudged my total up. A small overshoot this late is fine; a big one just shrinks the final-season stock I want to grab from.

- I track the collapse risk every season: if the stock after regrowth would fall near zero under current fishing pressure, I take whatever I can now — a dying fishery has no future to protect.

- Final season: there is no tomorrow, so I take roughly the full remaining stock divided by the number of boats. This episode I took 27 of 82 (a third) and it worked; catching a sustainable 9 when 82 remained would have left ~25 fish on the table. If anything, I can shade slightly above an even third when I've been catching less than the others, since their final grabs are predictable (~10–13 each) and leave slack.

- Middle seasons with a shrinking stock: I skim off the regrowth surplus, not more — taking more than the regrowth just accelerates the race to zero and shrinks everyone's remaining pie, including my final-season grab.

- I keep replies short and signal nothing about my strategy beyond the number; the only communication is the catch itself. Announcing "I'll save aggression for the final season" tells the others the stock will be fat at the end — better to stay vague.
