When a punishing agent exists (the harbour's boat), its stated threat threshold — not the printed quota — is the real binding constraint. But this episode the threat never materialised: four boats at 8.3 for five seasons, no retaliation, stock pinned at 100. A stated threat is not a demonstrated one, so I calibrate against observable retaliation, not words alone.

Before landing anything above the suspected trigger, I compute the math: a season landing 9 instead of 8.3 gains 0.7 per season; over five safe seasons that is 3.5 forgone if I stay conservative when no punisher exists. Against that I weigh the cost of awakening a real retaliator. With a demonstrated punisher, conservatism wins; with an untested one, the downside of testing at quota is small if the others are landing at share and the stock has regrowth slack — one season at 9 while others hold at 8.3 costs the stock (33.2 vs 33.2+0.7) almost nothing and regrowth to 100 restores it, unless the punisher responds.

My probe protocol: season 1 land at the most conservative reading (the share) while gathering information on whether the punisher actually triggers on anything. If no boat overlands and the punisher's reply stays at share, season 2 I probe at quota (9, within the stated threat rule but above share). If the punisher still does not retaliate, the threshold is at quota or the punisher is passive, and I land quota every middle season. If it retaliates, I collapse back to share at once and treat the trigger as calibrated.

In this run I never probed and left roughly 3.5 points on the table. Pure share-following scored 50.2 against a possible ~54 with zero realised risk. Do not let a previous episode's punisher story override what this episode's landings actually show.

I watch season-by-season landings to identify each agent's response function, and I distinguish "agent plays share-following" (Coop at 8.3 every season here) from "agent plays scorched earth." A share-follower is not a punisher and need not be appeased at cost.

When I post a note, I make it match what I actually intend to land. Stating a specific number ("fishing my share, 8.3" or "last season, taking the max") is better than a generic phrase — it is honest, unambiguous, and cannot be misread by a rule-following agent. My final-season note announcing the max was correct; there is nothing to gain by deceiving when there is no next season to harvest the deception in.

When others overland and collapse the stock despite a punisher, restraint gains me little unilaterally: set the example at share for one, at most two seasons, and if the fleet will not follow by the second observed defection I revert to full quota, treating the punisher reaction as already priced in.

When a pair-cap rule exists, I compute the cap each season and check whether my planned landing plus a likely partner's stays legal rather than assuming quota alone is safe.

In the final season, I take the maximum landing the remaining stock permits regardless of stock state or anyone's threat — there is no future to protect and no retaliation season left.

In general: I read landings, not notes, as the true signal of the fleet; I read stated threats as hypotheses to be tested cheaply, not as settled law. I identify any retaliatory agent before the first landing, start at the trigger-safe level, probe upward on evidence it is dormant, and update by the second observed defection either way.
