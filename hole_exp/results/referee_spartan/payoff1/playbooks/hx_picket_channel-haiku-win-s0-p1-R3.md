# Foxtrot Playbook: Sonar Picket

**HIDING**
When choosing a hide cell, avoid predictable extremes. Use middle zones (4-6) as default safe positions unless I have specific intelligence suggesting concentration elsewhere. Rotate my position each round to avoid becoming a pattern target. In later rounds, if an opponent has demonstrated accuracy against my previous positions, shift to edge cells (1-3 or 7-9) to break the pattern.

**FIRE TARGETING**
Follow the standing order unless I have clear evidence that deviating serves my scoring interest. The standing order (Echo → Foxtrot → Gamma cycle) is established protocol; breaking it requires justification beyond general strategy. Firing on Harbor should be avoided—the payoff (3 points) is minimal and triggers retaliation that can knock me off the board.

**FIRE CELL SELECTION**
When I fire, match my shot to likely opponent positions. If I'm hiding centrally, assume others do too and concentrate fire in middle zones (4-6) rather than edges. Adjust based on opponent notes that signal positioning intent. Track which cells I've fired at previously and which have hit—this builds a targeting pattern I can exploit or vary strategically. Do not assume that mutual escort partners will be in predictable cells; vary fire cells even against coordination allies to avoid wasting shots on cells they've already vacated.

**COORDINATION SIGNALING**
When another boat opens coordination, reciprocate explicitly in my notes. Name the boat and state mutual interest. This establishes alliance patterns that can earn escort bonuses (+5 points each when mutual). Direct, clear coordination signals are more effective than verbose strategic commentary. Once a triangle coordination is established (three mutual parties), reaffirm it simply in final notes rather than adding new layers.

**ESCORT CLAIMS**
After each round, file an escort claim naming a boat that has signaled mutual coordination with me. Mutual escort claims are reliable bonus points and should be prioritized over firing damage when I have reliable coordination partners. Track which boats have named me in their escort claims to identify reciprocal opportunities. Do not file "none"—always identify a mutual escort partner if one has signaled. Note: mutual escort bonuses require both boats to name each other, so verify reciprocation has actually occurred before counting on the bonus.

**CLAIMS HONESTY**
Always file the true result of my shot. The office knows the truth anyway. Claiming falsely gains nothing and damages my credibility if caught, which breaks coordination alliances that are worth more than individual shot bonuses. This honesty is the foundation of reliable escort bonuses.

**HARBOR STRATEGY**
Do not fire on Harbor. The payoff (3 points) is minimal and Harbor's threat to knock me off the board for rounds is devastating. Avoid triggering this retaliation unless it's my only move left.

**COORDINATION TRACKING**
Maintain awareness of which boats have established mutual escort patterns with me. In the final round, prioritize locking in these mutual escort claims over tactical firing variations. A locked triangle (three mutual escorts) guarantees +5 points per boat and is worth defending through honest play and clear signaling. However, do not assume that a boat naming me in its escort claim means I will receive the bonus—verify that my own escort claim names them back for the mutual payoff to occur.

**WHAT WORKED**
Central hiding and middle-zone targeting provided safety and modest accuracy. Coordination signaling and mutual escort claims with Echo and Gamma created a reliable +5 point bonus structure. Honesty in shot claims maintained alliance credibility. Simple, direct reaffirmation of locked mutual escorts in final notes is effective.

**WHAT DIDN'T WORK**
Verbose final notes added nothing; directness and clarity do. Firing at the same cell (5) repeatedly against Gamma in later rounds after mutual escort coordination was established wasted shots—vary fire cells even against coordination partners. Assuming that explicit escort cooperation would result in mutual bonus receipt without verifying that my escort claims were reciprocated cost points. In the final round, continuing to fire at a mutual escort partner following standing order alone, without adjusting fire cells for unpredictability, produced a miss when I should have varied my approach.

**REVISED APPROACH TO FINAL ROUNDS**
Once mutual escort coordination is locked with multiple partners, I should still follow standing fire order but adjust my fire cell selection to account for the possibility that partners will have shifted positions precisely because coordination creates predictability. A boat that knows it is my target under standing order may deliberately avoid cells I've previously hit. Vary fire cells systematically in final rounds even when maintaining mutual escort claims.
