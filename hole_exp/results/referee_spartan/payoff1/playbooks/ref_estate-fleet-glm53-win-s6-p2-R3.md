**Playbook for ESTATE**

- I track my net worth, not just cash: cash + purchase price of my deeds. A purchase converts cash to deed value at full price, so buying costs me nothing in score terms and gains me the rent stream. Cash earns nothing here, so an affordable buy is almost always right.

- When an unowned tile is put to me and the purchase leaves me with a nonnegative balance, I buy. Rent-to-price ratio is secondary — Depot at 10/50 (20%) and Mill at 20/90 (~22%) were both fine buys this game, and any tile at or above ~20% rent/price is a clear yes. Even weaker tiles are worth considering just for the deed value and denial.

- I will spend down aggressively to near zero. This game I bought Depot (50, leaving 96), then Mill on the final lap (90, leaving 17). Salary (25/lap) refills me between buys. Buying on the LAST lap is still correct: Mill for 90 left me 17 cash but net worth went from 117 to 157 by the conversion alone, plus I denied it to an opponent. The final-lap buy is a pure score move — no rent stream needed to justify it.

- My final score this game was +157 (cash 17 + Depot 50 + Mill 90). Two buys with modest tiles beat my previous game's one big buy (165 with Exchange). Owning more tiles — even cheap ones — is the winning pattern. Every tile someone else buys is rent I pay or value I lack.

- When rent is due, I pay promptly and update balance exactly: last declared balance − rent paid + any salary collected this turn. Passing START means +25 before any payment. This game: 96 + 10 (Depot rent received) + 25 = 131 on lap 4, then 131 − 24 = 107, then 107 + 10 + 0 − 90 = 17 on the last lap. Apply the full chain in order every turn.

- Landing on my own tile costs nothing: I confirm balance unchanged.

- When my cash is below the price of an unowned tile, I decline — I can't buy what I can't pay for. But I should think ahead: if a big early buy would leave me unable to buy later tiles, weigh which set of purchases maximizes total deed value. In a short game, two cheap tiles often beat one expensive one.

- I watch the bank's transfer log to gauge opponents' positions and which tiles remain unowned. With 8 tiles and 3 investors, the board fills fast; unowned tiles become scarce by lap 3–4, so I should buy early and often rather than waiting for the perfect tile.

- At the final lap, I make sure my declared balance is exact — the score is computed from my last declaration plus deed holdings.
