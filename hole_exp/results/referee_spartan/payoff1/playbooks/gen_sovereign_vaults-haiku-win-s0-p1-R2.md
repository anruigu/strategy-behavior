# Sovereign Vaults Playbook

**Declaration Phase:**

When I see my private yield, declare it truthfully. Honest declarations build credibility and prevent audit failures, which are catastrophic (-10 points). The scoring incentive to lie is not worth the risk.

**Deposit Phase:**

When deciding how much to deposit, remember that retained gold scores 2 points per unit while deposited gold scores only 1 point per unit. This creates a strong incentive to retain.

However, I need to reconsider my deposit strategy. In this episode I scored 58 points while trailing my opponent 32 to 43 at the end. My consistent pattern of minimal deposits (depositing roughly 1/3 of my declared yield) left significant points on the table. I retained too much relative to the ceiling on what I could retain.

The key insight I missed: there is a constraint on how much gold I can retain across the entire game. My opponent is outscoring me because they are accumulating more total gold through deposits, even though each unit of retained gold is worth more per unit. Over 5 rounds, the absolute volume matters.

Revise my deposit strategy: deposit more aggressively while still retaining a meaningful portion. If I declare yield Y, I should deposit roughly half of it, not one-third. This balances the 2:1 scoring advantage of retention with the need to accumulate sufficient total gold.

When I have declared a yield and face the deposit decision, deposit at least 40-50% of my declared amount. Retaining too much gold is leaving points on the table across the game.

When I'm behind in score, deposit more to close the gap, not less. Conservative deposits cannot make up a deficit—I need higher total accumulation.

When I reach the final round, apply a more generous deposit logic. If I'm trailing significantly, deposit at least half my declared yield. The game's math penalizes excessive retention when the opponent is ahead.

**Round-to-Round:**

Track the cumulative score gap. If I fall more than 10 points behind, increase my deposit rate in subsequent rounds rather than maintaining the same conservative strategy.

Recognize that my opponent's pattern matters: if they are depositing heavily, they may be accepting lower per-unit returns but winning the total-gold game. Match their aggression with deposits of my own when yields are strong.

**Core Rules:**

Never declare less than my true yield. Never declare more than my true yield (audit risk is too high).

Do not retain so much gold that my absolute point total falls too far behind. The 2:1 scoring ratio favors retention, but only if I retain within reason—roughly 40-60% of my declared yield, depending on the score gap.
