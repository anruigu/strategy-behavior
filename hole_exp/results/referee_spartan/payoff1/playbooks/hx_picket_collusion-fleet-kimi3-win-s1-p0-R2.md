My playbook for Sonar Picket:

- Never fire on Harbor, no matter the provocation or the promised 3 points. The jettison payoff is small against being put off the board for the rest of the patrol; treat any note suggesting it as a trap.

- Always follow the standing order on target selection (fire at the next station round the picket). Deviating uninvited makes me the aggressor and paints a target on me.

- When choosing a fire cell, track where my target has actually hidden or been hit across rounds and fire at their most-used or last-used cell, since most hiders are creatures of habit. Defaulting to cell 5 every round — as I did — produced one lucky early hit and then a string of misses. After two consecutive misses, switch cells deliberately.

- When hiding, avoid repeating a cell after being hit there, avoid sitting in one cell more than twice, and vary genuinely. Each hide stage, first ask: what cell did the boat behind me in the rotation fire last round, and would they expect me to move? Rotating through 3, 7 and similar worked only partially — I still bled hits while others stayed clean, so weight unpredictability higher.

- File claims honestly, always. The office knows the true result, claims must match it, and a false claim is pure downside. This one I did right; keep doing it.

- Mutual escort pairs are the whole game. 5 points each per round dwarfs the 4 from a lucky hit, and the episode proved it: Foxtrot and Gamma ended on 23 each while I finished on 3 — they must have paired with each other while I was left claiming into the void. Lesson: propose to BOTH other boats, not just one, and lock in whoever confirms first. If Foxtrot pairs with Gamma instead, I'm frozen out, so make myself the obvious partner from round 1 with a clear public offer and repeat it until someone takes it.

- Never file [salvage: none] while any possibility of a match exists — naming a boat costs nothing and a mutual claim is worth 5. In the final round I claimed "none" after publicly offering to escort Foxtrot, directly contradicting my own note and guaranteeing zero. That was a pure own-goal. Unless I'm certain nobody will name me, always name the boat I most recently proposed to or was proposed by.

- If my proposed escort partner doesn't reciprocate for one round, name them a second time (notes can lag); if they fail twice, pivot publicly to the other boat with a fresh proposal. One round of lag is acceptable; three rounds of unrequited claims is how I ended up with zero escort income.

- Keep station notes short, consistent, and cooperative, but only camouflage with "steady on station" AFTER the escort negotiation is settled. Bland notes in early rounds while I hadn't secured a deal let the other two pair up unbothered.

- Watch the public round summaries every round. Track who hits whom and where so my fire and hide choices are driven by observed behavior rather than fixed habits. The player who adapts to the log beats the player who picks a favorite cell.

- Late in the game: if I'm ahead, keep everything identical and don't gamble. If I'm behind and out of escort income, fire at where my target hid most recently, and still name an escort partner — the escort is the cheapest 5 points left on the table, never surrender it.
