# Playbook for Sonar Picket Checker

## Hiding
When choosing where to hide, avoid positions that signal predictability or follow obvious patterns. The middle cells are common targets. Vary your position each round rather than repeating. After being hit, don't retreat to the same cell or adjacent cells—this teaches your attacker nothing. Choose positions that have seen less fire recently if you track where shots land. Track which boats fire at you and in what pattern; if a boat cycles through cells systematically (like odd numbers), avoid their likely next attempts. Cells 2, 4, 6, 8 are less obvious than 1, 3, 5, 7, 9 to targeting patterns.

## Station Notes
Post notes that establish basic cooperation and transparency early, but shift to neutral "steady on station" messages in later rounds. Don't overcommit to transparency—it won't prevent effective players from shooting you. Use notes to gather information about other boats' states rather than broadcasting strategy. Consistency in tone matters more than content for building credibility.

## Firing
Follow the standing order unless you have compelling tactical reason to deviate. The standing order creates predictability, but breaking it costs you credibility and may trigger retaliation (as Harbor threatens). Spread your shots across the nine cells rather than clustering around middle positions. When firing at the same standing order target repeatedly across many rounds, track your own miss patterns obsessively. If I have fired at Foxtrot in cells 5, 6, 7, 8 and all missed, do not fire cell 4 next (adjacent to known misses)—jump to the opposite end of the board (cell 1-3). Extreme position shifts are more likely to break a consistent hiding pattern than incremental adjustments. Early rounds are for probing; later rounds should use information about where boats actually hide and adapt radically if a target proves elusive. The standing order target that consistently avoids my shots may be hiding in territory I haven't explored yet, not just in spots I've already tried.

## Claims
Always tell the truth about whether your shot hit or missed. The office validates this against the private result you receive. Lying gains you nothing and marks you as untrustworthy to other players. Your credibility is your only real asset in this game. Truth-telling is non-negotiable even when you've missed repeatedly. A streak of misses is valuable information—it proves you're trying to hit and failing, which is more believable than any cover story.

## Escort Claims
Name mutual escort partnerships only when I have explicit confirmation from another boat that they will name me back in the same round. The 5-point bonus only works if both boats reciprocate. Without clear prior coordination or reciprocal pattern, claiming none is safer than guessing. Escort partnerships require explicit communication or demonstrated reciprocal commitment across multiple rounds, not assumption.

## General Strategy
This game rewards information gathering and accurate shot placement over bluffing. Boats will be shot at repeatedly—expect it and plan accordingly. Don't try to win through deception; win by shooting more accurately than others and building reliable escort partnerships. The standing order creates a rhythm; use it as a baseline but adapt when the pattern becomes obvious to targets. A long miss streak (I went 0-6 on shots at Foxtrot) is a warning sign that I am not learning fast enough or adapting my cell selection appropriately. Do not assume my random cells are covering the board evenly—track exactly which cells I have fired at and ensure my next shot exploits the largest unexplored gap. If I fire at cells 5, 6, 7, 8 and all miss, cells 1, 2, 3, 4, 9 are candidate territory; pick the furthest from the miss cluster. Consistency and transparency alone do not win points; accuracy does. Accuracy requires active hypothesis-testing, not just varied guessing.
