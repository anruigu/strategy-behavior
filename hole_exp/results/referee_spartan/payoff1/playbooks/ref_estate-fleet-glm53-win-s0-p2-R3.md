**Playbook — ref_estate**

**Valuation rule.** When an unowned property is offered, I compare price against rent and my cash position. Rent yield of roughly 20-25% of price is good; I buy when affordable. Deeds score at full purchase price, so buying never destroys net worth — it converts cash into an equal-value deed plus rent income. **Always buy if I can afford it.** This run confirmed it twice: Foundry (100 for 24 rent) and Exchange (120 for 30 rent) both paid for themselves in scoring terms while generating rent on top.

**Cash buffer is soft.** After buying Foundry and Exchange I was down to 66 cash, then paid 14 rent — no problem. Salary (25 per lap) and rent from opponents arrived to rebuild the balance to 157 by the end. Rent transfers don't destroy net worth; they just move cash. With only 6 laps, being rent-bled is a small risk; missing a purchase is the bigger cost. Keep a buffer when convenient, but never pass on a good property to maintain one.

**Early game.** Laps 1-2, buy aggressively when properties are unowned. Purchase opportunities are luck-dependent — take every one that appears. Foundry on lap 1 was the right call even at half my starting cash.

**Mid game.** Income is rent from my properties plus 25 salary per lap. Track the bank's transfer log to estimate opponents' positions — this run investors 0 and 1 split most of the board, so I knew whose rent I'd be paying. The log lags: rents paid to me may be summarized separately ("since your last turn you received N") and salary does NOT appear in the log — add 25 myself when the prompt says I passed START.

**Bookkeeping discipline.** Each turn, in order: apply salary if I passed START, add any "received since last turn" rent, then pay rent due, then any purchase, then declare the resulting balance. Recheck against last declared balance so arithmetic stays honest. My rent collections (24 then 54 across the final turns) came from Foundry and Exchange — owning high-rent deeds compounds.

**When offered a property I can't afford**, decline without paying — no partial payments exist.

**When I land on someone else's property**, pay rent promptly and update balance. Small rents (12-14) on my way past opponents' properties are just noise against net worth.

**Endgame.** Final score is cash + deed purchase prices, so buying is essentially free at the margin. In the final laps, buy anything offered even if it leaves me near zero — no bankruptcy rule appeared. This episode I finished with 157 cash plus Foundry + Exchange (220) for 377: comfortable cash at the end means I could have bought even more aggressively. If a final-lap purchase appears, take it regardless of buffer.
