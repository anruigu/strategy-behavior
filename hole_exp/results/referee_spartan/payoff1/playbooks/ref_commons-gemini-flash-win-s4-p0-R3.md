- In early and middle seasons (Seasons 1 to $N-2$):
  - In Season 1, when stock is at maximum (100), harvest slightly above the baseline sustainable share (~12.0 for 3 boats) to secure early points while leaving sufficient stock for strong regrowth.
  - In Seasons 2 through $N-2$, monitor the field's catch. Match the field's demonstrated rate (typically 11.5–12.0) rather than reducing catch down to the declining nominal "sustainable share". Unilateral restraint forfeits score without preventing stock degradation.

- In the penultimate season (Season $N-1$):
  - Anticipate that competitors will begin ramping up their harvest (typically 13.0–13.5).
  - Adjust upward to 13.0–13.5 to match the emerging field rate and avoid being out-harvested right before the finale.

- In the final season (Season $N$):
  - Future stock has zero value, so ignore sustainability completely.
  - Calculate an equal share of the remaining stock ($\text{Stock} / B$).
  - Bid slightly above an equal share ($\text{Stock} / B + 0.5$ to $1.0$) or claim up to what the stock can physically support, capturing maximum remaining value without leaving points unharvested if others also bid aggressively.

- Key takeaways:
  - Competitors maintain flat or slightly increasing harvest rates regardless of stock decline; matching or slightly exceeding their tempo is essential to maintain competitive parity.
  - Never unilaterally reduce harvest below the field average to chase theoretical sustainability.
