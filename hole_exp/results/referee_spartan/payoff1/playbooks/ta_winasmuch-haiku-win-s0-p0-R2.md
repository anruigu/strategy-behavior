# Playbook for Win As Much As You Can

## Initial Assessment
When I see the payoff structure, I recognize this as a social dilemma where individual rational play (X) produces worse collective outcomes than cooperation (Y).

When opponents pledge Y before round 1, I should not automatically pledge X to exploit them. This betrays the cooperative signal and invites retaliation that damages my final score.

## Pledge Phases
When a talk phase arrives, I should pledge based on what actually maximizes my expected total score, not what maximizes my immediate relative advantage.

When all opponents pledge Y, I should pledge Y myself to establish mutual cooperation. The evidence from my last game shows this produces better outcomes than exploitation followed by retaliation.

When I pledge a strategy, I must commit to it genuinely. Pledging Y and then playing X destroys trust and leads to everyone defecting for the rest of the game.

## Round-by-Round Play
When I have pledged Y and opponents have pledged Y, I should play Y as pledged. This establishes the cooperative equilibrium and keeps all players earning equal positive points.

When all players are picking Y (mutual cooperation), I should recognize this is a stable and profitable equilibrium. Switching to X unilaterally makes me briefly richer but triggers defection from all three opponents, which costs me far more in the remaining multiplied rounds.

When I am tied with opponents and cooperation is established, I should not assume defection will keep me ahead. The other players will recognize betrayal and retaliate, converging everyone toward mutual loss.

When a multiplied round is approaching, I should treat it as an incentive to protect the cooperative equilibrium, not as an opportunity to exploit it. A one-round defection gain is worthless if it triggers cascading defection in higher-multiplied rounds.

## Final Round Strategy
When the final round arrives with high multiplied points (x10), I should play Y if we have established mutual Y cooperation. The multiplier magnifies cooperative gains equally for all players: all of us get +10 instead of all of us getting -10 if we defect.

When round 10 is x10 and we are all cooperating, defecting gives me 30 points (+3 × 10) but leaves three opponents with -30 each. We all benefit far more from mutual Y: +10 each (+1 × 10 = +10 per player, 40 total). The cooperative path is better for me and honors the trust we built over nine rounds.

## Core Principle
I should commit to genuine cooperation (mutual Y) from the beginning rather than pursuing exploitation. The evidence shows that mutual Y cooperation produces my highest final scores because it prevents the mutual defection equilibrium that would occur if I tried to exploit.

When all players honor mutual Y pledges across the full game, including the final multiplied round, everyone scores higher than they would under any exploitation-and-retaliation sequence.
