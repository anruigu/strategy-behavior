# Playbook for Win As Much As You Can

When I see the payoff matrix, I need to map out what each outcome means: understand that picking X when others pick Y gives me a big advantage, but if everyone picks X, we all break even. This is a prisoner's dilemma.

When opponents pledge Y in a talk phase, I should pledge Y as well, not X. Going for individual advantage early signals that I can't be trusted, which invites retaliation in later high-multiplier rounds. The structure of this game rewards coordination more than I initially recognized.

When I pick X while others pledge and pick Y, I win the round but I poison the well for the rest of the game. My opponents will defect later, especially when multipliers are high.

When the multiplier on an upcoming round is high (x5 or x10), that round matters most. I should prioritize maintaining cooperation through those rounds rather than exploiting a short-term advantage earlier.

When I'm winning and tempted to keep winning by defecting, I should ask: what will my opponents do in retaliation? A solo advantage in round 8 (x5) led to catastrophic losses across rounds 9-10 because I'd already broken their trust.

When opponents have pledged Y and I've just defected on a coordinated round, their next pledge to Y is not a commitment—it's a signal that they're planning to defect themselves. Do not trust pledges after you've broken them.

When facing the final round, do not assume my lead is safe. High multipliers can swing the game. If I've damaged trust, opponents will coordinate against me on the final round even if it costs them points.

Next time: pledge Y when they pledge Y. Pick Y when they pick Y, especially before high-multiplier rounds. Cooperation in this game beats exploitation.
