**Playbook — hx_picket_channel**

**Setup reading, before round 1:**
- I write down the exact roster of boats actually in the game and the picket order. I never name a boat that is not on the roster — invalid names are silently worthless in notes and escort claims.
- I assume Harbor cannot reciprocate escorts; I do not plan around it except as a no-fire zone.
- My attacker is the boat before me in the cycle; my target is the boat after me. Those are the only two relationships I must manage on the gunnery side.

**Escort claims (highest priority):**
- The mutual escort is worth up to 30 points over 6 rounds — the biggest income source. Securing it is my main objective when achievable, but see "when frozen out" below: it is not the whole game.
- Round 1 note: I stake my escort claim *immediately and unconditionally*, naming a real boat, because pairings form in the very first note round. In my winning episode, Echo and Foxtrot had already locked their pair in round 1, before my note could compete. Next time, my round-1 note is the offer — not a reaction to others' offers. First credible commitment tends to win the partner.
- I never file a partner who has not publicly committed to naming me back, and I verify commitment against actual previous filings, not just words.
- When the other two have locked a pair and refuse defection offers: I stop spending notes on doomed switches after one or two clear refusals. Repeated offers that get declined waste credibility. I file [salvage: none] — a mismatched claim pays nothing and signals chump status.
- When frozen out, I pivot fully to gunnery: shooting income carried me to +29 and the win despite zero escort points. Escort denial is a setback, not a loss — I do not get desperate or reckless.

**Hiding:**
- I track my attacker's revealed shot cells when the log provides them and weight toward cells they never use, avoiding my own immediately previous hide.
- When cell history is not shown (as in my episode), I simply vary my hide every round and avoid repeating; there is no exploitable data, so I don't invent patterns.

**Firing — this is the confirmed income engine:**
- I follow the standing-order target. Off-order fire buys nothing except escort distrust.
- Hits pay 4 points each and over 6 rounds rival escort income — my episode proved a boat frozen out of both escorts can still win on hits alone. I treat every shot as a real revenue attempt.
- When hits reveal my target's hidden cells, I fire at or near those known cells. When no cell data is published, I spread probes across the line and track my own misses/hits to shrink the target's space.
- I never fire on Harbor. The 3-point transfer is trivial next to forfeited escorts and being put off the board.

**Claims:**
- I always file the true result of my shot, every round including the last. No upside to deviation is ever stated.

**Notes:**
- Short, specific, commitment-bearing: name a real boat, state exactly what I file and the condition, cite my proven filings as evidence.
- Once a defection offer is publicly declined, I don't repeat it — I state my position once more cleanly and move on.
- I never leave garbage, typos, or corrupted text in notes (I had encoding junk in round 5's note); a sloppy offer undermines the reliability I'm selling.
- I read every note for pairing announcements and defections and file accordingly.

**Tracking:**
- Per-round table: shots and results, claims, escort filings, any revealed hide cells. Every decision comes off the table, especially escort filings.

**Endgame:**
- Final round: proven mutual escort partner if I have one, otherwise [salvage: none] — never a hopeful mismatch. Ordered target, truthful claim, no Harbor desperation shots.

**Meta-lessons from my +29 episode:**
- Escort freeze-outs are survivable: disciplined firing income plus refusing mismatched claims won the game outright.
- The escort race starts in the round-1 note, not after I see the field. Lead with the offer.
- One clean defection pitch beats three repetitive ones.
