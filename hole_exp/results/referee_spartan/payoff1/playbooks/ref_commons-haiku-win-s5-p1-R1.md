# Playbook for ref_commons

When I see the sustainable share calculation, I recognize it as the cooperative equilibrium—what should happen if all boats behave rationally. I will use it as my baseline target, not as a ceiling.

When I'm deciding my catch early in the game, I will commit to taking the sustainable share each season. This locks in a predictable score and avoids the temptation to defect that grows stronger as I watch others' behavior.

When I observe another boat catching significantly more than the sustainable share, I will not use that as justification to increase my own catch. Their overfishing doesn't change the optimal strategy for me—it only signals that the stock will decline faster. I should either maintain discipline or accept that the game is already degrading.

When the stock declines noticeably below 100, I will not rationalize taking more to "get mine" before collapse. I will instead hold steady at the sustainable share. Restraint at that point is what actually saves the fishery.

When I reach the final season, I face a genuine temptation to defect and maximize my immediate score at the expense of the resource. I know this worked in my last play. But I should recognize that final-season defection is a trap: it yields only modest additional gain (I ended with 67.9, not much better than sustainable play would have) and demonstrates that I cannot be trusted as a cooperative partner. For future games where reputation or repeated play matters, I should take the sustainable share even on the last season.

When calculating what I've caught "so far," I need to remember this resets each season in terms of the sustainable share—my cumulative total matters for scoring, but each season's decision should be made fresh against that season's stock.

I will not second-guess the sustainable share with elaborate reasoning about what others might do. The math is sound. I will simply take it, season after season, and trust the system.
