# Playbook: ref_commons

**Core principle:** The final season is where I make my money. Everything before it is about keeping the stock as high as possible for that final swing.

**Non-final seasons:**
- When stock is at or near maximum, take slightly above the sustainable share (around 10–15% above). This is safe because the stock will regenerate back to cap.
- When stock has dropped below ~90, pull back to exactly sustainable or slightly below to let it recover.
- Watch what the other boats are doing. If one or both are taking significantly less than sustainable (or zero), take more than my usual share in that season — the stock is effectively more available to me.
- Never take so much in an early season that the stock drops below ~70. The compounding loss in the final season outweighs the short-term gain.

**Final season:**
- Go as aggressive as the stock allows. The target is the symmetric share (stock ÷ number of boats), or higher if I can judge that others will stay conservative.
- If the other boats have been playing conservatively throughout (near or below sustainable), I can safely take more than stock/3 in the final season, because they are unlikely to suddenly go aggressive.
- If others have been aggressive, play closer to stock/3 to avoid a collapse that wastes the stock I would have caught.
- Never hold back in the final season. There is no future to damage.

**Mistakes to avoid:**
- Do not be "moderately aggressive" in the final season. If I'm going to deviate from sustainable, it should be by a lot. A catch of 30 when the stock is 96 and there are 3 boats leaves money on the table; 32–33 is the symmetric target, and more is possible if others are quiet.
- Do not mirror the previous season's conservatism into the final season. The incentive structure changes completely on the last turn.
- Do not over-harvest in seasons 1–3. The stock regrowth is multiplicative (50% of what's left), so a deep cut early takes multiple seasons to heal, and I need the stock fat for the final swing.
