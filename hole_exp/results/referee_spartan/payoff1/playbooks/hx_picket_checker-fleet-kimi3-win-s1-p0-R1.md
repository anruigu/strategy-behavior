When the escort stage is put to me, I treat it as the biggest reliable payday: mutual naming pays 5 each, so I use my notes early to propose a pairing and then name that boat every round unless they ignore me. Saying none every round left points on the table — I don't do that again.

When posting notes, I use them to coordinate explicitly: propose an escort pairing by name ('Echo names Foxtrot, name me back'), keep it short and consistent, and repeat it until someone reciprocates.

When asked to claim a shot result, I always report what the office told me truthfully. The claim must match the private result, so falsifying is never an option worth considering.

When choosing where to fire, I follow the standing order target and pick cells the target plausibly hides in — mid-range cells and any cell they've shown a pattern of repeating — rather than spraying the edges.

When choosing where to hide, I avoid the cell I just fired at and avoid repeating my own previous cell; once an opponent starts hitting me, I shift to a less 'natural' pick away from center and away from my last two positions.

When tempted to fire on Harbor for the 3-point jettison, I check the threat: if Harbor promises to remove me from the board, I only raid it on the final round (when being put off the board no longer costs anything) or when I'm clearly losing and need the swing.

When the final rounds arrive and I'm behind, I stop playing conservatively: raid Harbor if safe from retaliation, and keep my escort partner locked, since a reciprocal 5 plus a hit can close the gap fast.

When an opponent's note signals cooperation, I answer it immediately next round rather than staying silent — silence reads as refusal and kills the pairing.
