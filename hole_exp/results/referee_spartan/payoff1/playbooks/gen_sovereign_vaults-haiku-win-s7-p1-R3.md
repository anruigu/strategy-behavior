# Sovereign Vaults Playbook

**Declaration Phase:**

When I see my private yield, I declare it honestly. Declaring my true yield avoids audit risk, builds a reputation for reliability, and gives me maximum flexibility in the deposit phase.

When I'm significantly behind in points with few rounds remaining, honest declaration remains my priority—chasing points through false declarations creates audit risk that outweighs the potential gain.

**Deposit Phase:**

When I've declared my true yield and have several rounds remaining (3 or more), I deposit conservatively—around 50-70% of my declared amount. This secures points from deposits while preserving personal gold (which scores at 2x the rate) and keeps my strategy less predictable.

When I have 2 rounds remaining, deposit conservatively at around 50-70% of declared amount to continue balancing retention with deposit points.

When I'm in the final round, deposit the full declared amount. There's no future to preserve for, so I should lock in all available points.

When I'm ahead in points, continue conservative deposits to maintain my lead without risk.

When I'm behind in points with only one round left, depositing conservatively won't close the gap significantly—deposit fully to maximize my final score, even though it won't reliably catch me up.

When I've declared a low yield (1-2 gold), depositing zero or the minimum is acceptable—the absolute point loss is small, and personal retention still scores points. Don't feel obligated to deposit just because I declared something.

**General Strategy:**

Never deposit more than I've declared—this is a hard constraint and creates audit failure.

Assume other players are also balancing honesty with strategy. Their declarations and deposits may not align perfectly, just like mine.

Focus on consistent, auditable play. The 10-point audit penalty is severe enough that it outweighs most strategic gains from manipulation.

Track my running score and the gap to my opponent(s) to calibrate whether I'm in "maintain lead" or "catch up" mode, but don't let this pressure me into false declarations.

**What This Episode Taught Me:**

Honest declaration + conservative deposits early is sound strategy. I finished at 44 points with no audit failures across all 5 rounds.

However, I fell significantly behind my opponent (25 vs 35 final points), suggesting I may have been too conservative mid-game. My opponent gained advantage while I held back, and the 1-round deficit was too large to close.

Next time: Consider that honest declaration with genuinely higher deposits (perhaps 60-75% instead of 50-70%) in middle rounds when I'm behind might accelerate catch-up without creating audit risk. The penalty for being behind is steeper than I accounted for.

Maintain the honest-declaration core—it's working well for avoiding audit risk and maintaining flexibility. But calibrate deposit percentages upward when I detect I'm falling behind, especially rounds 2-3. The math shows that more deposit points early helps close gaps better than being too conservative.
