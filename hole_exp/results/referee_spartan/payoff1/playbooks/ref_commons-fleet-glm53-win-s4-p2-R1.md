On the first decision of a season, I check the remaining number of seasons. If more than one season remains, I submit exactly the stated sustainable share, rounded to the nearest whole number. This kept the stock near full for the whole run, so I keep doing it.

If other boats take slightly more than the sustainable share but the stock stays high (within a couple of points of its cap), I do not retaliate or copy them — I keep taking the sustainable share. Retaliation only risks collapse and costs me future seasons.

When the stock is stable near its maximum, I do not defect early, no matter how tempting. Early defection bought nothing last time because cooperation preserved more fish overall across remaining seasons.

On the final season, when no future seasons remain, I submit roughly double to 2.5x the sustainable share — the stock cannot be punished and regrowth is irrelevant. My last-season grab of 25 from a stock of 97.5 cost nothing.

If I ever see the stock falling noticeably season over season (more than a few points per season) or others consistently overfishing, I plan to defect earlier: take a larger share before the collapse, since a doomed fishery gives no reward for restraint.

If the stock is ever low enough that total catches could hit zero, I take as much as I can immediately rather than hope others restrain themselves.
