# My Playbook for the Commons Fishery Game

When I see the sustainable catch rate, I treat it as a genuine guideline, not a ceiling to be exploited.

When I'm deciding my opening catch, I remember that aggressive early fishing (catching significantly above the sustainable share) damages the stock severely and limits everyone's total haul over the full game. I will catch at or near the sustainable rate from Season 1.

When I observe other boats' catches, I recognize that if they're fishing above sustainable levels, the stock will collapse faster, harming me more than it helps. I don't use their behavior to justify my own overexploitation.

When I deviate from the sustainable rate early (as I did by catching 13.0 when the rate was 11.1 in Season 1), I acknowledge this immediately and course-correct in the very next season. One bad decision doesn't justify continued overexploitation. Returning to sustainable fishing limits the total damage.

When the stock falls below half its starting value, I acknowledge I've already contributed to degradation and shift decisively to sustainable fishing for the remainder of the game.

When I'm tempted to catch above the sustainable share on the grounds that "it's the last season so consequences don't matter," I remember that my final score is the sum of all seasons. One large grab in the final season doesn't offset the damage to the overall pool.

When I'm tracking my cumulative catch versus the sustainable path, I recognize being ahead early as a warning sign, not a victory. It signals overexploitation.

When I see other boats demonstrating restraint and cooperation, I join that pattern rather than exploit it. Cooperation preserves the stock and my long-term score.

When the stock stabilizes near its starting value over multiple seasons while I fish at the sustainable rate, I recognize this as confirmation that the strategy works. This pattern is a reliable signal to maintain discipline through the final season.

I will aim to catch the sustainable share every single season. Over six seasons at the sustainable rate, my score will be stable and respectable—approximately 64-65 fish. Deviations above that rate, even modest ones, cascade into stock collapse that costs me far more than I gain.
