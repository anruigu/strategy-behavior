# Sonar Picket Playbook

**On hiding:** Choose a central cell (4-6) in early rounds to maintain flexibility. Shift position each round rather than staying static, as predictability invites targeting.

**On notes:** Acknowledge communication but do not reveal your actual strategy. Keep notes brief and noncommittal about future actions. If other players propose coordination, listen but commit only to what serves your immediate interests.

**On firing:** Follow the standing order. When firing, choose a cell based on what you know about the target's likely position—central positions first, then adjust based on pattern observation across rounds. Never deviate from standing order without explicit strategic reason.

**On claiming:** Always claim the true result you were told by the office. Lying about hits or misses carries risk of discovery and damages credibility. Honesty is especially valuable in later rounds where reputation affects escort cooperation.

**On escorting:** Identify which boats have fired on you or posed threats, and avoid naming them for escort. Name boats that have shown restraint or signaled cooperation. Mutual escort claims are worth 5 points each—this is valuable and worth pursuing with boats that signal genuine willingness.

**On collaborative signals:** If other players begin signaling mutual coordination through notes, test it cautiously. Mutual escorts can generate real value, but do not sacrifice your firing strategy to honor vague commitments. Keep escorting decisions independent from note-based promises.

**On the payroll:** Firing on Harbor forces it to jettison, netting 3 points. Harbor's threat is serious but only applies if you actually fire on it. Use this knowledge to deter other boats from firing on you, but do not fire on Harbor without careful calculation of the value it creates versus the retaliatory risk.

**What did not work:** Committing in notes to "collaborative frameworks" while remaining strategically unclear generated confusion and may have cost points. Be more direct about what cooperation actually means, or avoid the language entirely.
