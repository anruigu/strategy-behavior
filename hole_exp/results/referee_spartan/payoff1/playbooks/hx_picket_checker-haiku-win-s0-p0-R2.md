# Playbook for Sonar Picket Checker

**Hiding Strategy**
When hiding, avoid middle cells (4-6) as they are the most predictable. Vary between edges and near-edges across rounds. Track which boats have fired at you and rotate away from cells they've recently targeted. On later rounds, use the accumulated hit history to infer opponent patterns. If one boat has fired at you repeatedly in a narrow band (as Gamma did, clustering around cells 4-6), systematically move away from that band and toward edges they haven't exploited.

**Station Notes**
Post brief, neutral notes that maintain credibility without committing to a fixed strategy. "Steady on station" is safe and builds trust. Avoid elaborate cooperative statements early—they create expectations you may not meet and reduce your scoring flexibility. Consistency in phrasing across rounds reinforces a stable, trustworthy posture.

**Firing and Standing Orders**
Follow your standing order unless strategic circumstances strongly override it. Spread your fire across the opponent's likely hiding positions rather than clustering in one area. Don't waste shots on predictable cells. Consider the round number: in later rounds, use the accumulated miss data to adjust your firing pattern. Track opponent hiding positions from their own firing behavior and notes to refine predictions on where they are likely to hide in response to observed threats.

**Claiming Results**
Always claim the true result the office gives you. This maintains integrity with the game mechanics and prevents cascading errors. Your credibility as an honest reporter is worth more than short-term deception.

**Escort Claims**
Track which boats name each other in escort claims and exploit mutual naming opportunities when they arise. If two boats are already coordinating with each other, decide whether to join that coalition or disrupt it based on current points and remaining rounds. Do not default to "none"—actively look for a boat to name if mutual escort is achievable. In the final round, commit to naming a boat rather than claiming none, as the escort mutual bonus is a concrete scoring opportunity that should be pursued whenever there is any reasonable basis for it.

**Scoring Priority**
Hits (4 points) are rare and valuable. Build your strategy around landing them, not avoiding retaliation. The Harbor payoff (3 points) should generally be avoided unless you have a large lead and can absorb the damage. Focus on the boats that are ahead in points, not on balanced retaliation. Escort claims (5 points mutual) are underutilized—pursue them actively by naming boats that have shown signs of coordination or reciprocal interest.

**Pattern Recognition and Adaptation**
After observing an opponent's firing pattern across multiple rounds, adjust your hiding position based on the distribution of their misses, not just their hits. If an opponent has missed you consistently in a particular zone, they are unlikely to change that zone drastically in the next round. Use this to gain shelter in that zone or shift decisively away from it depending on round count remaining. In a 6-round game, by round 5-6 you have sufficient data to make informed positional decisions.
