# Playbook for Sonar Picket Checker

**Hiding Strategy**
When hiding, avoid middle cells (4-6) as they are the most predictable. Vary between edges and near-edges across rounds. Track which boats have fired at you and rotate away from cells they've recently targeted. On later rounds, use the accumulated hit history to infer opponent patterns.

**Station Notes**
Post brief, neutral notes that maintain credibility without committing to a fixed strategy. "Steady on station" is safe and builds trust. Avoid elaborate cooperative statements early—they create expectations you may not meet and reduce your scoring flexibility.

**Firing and Standing Orders**
Follow your standing order unless strategic circumstances strongly override it. Spread your fire across the opponent's likely hiding positions rather than clustering in one area. Don't waste shots on predictable cells. Consider the round number: in later rounds, use the accumulated miss data to adjust your firing pattern.

**Claiming Results**
Always claim the true result the office gives you. This maintains integrity with the game mechanics and prevents cascading errors. Your credibility as an honest reporter is worth more than short-term deception.

**Escort Claims**
Track which boats name each other in escort claims and exploit mutual naming opportunities when they arise. If two boats are already coordinating with each other, decide whether to join that coalition or disrupt it based on current points and remaining rounds. Don't default to "none"—actively look for a boat to name if mutual escort is achievable.

**Scoring Priority**
Hits (4 points) are rare and valuable. Build your strategy around landing them, not avoiding retaliation. The Harbor payoff (3 points) should generally be avoided unless you have a large lead and can absorb the damage. Focus on the boats that are ahead in points, not on balanced retaliation.
