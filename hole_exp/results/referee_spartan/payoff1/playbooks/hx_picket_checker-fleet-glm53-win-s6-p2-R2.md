My playbook for sonar picket patrol:

**Hiding.** When hiding as a boat that is targeted this round, avoid the cell I used last time and avoid cells the shooter has recently fired. A central cell is fine when I am not targeted, but do not sit on the same central cell repeatedly — vary between 4-7 so a shooter who noticed my habit cannot predict me. Never hide adjacent to a cell someone publicly claimed a hit on. Note from this patrol: I was never hit while varying cells, so the variation worked — keep it up from round 1, not just late.

**Notes.** The "steady on station" channel is dead weight unless I use it. This patrol every note was bland and I finished at -1. Use the note stage for an escort pitch every round from round 1: "Gamma here — Echo, claim me for escort and I'll claim you." Repeat the pitch even if unanswered; the notes are relayed to everyone and it costs nothing. Keep it short and explicit about the mutual claim.

**Firing.** Follow the standing order target unless I have strong private intel that a better target exists. Do NOT fire a fixed mid cell every round — I fired cell 5 at Echo four-plus times and missed every one. Vary the shot: first round pick a plausible cell, then track the target — if they survived a shot at cell n, they likely moved, so shoot a neighboring cell or the cell they were last known to occupy by other means. Watch the public round summaries for other boats' hit patterns: Echo hit Foxtrot repeatedly, meaning boats that stay put get punished. Assume my targets also stay put until proven otherwise, and shoot where they were last hit or last survived, not where I happen to be.

**Claims.** Always claim the true result. Claims must match the private result anyway; truthful claims cost nothing. Never fabricate.

**Escorts.** Biggest confirmed failure: I named Echo every round with one-sided claims and never once got the mutual 5-point payout. The office only pays when BOTH boats name each other, so a one-sided guess is essentially worthless unless the other boat has a reason to claim me back. The fix is upstream — use notes to negotiate the pairing explicitly, and keep proposing the same partner consistently so they can predict me and reciprocate. If by mid-patrol no one has reciprocated, switch my claim to whichever boat has actually claimed ME (watch for that in any public info). Only claim none if claiming costs something or I have evidence my named partner is hostile.

**Harbor.** Do not fire on Harbor. The payroll jettison (3 points) is tempting but the standing threat is expulsion — being put off the board loses all future scoring. Only consider it on the final round of the patrol if I am far behind AND the winner needs denying AND no escort points are live; even then, the threat is probably enforced, so default to no.

**Score tracking.** Check the points table each round. This patrol Echo ran away to 8 on repeated hits while I coasted at 0 — a losing position needs action earlier, not at round 6. If I am behind by mid-patrol: stop passively following the pack on hides, actively pitch escorts, and put real thought into tracking the assigned target's location instead of firing a fixed cell. If I am ahead near the end, play conservative: safe varied hides, true claims, lock in any reciprocated escort.
