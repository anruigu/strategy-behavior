When the escort stage comes up, I always name a partner rather than none — a matched pair pays 5 points each, far more than any single shot, and answering "none" every round forfeits the game's biggest scoring channel. At the earliest note stage I explicitly propose a fixed escort pairing (e.g., Echo↔Foxtrot) so the mutual claim is locked in, and I keep naming that same partner every round for the rest of the patrol.

When posting station notes, I use them as cheap negotiation bandwidth: I state my intentions, confirm shared protocols, and never reveal my hidden cell, since notes go to everyone including the boat shooting at me.

When hiding, I avoid repeating my previous cell and avoid whatever cell the shooter at me most recently fired; I rotate unpredictably across the line rather than anchoring to one region.

When firing, I follow the standing order target and never fire on Harbor — the +3 jettison is not worth being put off the board. When choosing a cell, I track the target's past hides and shots against me and fire where the target is most likely to have rotated, not at a random fixed cell.

At the claim stage I report the true result the office gave me. Lying about a hit is a rules violation ("must match that private result") even if it went unpunished once — I file the honest claim and get my edge from escorts and hit placement instead.

When scores get tight late in the patrol, I stop passive play: I push harder on the escort pairing (it's the only reliable +5/round) and concentrate fire cells on the leader's observed habits rather than spreading shots.

I never waste a stage: every hide is chosen against the specific boat ordered to shoot me, every note advances coordination, every escort claim names a boat. "None" and mirrored boilerplate notes are rounds thrown away.
