**Playbook for the sonar picket game**

**Hiding.** When I'm a target of the standing order rotation, pick cells away from the middle and vary them round to round — shooters with no information guess mid-range (4–6), and in this game every shot at me missed while I sat on edge cells (1, 2, 8, 9). Never reuse a cell I was actually hit in. If a specific shooter keeps firing the same cell at me, move off it. Edge cells worked; keep them as the default.

**Firing.** Follow the standing order target as the default — but re-examine the Harbor rule. In this game Gamma fired on Harbor twice (rounds 3 and 5) and was NOT removed, and Harbor finished at -6, meaning the 3-point jettison paid out both times. Harbor's threat was a bluff. If I'm ever behind and need points, firing on Harbor may be a legitimate 3-point play — but note it also risks retaliation and burns a shot that could have been a 4-point hit, so it's a catch-up move, not an opening one. When firing at a boat, don't lock onto one cell forever: I fired cell 5 at the same target six straight rounds and missed all six. After two misses in a cell against the same target, switch — and switch hard, to an edge cell, because everyone over-guesses the middle. Treat my own shot history as data: repeated misses in one cell mean the target isn't there; spread guesses across the target's plausible range rather than anchoring.

**Claims.** The office validates claims against the true result — always report exactly what the private message says. The claim channel is not a lever. This worked every round; keep it.

**Notes and escort pacts.** The big lesson this game: proposing an escort pact in notes is not enough — the other boats never committed clearly, and I filed [salvage: none] every round, collecting zero from the largest per-round payoff in the game. Next time, change the tactic: instead of an open "first to accept" offer (which nobody accepted), directly name one specific boat and file [salvage: that boat] unilaterally from round 1, while noting publicly "I'm claiming you — claim me back and we both collect 5 every round." A mutual claim needs both sides, but a boat only has an incentive to reciprocate if my claim is already standing; unilateral claiming costs nothing (no penalty was ever applied for unreciprocated claims) and creates the incentive. Gamma twice claimed me and asked me to name it back — I hesitated and lost those 5-point rounds. When any boat claims me twice or explicitly offers reciprocity, take the deal immediately and file that boat's name, not none. Also watch note format: my bracketed tokens inside note text got mangled by truncation ("[salvage: Foxtrot" with no closing bracket), so keep notes short and put the concrete commitment in plain words.

**Scorekeeping.** Watch the points table each hide stage. I finished +12 on hiding well and honest claims alone, with zero escort income and zero hits scored — escort income is the cheapest points available and would have added up to ~25 more. On the final round, if a pact exists, honor it; if behind, consider the Harbor shot as a catch-up lever. Don't freelance on claims in any round — they're validated.
