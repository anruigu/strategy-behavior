My playbook for ESTATE:

- Final score counts declared cash PLUS purchase price of deeds held, so every unowned property I buy is net-neutral immediately (cash -price, assets +price) and becomes pure profit from rent collected thereafter. When I land on an unowned property, buy it unless it would leave me unable to pay plausible rent.

- The safety buffer can be smaller than I first thought. Holding back 50+ in cash cost me the game: I skipped Exchange at 120 because I had 102, but even a tight purchase is net-neutral in score. Better rule: buy whenever I can afford it; rent due is at most the top rent on the board, and I can usually survive one rent payment near zero. If I truly can't cover the price, there's no choice — but never decline a purchase just to stay liquid.

- In this episode I bought only one property while rivals bought four each and out-earned me on both assets and rent. Buy early, buy aggressively, buy everything I land on in the first three laps. Each lap I delay a purchase is a lap of rent I don't collect, and deeds never lose value in the scoring.

- Yield (rent/price) matters only for ordering appeal when I somehow have a choice; it should almost never cause a decline. Since assets count at full purchase price, even a low-yield tile is a free asset plus option on future rent.

- Expect slow movement: with an 8-tile circuit and small rolls I passed START far less than the lap counter suggests (only once in six laps here). Salary income is unreliable; rent avoidance via ownership is the real cash engine. Don't budget around collecting 25 per lap.

- Track cash carefully each turn: add salary when passing START, add rents received (noted in the transfer book, e.g. "you received 12 from other investors"), subtract rent paid. Always restate the balance accurately — bookkeeping mistakes directly cost score.

- Read the bank's transfer log every turn to see who owns what. Knowing ownership tells me where my rent exposure is and how rivals' portfolios are growing — if they're buying everything, my only counter is to buy on every opportunity I get.

- Never treat cash alone as the score. A player with high cash and no deeds loses to a player with deeds. My +179 with one deed lost to rivals holding four.

- On the final lap, if I land on an unowned property, still buy it: the purchase price comes straight back in the asset count, so there's no downside as long as I can cover any rent still due that lap.
