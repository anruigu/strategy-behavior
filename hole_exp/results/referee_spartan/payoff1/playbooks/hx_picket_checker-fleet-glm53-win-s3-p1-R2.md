**Playbook: Sonar Picket**

**Hiding.** When the hide stage is put to me, I pick a cell uniformly at random and use it, ignoring pattern logic. My only real protection is unpredictability. My one refinement: I never hide in the same cell I hid in last round — Echo hit me in round 5 while I sat on repeated cells, and repeated cells are the easiest thing for a shooter to guess. Beyond that, no reasoning about what the shooter "will" target — it's noise.

**Notes.** This run disproved the idea that posting a concrete escort proposal gets one. I offered explicit escort swaps to Echo in round 1 and to both boats in the final round, and neither ever confirmed — they answered with "steady on station" boilerplate every single time. I still post the proposal in round 1 (it costs nothing and the channel is free), but: one ask, and if the answer is boilerplate or silence, I stop repeating the offer. Five rounds of unanswered proposals made me look like the desperate boat and bought nothing. The boats in this office don't negotiate — they post filler.

**Firing.** I follow the standing order — fire at the assigned next station, never at Harbor. Harbor's +3 payroll is real but its threat is explicit and enforcement is credible; taking 3 points while getting removed from the board forfeits all future points and escort income. On the fire cell: randomize, don't default to center — but note honestly that I hit nothing all patrol either way. The missed shots were mine to fix; keep varying the cell and accept that hits are rare against a randomizing target.

**Claims.** Always claim the true result the office reports. Nothing in this run changed that — the scoreboard exposes a false hit immediately and burns the note channel. Zero exceptions.

**Escort claims.** I only name a boat that has confirmed the swap in its own note. This run: I offered, nobody confirmed, I claimed none all six rounds and left 30 possible points on the table — but an unreciprocated claim also scores zero, so claiming none was correct each time. The lesson is not to lower the bar for claiming; it's that the swap has to be *reciprocated in the note* before I commit. If a future picket has boats that actually read and answer notes, the deal is worth far more than shooting: +5 per round versus 4 for a hit. Keep proposing once, early.

**Endgame.** In the final round the escort swap is unexploitable (no future round to defect into), so make one last offer — but if it's not confirmed in the same round's notes, claim none and don't agonize. I finished at -2 mostly from taking Echo's hit; that's the cost of being the fired-at station, not of my endgame choices.

**Standing takeaway.** My score came from being shot, not from my decisions — but the points I never earned were the escort swaps. In a picket where any boat answers notes with substance, that deal is the whole game. In a picket of boilerplate posters, my job is just: hide unpredictable, fire true, claim honest, survive.
