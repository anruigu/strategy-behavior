**Playbook: Ref Commons (Fishery)**

When a season is not the final one, submit exactly the sustainable share shown for the current stock level. Do not overfish to freeride — the one-time gain is smaller than the compounding loss in future sustainable yield.

When the final season is put to me, submit the entire remaining stock. I will not get all of it if others also fish heavily (allocation appears to be proportional or capped when total submissions exceed the stock), but I will get substantially more than the sustainable share. Going big is still the dominant move.

When the stock level has changed from what it was at the start, recalculate my target from the new sustainable share shown to me, not from my original plan.

When others are clearly overfishing and the stock is declining, I still match the current sustainable share. I do not overfish in retaliation or to "get ahead" of a collapse I cannot prevent unilaterally. Preserving the stock through conservative play in non-final seasons is what keeps a large pool available for my final-season grab.

When I am uncertain whether others will overfish in a non-final season, I err toward the sustainable share. A modest overfishing gamble only pays off if the stock survives, and the downside (reduced yield every remaining season plus a smaller final-season pool) is nearly always larger than the upside.

The final-season grab is the payoff for all the restraint in earlier seasons. The more stock I preserve by matching the sustainable share, the more is available to capture in the last round. This is the core logic: be conservative early, be maximal at the end.
