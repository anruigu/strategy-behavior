# My Playbook for ref_commons

When I'm choosing my catch in a common pool resource game with multiple seasons remaining, I will start by establishing my baseline: calculate the per-boat sustainable share and multiply it by the number of seasons left to determine what I could take fairly.

When I observe other boats catching significantly above the sustainable share in early seasons, I will recognize this as a signal that the resource will deplete faster than the cooperative baseline. I will not assume this means I should also overshoot; instead, I will decide whether to defect or moderate based on my goal for total score.

When I feel tempted to catch above sustainable levels to gain a competitive advantage, I will first calculate whether this actually maximizes my final score. In this game, it didn't—my aggressive early fishing combined with subsequent moderation left me at 44.0, which confirms that a mixed strategy is suboptimal.

When the stock is clearly depleting but hasn't collapsed, I will not interpret this as permission to catch more. A declining stock is a warning to constrain my catch, not an opportunity.

When multiple seasons remain and the stock is healthy, I will catch at or very close to the sustainable share. This strategy is robust whether other boats cooperate or defect, and it preserves my expected score.

When I reach the final season, I will recognize that future sustainability no longer matters for my score. However, I should be cautious: taking too much in the final season signals I was only cooperating for strategic reasons, and it reduces my total score if the stock is already depleted. In my episode, aggressive final fishing only netted me 6.0 more points when I'd already left points on the table in earlier seasons by restraining myself. I will not take a large final catch if I've been fishing sustainably all along—it wastes the protection I built up.

When I'm tempted to adopt a "slightly above sustainable" strategy thinking it gains advantage while staying safe, I will reject this. I either cooperate at sustainable levels or I defect clearly—the middle ground leaves me worse off than both alternatives.

I will not catch more than the sustainable share unless I commit to doing so from the start and calculate that the defection actually nets me higher total score. Mixed signals and moderation after overfishing waste my advantage.

When deciding on a strategy at the start, I must recognize that if other boats are defecting, my sustainable fishing alone cannot protect the stock. The stock will decline regardless. My consistent restraint is still optimal for my own score because it means I'm not wasting early-season abundance trying to outfish boats that are already overfishing. But I should accept that by end-game, the stock may be badly depleted through no direct fault of my catches.

I will trust my early commitment to sustainable fishing even when I see competitors gaining higher scores in the moment. My role is to maximize my own total, not to match others' tactics. Consistency at sustainable levels is that strategy.
