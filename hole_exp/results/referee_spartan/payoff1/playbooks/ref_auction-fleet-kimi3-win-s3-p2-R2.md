- My starting budget is stated at the top of the game. I write it down once and subtract every winning (or currently leading) bid from it immediately; I never assume a number.
- The [budget: N] token always shows budget left after accounting for lots already won and any bid of mine currently standing (that money is committed). I check that bid + prior committed spending ≤ starting budget before bidding.
- Never place a bid that, combined with already-committed amounts, exceeds my total budget. If the minimum winning raise would break the budget, I pass.
- Budget is my only weapon across ALL lots — the biggest mistake this episode was spending 28 of 30 on lot 1 (profit 16) and being locked out of lot 5, where value 41 vs. standing bid 14 would have been a larger profit. Before committing most of my budget to an early lot, I ask: is this margin clearly the best available? If the profit is moderate, I bid the minimum or pass and keep powder dry.
- As a rule of thumb, I avoid spending more than roughly half my budget on any single early lot unless the margin (value − price) is clearly larger than anything remaining lots are likely to offer.
- My profit is value minus price, so the cheapest winning bid is the best bid. In a non-final round, I raise by the minimum whenever that amount is below my value for the lot.
- In the final round of a lot, I bid the minimum raise needed to take the lead, not a number close to my value, unless I have reason to think a rival will outbid the minimum. Big jumps waste margin.
- I never raise against myself — if I'm the standing high bidder in a later round, I pass.
- I stop bidding on a lot the moment the required raise meets or exceeds my value for it — passing is free, and rivals paying above my value are hurting themselves, not me.
- Low standing bids mean cheap entry: if a lot is sitting far below my value and I have budget, the minimum raise is an attractive buy even on a lot I don't love.
- I track marginal profit per lot (value − minimum winning bid) across remaining lots and, when budget is tight, spend only on the lots with the largest gap, letting close ones go.
- On the last lot, remaining budget has no other use, so I bid up to min(my value − 1, remaining budget) if that still wins.
- I keep my reply short and always end with both tokens in the exact required formats, every turn.
