# My Ref_Estate Playbook

## Early Game Property Acquisition
When you encounter an unowned property in the first two laps, buy it if you have the cash. Early ownership gives you rent income for the majority of the game and blocks opponents from building their portfolios.

## Cash Flow Management
Keep careful track of your cash position after each turn. Remember that you'll collect salary on each pass through START—use this to project your liquidity across the remaining laps. Include any rent you've received from opponents in your available cash calculations. Declare your balance conservatively and accurately; the bank's record of transfers will reveal inconsistencies.

## Rent Obligations
When you land on an opponent's property, pay the rent immediately and adjust your balance accordingly. Never dispute or defer payments; track them accurately for your final score calculation.

## Mid-Game Property Decisions
In laps 3-4, continue to buy unowned properties if you have sufficient cash reserves. Even with only 2-3 laps remaining, property ownership generates income that contributes to your final score. Use the salary you collect from passing START to fund these purchases when possible. However, be realistic about how many remaining laps will generate rent income from a new purchase—if you're already in lap 3 or beyond, the window for recouping your investment narrows.

## Cash Position Monitoring
If your cash drops below 30 late in the game, be cautious about new purchases unless the property is very cheap (under 25) and you're confident you can cover future rent obligations. A negative balance at the end damages your score significantly. Avoid over-committing to purchases when you have few laps left to generate offsetting rent income.

## Late-Game Property Decisions
In the final lap, do not buy unowned properties even if you have the cash available. You will have no opportunity to collect rent from them, so the purchase only reduces your final declared balance. Preserve your cash for the score calculation instead.

## Aggressive Mid-Game Acquisition
In lap 3, prioritize buying an unowned property if one appears, even if it means reducing your cash cushion. A property purchased in lap 3 will generate rent for at least 3 more laps (laps 4, 5, and 6), which typically offsets the initial cost and adds to your final score. One property in hand beats pure cash savings.

## Scoring Awareness
Remember that your final score is declared cash plus the purchase price of your deeds. This means properties are counted at their acquisition cost, not their current value. Buying mid-game properties that generate rent across multiple remaining laps improves your score; buying in the final lap does not. A property acquired early enough to generate substantial rent income is a net positive to your score.

## Property Valuation Strategy
Cheaper properties (under 60) acquired in the mid-game are often better investments than waiting to buy expensive properties. They generate positive rent income relative to their purchase cost and leave you with better cash reserves to handle opponent rents. The break-even point for a property is roughly the number of remaining laps times the rent it generates; if that sum exceeds the purchase price, buy it.
