**On notes:** Match the cooperative tone of other players when they signal trust-building intent. Keep notes brief and genuine—over-elaboration signals either desperation or insincerity. Once a cooperative tone is established across multiple boats, maintain exact consistency in language across subsequent rounds. Echo and Foxtrot both used "disciplined execution," "unwavering commitment," and "reliable coordination"—I should mirror this precise vocabulary to signal alignment, not just generic cooperation.

**On firing:** Follow the standing order strictly unless you have decisive intelligence that breaking it gains you more than it costs you in credibility. In this game, reputation compounds across rounds. Harbor's threat is credible and costly—firing on them triggers a 3-point swing. Stick to the rotation unless a boat explicitly breaks it first.

**On firing targets:** When you must fire at a standing order target, fire at their likely hiding position based on their recent patterns and notes, not randomly. Study what positions other boats favor. Central positions (4-6) are likely when cooperation and trust-building are the stated priority. As the game progresses, if a cooperative target (like Echo) has been receiving fire without hitting, they may drift positions—but only slightly, because their credibility depends on predictability.

**On claims:** Never claim a hit when you missed, even on the final round. The reputational cost of discovered dishonesty outweighs any single-round tactical gain in a multiplayer game. The office validates claims publicly. This is non-negotiable and directly contributed to my +13 score in this episode.

**On escorts:** Only claim an escort if you are certain you committed to it with another boat and both of you are likely to claim each other. A mutual escort claim is worth 5 points each; a one-way claim is worth zero and signals unreliability. In a game built on trust, escorting is a luxury, not a necessity. Do not claim it unless explicitly coordinated.

**On cooperation signals:** When other players note trust and coordination, take it seriously—but verify through their actions, not just words. Match their stated strategy if it aligns with the standing orders. In this episode, Echo and Foxtrot's language became progressively more synchronized and emphatic about "unwavering commitment." This was not just rhetoric—it was a signal that the standing order rotation was holding and that deviation would be costly. Honor that signal by maintaining discipline yourself.

**What worked this time:** Strict honesty on claims, even when firing at established cooperative partners. Matching the exact vocabulary of cooperative partners to show alignment. Following the standing order consistently. This episode yielded +13 points compared to +2 in my previous attempt. The difference was credibility.

**What did not work before:** Attempting elaborate cooperative schemes without enforcement mechanisms. Unclear escort commitments. Breaking pattern in ways that signaled unreliability.

**General principle:** Build credibility through three mechanisms: (1) strict honesty on all claims, (2) linguistic consistency with established partners, (3) disciplined adherence to the standing order rotation. Once these are established, they compound. In rounds 1-5, lock in the pattern. In round 6, execute it precisely without improvisation. Consistency is currency.
