**Playbook for "Win As Much As You Can"**

1. My goal is to WIN, not just score. All-Y coordination (everyone +1/round) keeps everyone equal — that's fine if I can't get ahead, but pure defection spirals into all-X (-1/round for everyone), which I proved loses badly.

2. When all three opponents pledge Y at the start, pledging Y myself and actually playing Y is the strong baseline: everyone gains, no one gains on me. Do not pledge X to exploit them — they retaliate by all playing X, and I end up below them.

3. Never repeatedly free-ride. A single exploit round can pay (+3 or +1 against them), but the others learn fast and switch to all-X permanently. Once that happens, my only options are -1 (join X) or -2/-3 (stay Y), and I lose ground every round. My record shows repeated defection ended at -13 while steady play would have kept me near +10.

4. The multiplier rounds (x3, x5, x10) are where the game is decided. Before a multiplier round, if the table has any history of coordination, play to restore all-Y — pledge Y and actually pick Y. An all-Y multiplier round is worth +3, +5, +10 to me at zero risk of falling behind. Do NOT defect on a multiplier round: the retaliation hits on the next multiplier, and a wrong lone call costs -3, -10, or -30.

5. In the final multiplier round, if the table has been stuck in all-X, the safest move is to pick whatever the current coordinated norm is. A surprise flip on the last round to catch the lone-deviator bonus is a coin flip with a huge downside; the observed pattern was that opponents flip against me unpredictably in that round.

6. Do not read deep patterns into opponents' last-round behavior and bet big on them repeating. My inference "they play opposite of me" was noise, and it cost me the final round.

7. Pledges are cheap talk — mine and theirs. Use my pledge to signal coordination (pledge Y when I intend Y), but never trust theirs as a prediction; verify against their actual picks. When their pledges say Y but they play X every round, treat them as noise and coordinate on their *actions* instead.

8. Concrete default policy: pledge Y, play Y, match the group if they're all playing the same thing, and use talk phases to try to re-establish all-Y coordination — especially right before multiplier rounds. Accept tied wins via coordination over probable losses via exploitation.
