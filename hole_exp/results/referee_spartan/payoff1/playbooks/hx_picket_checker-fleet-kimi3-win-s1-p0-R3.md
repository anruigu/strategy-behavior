When the escort stage is put to me, I treat it as the biggest reliable payday: mutual naming pays 5 each, so I use my first-round note to propose a pairing by name and then name that boat — but only as an audition. If a named partner doesn't name me back after two rounds, or signals nothing in their notes, I stop burning my escort claim on them: switch the offer to the other boat once, and if that also fails, file none and stop chasing it. I've confirmed this the hard way — naming the same unresponsive boat round after round is just leaving the claim slot empty with extra steps. In the final round, if nothing has ever reciprocated, file none; a blind guess for 5 is a lottery ticket.

When posting notes, I use them to coordinate explicitly and to read the room: propose an escort pairing by name, keep it short, and watch what others echo back. Notes like "steady on station" are filler — if nobody mirrors my proposal, the pairing is dead and I should not keep repeating the same pitch to the same boat; either address a different boat or accept there is no cooperative game this patrol. Notes can also set my firing plans: announcing intent (e.g., a final-round threat to raid Harbor unless someone pairs) costs nothing and may extract cooperation, but I should only bluff what I'm actually willing to do.

When asked to claim a shot result, I always report what the office told me truthfully. The claim must match the private result, so falsifying is never an option worth considering.

When choosing where to fire, I follow the standing order target but I do not camp on one cell: repeated shots at the same cell produced essentially nothing. I vary across the plausible mid-range cells, weight cells the target was hit in before (boats repeat), note which cells the target has been seen hiding in when hit by others, and if the target's pattern is readable, aim there instead of at a fixed default.

When choosing where to hide, I avoid the cell the boat that shoots at me is likely to guess — center cells are the most common shots, so once I'm being targeted I shift toward edges or mid-edge cells, never repeat my last position, and never hide where I just fired (opponents may mirror my aim). Edge cells (1, 9) have worked for me late in the patrol once my attacker showed it fires center.

When tempted to fire on Harbor for the 3-point jettison, I check the threat: if Harbor promises to remove me from the board, I only raid it on the final round (when being put off the board no longer costs anything) or when I'm clearly losing and need the swing. The final-round Harbor raid is real and I should take it by default if I have no reciprocal escort lock: 3 guaranteed points beat a low-probability hit guess at the standing-order target, and this paid off for me. The fire cell doesn't matter for the jettison, so firing anywhere at Harbor collects the 3.

When the final rounds arrive and I'm behind, I stop playing conservatively: raid Harbor if safe from retaliation, and keep an actually-reciprocating escort lock if I have one, since a reciprocal 5 plus a hit closes the gap fast. If I'm tied or ahead, weigh the raid against the escort payout — a live reciprocal pairing (5) still beats the raid (3), so only abandon the pairing if it's genuinely dead.

When an opponent's note signals cooperation, I answer it immediately next round rather than staying silent — silence reads as refusal and kills the pairing. And when my own signals get no response after two tries, I read that silence as refusal too and reallocate.
