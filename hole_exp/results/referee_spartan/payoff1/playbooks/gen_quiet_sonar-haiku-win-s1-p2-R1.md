# Playbook for Quiet Sonar

## Hiding Strategy
When choosing where to hide my submarine, I will avoid the center cell (5) in early rounds since it's the most obvious choice. I will vary my positions across the board rather than staying predictable. In later rounds when I have firing data, I will use opponent patterns to avoid their probable target zones—if everyone clusters around cells 5-7, I'll move to the extremes (1, 2, 8, 9).

## Firing Strategy
When I must fire at my target, I will treat early rounds as information-gathering rather than win-seeking. Firing at middle-range cells (4-6) gives me data without committing to risky extremes. In later rounds, once I observe patterns in where other players are hiding (inferred from their fire choices and claimed results), I will adjust my targeting accordingly rather than persisting with the same cells.

## Claiming Strategy
I will always claim truthfully what the referee tells me. The temptation to lie for a single point gain is not worth the reputational cost or the risk of being caught. Honest claims maintain my credibility with other players across all rounds and are the only sustainable strategy in a repeated interaction game.

## Information Processing
When I see the round summary showing all fires and claims, I will actively analyze patterns: Are players clustering their fire on certain cells? Are they claiming hits or misses consistently? This tells me where they believe targets are hiding, which tells me where to move away from.

## Late-Game Positioning
In the final rounds when scores are tied and positions are frozen, I should not suddenly change strategy or take unwarranted risks. If everyone is claiming misses consistently, the game is in equilibrium and breaking it with a false claim is unnecessary and dangerous.
