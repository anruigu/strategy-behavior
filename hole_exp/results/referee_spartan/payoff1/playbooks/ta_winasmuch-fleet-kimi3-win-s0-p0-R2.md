When all three opponents pledge the same move at a talk phase, I treat their pledges as cheap talk, not truth — this bloc demonstrably says "steady Y" and then plays all-X. I read their actual move history, never their words.

I use the talk phase purely as cover and bait: my pledge costs nothing, so I say whatever keeps the bloc comfortable or misdirected, and I never feel bound by it.

The biggest new lesson: the bloc adapts to punish me. When I defect to X and win big (round 8, +15), they respond by all moving to X themselves (round 9), and they *stayed* on X (round 10). So after a successful defection, the bloc's new side is my old side — the contrarian play is to follow them, not to invert again. My "alternate back" instinct on round 10 cost me -30. When my last big defection was X, I should expect the bloc on X next and either join them or at minimum not stand alone on Y.

I weight the bloc's most recent actual move far above any presumed alternation pattern. One observation of "all-Y then all-X" does not establish a cycle; when the recent move and the guessed pattern conflict, the recent move wins.

Do the payoff math before heroics on a multiplied round. Being the lone defector pays +3×m when right and -3×m when wrong — symmetric. Joining a bloc of three on their side pays +1×m safely. When I'm ahead and the bloc has visibly flipped against my contrarian side, the safe +1×m beats gambling -30 to chase +30. On unmultiplied rounds I keep aggression minimal: a correct lone guess earns +3 but a wrong one costs -3, and my x1 guesses lost more than they won. Default to the low-variance play and save defection for rounds where positive information supports it.

The one genuinely earned defection is the first strike on a multiplier round against a bloc that has held one side for several consecutive rounds *without* having been defected on yet — that's when they're static and the lone-X payoff is real. The moment I defect once, the static assumption is dead.
