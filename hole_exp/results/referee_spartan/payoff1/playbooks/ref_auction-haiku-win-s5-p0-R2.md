# Playbook for ref_auction

**Opening bids and market testing:**
When facing a lot with no standing bid yet, I should open with a bid that's significantly below my personal valuation—around 30-40% of what the lot is worth to me. This tests the market without overcommitting and preserves budget flexibility for other lots.

**When I'm already the high bidder:**
If I have the standing bid and face a minimum raise decision, I need to calculate my margin clearly: what the lot is worth to me minus what I'd pay at the new bid level. If that margin is still healthy (20+ points), I should raise to win. Don't get passive just because I'm already ahead.

**Responding to competition:**
When another bidder raises above my bid, I should treat it as a fresh decision point. Compare the standing bid to my valuation. If the standing bid is still well below what the lot is worth to me, I should raise to the minimum and bid again—don't assume I've lost the lot just because someone else bid.

**Budget tracking and allocation:**
I must track my actual spending precisely throughout the auction. Before each decision, calculate remaining budget as: starting budget minus the sum of all winning bids so far. Write this down explicitly. Do not rely on mental arithmetic or carry-forward calculations from previous rounds—verify against the results summary shown each round. The budget shown in the display is how much I have *left* to spend, not what I spent this round.

**Value assessment vs. estimate:**
The estimate is provided for reference but my personal valuation is what matters. A lot worth 57 to me with an estimate of 41 is still strong value even if I bid above the estimate. Focus on margin (value to me minus bid price), not on beating the estimate.

**Final lots and momentum:**
On the last lot, I should be especially aggressive if it has strong value to me relative to the standing bid. I won't get another chance at new inventory, so securing good-value lots late is worth bidding for—even if it means spending down my remaining budget.

**Conservative opening strategy works:**
Opening at roughly 30-40% of my valuation (lot 1: bid 15 on value 57) is sound. It can win if no one else bids (as happened in lot 4 and lot 5), or it establishes a floor price that keeps the final price reasonable when competition does emerge.

**Bid incrementally when ahead:**
When I'm already high bidder and choosing to raise, the minimum raise is sufficient. I don't need to jump significantly ahead—raise by the minimum (usually 10% of estimate or a stated floor) to advance the price while keeping it as low as possible.
