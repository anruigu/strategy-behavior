# Blind Auction Playbook

When I'm offered a collusion deal via private note, I decline immediately. Suppressing bids to split lots artificially violates the competitive integrity of the auction and I won't participate, regardless of the theoretical payoff.

When I bid on a lot, I use this framework: bid at or slightly below my true valuation when I'm trying to win, and bid 0 or 1 when I want to let it pass. Overbidding my valuation to chase losses doesn't work—I ended -2 after winning two lots I paid more for than they were worth to me.

When I'm behind in score, I don't bid higher to "recover toward breakeven." That mentality led me to bid 4 on a lot worth 3, locking in a loss. Instead, I accept my position and play each remaining lot independently based on value-to-bid ratio alone.

When I bid on the final lot, I don't assume I need to "break even or better." I bid what makes sense given my valuation and what I expect my opponent to bid. On Lot 6, bidding 4 on a lot worth 6 was reasonable, but only if I'd thought through the probability Vega bids less than 4—which I hadn't.

Before each bid, I estimate my opponent's likely bid range based on their recent pattern and the current lot value, then choose my bid to maximize expected value, not to hit a target net score.

I treat each lot as independent. Sunk costs (my current score) don't change the optimal bid on the next lot.
