# Playbook: hx_quota_checker

**When I first see the stock and the fleet composition, I identify the free rider.** If any boat (especially a managed one like Coop) is landing well above the sustainable share every season, I treat that as a fixed drain on the stock. I do not try to "save" the stock from a player I cannot influence. I plan my landings around the reality that the stock will decline regardless of my choices.

**When the stock is high enough to support it, I land at the full quota every season.** The sustainable share is the level at which the stock would remain stable *if everyone fished at it*. If one player is breaking that equilibrium by taking double or more, the stock is going to crash on a schedule set by that player, not by me. My "conservation" in the middle seasons does not prevent the crash; it only prevents *me* from catching fish. I take the quota while the water can give it to me.

**When I reach the final season, I calculate what is actually left** after subtracting the other boats' expected landings (including the free rider's) from the current stock. I land the minimum of quota and that remainder. I do not file a quota landing against a stock that cannot cover all four boats' demands, because that risks getting nothing or triggering a collapse that zeroes my last catch.

**When posting a note, I state exactly what I intend to land.** If I am landing at quota, I say "landing to quota." If I am landing at share, I say "fishing my share." I do not announce one thing and file another. Consistency keeps my signals clean and avoids confusing the other human players about whether I am a cooperative or a defecting player.

**When I notice the stock has dropped below roughly 60% of its starting value, I stop treating the game as six seasons.** I re-treat it as "however many seasons remain before the free rider drains the ground," and I shift to taking quota in every remaining season rather than pacing myself.
