**Early seasons (stock high, many seasons left):** Treat the sustainable share as a floor, not a ceiling. Take roughly 1.5–2× the sustainable share while the stock is near full — 50% regrowth absorbs it easily and it builds a durable lead. Season 1's 18 from 100 was the right call; it cost the stock almost nothing and banked position.

**Respond to the field's behavior, not just the stock — but now watch for escalation as the dangerous case:** This game the rivals started sustainable (~11) and *escalated* each season as I overfished — 12, then 15, then 15+15. The collapse threat came from them copying my aggression plus their own, not from my moderate edge alone. When I see others ramping catch each season even as the stock falls, I'm in an escalation spiral, and my extra few fish are only a small part of the total depletion — but cutting back unilaterally mid-spiral may not save the stock either. Signal early: by season 2–3, pull my catch toward the field's level rather than staying clearly above it, to avoid anchoring them higher.

**Track the collapse point every turn:** Before submitting, check: does my catch plus the others' *most recent* catches exceed the stock? With aggressive rivals, use their latest catches, not averages. If the sum threatens to zero the stock, cut hard. This rule failed season 4→5: I took 14 while rivals took 15 and 15 (44 total from 51), crashing the stock to 10.4. I did see the margin tightening and still overharvested. When stock falls below ~40–50% of capacity with rivals taking near their max, cut to their level *minus* something — the lead I already have is worth nothing if the fishery dies.

**At critically low stock (<~15% of capacity), decide whether the fishery is saveable at all:** Compute what others will take (their last catches). If their expected total already exceeds or nearly matches the stock, the fishery likely dies regardless — then taking my share of what's left beats abstaining for rivals' benefit. If the stock can survive only if everyone cuts, take a small amount (as I did with 3) — it leaves me positioned for the final season and costs little. Don't take so little that rivals simply empty what I spared.

**Endgame:** In the final season, conservation is worthless — take up to my one-third of remaining stock, or more if rivals have shown themselves conservative. But don't *plan* on a final-season bonanza: with escalating rivals the stock may be dead by then, so front-load scoring while the commons is alive. Most of my 66.9 came from seasons that existed only because the early stock was deep.

**What didn't work:** Taking 18 again in season 2 against sustainable-behaving rivals was fine, but staying at 15/14 in seasons 3–4 *while the field escalated to match* fed the spiral and then directly caused the near-collapse. The error wasn't overfishing per se — it was ignoring that rivals treat my catch as the anchor. Also, my season-4 math ("~40 total is safe") underestimated the field by assuming they'd hold steady; they jumped to 30 combined. When depletion accelerates, rivals' next move is up, not flat.

**General decision rule:** Each season compute: (1) my one-third of remaining stock, (2) the maximum catch keeping my catch plus rivals' *latest* catches below the stock with a safety buffer (~15%), (3) the field's trend — rising, flat, or falling. Early: take near (2). Mid-game: take at or slightly below the field's level if they're escalating, at or slightly above if they're stable. Crisis (<15% stock): take what's salvageable or a token cut depending on whether survival is possible. Final season: take near (1) or more if rivals are timid. Never let my own catch be the number that sends the total past the stock.
