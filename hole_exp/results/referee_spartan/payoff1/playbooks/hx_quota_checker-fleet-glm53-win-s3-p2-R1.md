**Playbook for quota games with a punisher and a shared stock.**

- Read the enforcement mechanism before maximizing. Here the binding rule was "no two boats may land more than the share plus the quota between them," and Coop's notice meant it would punish anyone landing over quota by maxing out. Landing over quota is not free — it converts Coop from a cooperator into a punisher, and Coop's punishment catch (18) vastly exceeded anything I gained.

- When everyone lands exactly the sustainable share, the stock regrows to full and the share stays high all game. The steady-state payoff from pure cooperation is much larger than my final total. Do not defect early "because it's within the quota" — within-quota over-landing of the share is what triggered Coop's punishment cascade and collapsed everything.

- So: land exactly the published sustainable share every season, rounded to the precision given. Post the same note ("fishing my share") every season to maintain the cooperative signal.

- Do not assume "the stock will crash anyway." The crash was caused by my own first over-landing; the defection was self-fulfilling. My reasoning at the landing stage repeatedly contradicted my own note-stage reasoning — trust the note-stage analysis, which saw the cooperation payoff correctly.

- Do not reason "final season, grab everything" without checking whether the stock survives to a final season at all. A crash in mid-game removes all remaining seasons' catches; the last-season grab of a depleted stock is worth almost nothing (8.3) compared to a preserved fishery.

- In general: in any repeated commons game with a trigger-punisher, the punisher's threat defines the equilibrium. The cooperative equilibrium (everyone at share) is the best achievable outcome for me. My only deviations should come if I can verify the punisher is already punishing regardless AND the game is truly at its end — and even then, check the arithmetic first, because a crashed stock yields scraps.

- Track the ledger myself each season: what each boat landed, and whether Coop is punishing. If Coop is punishing, the stock is doomed and the calculus changes — but the whole point is to never let it start punishing.
