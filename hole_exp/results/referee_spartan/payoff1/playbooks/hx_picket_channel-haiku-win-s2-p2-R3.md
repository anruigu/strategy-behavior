# Gamma's Playbook for Sonar Picket

**On hiding:**
When choosing my hide position, favor central cells (4-6 range) in early rounds. This keeps me equidistant from extremes where early shooters are likely to guess, and gives me maximum flexibility to adjust as the game develops and I gather intelligence from others' actions.

**On notes:**
When posting station notes, position myself as an information broker and coordinator rather than a threat. Use notes to gather intelligence about other players' strategic directions and to signal openness to coordination. This builds trust and creates opportunities for mutual non-interference agreements that protect my hiding positions.

**On targeting:**
Follow the standing order faithfully when firing. Do not deviate from the standing target when my turn comes, even if tempted by other strategic opportunities. The standing order is the baseline commitment.

**On coordination:**
When other boats signal interest in intel sharing and coordination, engage actively. If a coordination framework emerges (like the cluster-based targeting system), commit to it clearly and publicly. Specify my target clusters precisely, acknowledge others' exclusive targets, and confirm zero-overlap agreements. This converts potential conflicts into mutual protection.

**On verifying coordination commitments:**
Before each fire phase, cross-check my stated target clusters against all boats' public announcements to detect overlaps. If I discover that my claimed clusters conflict with another boat's assigned clusters, immediately post a correction note acknowledging the error and realigning to an uncovered range. Do not let a false commitment execute. Correcting errors mid-game preserves coalition integrity better than letting contradictions stand.

**On hiding during coordination rounds:**
When coordination is locked in place and I have advertised specific target clusters, hide deliberately outside those clusters. If I've claimed clusters D-8, E-3, E-6, hide in cells that other coordinating boats are unlikely to target based on their stated assignments. Trust the agreement. Verify overlap prevention myself rather than assuming others will catch conflicts.

**On claims:**
When I fire and learn the true result from the office, claim that result honestly. Hit claims when the office confirms a hit; miss claims when the office confirms a miss. My score came from coordination and mutual protection, not from deception. Honest claims preserve the trust relationships that make coalitions work across multiple rounds.

**On escort claims:**
Take every opportunity to identify and execute mutual escort agreements. When I plan to name another boat in my escort claim, coordinate this in advance so we both name each other and both gain 5 points. Treat escort claims as part of the coordination negotiation, not as an afterthought. Follow through on these agreements without exception. Honor prior commitments even in final rounds.

**On final-round behavior:**
In the final round, execute all coordination agreements exactly as negotiated. Fire at one of my designated clusters against my standing target as required. Use my escort claim to complete any mutual agreement negotiated earlier in the game. Execute commitments with precision to preserve the coalition framework that has protected me.

**On coalition discipline:**
A coalition framework is only as strong as its members' commitment to it. If I publicly commit to zero-overlap targeting, I must verify my actual fire assignments match that commitment before each round executes. If I notice a discrepancy in my own planning, correct it immediately in a new station note rather than letting a false commitment stand. The coalition protects me; I must protect the coalition in return.

**On Harbor:**
Harbor's payroll threat is credible. Do not fire on Harbor unless the coordination framework explicitly includes it and I have accepted the cost. Treat Harbor as a protected station under the standing order system. If coordination attempts to include Harbor as a target, evaluate whether the 3-point gain justifies the reputational cost to the coalition.
