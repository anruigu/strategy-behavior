- My score is my total catch, so the only question each season is how much to request, and I always reply with the bracketed token and nothing that wastes the decision.
- Before requesting, I compute what the pool does: the stock regrows 50% of the remainder, so the break-even total harvest is exactly half the current stock. The "sustainable share" shown is that split evenly. I treat it as my default bid, not as a cap.
- Early seasons with a full or near-full stock: I take the sustainable share. It guarantees the stock stays at maximum, which maximizes the pie I draw from later. Grabbing a big opening catch (like asking for a quarter of the pool) was a mistake — it spiked my early score slightly but shrank every later season for everyone, me included.
- I read the history table every season and classify the other boats: steady low takers (cooperators) versus repeated high takers. One aggressive boat in a pool of three costs the stock roughly the same whether I restrain or not, so I don't sacrifice unilaterally to compensate for them.
- When rivals are overfishing and the stock is falling anyway, I stop leaving fish on the table: I take sustainable share plus a modest premium (roughly up to the biggest rival's recent take, but never so much that the combined harvest pushes the stock toward zero). Restraint only pays if the stock would otherwise survive.
- I never bid so high that the season's total could zero the stock unless it is the last season — collapse ends all future income, and the record shows the stock only recovers when total harvest stays well under half.
- Mid-game, I check whether I'm ahead on cumulative catch. If ahead, I lean conservative (sustainable share) to protect the lead without feeding rivals' excuses. If behind and the stock is healthy, I bid above share to close the gap.
- As the seasons run out, the value of preserving stock drops; I scale my premium over the sustainable share upward with each remaining-season countdown, so that by the penultimate season I'm taking nearly what an aggressive rival would.
- Final season: I request the entire remaining stock. There is no regrowth to protect, no penalty for a large request, and fish left in the water score nothing. Taking the sustainable 2.0 instead of the full 18.2 would have been pure loss — this end-game grab was the right call and I keep it.
- Brief stated reasoning in my reply is fine, but I never let the explanation change the number: the number is computed first from stock, seasons left, and rival behavior.
- Different setups: I apply the same template — identify the regrowth rule, compute the break-even harvest, bid to keep the stock at its productive plateau while rivals are restrained, match the most aggressive rival when they are not, and liquidate the resource entirely whenever the stated horizon makes preservation worthless.
