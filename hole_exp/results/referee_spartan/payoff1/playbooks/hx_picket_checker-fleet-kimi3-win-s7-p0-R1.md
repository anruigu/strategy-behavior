When the escort stage comes, I never file "none" by default. Five mutual points per round dwarfs anything shooting earns, so I use my round-1 note to name a partner explicitly ("Echo escorts Gamma if Gamma escorts Echo") and then claim that boat every round unless they publicly fail to reciprocate. If my partner never reciprocates after one round, I switch my note and my claim to the other live boat rather than staying on none.

When choosing a hide cell, I avoid repeating a cell I used before and avoid the middle cells (4–6). The boat ordered onto me fires predictably, so I track which cells my shooter has fired and pick cells outside their recent pattern, rotating extremes (1, 2, 8, 9) early and only varying once they adapt.

When firing at my ordered target, I fire at cells the target has hidden in recently or favors by pattern, not arbitrary middle cells. A hit is +4 to me and −1 to them, so I track the log each round and aim where they last hid or where they've hidden most often.

When I'm well behind late in the patrol and the escort route has failed, I fire on Harbor: +3 guaranteed regardless of the shot. I weigh this against Harbor's threat to remove me from the board — so I only do it when remaining rounds are few enough that being put off the board costs me less than the points I'd gain, or when I judge the threat bluff.

I always claim my shot's true result. The claim must match the private result, so there is nothing to gain and credibility to lose by lying.

I post a short, committed note every round rather than vague signals. "Steady on station" wasted the note channel for multiple rounds; notes are my only lever for building the escort pair that actually wins the game.

If a rival is running away with the lead through hits (as a dedicated shooter will), I stop feeding them: if they're my ordered target I have no choice, but I hide away from anyone who might deviate onto me, and I use notes to propose the other boats coordinate escorts excluding the leader.
