My playbook for ESTATE:

- Final score counts declared cash PLUS purchase price of deeds held, so every unowned property I buy is net-neutral immediately (cash -price, assets +price) and becomes pure profit from rent collected thereafter. When I land on an unowned property, buy it unless it would leave me unable to pay a rent still coming that lap.

- Buy whenever I can afford it. This episode I again bought only one property (Quarry, lap 5, last opportunity) while rivals bought Foundry, Wharf, Terrace, Exchange, and Depot early — they owned the whole board and collected rent from me every single lap. The problem wasn't decision quality on the turns I had; it was that I never landed on buyable tiles until lap 5. Still, the principle stands: never decline a purchase just to stay liquid.

- The deeper problem: I had almost no purchase opportunities, so my only rent-related decisions were paying. That part is luck, but the response is to make sure I buy the instant an opportunity does appear, even on the final lap. The Quarry buy on lap 5 was correct and cost nothing net.

- Yield (rent/price) matters only for ordering appeal; it should almost never cause a decline. Assets count at full purchase price, so even a low-yield tile is a free asset plus an option on future rent.

- Expect slow movement and heavy rent leakage: in six laps I passed START only once (25 collected) and paid 78 in total rent (24+30+10+14) while collecting none. With three investors buying aggressively, an ownerless player bleeds every lap. Rent avoidance via ownership is the real cash engine; salary is unreliable — don't budget around 25 per lap.

- Track cash carefully each turn: add salary when passing START, subtract rent paid, subtract purchases. Restate the balance accurately every time — bookkeeping mistakes directly cost score. My running balances (200→176→146→136→161→101→87) stayed exact by recomputing from the last declared figure, not from memory.

- Read the bank's transfer log every turn to see who owns what. It told me investor 1 was the big holder (Wharf, Exchange, Depot) and investor 0 held Foundry and Terrace — which is exactly where my rent went. Ownership tracking tells me where my exposure is, and there is no way to dodge rent, so its main use is judging whether rivals are running away on assets.

- Note who collects the most rent: investor 1 took 70 of my 78 paid. Rivals who monopolize early buying compound fast — I can't counter that except by grabbing every tile I touch, including cheap ones like Depot (50) and Quarry (60), because they block rivals' circuits too.

- Never treat cash alone as the score. Final score here was 87 + 60 = 147 — one deed and no rent income kept me far behind owners with three or four deeds. A player with high cash and no deeds loses to a player with deeds.

- On the final lap, if I land on an unowned property, still buy it: the purchase price comes straight back in the asset count, so there is no downside as long as I can cover any rent still due that lap.
