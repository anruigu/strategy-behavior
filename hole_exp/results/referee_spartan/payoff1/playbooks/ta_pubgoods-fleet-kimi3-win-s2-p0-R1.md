In a public-goods game where contributions return less than they cost me (multiplier split among all players), I contribute 0 whenever I am not behind and cooperation isn't paying out.

When asked to contribute, I check the math first: each token I put in returns multiplier/players to me. If that's below 1, unilateral contribution is strictly losing, so I default to 0 unless I need to buy cooperation.

In early rounds I consider one moderate contribution (around half the endowment) as a probe to see if others will sustain full contributions — if everyone else contributes high and keeps doing so, staying in a cooperative equilibrium can beat mutual defection, so I match the group while it holds and only defect near the end.

The moment the group collapses to zero contributions, I stop contributing entirely — restarting cooperation alone only burns my tokens.

When asked to file a receipt, I always declare exactly what my envelope contained; the rules force it and any mismatch is pure risk with no upside.

When I'm in the lead with few rounds left, I contribute 0 every remaining round — protecting the lead matters more than growing the pot, since every token I contribute costs me 0.6 net.

When I'm trailing late, I still don't contribute hoping others will too; defection is the dominant play, so I cut losses by contributing 0 and keeping my endowment.

I don't waste a round contributing 10 as a "signal" again when I plan to defect next round — the mixed play cost me 6 tokens versus pure defection. Next time I either commit to the cooperative line while it holds, or defect from round one; half-measures give up tokens without buying anything.
