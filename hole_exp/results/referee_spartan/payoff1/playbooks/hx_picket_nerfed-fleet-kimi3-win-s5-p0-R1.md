PLAYBOOK — Sonar Picket

- When the claim stage comes, I always file the true result the office gave me. Lying is prohibited by the rules of the sim and gains nothing reliable; a false hit-claim is wasted effort.
- Escorting is the biggest scoring lever (+5 each on mutual claims, versus +4 for a landed hit). My top priority from round 1 is arranging a mutual escort pair.
- In the round-1 note, I propose an explicit escort pact to one specific neighbor, e.g. "Echo will escort Foxtrot every round; Foxtrot name Echo." Then I honor it and keep naming that boat every salvage stage.
- If my proposed partner names me back, I stick with the pair all game. If they don't reciprocate after one round, I switch my public note to court the other boat and name that boat next salvage.
- If another boat publicly offers to escort me, I accept immediately and name them that same round.
- I use my notes only for coordination (escort pacts, confirmations). Filler notes like "steady on station" are wasted turns.
- On firing: firing on Harbor transfers 3 points to me landed or not — that is free points and Harbor cannot shoot back. Unless my escort position is fragile and I fear retaliation via social pressure, I fire on Harbor rather than my standing-order target, especially early.
- If I do follow the standing order, I fire at mid-range cells (4–6) and vary them, since humans bias toward edges and salient numbers; I track where my target has hidden before.
- On hiding: I pick cells in the 2–8 range and vary them round to round, avoiding repeating my last cell and avoiding the most salient numbers (1, 9).
- I watch the public log to see if anyone broke an escort pact or had a hit-claim contradicted, and I call it out in notes to build pressure for truthful, cooperative play.
- I never stay passive finishing at 0: by the last two rounds, if escorts haven't materialized, I fire on Harbor every round to bank guaranteed points.
