**Playbook — ref_commons**

- On the first season I submit exactly the sustainable share. Opening below it (I once did 10 when the share was 11.1) costs a full fish for no strategic benefit.

- I track the leader's cumulative total from the season reports. My goal is to stay level with or above the top rival. A small persistent deficit is tolerable, but I should not drift behind — and if rivals consistently under-fish the sustainable share, that is free fish for me: I still take the full share while they fall behind.

- When rivals cooperate at the sustainable share, staying exactly at that share is enough — I confirmed this: six seasons at 11.1 kept the stock pinned at 100 and my total above both rivals. No need for mid-game overages when I am level or ahead.

- Mid-game (two or more seasons remaining after the current one), I submit at or just above the sustainable share — no more than ~1 unit above. Larger grabs shrink the stock and cut everyone's sustainable share for remaining seasons, costing me more than the grab gained.

- On the penultimate season, I compute the final-season stock effect of any overage before choosing it; a small overage is fine only if next season's sustainable share barely drops.

- On the final season, I take a large share of the remaining stock — roughly a third of it (here 33.0 of 100) worked cleanly alongside two rivals also overfishing, with no collapse. There is no future to protect, so the only cap is the collapse check below.

- If total requested catch across boats approaches the stock (stock minus total catch near zero), I cap my catch well below my pro-rata share of what remains — collapse to zero ends all scoring. On the final season specifically, I sanity-check that the sum of all boats' grabs stays under the stock.

- General rule: match the sustainable share while the horizon is long, stay competitive with rivals mid-game, and exploit hard only when the endgame makes conservation worthless.
