# Harbor Customs Playbook

## Declaration Strategy

When declaring, calculate both routes' expected payoffs and declare your true total value. The -40 penalty for underdeclaring (when your largest crate exceeds your declaration) is severe enough that honest declaration is nearly always safer than trying to game the system.

When you have weak crates (small values), declare conservatively to avoid the flag penalty—there's no upside to declaring high if you can't cover it. When you have strong crates, you can declare higher without fear of the flag.

Study the table's declaration patterns across rounds. If declarations tend to cluster in a range (like 55-65), stay within that range unless you have a compelling reason to deviate. Outlier declarations draw attention and offer no advantage.

Adjust your declaration based on your current position relative to the leader. If you're within 70 points, you can afford moderate declarations. If you're 100+ behind with 2+ rounds remaining, you need to declare higher to swing scoring in your favor—but only if your crates support it.

## Route Selection

When you declare your true value and have solid crates, take the main route. It is simpler, more predictable, and rarely the wrong choice when your math is sound.

The night route should only tempt you when your declared value is moderate-to-high and your smallest crate is genuinely small relative to your total. Running the night route with, say, a declared value of 65 and crates of 40, 24, 19 means losing the 19—you keep 63 crate value and pay 15% duty instead of 30%. Check whether that actually beats main before committing.

In the final round, default to main route unless you've done the math and night clearly wins. The variance of night is not worth chasing in a close endgame.

## Scoring and Positioning

Do not obsess over table position mid-game. Focus on maximizing your own score each round. Chasing leaders with reckless declarations costs more than it gains.

When reviewing your route choice, recognize that declaring high but playing it safe with main route is your baseline. You defaulted to this repeatedly and finished competitive (second place overall). Stick with it.
