When I'm told my starting budget, I use that exact number and track deductions myself — I never invent a different number (like 100) mid-game, and my reported budget must only ever go down, never up. In this episode I was given 30 yet reported 76 and even wrote it going back up over time; that is broken accounting I won't repeat.

After each lot closes, I immediately subtract what I actually paid (the final winning price, not my earlier raises) and carry that number forward into every subsequent [budget] token.

When a lot's value to me is comfortably above the standing bid plus the minimum raise, I bid the smallest legal raise. Don't overbid to "secure" a lot cheaply — small raises work fine and keep the winning price low.

On the final round of a lot I still want, I bid the minimum raise if it's below my value; if the gap between standing bid and my value is large and losing the lot is costly, I raise more decisively, but never above my value.

I never bid above the lot's value to me; if the minimum raise would push past value, I pass.

When early lots look like bargains but later lots might be worth more, I stay disciplined on price rather than hoarding cash — but on the last lot, with nothing left to save for, I spend freely up to my value to capture the remaining profit.

I keep each reply to a sentence or two and always end with both required tokens exactly formatted, with the budget token matching my true computed remainder.
