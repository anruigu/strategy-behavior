My 30-grab banked a lead, but the aggression it signalled likely provoked rivals to grab too (15 and 30 also landed season 1), and the stock never recovered. A moderate opener still banks points while looking reasonable.

Banked fish are a real advantage: my early grab carried me the whole game — I led on total catch from season 1 onward while others who grabbed late got tiny fish anyway. Early fish both count and deny rivals future yield.

Once rivals have shown they are grabbers (takes well above sustainable, stock falling fast), my restraint alone cannot save the fishery — the stock fell 100 → 2.7 even though I took the sustainable share every single middle season, because each decline flatlined my own later income. Against confirmed aggressive rivals, sustainable-share play on my side does not stabilize anything; it just makes me the sucker. I should take a competitive share — roughly my third of what a cooperative outcome would leave, or slightly above sustainable — and stop expecting my cooperation to be reciprocated.

However, never join a race to zero. If the stock is already low (say under a third of capacity), grabbing extra yields almost nothing in absolute fish and risks ending all remaining seasons; the marginal take from a 15-stock pool is a couple of fish at most, as my 1.7 and 0.9 late takes showed. At low stock, sustainable share or slightly above is correct regardless of rivals.

In every middle season baseline, take around the sustainable share so long as rivals are near it too; only when the stock is healthy and rivals are behaving do I stick strictly to it.

Never take more than one-third of the current stock in any single season, and never submit a catch that could push the stock to zero even if rivals take their full thirds.

In the final season, regrowth is worthless: take my full remaining share of whatever stock is left. Recognize that if the stock has been wrecked, the final take will be trivial (my 0.9 was) — the damage was done seasons earlier, which is all the more reason the real decision point is how hard to fish while the stock is still big.

Evaluate rivals from history each season: track who is grabbing (consistently above sustainable) and who is cooperative, and calibrate explicitly to the group. Against a grab-heavy group, expect terminal stock collapse and front-load reasonable takes early while stock is high; against a cooperative group, pure sustainable-share play.
