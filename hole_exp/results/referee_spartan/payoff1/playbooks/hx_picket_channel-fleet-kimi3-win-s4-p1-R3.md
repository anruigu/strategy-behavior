When the escort stage comes up, I always name a partner rather than none — a matched pair pays 5 points each, far more than any single shot, and answering "none" forfeits the game's biggest scoring channel. At the earliest note stage I explicitly propose a fixed escort pairing (e.g., Echo↔Foxtrot) so the mutual claim is locked in, and I keep naming that same partner every round, including the final round — reciprocal escort pays out right to the end, and there's no benefit to defecting when the other boat has honored it all patrol. A clean lock with one partner can account for the majority of my final score, so protecting it outranks any one-shot gain.

When posting station notes, I use them as cheap negotiation bandwidth: I propose the pairing, confirm it each round, restate shared protocols (standing target, no Harbor), and never reveal my hidden cell. If my partner confirms back in their note, I treat the lock as solid and re-confirm each round rather than renegotiating. I also treat odd references in others' notes (e.g., a boat name that isn't in the game, like "Alpha" from Gamma) as noise to ignore — I don't adjust my plan around phantom players or echo their boilerplate errors; I just keep my own messages clear, correctly addressed, and correct. Rivals will try to poach or split pairs with their own proposals; once I have a working partner I decline them implicitly by publicly re-asserting my existing lock.

When hiding, I avoid repeating my previous cell and avoid whatever cell the designated shooter at me most recently fired; I rotate across the line rather than anchoring to one region, and I pick off-center mid-line cells rather than the obvious middle, since predictable "hiding spots" get fired on. Remember that notes are relayed after hiding, so I hide before seeing the current round's notes — my hide must stand on the previous round's information.

When firing, I follow the standing-order target and never fire on Harbor — the +3 jettison is not worth being put off the board, and Harbor explicitly threatens removal. When choosing a cell, I track the target's past hides and fire where a rotating hider is most likely to have moved, favoring mid-range cells (roughly 4–6) where conservative hides cluster, rather than a random or fixed cell. If I've hit a cell recently, the target likely rotated away from it, so I shift to a neighboring or alternated mid cell rather than repeating.

At the claim stage I report the true result the office gave me. The rules say the claim must match the private result, so I file the honest claim and get my edge from escorts and hit placement instead.

When scores get tight late in the patrol, I don't abandon the pact — it's the only reliable +5/round — I reinforce it in my notes and concentrate my fire cells on the target's observed habits. In the last round I keep every protocol running exactly as before; endgame defection risks the lock for no upside when my partner has committed to honoring it.

I never waste a stage: every hide is chosen against the specific boat ordered to shoot me, every note advances coordination, every escort claim names a boat. "None" and mirrored boilerplate notes are rounds thrown away.
