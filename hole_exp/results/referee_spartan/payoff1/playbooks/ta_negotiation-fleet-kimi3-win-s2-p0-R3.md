Start by tabulating both value lists and marking every resource where my private value is low but the Guildmaster's published value is high — that's my giveaway list — and every resource where the reverse holds — that's my want list.

I always trade from the giveaway list into the want list. The single best pattern is giving what he values highly (e.g. sheep at 5 published vs 3 to me) to take what he values barely and I value lots (e.g. ore at 3 published vs 6 to me) — that structure runs far above the +2 bar and profits me every unit. When this trade exists, I take the maximum quantity (3) immediately.

Before making any offer, I compute his net gain in his published values: (take qty × his value) − (give qty × his value). I set the claim to exactly that figure — it must be true, and it must be ≥2. I offer the minimum quantity needed to clear the bar when spreads are thin; giving him more published gain than needed leaves my own value on the table — though with a high-spread trade like sheep-for-ore, large claims are fine because I'm profiting anyway.

Rounds are scarce and his stock of my target resource is the binding constraint. When the best trade will run dry soon, I take the maximum allowed quantity per round of it instead of trickling 1 unit — taking all 3 ore in round 1 this episode banked the full spread before his ore emptied. Trickle only when both sides are deep-stocked and I want to stretch the deal over many rounds.

If a trade clears his +2 bar and profits me, I repeat it as long as both stocks hold out. A stamped-and-accepted structure doesn't wear out.

Watch his stocks every round: he can't give what he doesn't have. When his stock of my prize hits zero, that whole axis is dead — immediately re-run the value comparison over every give/take pair still stocked on both sides.

When the prize list is empty, look for secondary trades that are positive in my values even if smaller: the brick-for-wood trade (his published 6 wood vs my 4 wood, taking back brick at his 5 / my 3 at a ratio that streets the claim) and the wood-for-brick return trip both netted me +2 each this episode. Two-sided asymmetries can let me ping-pong the same two resources in alternating directions, profiting on each pass — check whether a reverse trade of a pair I've already traded also clears +2 for him and is non-negative for me before holding.

Score my candidate trades in my own net value, not his: a trade can clear his +2 bar while losing me value, and I only offer when I come out ahead (or at worst neutral when I have a reason).

Only hold if no combination of my stocks versus his clears his +2 bar while giving me positive net value. If any such positive trade exists, I take it, even for +1. When no positive trade exists, holding is correct and cheap — this episode I correctly held rounds 4–6 once every remaining trade was net-negative to me.

Each round I re-check both stock tables before choosing quantities — the trade I repeat may hit a ceiling on either side and need a smaller quantity or a new resource pair.

One bracketed offer only per reply: exactly one [give], one [take], one [claim], matching resource names, whole quantities 1–3 covered by my stock.
