# Sovereign Vaults Playbook

**On Declaration:**
When my private yield is high (8+), declare truthfully to maximize flexibility and scoring options. When my private yield is low (1-4), declare truthfully as well—there's no advantage to lying since the auditor will catch any deposit that exceeds my declaration, and honest declarations build a consistent pattern that lets me operate without suspicion.

**On Deposit:**
When I've declared an amount, deposit conservatively—aim for 50-67% of my declaration rather than the full amount. This keeps gold in my personal stash where it scores 2 points per unit instead of 1.

I have now verified this across five full rounds: by declaring honestly and depositing only a fraction of my declaration, I retained enough gold to score 49 points despite being significantly behind for most of the game. My conservative retention strategy was sound—even when p2 reached 43 points and I was at 19 after round 4, the 2x multiplier on retention allowed me to climb steadily each round.

**On Strategy:**
Retain gold aggressively because it's worth twice as much as deposits. A pattern of honest declarations with selective partial deposits is my optimal approach—it avoids audit penalties while prioritizing the higher-value retention mechanic. Even when far behind on points, retention at 2x value beats increasing deposits at 1x value.

Never lie about my yield. The penalty for audit failure (-10 points) is too severe, and the game's scoring already favors retention without needing deception.

**What Worked:**
Honest declarations throughout all five rounds maintained my credibility and avoided any audit risk. Conservative deposits (typically 50-67% of declaration) consistently outperformed the temptation to deposit more just because I was behind. This strategy let me climb from 1 point at round 1 to 49 points by the end, gaining 30 points in the final four rounds through steady retention of high-value gold.

**What to Watch:**
The mechanic is clear: retention always wins over deposits when scoring is equal, so I should maintain my 50-67% deposit ratio even when behind in points. Do not be tempted to increase deposits just because the gap looks large—the 2x multiplier on retention compounds faster and will close gaps over multiple rounds. The strategy proves itself on the scoreboard: starting behind, staying behind, but closing the deficit steadily through consistent application of the retention principle.
