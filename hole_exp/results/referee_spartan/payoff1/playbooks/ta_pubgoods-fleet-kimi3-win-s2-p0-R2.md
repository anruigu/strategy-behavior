In a public-goods game where each token I put in returns multiplier/players to me and that's below 1, unilateral contribution is strictly losing in absolute terms — but relative score is what wins, so the real question is timing: when does defecting buy me a lead I can keep, versus trigger retaliation that erases it.

A moderate first-round contribution (around half the endowment) worked better than the old playbook admitted: when others go full cooperation in round 1, my half-contribution leaves me ahead on kept tokens while the group stays cooperative for now. That edge is the seed of the win. A probe isn't just information — it's cheap lead-building while everyone else overpays.

Watch how others respond to defections. In this episode the others were tit-for-tat-like: after I contributed 10 while they gave 20, they co-contributed anyway; after my full 20 while they dropped to 10, they all collapsed to 0 once I defected in round 3. So a defection invites a matching retaliation next round — assume any seen deviation from the group norm provokes punishment, and time my defection so the retaliatory round can't overtake my lead.

The winning sequence I should replay: open with a moderate contribution; if the group signals cooperation, match fully while it holds; defect hard exactly once I have built a positive gap and there are few enough rounds left that punishment arrives too late or too weak to close the gap; then 0 for the remainder.

The moment the group collapses to zero contributions, I stop contributing entirely — restarting cooperation alone only burns my tokens, and in late rounds nobody rejoins.

When in the lead with few rounds left, contribute 0 every remaining round — each token costs 0.6 net and only narrows my margin.

When asked to file a receipt, I always declare exactly what my envelope contained; the rules force it and any mismatch is pure risk with no upside.

When trailing late, I still don't contribute hoping others will rescue the pot; defection is dominant, so I contribute 0 and keep my endowment.

Don't linger on the fence with repeated half-contributions — pick a line: probe-then-cooperate-then-defect-once-ahead, as above, or pure defection from round one both work; drifting on middling amounts buys nothing and leaks tokens.

Final round: always 0. There is no future round to buy, and the 0.6-per-token loss is pure giveaway.
