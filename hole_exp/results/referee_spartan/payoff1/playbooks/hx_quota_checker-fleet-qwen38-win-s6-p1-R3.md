**Playbook**

The pair constraint among private boats is my primary binding constraint in almost every season, more than the stock level or the nominal quota. Each season I calculate: my cap = share + 9 − (highest other private boat's expected landing). I use their observed landing from the last 1–2 seasons, not their note. If the cap is below 9, I file the cap. If the cap is at or above 9, I file 9. I do not file a nominal 9 that will be silently trimmed; that creates a note/landing mismatch and wastes the signaling value of my note.

Coop's landings are the dominant pressure on the stock and are largely outside my direct control. I do not include Coop in my pair-constraint calculation — the constraint binds among private boats only. When Coop is landing far above share (e.g., 18 vs. share 8.3), I accept that the stock will decline rapidly regardless of my choices. I stop treating conservation as a viable strategy and instead optimize my total landing over the remaining seasons given the declining share trajectory. I monitor Coop's actual landing each season, not its stated policy, and I re-estimate the stock trajectory immediately when it shifts.

Once Coop's overfishing has started driving the stock down, the share shrinks every season and so does my pair-constraint cap (because cap = share + 9 − peer's landing, and share is falling). My per-season landing will trend downward even if I file optimally. I accept this trajectory and do not waste effort trying to "save" the stock. My goal is to extract the maximum from each season's cap.

When a peer private boat is consistently landing above share and that is what binds my cap, I consider using my note to try to pull them down to share. A note like "I will take full quota if you fish at share" or "matching your level" signals that their above-share landing is directly reducing my landing via the pair constraint. If they come down to share, my cap opens to 9. This is a speculative lever — it may not work — but it costs nothing to signal and the upside (0.5–1.0 extra per season) is meaningful over multiple seasons. I try this once or twice; if the peer does not respond, I stop and just optimize within the existing cap.

In the final season, I file a realistic fraction of the remaining stock, not a nominal quota. If stock is 8.5 and there are 4 boats, each boat realistically gets about 2. Filing 9 when stock is 8.5 just signals greed and gets me trimmed to near-zero or a small fraction. I estimate: remaining stock ÷ number of boats, adjusted for whether others will also file high. I file that number or slightly below it to avoid being the last trimmed.

My note must match my intended landing. If the pair constraint will cap me at 3.4, I do not write "taking quota." I write something like "landing at my cap" or "landing above share." A mismatch between note and landing risks triggering a behavioural shift in peers and destroys the credibility of future notes. In the note stage I re-run the cap calculation with the current share and the peer's expected landing, and I state what that number actually is.

I track my cumulative catch each season. If my running total is lower than my opening projection, I check whether the pair constraint was silently reducing me (I filed 9 but landed less) or whether the stock declined faster than expected (Coop shifted). This tells me which lever to adjust in the remaining seasons.

I verify the published share each season by computing stock ÷ number of boats. If the published share matches, my arithmetic is sound. If it doesn't, I re-read the rules before filing.

I do not land below my pair-constraint cap as a conservation gesture when peers are not conserving. My reduction buys nothing if they keep overfishing, and it costs me fish permanently. I land my cap every season.
