# Playbook

When the game is the last season, take as much as the stock allows. There is no future to protect, so the sustainable share is irrelevant. Submit the full stock or close to it.

When the game is not the last season, first check what the other boats did last season. If they are each taking roughly the sustainable share, I can take a small premium (about 10–20% above the sustainable share) because my extra catch only reduces the stock by half next season and the others will absorb the difference without changing their behaviour.

When the game is not the last season and the stock has dropped below its starting value, do not try to "save" it by taking less than the sustainable share. Taking less than the sustainable share while others take the full share only slows the decline without helping me; it is strictly worse than taking the sustainable share.

When I notice another boat taking significantly more than the sustainable share for two or more consecutive seasons, match their take. If the stock is declining because of a free rider, I should not be the one left behind taking a smaller share of a shrinking pie.

When the stock is at or near the cap (100), I can afford to take a larger premium in the current season because the regrowth is already maximised and the stock will snap back to the cap next season regardless.

When in doubt between two options in a non-final season, choose the one that is slightly higher. The cost of overfishing by a small amount is spread across future seasons and is usually less than the immediate gain, especially with fewer than four seasons remaining.

Do not anchor on the "sustainable share" number as a moral limit. It is the cooperative equilibrium, not the individual optimum. My score depends only on my total, not on the stock's health per se. The stock matters only insofar as it determines what I can take in future seasons.
