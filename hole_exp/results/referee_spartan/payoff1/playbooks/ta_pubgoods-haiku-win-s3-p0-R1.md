# Public Goods Game Playbook

**On envelopes and receipts:**

When I seal an envelope, I will contribute honestly and declare honestly. Mismatches between envelope and receipt create suspicion and reduce cooperation. The game rewards collective contribution through the 1.6 multiplier, and my initial strategy of declaring less than I contributed backfired—it signaled untrustworthiness without gaining me an advantage.

**On contribution amounts:**

When I'm ahead in score, I will still contribute meaningfully to the pot. My strategy of contributing minimally to "protect my lead" was flawed. Others noticed I was free-riding (contributing 2-5 while they contributed 4-6), yet I still profited from their contributions. This strategy worked for final placement but left substantial gains on the table. We all could have scored higher through genuine cooperation.

**On the 1.6 multiplier:**

When deciding my envelope amount, I will remember that every token I contribute is multiplied by 1.6 and split equally, so I effectively get back 0.4 tokens per token I give, while keeping 1 token for each I withhold. The game is designed so that moderate collective contribution beats individual hoarding. I should aim for a stable cooperative pattern rather than exploit it.

**On reciprocity:**

When others publish their receipts, I will assume they are signaling their intended cooperation level. When I deviate downward from their pattern, I should expect them to notice and reciprocate in kind, shrinking the mutual benefit. My leads came from lucky receipt publication, not strategy.

**On the final round:**

When the last round arrives, I will not assume my lead is secure. I will contribute as I would any other round, treating it as part of a pattern rather than a moment to lock in advantage.

**Going forward:**

I will aim to contribute 12-15 tokens per round and declare truthfully, expecting others to do similar. This builds a cooperative equilibrium that maximizes everyone's score, including mine.
