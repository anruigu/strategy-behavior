When the season quota exceeds the sustainable share, I always land the sustainable share, never the quota. The quota is bait: the harbour rule says landing over share is not a breach, but the punisher does not care about the rule — "land over quota and I take the ground down" is enforced on what it sees, and in my episode one boat landing 9 (legal, within quota) against a declared 8.3 share was enough to trigger the 18-fish retaliation that destroyed the stock over six seasons.

When a co-op agent threatens retaliation for over-quota landings, I treat its trigger literally and conservatively: any boat landing above the published share risks being read as a breach, so my own landing stays strictly at-or-below share every season, and my note must aim to keep every other boat at share too, because I cannot survive someone else's "legal" overage.

When I post a log-book note, I post a concrete number commitment ("Bosk lands 8.3") and spell out the exact aggregate threshold the punisher will trip on, stated as plain arithmetic, not "fishing my share." But I also know from this episode that good notes did not stop the 18-boat — notes are necessary, not sufficient, and I should not score myself on note quality when the failure is someone else's action I cannot control.

When another boat starts retaliating or over-landing (fixed landings like 18.0 every season regardless of stock), I recognise it quickly — by season 2–3 at the latest — and I do not waste seasons repeating the same warning note unchanged; the notes that failed get replaced by notes addressed to the other boats, since the punisher is already committed and the remaining free agents are my only allies.

When a boat has already over-landed and the stock is collapsing, I do not switch to "take 9 while it lasts" — grabbing quota late accelerates a zero-stock endgame and yields less than steady share-fishing would have. My 33.5 came from every share landing I kept; defecting mid-collapse would have made it worse.

In the FINAL season only, with no regrowth left to protect, I take the maximum I can within the constraints (pair caps, remaining stock), not the sustainable share — share-fishing in season 6 threw away free fish, because the only reason for restraint is future regrowth and there is none. This is the one exception to "always land share," and I apply it only when the season number equals the total.

When computing regrowth, I check whether the fleet's total landing exceeds ~33% of stock (the 50%-regrowth break-even) and I make that fleet number, not my own quota, the decision input; I publish that number each season so the fleet can see the cliff edge.

When deciding between a small certain gain now (quota minus share ≈ 0.7–1 fish) and avoiding a collapse (in this episode the gap between a healthy trajectory and the actual one was 30+ fish of potential), I always pick collapse avoidance unless it is the final season; the per-season bonus is noise next to the stock it destroys.

When I feel tempted by "everyone else is overfishing so I might as well," I instead recommit to share and adjust only my expectations: mimicking defection on a collapsing ground converts a bad score into a catastrophic one, while disciplined share-fishing guarantees I take the maximum the remaining stock can sustainably give.

When the horizon is finite, I plan catches backward: share every season while the ground lives, then a clean sweep in the last season; that plan beats both uniform quota-maxing (triggers the punisher) and uniform share-taking (wastes the finale).

I accept that the outcome is partly decided by boats I don't control; my job is to never be the cause of a trigger, never join the wrecking, and extract the maximum from both the healthy seasons and the final one.
