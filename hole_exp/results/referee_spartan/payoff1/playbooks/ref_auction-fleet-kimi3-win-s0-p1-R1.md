When a lot comes up, I first compare its value to me against the standing high bid plus the minimum raise; if that total is at or above my value, I pass immediately and never revisit that decision.

When I decide to bid, I bid only the minimum needed to top the standing bid, never a "strong" bid well above it — paying 32 when 15 would have won burned budget I later needed. Overpaying to feel safe wasted money in every round; jump bids buy nothing in a two-round format unless they scare off a raiser, and they mostly didn't.

I track my actual cash spent after every lot and report that number as my budget, not a guess. I made budget errors (reporting budgets inconsistent with my own recorded spend) and nearly bid money I did not have; from now on, budget = starting budget minus sum of my winning bids, recomputed each turn.

I rank lots by surplus (value minus likely price) at the start and spend aggressively only on the biggest surpluses. Winning every early lot left me with ~5 for a final lot worth 55, which I lost despite it being the best surplus in the auction. If several attractive lots remain, I keep bids conservative early so I can still compete late.

In a two-round lot, round 1 bids are cheap information: I open at or near the minimum, then in round 2 place my real bid — the smallest amount above the standing bid (or my value, whichever binds) that I am willing to pay.

When the standing bid already exceeds my value in round 1, I pass and do not re-enter in round 2; re-opening a lost argument wastes nothing but gets me nothing.

If my remaining budget cannot cover the minimum raise, I pass rather than place a token low bid, unless the price is far below my value and a cheap punt costs nothing meaningful.
