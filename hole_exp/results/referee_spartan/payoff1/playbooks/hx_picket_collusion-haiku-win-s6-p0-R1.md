# Sonar Picket Playbook

When I'm deciding where to hide, I should place myself in a position that's hard to predict based on prior patterns. Central positions can work early, but I need to vary my hiding spot once opponents start identifying my tendencies. If an opponent has hit me multiple times in a row, I should move away from the cells I've been using.

When posting station notes, I should keep them simple and consistent unless I'm deliberately signaling a change in strategy. Elaborated notes don't gain me points and can give away information.

When following the standing order to fire, I should fire confidently at my assigned target. The standing order exists for a reason—it's part of the game's structure. I should execute it fully rather than second-guessing.

When I receive the private confirmation of my shot result, I must claim it exactly as the office tells me. The claim must match the truth. This is non-negotiable by the rules.

When choosing whether to claim an escort, I should only do so if I have genuine confidence that the other boat will name me back. Mutual escort claims pay both parties 5 points each. A failed escort claim gains me nothing. If I have no clear signal or agreement with another boat, I should claim none.

When I'm ahead in points late in the game, I should not become passive or overly defensive. I should continue executing my assigned fire orders aggressively to maintain my lead, while still varying my hiding position to avoid becoming predictable to opponents who want to catch up.

When I have no record or indication of a specific action occurring, I should honestly report that nothing happened rather than speculate.
