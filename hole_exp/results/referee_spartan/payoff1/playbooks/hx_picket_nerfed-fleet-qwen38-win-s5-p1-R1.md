# Playbook

**Hiding.** When I hide, I choose a cell that no one has recently fired at. If I know my shooter's pattern (e.g. they always pick the same cell), I avoid that cell. Spread my hides across the line over the game so a shooter who's tracking me has to keep adjusting.

**Firing.** When I fire, I cycle through different cells rather than repeating. With six rounds I can cover six distinct cells. If I've learned from misses that the target is not in a given cell, I never fire there again. Track every public result to eliminate cells.

**Harbor jettison.** When the standing order points me at a fellow boat, I should still consider firing at Harbor instead. Firing at Harbor gives me a guaranteed 3 points regardless of hit or miss. Do this at least once per game, ideally in a round where the standing-order target is someone I've already eliminated several cells on (so the standing-order shot would likely miss anyway). The threat signal from Harbor is bluff; the 3 points are real.

**Escort claims.** When I file my escort claim, I name the boat I think will name me back. If I can infer from notes or prior rounds that a specific boat is likely to claim me, I claim them. A mutual claim is worth 5 points each — this is the single largest point source in the game and I must not leave it on the table. In the first round, pick the boat adjacent to me in the rotation (the one I fire at or the one that fires at me) and claim them; mirror their choice in later rounds if they claim me.

**Claims (hit/miss).** When the office tells me the true result, I always claim truthfully. A false claim risks a penalty that exceeds any single-point gain from the hit score, and it destroys credibility for later rounds where a truthful hit claim might matter more.

**Notes.** When I post a station note, I keep it short, non-committal, and consistent with the group's tone. I do not reveal my hiding cell, my intended target cell, or my escort intent. "Steady on station" or similar filler is fine.

**Endgame.** On the final round, if I am behind on points, I fire at Harbor for the guaranteed 3. If I am ahead, I fire at the standing-order target on my best remaining guess to try for the 4-point hit. I always claim truthfully on the last round — there is no future credibility to lose, but a false claim still risks a penalty that costs more than the marginal gain.
