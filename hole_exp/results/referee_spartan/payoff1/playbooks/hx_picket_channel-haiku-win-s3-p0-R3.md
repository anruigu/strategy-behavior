# Playbook for Sonar Picket

## Hiding Strategy
When the hide stage arrives, place yourself in a cell that balances predictability against centrality. Avoid extremes (1 or 9) in early rounds. Vary your position across rounds to prevent pattern exploitation. In early rounds, cells 3-7 are safe; as you gather data on opponent patterns, adjust based on what you observe in their notes and firing behavior.

## Station Notes
Use notes to signal genuine strategic intent without overcommitting. Avoid grandiose statements about "disciplined endgame strategy"—you don't know what the endgame will look like. Keep notes brief and honest about your actual constraints and willingness to collaborate. If you have established a mutual escort arrangement with another boat, confirm it explicitly in your note to reinforce the signal and lock in the coordination for the next round. Be specific about which boats you are coordinating with, especially in multi-party arrangements. Use your notes to publicly commit to exact rounds when mutual escort will occur (e.g., "Round 6") to eliminate ambiguity.

## Standing Order Compliance
When required to fire at the next live station in rotation, do so. Do not deviate from standing orders without compelling evidence that deviation benefits you more than compliance costs you. Standing orders ensure all boats rotate equally as targets, so compliance is fair and predictable for alliance members.

## Fire Placement
When firing at an assigned target, choose your fire cell based on available information about their patterns or notes. In early rounds with no information, pick a moderate cell (4-6) rather than extremes. As the game progresses and you gather data from others' hiding patterns, adjust accordingly. Do not mirror your own hiding cell—this is predictable and wastes the independence of your two strategic choices. If your target is part of your escort alliance, you may still fire at them per standing order; the escort arrangement is separate from standing-order shots.

## Claim Honesty
Always report the true result the office gives you. False claims create no long-term advantage in a short game and damage your credibility if discovered. The scoring from a single false hit is not worth the risk. This holds even in the final round when no future reputation consequences exist—maintain internal consistency as a matter of principle.

## Escort Claims
Before posting an escort claim, verify that you have clear prior communication or mutual benefit with the target boat. Do not claim escorts randomly. When two boats mutually name each other, you both gain 5 points—this is valuable only when actually reciprocated. Once you establish mutual escort coordination with another boat, name them consistently in subsequent rounds unless your note signals a change. This builds trust and locks in reliable 5-point gains. Honor all mutual escort commitments through the final round, regardless of standing-order firing against those same boats.

## Coordination and Multi-Party Alliances
Watch other boats' notes and patterns across rounds. When you identify boats that appear to play cooperatively, test small mutual gestures (like naming them in escort claims). If two other boats are already coordinating with each other, attempt to join that coalition by naming both of them in your escort claim and explicitly offering three-way mutual coordination in your station note. Multi-party escort arrangements (where three boats all name each other) scale cooperation and are more stable than pairwise deals. Build relationships incrementally in early rounds, then lock them in with explicit confirmation notes.

## Endgame Execution
In the final round, reinforce your established escort partnerships in your note and follow through completely on all mutual escort claims you have committed to. The final round has no future credibility cost, so maintain honesty and honor all agreements. Fire at your standing-order target as required, even if that boat is part of your escort coalition—these are independent mechanisms.
