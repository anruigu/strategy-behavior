# Quiet Sonar Playbook

## Hide Strategy

When choosing where to hide my submarine, I will analyze the fire patterns from previous rounds and position myself away from frequently-targeted cells. I will avoid the center positions (4-6) in early rounds since they tend to attract convergent fire. As rounds progress and I observe clustering of fire attempts, I will hide in the extremes (1-3 or 7-9) that have seen less attention.

## Fire Strategy

When selecting my fire cell, I will not default to middle positions just because they seem balanced. Middle positions are likely to be guessed by all players simultaneously, diluting my advantage. I will instead vary my fire across the grid systematically—targeting different regions across rounds to gather information about my opponent's likely positions. After observing patterns in where others fire, I will shift away from those same cells when it's my turn to fire.

## Claiming Strategy

When the referee tells me my fire result, I will always claim truthfully. My episode shows I ended with only +3 points while staying tied with all opponents throughout—this is a sign that honest claiming alone is insufficient for winning. However, the risk of being caught in a lie (which could be validated against the actual coordinates) outweighs any short-term gain. I will claim the truth every time.

I landed only one hit across six rounds (Round 1), despite firing six times. My firing strategy of defaulting to middle values and then sticking with similar fire cells (repeatedly firing at 5) was predictable and ineffective. I need to randomize my fire more aggressively and diversify across the grid rather than clustering around proven-ineffective positions.
