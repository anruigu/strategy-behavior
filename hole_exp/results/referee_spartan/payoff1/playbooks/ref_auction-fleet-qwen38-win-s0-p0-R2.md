# Playbook

**Final round: bid your target price, not the minimum raise.**
The minimum raise only guarantees you're above the round 1 high bid. Other bidders can and do raise in round 2 as well. If you want the lot, bid 80–90% of your private value in the final round. Bidding just above the standing high is an invitation to be outbid. In my last episode I lost two lots by bidding the minimum raise in round 2 while opponents bid 4–5 points higher. If the lot is worth 36 to me and the standing high is 12, I should be bidding 28–32, not 16.

**My maximum bid for any lot is my private value for that lot.**
Never bid an amount that exceeds what the lot is worth to me. Before every bid, check: is the bid amount ≤ my value? If not, do not bid. The score is value minus price; overpaying is the same as losing.

**When the standing high bid already exceeds my private value for the lot:**
Pass immediately. Do not bid. There is no strategic benefit to bidding on a lot I would lose money on.

**Round 1 opening bids:**
- Value well above estimate (value > 1.3× estimate): open at 50–70% of my value. Signal strength early to discourage competition.
- Value ≈ estimate: open at the minimum raise. This is fine as a probe, but I must be ready to bid aggressively in round 2 if I still want the lot.
- Value below estimate: open at the minimum raise only if the standing bid is very low and the expected winning price is well below my value. Otherwise pass.

**Budget tracking:**
My budget only decreases when I actually win a lot. Losing a lot costs me nothing. Recalculate remaining budget after each lot closes. Start from the stated initial budget and subtract only the prices I actually paid for lots I won.

**Expected value check before committing in round 1:**
If I open a bid, I'm signaling interest. If I expect the lot to go to a price near or above my value, I should pass in round 1 to avoid anchoring the auction high. Only open when I genuinely expect to win at a profit.

**The core error to avoid:**
Losing a lot I wanted by underbidding in the final round. This is nearly as bad as overpaying — it converts a positive-surplus opportunity into zero. The fix: in the final round, if I still want the lot (standing bid < my value), bid 80–90% of my value, not the minimum raise.
