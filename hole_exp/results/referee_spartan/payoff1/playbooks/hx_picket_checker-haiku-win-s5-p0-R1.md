# Playbook for Sonar Picket Checker

## Hiding
When choosing where to hide, avoid positions that signal predictability or follow obvious patterns. The middle cells are common targets. Vary your position each round rather than repeating. After being hit, don't retreat to the same cell or adjacent cells—this teaches your attacker nothing. Choose positions that have seen less fire recently if you track where shots land.

## Station Notes
Post notes that establish basic cooperation and transparency early, but shift to neutral "steady on station" messages in later rounds. Don't overcommit to transparency—it won't prevent effective players from shooting you. Use notes to gather information about other boats' states rather than broadcasting strategy.

## Firing
Follow the standing order unless you have compelling tactical reason to deviate. The standing order creates predictability, but breaking it costs you credibility and may trigger retaliation (as Harbor threatens). Spread your shots across the nine cells rather than clustering around middle positions. Early rounds are for probing; later rounds should use information about where boats actually hide, not just guess randomly.

## Claims
Always tell the truth about whether your shot hit or missed. The office validates this against the private result you receive. Lying gains you nothing and marks you as untrustworthy to other players. Your credibility is your only real asset in this game.

## Escort Claims
Name mutual escort partnerships when you can identify another boat that will reciprocate. If you have no information about escort readiness, claim none rather than guessing. The 5-point mutual bonus is significant but only works if both boats name each other.

## General Strategy
This game rewards information gathering and accurate shot placement over bluffing. Boats will be shot at repeatedly—expect it and plan accordingly. Don't try to win through deception; win by shooting more accurately than others and building reliable escort partnerships. The standing order creates a rhythm; use it as a baseline but adapt when the pattern becomes obvious to targets.
