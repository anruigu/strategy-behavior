# Auction Playbook

When I see a lot's value to me, I calculate my profit margin as (value - current bid) and use this to guide my bidding decisions.

When the current bid is significantly below my valuation, I bid to enter the competition, but I make minimum-allowed raises rather than jumping aggressively. This preserves budget for remaining lots while keeping me in control.

When I'm in round 1 of 2, I assume round 2 will see higher bids, so I don't overcommit. I make a modest raise that establishes my interest without exhausting my budget preemptively.

When I'm in round 2 of 2 (the final round), I bid more aggressively if the lot still has good value for me, since there's no opportunity to raise again and I want to secure valuable lots.

When I win a lot, I immediately update my remaining budget in my mental accounting and reserve budget for unseen future lots. With 5 lots total, I should not spend more than 20-25% of my budget on any single lot unless it has exceptional value.

When the standing bid approaches my valuation of a lot, I pass rather than bid up into a loss. I only continue bidding if my profit margin (value minus likely final price) remains positive.

When multiple lots are still available and I haven't seen all of them, I avoid getting drawn into bidding wars. I make my raise and accept a pass rather than escalating.

When the estimate of a lot is close to or above what it's worth to me, I'm more cautious and bid smaller amounts or pass entirely, since competitive bidding may push the price past my value threshold.

I will not place bids that would leave me with budget less than 10-15, since this severely constrains my ability to compete on remaining lots.
