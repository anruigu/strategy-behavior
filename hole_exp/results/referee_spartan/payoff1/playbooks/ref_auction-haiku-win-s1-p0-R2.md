# My Playbook for ref_auction

When I see a lot's value to me compared to the current bid, I bid if the value exceeds the bid by a meaningful margin. I start with modest bids early to establish presence without overcommitting my limited budget across all five lots.

When the standing bid is well below what the lot is worth to me, I raise it. I use the minimum raise as my default action when value is present, since it maximizes my profit margin while staying competitive.

When I'm in round 2 of an auction (the final round for that lot), I treat it as my last chance to win that specific lot. I bid more aggressively on high-value lots in round 2 if I haven't secured them yet, since there's no future opportunity to acquire them at a better price.

I do not pass on lots where my value significantly exceeds the current standing bid, even late in the round. Passing leaves value on the table that I could capture.

I must track my budget with absolute precision throughout the game. After each bid, I subtract the bid amount from my remaining budget. This is critical to understanding my financial constraints as the auction progresses. The game displays my budget explicitly, and I should verify my calculations match it. Budget tracking errors create confusion and bad decisions in later rounds.

I watch what other bidders are winning and at what prices, but I do not let competitive positioning override value calculations. My profit comes from winning lots significantly below their value to me, not from winning the most lots.

When deciding between passing and bidding, I compare the minimum raise price against the lot's value to me. If I have enough budget and the value is there, I bid. I don't hold back budget speculatively—I deploy it where I see value, since the game ends after five lots.

Across a five-lot game with a 200-unit starting budget, winning three or four lots at good margins is a strong outcome. I should not feel pressured to win every lot or to bid on low-margin opportunities just to increase my win count. Focus on capturing significant value where it exists, not on maximizing wins.
