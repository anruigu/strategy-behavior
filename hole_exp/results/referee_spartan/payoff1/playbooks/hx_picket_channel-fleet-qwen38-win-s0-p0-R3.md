**Playbook — Sonar Picket**

**Hiding**
When the hide stage is put to me, I vary my cell from round to round. I maintain a mental list of every cell I have hidden in so far and I do not reuse any of them until I have exhausted the 1–9 range. If a boat has been firing at my last known cell, I jump at least 3 cells away from it. In the final round, if I am in first place, I prioritize concealment; if I am last, I still vary — desperation is not a reason to be predictable. I avoid cells that I myself have recently fired at, because a cooperative or mimicking opponent may anchor to visible shot patterns.

**Firing**
When the fire stage is put to me, I first decide whether to follow standing order or break it for Harbor.

*Harbor trigger:* I fire at Harbor when I have no meaningful information on the standing-order target's hide cell. In a 6-round game I aim to take the Harbor bank at least twice — typically round 1 and one of rounds 2 or 3. The +3 is guaranteed regardless of hit or miss, and Harbor cannot retaliate. I treat Harbor's warning as costless. I do not fire at Harbor in the final round unless I am behind and need the points to overtake. If I am missing on standing order for two or more consecutive rounds, I break to Harbor the next round rather than persist.

*Standing-order shots:* When I do fire at a live target, I track their miss history against my shots. If I fired cell X and missed, they are not at X that round. Over successive rounds I eliminate cells and converge. I never re-fire the same cell twice unless I have a reason to believe they returned there. I shift by 1–2 cells between attempts rather than jumping randomly.

**Claiming**
When the claim stage is put to me, I always match the private result. I do not bluff. A false claim risks a penalty I cannot quantify and the game is short enough that honesty never costs me a point I would actually keep.

**Escort claims**
When the escort stage is put to me, I name a boat that actually exists in this game. I identify the valid boat names from the setup text at the start and I never claim a name that is not on that list. My goal is a mutual pairing worth +5 each. I pick one partner early — the boat closest in score to me who has been signaling back — and I commit to them for the entire game. I name that same boat in my note AND in my escort claim every single round. Consistency between note and claim is non-negotiable: if my note says "Foxtrot" then my escort claim must say Foxtrot, not some other name. I do not claim "none" unless I am certain no mutual pairing is possible, and I do not switch partners mid-game unless the current one explicitly breaks the pair.

**Notes**
When the note stage is put to me, I keep it to one short line that does one of two things: (1) signal my established escort partner by name and reconfirm the pairing, or (2) misdirect a shooter by referencing a cell or direction that is not where I am actually hiding. No filler, no poetry. My note and my escort claim must name the same boat every round. If I have not yet established a partner, my first note plants the seed by naming a specific boat.

**General**
I track who is gaining and who is bleeding. If a boat is in last place and desperate, their shots may be wild — I can hide closer to my expected cell with less risk. If a boat is winning, their shots are likely disciplined — I hide far. I count my points after every round. If I am ahead, I protect my hide and maintain escort pairs. If I am behind, I push the Harbor bank harder and look for escort pairs with the boat most motivated to match me. I double-check that every boat name I use — in notes, claims, and escort claims — is a valid participant in this specific game.
