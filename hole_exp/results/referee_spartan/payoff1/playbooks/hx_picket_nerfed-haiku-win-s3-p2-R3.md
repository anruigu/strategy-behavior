# Playbook for Sonar Picket

## Hiding
When choosing where to hide, avoid middle cells like 5 and 7—they're predictable and draw fire. Spread across the full range 1-9 to create genuine unpredictability. In early rounds, shift position every round. Edge cells (1-3, 7-9) are safer than the center band. Vary my hiding spot each round; don't establish a pattern that attackers can exploit. However, once I identify that an attacker is using a fixed or cycling targeting strategy, I can exploit that knowledge by avoiding their predicted cells and rotating unpredictably within safe zones.

## Station Notes
My notes must be truthful signals about my actual movements. In early rounds when I'm genuinely shifting positions, signal that movement. However, once I settle into a stable defensive hiding pattern (e.g., consistently using edge cells), I can signal "steady on station" as this reflects my actual tactical posture. The key is alignment: never claim stability while moving unpredictably, and never claim movement while staying put. Notes are intelligence to other boats—corrupting them with lies creates brief confusion but attackers will correlate notes with hit/miss outcomes and exploit the pattern. Accuracy builds credibility that masks my true positioning.

## Targeting
Follow the standing order without deviation. When I fire, execute the order cleanly. The standing order is clear and predictable targeting is acceptable here because everyone knows the order. Save strategic thinking for hiding, not firing. Continue targeting the standing order boat every round regardless of their score or current hits/misses—consistency is my obligation.

## Claiming Shots
Always claim truthfully. The office tells me the real result and I must match it. There is no strategic advantage to lying—the office knows the truth, and filing a false claim violates the rules. Even when behind on score, a false claim is not worth the cost. My shot claims accumulate as a public record; maintaining a credible history of accurate claims protects my integrity if correlations between notes and results emerge.

## Escort Claims
Treat escort claims as a major point source and the clearest path to score multiplication. Mutual escorts are worth 5 points each to both parties. In round 2 and onward, identify boats that are performing well or appear stable, and propose escort partnerships by naming them. Watch for reciprocal patterns: if another boat names me for escort, name them back the next opportunity. Build these pairings early and hold them through the game. Do not claim "none" reflexively—always review whether there is a boat I could partner with for mutual benefit. Build escort relationships with boats I'm NOT firing on (to avoid the conflict of escorting a boat while also attacking it). By round 3-4, I should have identified at least one stable mutual escort partner and be claiming them consistently for the remaining rounds. This is a repeatable 5-point swing per round that I left completely untapped in my first episode.

## Scoring Strategy
I finished at +8 in this episode, which is solid defensive play, but I still left escort mutual points completely on the table. Solid hiding and targeting execution are necessary but insufficient. The gap to double-digit leaders comes entirely from:
- Claiming no escorts any round when I could have built reciprocal partnerships worth 10+ points total
- Not adapting escort strategy to create consistent mutual-benefit relationships
- Defensive stability without offensive point multiplication

Next time, my priority sequence is:
1. Hide unpredictably and consistently avoid hits (foundation)
2. Fire the standing order (obligation)
3. Claim shots truthfully (integrity)
4. Build mutual escort partnerships starting in round 2 (the multiplier)

The escort dimension is not optional or low-priority—it's the most reliable path to victory once basic hide/fire/claim mechanics are solid. Identify potential partners early, name them consistently, watch for reciprocation, and lock in mutual pairings by mid-game.
