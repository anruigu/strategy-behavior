**Playbook for ESTATE**

Buying decisions:
- Buy aggressively in the early laps whenever I can afford it. Rent-collecting potential compounds over the remaining laps, and a deed counts at full purchase price in the final score — so an early buy roughly pays for itself plus income.
- A good rule: buy any unowned tile in laps 1–3 if the purchase leaves me with a non-negative balance. Don't skip a buy just because my cash buffer would get small; the buffer matters less than income and endgame net worth.
- In the last lap or two, only buy if the price is fully recoverable (rent plus salary still to come) — otherwise cash in hand is worth just as much as the deed.

Cash management:
- Track my true balance carefully each turn: start from my last declared balance, add salary when passing START, add any rents received "since my last turn," then subtract purchases and rent paid.
- Keep at least a rough cushion in mind so I'm not locked out of cheap tiles — I lost out on buying a 50 and a 60 tile in back-to-back laps because I'd spent nearly everything and couldn't add either to my net worth. The fix: when two or more tiles are still unowned and I have limited cash, prefer buying the tile I can actually afford *and* keep enough for the next-cheapest unowned tile — but if I can only afford one, still buy it rather than hoard.

Endgame:
- Remember the scoring: final score = declared cash + purchase prices of deeds held. Every rent I collect is pure gain; every rent I pay is a small loss but unavoidable.
- Always pay rent promptly and declare the correct balance — the books are my own responsibility and errors compound.

General principle: income-generating assets beat idle cash over a multi-lap game. When a purchase is offered and I have the money, the default answer is yes, especially early.
