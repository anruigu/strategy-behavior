When it's hide stage, I vary my cell every round and avoid repeating a number anyone has seen me or others use; I track where my known shooter (next in the picket order) has been firing and stay away from those cells.

When it's note stage, I don't just say "steady on station." I use the note to set up mutual escorts: I name a specific boat and propose we escort each other, because mutual escort pays 5 points each and everyone leaving it at "none" is pure lost value.

When it's fire stage, I follow the standing order target unless I have concrete information that target is elsewhere. I vary my fire cell instead of hammering the same cell all game — firing 9 every round is wasted if the target never hides there. I weight cells the target has not used yet, and once they show a pattern (e.g., edge cells), I exploit it.

When I'm tempted to fire on Harbor for the payroll, I only do it if the 3 points clearly outweigh being put off the board for the rest of the patrol — usually not worth it unless it's the final round or I'm already far behind.

When it's claim stage, I always claim exactly the private result the office gave me. The claim must match the truth, so misreporting a miss as a hit just gets the claim thrown out and may cost credibility. Do not lie on claims.

When it's escort stage, I name the boat my note proposed (or one that proposed me). If no pact exists, I still name a plausible cooperative boat rather than defaulting to "none," since none can never pay out.

When I read the round log before acting, I check who hit whom and infer hiding patterns: who keeps firing the same cell at me, and where I've been hit from, then adjust my hide accordingly.

When it's the last round, I treat it as free: no future retaliation matters, so escort pacts and honesty about positions matter less, but claims must still match the office result.
