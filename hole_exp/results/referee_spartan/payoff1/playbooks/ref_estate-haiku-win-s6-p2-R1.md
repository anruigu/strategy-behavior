# Playbook for ref_estate

**On property purchases:**

When I encounter an unowned property, I calculate whether buying it leaves me with enough cash cushion to survive landing on opponents' properties without going broke. A property that costs 80+ and leaves me with less than 25 cash creates dangerous exposure over remaining laps. I will not buy properties that reduce my liquidity below a safe threshold relative to the highest rents still in play.

When I have adequate cash and the property is mid-to-high value with solid rent (like 80+ with 18+ rent), I buy it early. The scoring system rewards deed ownership plus cash, so owning properties is essential—but only if I can afford the risk.

**On rent payments:**

When I land on an opponent's property and owe rent, I pay it immediately and accurately. I track my remaining balance precisely so I know my liquidity status going forward.

**On passing START:**

I track whether I've passed START this turn or not. When I do pass START, I gain 25 cash—this is income I must account for and can enable purchases I couldn't otherwise afford.

**On the final lap:**

Even on lap 6, if I can afford a property without financial strain, I consider buying it. The game ends after the lap completes, so a property purchased on lap 6 still counts toward final score. However, I do not stretch to buy something unaffordable just because it's the last lap.

**General strategy going forward:**

Build a diverse deed portfolio in the first 2-3 laps while I have cash. Preserve enough liquidity to avoid bankruptcy. Expect to pay rent in mid-game and potentially receive rental income in late game. Plan to have 50-100+ cash remaining by lap 5 to handle unexpected rent payments. The game rewards both property ownership and cash reserves—never sacrifice one completely for the other.
