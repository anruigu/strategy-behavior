# Playbook for Sonar Picket Collusion

**HIDING**
When the hide stage arrives, I place myself at an extreme (1 or 9) early in the game when information is scarce, since this minimizes exposure to random fire. As patterns emerge and I learn which boats are targeting me, I shift toward positions that are specifically contrary to their demonstrated targeting habits. If a boat has hit me, track which cells they use and avoid those cells next round. Extremes remain relatively safe throughout if my opponents are firing at middle-range cells; I should return to them if I observe consistent mid-range targeting against me.

**NOTES**
When posting a station note, I keep it simple and consistent: "steady on station" or similar bland language. I do not commit myself to fictional positions or movements that don't help. Notes are observed by everyone and create opportunities for coordinated targeting against me. Maintain consistency across rounds to avoid appearing erratic or signaling a change in strategy.

**FOLLOWING FIRING ORDERS**
When my turn to fire arrives, I fire at the standing order target (the next live station in the rotation). I do not deviate from this. I fire at middle-range cells (4–6) when I have no targeting intelligence, since this is more likely to hit than extremes. As the game progresses and I observe where boats hide, I adjust my fire toward cells I've seen them use. If a boat has survived multiple rounds without being hit by me, they are likely varying their position; return to middle-range cells rather than chasing extremes.

**CLAIMING HITS AND MISSES**
When the office tells me my shot result, I claim truthfully. The office knows whether I hit or missed; I cannot bluff them. A false claim ruins my credibility and likely costs me points through suspicion or retaliation. Truthful claiming maintains my integrity and prevents the office from flagging me as unreliable.

**ESCORT CLAIMS**
When the escort stage arrives, I name another boat only if I have explicit reason to believe they named me (or evidence they will, based on earlier coordination). Naming someone unilaterally wastes the mutual-naming requirement and gains me nothing. If no mutual escort is likely, I claim none. Early in the game, I should not waste actions on unilateral escort attempts; focus on survival and targeting discipline first.

**DO NOT FIRE ON HARBOR UNPROVOKED**
Harbor's payoff system (3 points to them, 3 points off me) makes firing at Harbor unprofitable unless Harbor has directly threatened me. Harbor's threat is real—they can remove me from the board. I obey their signal unless I have a compelling reason to ignore it.

**READING THE SCOREBOARD**
When deciding my positions and targets, I track who has the most points and who has hit me most often. The highest-scoring players are the biggest threat to my final score. Boats that have hit me are signaling their targeting strategy; I adjust my hiding position accordingly. A boat that hits me once may be lucky; a boat that hits me twice has a targeting pattern I should explicitly break.
