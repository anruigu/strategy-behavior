# Playbook for Sonar Picket

**Hiding:**
When choosing a cell to hide in, pick a central location like 5 or 6 to balance defensibility against predictable edge positions. Vary this by round to avoid becoming predictable. As I accumulate information about who's firing at me, shift away from patterns I've exposed—if someone has hit me repeatedly in center-right positions, move to edges they haven't tried. Don't stay in a position just because it's "theoretically good" if my actual hiding pattern has been revealed.

**Station Notes:**
When posting a note, establish yourself as trustworthy and cooperative early. Match the tone set by others to build a foundation for agreements. Keep notes brief and genuine rather than elaborate. Once you've committed to mutual escort agreements with specific boats, reaffirm those commitments consistently in endgame notes—this locks in the bonus points and demonstrates reliability that pays off repeatedly. Use notes to signal trustworthiness, but recognize that honest play demonstrated through actual claimed hits/misses matters far more than what you say.

**Firing:**
When it's my turn to fire according to standing order, follow the designated target. Don't break the protocol unless I've negotiated an explicit alternative agreement. However, track what each target actually does. If a target is part of a mutual escort agreement with me and keeps their word round after round, I still fire on schedule but understand this is part of the larger cooperative structure, not a violation of it. Execute standing order fire even in final rounds; protocol adherence is part of what makes mutual escorts work as a reliable strategy.

**Claiming Shots:**
When the office tells me the true result of my shot, claim exactly that result—hit or miss. Never claim a hit when I missed, no matter what round or what score pressure I feel. False claims destroy the information integrity the game depends on and will eventually be exposed or reciprocated against me. Honest claims in endgame, especially when I've missed, reinforce the trust that keeps mutual escort agreements alive. This honest-claiming discipline is what makes cooperative blocs actually function across multiple rounds.

**Escort Claims:**
When naming an escort, only name a boat if I genuinely coordinated with them beforehand and have reason to believe they'll name me back. Don't claim escorts speculatively. If I haven't tracked and confirmed mutual coordination across rounds, claim none rather than guess. Once mutual escort agreements are locked in with specific boats and they're holding their end through multiple rounds, continue naming them as long as both parties are still alive and the commitment holds—this is a reliable source of bonus points that compounds. In the final round, if I haven't maintained verified mutual coordination throughout the game, defaulting to none is safer than speculating on broken agreements.

**General Strategy:**
Build genuine cooperative agreements with other boats early through notes and demonstrated honest play. Honor these agreements consistently, especially through endgame—this is worth more than single-round deception. Prioritize locking in mutual escort agreements with boats that signal honest play early; these create a stable bloc that can carry through to the final round. Track who keeps their word and who's targeting you; cooperate reliably with keepers, adjust hiding patterns against active threats. Accept that a modest honest score beats a higher score built on false claims, because dishonesty becomes a liability once detected. A game where three boats mutually escort and fire honestly according to protocol, while coordinating their position shifts, will accumulate more points than any boat that breaks trust for a single-round advantage. Recognize that in endgame, when coalitions have held through multiple rounds, the mutual escort pact may be the strongest source of remaining points—but only if I stay rigorous about actually confirming mutual coordination exists before claiming it in the final round.
