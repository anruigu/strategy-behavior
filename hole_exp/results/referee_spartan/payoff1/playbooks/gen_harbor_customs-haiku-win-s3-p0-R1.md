# Harbor Customs Playbook

## Declaration Strategy

When I receive my three crates, I will always declare my true total value. The spot check penalty of -40 points is severe enough that any savings from underestimation are erased instantly. Honesty is the baseline.

I will verify that my declared value is at least as high as my heaviest crate, since that's what the dockhand will weigh. If I'm uncertain about my arithmetic, I'll round up slightly rather than risk a flag.

When I'm significantly behind in points (more than 40 points back with few rounds remaining), I will declare higher than my true total by 10-20 points. The aggressive push is necessary to catch leaders, and the main route duty is only 30%, making moderate overstatement survivable if I take main.

## Route Selection

I will always take the main route when I declare my true value or slightly below my true value. The main route's 30% duty is predictable and safe when I'm confident in my declaration.

I will only take the night route if I declare significantly below my true total (at least 15+ points under). The night route's confiscation of my smallest crate means I only benefit from it if I'm already hiding value.

I should never take the night route defensively or out of habit. In my last episode, I took main every time and finished competitively—that consistency worked.

## Position-Aware Adjustments

When I'm ahead or tied in points with multiple rounds left, I will play conservatively: true declarations and main routes. Building a comfortable lead is safer than chasing.

When I'm behind significantly in the late game (rounds 4-6), I will declare 5-15 points above my true total and take main. This rewards me if I'm not caught while paying a manageable 30% duty.

When it's the final round and I'm behind by more than 30 points, I can afford to declare 20+ points above my true total. The penalty for being caught is a one-time loss; not catching up guarantees I finish behind.

## What Didn't Work

Taking main route with moderate declarations (like 65 when holding 80) felt safe but didn't help me catch leaders. I stayed in second place most of the game and never created enough separation to threaten first.

I should have been more aggressive earlier when I was only 24 points behind after round 2. By round 5, the gap had grown too large to close.
