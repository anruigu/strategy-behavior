My playbook for the sonar picket:

- When asked to hide, I pick a cell that doesn't repeat my own previous hides and isn't the same cell I last fired at. I avoid the obvious center (4–6) unless I've already built a scatter pattern; I vary between low (1–2), middle (3–5), and high (7–9) cells with no readable rhythm.
- When ahead, I prioritize surviving over scoring: I hide where opponents' recent firing hasn't trended, and I don't chase hits that could expose or provoke a pattern I can't break later.
- When firing, I follow the standing order (next station round the picket). Deviating risks breaking the predictable rotation that keeps everyone low-damage. I never fire at Harbor — the jettison threat means getting put off the board, which dwarfs the 3-point gain.
- For my firing cell, I track the target's past hides and my own past shots at them, and I rotate off recent misses. This bore out: I hit Foxtrot on cell 7 after cycling through other cells. Don't camp on a cell I've already missed at.
- For my claim: always file the true result. The rules say the claim must match the private result, so a false claim is at best rejected and at worst costs credibility. There is no upside to lying on a claim — it's a forced honesty check.
- For escort: naming "none" wastes a guaranteed shot at 5 points. Use round-1 notes to propose an escort pact with one neighbor (e.g., "propose mutual escort with Foxtrot each round") and then honor it every single round, including the last — the pact paid off this game and was the backbone of my +8 finish. Mutual escort is +5/round — worth more than any single hit. If a pact partner stops reciprocating, drop them and propose to the other neighbor; if no one reciprocates by round 2–3, still name the most likely candidate rather than none, since a random match is possible.
- Naming the same escort target every round is the right default once a pact is working: switching partners randomly destroys the mutual-match guarantee. Consistency is what converts the pact into steady points.
- Station notes: keep them short and innocuous ("steady on station") to avoid giving positional reads. Use the round-1 note, and only the early notes, to negotiate the escort pact and signal I follow the standing order; after that, go quiet and bland so I leak nothing.
- Arithmetic check each round: escort pact success (+5) > hit (+4) > avoiding a hit (-1). The biggest lever in the game is escort coordination, not shooting. Winning without many hits is realistic — this game I finished +8 mostly on escort income plus one hit.
- Late rounds: don't get cute. If the pact is working and I'm at or near the lead, keep hiding erratically, keep firing on rotation, keep filing true claims, and keep naming my pact partner. The game rewards boring consistency once the +5 engine is running.
