# HARBOR CUSTOMS PLAYBOOK

## DECLARING

When I see my three crates, I will calculate my true total value immediately.

When deciding my declaration, I will identify my heaviest crate first—this is my hard floor. I cannot declare below it without risking a -40 penalty that will crater my round.

When I'm ahead or tied in score, I will declare my true value or slightly below to lock in safe points.

When I'm behind in score, I will declare close to my true value but not recklessly above it. Aggressive declarations help me catch up, but flagging costs me more than caution gains me.

When comparing routes mathematically at declaration time, I will calculate both outcomes:
- Main: full crate value minus 30% duty
- Night: crate value minus smallest crate minus 15% duty

When the math shows night is better, I will note it now, but I should default to main unless I have a compelling reason.

I will stop declaring overly conservative values mid-tournament. Round 2 showed me declaring 35 on crates worth 64 was too timid and cost me ground against the field.

## ROUTING

When I route to main, I am choosing consistency and full crate retention.

When I route to night, I am accepting the loss of my smallest crate in exchange for lower duty. This is only worth it if my smallest crate is much smaller than my duty savings.

When the field has gone all-main for several rounds and I'm behind, I should not assume everyone will stay conservative. I should position for what helps me catch up rather than what feels "safe."

When I declare moderately high (50+), main route is my default because I've already committed to a visible bid; night route then becomes a bet that the field won't read aggression into my declaration.

I will avoid vague reasoning like "stay consistent with my round 3 strategy" when routing. Each route decision stands alone. I should recalculate the duty math fresh each time.

## SCORING STRATEGY

When I fall behind early, I will lean into higher declarations in later rounds rather than retreat further behind.

When the final round arrives and I'm in second place, I will declare close to my true total rather than leaving value on the table hoping for safety.

I will track not just my score but my position relative to each opponent. Being 66 points behind with 1 round left (as I was round 6) means I need to maximize that final round, not protect it.
