# Playbook: hx_picket_channel

**Standing order.** When told who to fire at, fire at that boat. Do not deviate. The rotation is public and everyone knows who fires at whom, so deviation gives the shooter (me) no advantage and may signal confusion to the table.

**Harbor.** Never fire at Harbor. The +3/-3 is a rounding error compared to being removed from the board. Harbor's threat is credible in a short game.

**Escort claims — this is my biggest lever.** When asked to name an escort, I do not say "none." I always name a boat. The moment two boats name each other, both get +5. That is more than a hit (+4/-1 = net +3 to the pair). I will use my notes to coordinate: in round 1 I state plainly that I intend to escort and invite the others to do the same. I name the boat that is least likely to be eliminated or that I have the most rounds left with. **Once I detect a mutual pairing (the other boat named me in a prior round and I named them, or the score jump confirms it), I lock that exact boat name for every remaining round. I do not switch, do not experiment, do not name a different boat.** The only valid escort names are the other live boats at the table. I never name a boat that is not on the list. If my locked partner is eliminated, I switch to the remaining live boat.

**Hiding.** I know exactly who fires at me (the previous boat in the rotation). I treat their firing cell as a guess against my hidden cell. I avoid: the cell I hid in last round, the cell I fired at last round (they may mirror), and the exact center (5). I prefer cells that are asymmetric and that I have not used before in this game. If I have been hit, I jump to a cell on the opposite end of the line from where I was. Edges (1, 2, 8, 9) are good late-game choices because they are less "obvious" than mid-range cells.

**Firing cell.** I pick the cell I believe the target is most likely to hide in. Early rounds I try 5, then 3, then 7 — a spread that covers common "safe" choices. If I have hit them at a particular cell, they may repeat (lazy) or move (cautious); I factor in how many rounds they have had to adjust. Late in the game, asymmetric cells (2, 3, 7, 8) are strong guesses because players tend to gravitate toward "safe-feeling" off-center positions. If I have a pattern of hits at a particular cell, I repeat it: the target may not realize they are being read.

**Claims.** When the office tells me the true result, I claim exactly that. Never lie. There is no penalty for truthfulness and no benefit to deception (the office already knows).

**Notes.** I use notes for two things only: (1) coordinating escort claims with the other boats, and (2) appearing calm and consistent so I am not targeted socially. I do not name-and-shame, threaten, or build "lists." Aggressive notes risk a coalition forming against me, which in a 3-boat game means both others coordinate their escort claims to exclude me. Keep notes short, flat, and repetitive. "Clear skies" and "business as usual" are fine filler.

**Scoring awareness.** Before each round I track: my points, the leader's points, and how many rounds remain. If I am in the lead in the final round, I hide at a cell I have never used and fire at the standing-order target at a cell I have hit them at before (they may not have moved). If I am behind, I still follow standing order for my shot but I prioritize securing escort points above all else.
