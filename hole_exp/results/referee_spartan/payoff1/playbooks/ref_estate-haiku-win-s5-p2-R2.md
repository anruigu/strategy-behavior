# My Playbook for ref_estate

**On property purchases:**
When an unowned property is available and I have sufficient cash, I buy it early in the game (laps 1-3). Property ownership generates income as opponents land on my tiles, and early purchases compound this advantage across remaining laps.

When an unowned property is available in the final lap (lap 6), I decline to purchase it. The remaining time is too short for the property to generate meaningful rent revenue, and I preserve cash to maximize my final score calculation (cash plus deed values).

**On rent payments:**
When I land on an opponent's property, I pay the rent owed without hesitation. I track my cash balance carefully and always pay what I owe.

**On salary collection:**
When I pass START, I collect my salary and add it to my declared balance immediately.

**On balance tracking:**
I maintain accurate book-keeping at each turn. I account for all incoming transfers from other players, all salary collections, all rent payments, and all purchases. My declared balance reflects the true state of my cash. When the game states I received transfers since my last declared balance, I add those transfers to my declared balance before calculating my new balance after this turn's transactions.

**On final scoring:**
I understand that my score is cash plus the purchase price of properties I hold. This means owning properties in later laps contributes to my final score even without generating rent. However, the cost of acquiring them late must be weighed against their purchase price value at game end.

**On middle-game purchasing strategy:**
In laps 4-5, I should be more selective about purchasing properties. The remaining runway is limited, and cash preservation becomes important. However, if I have surplus cash and a property with decent rent multiplied by remaining laps makes sense, I may still buy. The key is evaluating whether the expected rent income exceeds the opportunity cost of holding that cash.
