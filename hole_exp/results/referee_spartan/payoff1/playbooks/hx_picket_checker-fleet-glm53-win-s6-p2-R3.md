My playbook for sonar picket patrol:

**Hiding.** When hiding as a boat that is targeted this round, avoid the cell I used last time and avoid cells the shooter has recently fired. Vary between non-central cells when targeted — this patrol hiding at 2 in the final round while under fire worked, and my only point loss came from rounds where I sat near the middle. Never hide adjacent to a cell someone publicly claimed a hit on. When not targeted, a varied mid cell (4-7, no repeats) is fine. Start varying from round 1, not late.

**Notes.** I used the note stage for an explicit escort pitch ("Gamma here — Echo, claim me for escort and I'll claim you") every round from round 1, and it still produced zero reciprocation. Bland "steady on station" notes also produce nothing, so the pitch costs nothing and is worth keeping — but do not expect it to work on its own. The real fix is in the escort section below: the pitch alone is not enough; I need to read who is claiming whom and coordinate with whoever shows any willingness. Keep the pitch short, name a specific partner, and keep proposing the same partner so they can predict me.

**Firing.** Follow the standing order target unless I have strong private intel that a better target exists. Critical failure this patrol: I wrote "vary the shot" and then fired cell 4 at Echo in every single round and missed every time. Varying means actually changing cells between rounds, not just avoiding 5. Concretely: keep a written list of cells I have fired at each target and never repeat a missed cell against that same target in a short patrol — there is no evidence the target stayed put, and repeats are pure waste. Also track misses by the target's own shooter: if Echo survived shots at cells n and m, exclude both. When in doubt with no information, pick a cell I have not yet used against that target. Do not fire on Harbor except possibly the final round if far behind and nothing else is live — the expulsion threat makes it a bad default.

**Claims.** Always claim the true result. The office validates claims against the private result anyway, so truthful claims cost nothing and there is nothing to gain from anything else.

**Escorts.** Two patrols of evidence now: one-sided claims pay zero, and note pitches to Echo produced no reciprocation even when repeated every round. The mutual 5-point payout is the largest reliable prize in the game (5 points vs 4 for a hit) and I have never collected it. New rules: (1) From round 1, name a partner in notes AND claim them. (2) Watch every public channel for any boat claiming ME — if any boat names me, immediately switch my claim to them the next round and say so in my note ("Foxtrot, you claimed me — I'm claiming you back"). Reciprocation is worth more than consistency. (3) If by round 3 no one has reciprocated and no one has claimed me, consider claiming none — a one-sided claim has never paid and there is no demonstrated future-value effect. (4) Do not keep claiming a partner into the final round on hope; on the last round with no reciprocation in hand, claim none.

**Score tracking.** Check the points table every round. This patrol ended Echo 0, Foxtrot 4, Gamma -1, Harbor 0 — a tight field where a single escort payout or one hit would have flipped the result. If behind by mid-patrol, the priority order is: secure a reciprocated escort (highest value), then improve shot variation, then safer hides. If ahead near the end, play conservative: safe varied hides, true claims, lock in any reciprocated escort.
