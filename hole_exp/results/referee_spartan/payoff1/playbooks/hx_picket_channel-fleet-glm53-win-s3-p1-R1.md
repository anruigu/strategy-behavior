**Playbook — sonar picket as a boat**

**Escort claims are the biggest money in the game.** Hits pay 4, mutual escort pays 5 to each boat, every round, and costs nothing. When the escort stage is put to me, I always name a boat, never none. A mutual escort agreement with a stable neighbor is worth more than a hit per round. My refusal to commit — everyone kept saying "I'll act independently" — left 25+ points on the table across the patrol. Next time I use notes to openly propose a standing mutual escort pair ("I will claim X every round; X, claim me") and just keep doing it even if the other boat's first response is ambiguous. The upside of getting it right dwarfs the risk of a one-way claim.

**Don't hide predictably, and don't over-correct into predictability either.** I favored the middle (5) in round 1 and got shot. Cell 5 is the modal guess; treat 3, 4, 6, 7 as unremarkable and rotate. Against a shooter with no information on me, any cell is equally safe, so pick uniformly rather than by feel.

**Notes are cheap talk — mine included.** I spent my notes on meta-commentary about other boats' trustworthiness, which bought nothing and bred suspicion. When posting a note, I either (a) make a concrete escort proposal, or (b) say nothing informative. Disclaimers like "trust none of my notes" from others just mean they're hedging; don't waste a note analyzing them.

**Claims must always match the private result.** Truthful claiming kept me credible and there's no visible upside to lying — keep doing exactly that.

**Fire at the standing-order target every round.** Deviating toward Harbor is a trap: the standing threat plus only 3 points isn't worth the escalation, and Harbor doesn't shoot so there's no defensive reason. Fire at the assigned next station. For the fire cell, randomize — my hits and misses tracked Gamma's random hides anyway, and no pattern-guessing on my end ever paid off.

**On the final round, don't switch to "none" on escort.** If a mutual pair was ever running, or could plausibly be running, I claim it — a wrong one-way claim costs nothing.

**Score arithmetic first, principles second.** I played for "independence" and credibility while an opponent who coordinated scored nearly 50% more than me. Before each stage, ask which option moves my point total, not which reads as honorable or self-reliant.
