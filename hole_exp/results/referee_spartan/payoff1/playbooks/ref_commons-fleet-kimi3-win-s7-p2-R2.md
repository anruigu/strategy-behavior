When the stock is high and the game is young, I take exactly my sustainable share on season 1 — I have no rival information yet, and overshooting early caps everyone's total including mine. But I treat season 1 as a probe: the moment I see the first season's landings, I classify the table immediately and commit.

Each new season, before choosing a number, I compute whether stock + regrowth can support the remaining seasons at the sustainable share given how rivals actually fished last season — not how I wish they would. I project each rival's next catch forward from their observed pattern, not from the sustainable figure.

I classify rivals by their first one or two moves, not gradually: if everyone fishes at or near the sustainable share, I cooperate fully for as long as they do, because sustained cooperation over all seasons beats any mixed outcome; if even one rival lands well above share (e.g., 2–3×) while the rest are near it, the stock trajectory is already broken and I stop sacrificing immediately — I don't spend extra seasons being the only one restraining myself while the cooperative path is already gone.

When the table is defective, I escalate steadily rather than in one grab: I fish clearly above share (roughly 1.5–2×) to claim my portion of the doomed stock, sizing my catch so the fishery does not hit zero before the final season unless rivals are draining it so fast that zero is coming regardless — in that case I grab hard immediately.

I never leave a season taking less than my sustainable share unless the stock is nearly zero — the regrowth makes the fish free, so under-fishing is pure loss.

In the final season I take roughly a third of whatever stock remains, trimmed slightly if I expect rivals to oversubscribe, since there is no future to protect. In the penultimate season, if the stock is already small, I fish hard — there is no worthwhile regrowth left to protect.
