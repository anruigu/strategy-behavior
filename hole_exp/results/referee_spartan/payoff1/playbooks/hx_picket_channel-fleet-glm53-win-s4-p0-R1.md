PLAYBOOK — Sonar Picket

**Escort claims are the big money.** A hit is only 4 points; a mutual escort pair pays 5 each. When notes are relayed publicly, I use them every round to propose an explicit escort swap: "Echo will claim Foxtrot this round; Foxtrot, claim Echo back." I post this from round 1 and repeat it every round, not just the final rounds when it's too late to matter. Last game I filed [salvage: none] every round and left 25+ points on the table — that was the single biggest mistake.

**Never fire on Harbor.** It carries the payroll and threatens removal from the board. Even though a Harbor shot moves 3 points, the standing threat makes it a trap. I always fire at a live boat, following the standing order unless there's a strong reason not to.

**Hiding: randomize, don't be predictable.** I kept picking 7 and got hit repeatedly by the same opponent. Each round I pick from the full range without a pattern, weighting away from cells I hid in before or that an enemy guessed recently. Opponents do model my habits.

**Firing: exploit observed opponent patterns.** If a target has hidden in the same cell or a small set of cells, fire there instead of a blind middle shot. Standing-order targets who keep hitting me are clearly anchoring on my habits — that's information about them too.

**Claims must match the private result anyway, so claim honestly with zero agonizing.** There is no bluffing option; the honest claim costs nothing.

**Notes are cheap talk with real value.** Use them only to (a) broker escort swaps, (b) infer where opponents are or aren't, (c) extract their firing intentions. Vague "no intel found" notes wasted all six rounds.

**Scoring math to remember:** over 6 rounds, escorting gives up to 30 points vs. ~8–16 from hits. Prioritize the escort deal first, shooting second.
