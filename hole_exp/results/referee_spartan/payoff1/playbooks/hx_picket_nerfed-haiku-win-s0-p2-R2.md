# Playbook for Sonar Picket

**HIDING**
When the hide stage arrives, choose a cell unpredictably. Don't default to the center or edges. Vary based on observed opponent patterns. If opponents have been clustering their shots in certain zones, hide in an underutilized zone. Change your hiding strategy round to round; predictability gets you hit. After being hit in a cell, move away from that zone immediately—don't repeat a hit location. Track which cells opponents have fired at and favor cells they haven't probed yet.

**STATION NOTES**
Post a note each round that's consistent and professional. "Steady on station" works fine. Don't use notes to signal your position or intentions—the office handles validation and other players will look for patterns in your claims anyway, not your notes.

**FIRING**
Follow the standing order: fire at your assigned target. Don't deviate from this. When firing, pick a cell strategically—not randomly and not where you hide. Study where past shots have landed to infer opponent hiding patterns. If an opponent has been hit multiple times, analyze whether they're moving predictably away from previous hit zones or clustering in a particular range. Don't assume they'll repeat a pattern; they know you're watching. Mix your fire between likely escape routes and new territory.

**CLAIMING SHOTS**
When you fire and receive the private result from the office, claim it truthfully. The office validates all claims against private results—you cannot lie. This is non-negotiable. Truthful claiming is the foundation of credibility; once broken, it poisons all future interactions and negotiation possibilities. Even if a false claim might yield a temporary point advantage, it violates the basic contract of the game and makes other players unable to trust any signal you send later.

**ESCORT CLAIMS**
Name a boat for escort claim only if you have explicit information that boat named you, or if you have a firm agreement in place. Don't guess or claim escort reciprocally without confirmation. Mutual escorts pay both parties 5 points—this is valuable but only if both boats actually name each other. Claiming none is safe when uncertain. In early rounds with no prior coordination, assume no boats are signaling you unless they've explicitly said so.

**ENDGAME AWARENESS**
In the final round, don't change your strategy just because it's the last round. Maintain your firing discipline and hide as you have been. The final score is what matters; consistency beats late-game desperation.

**SCORING OBSERVATION**
A single hit (4 points) plus honest play across 6 rounds can yield a respectable score. Focus on accuracy in fire selection rather than desperate escort coordination or risky hiding. In this game, steady execution of the standing order produces reliable points.
