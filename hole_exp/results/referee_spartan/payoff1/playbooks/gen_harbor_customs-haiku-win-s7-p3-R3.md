# Harbor Customs Playbook

**Declaration Strategy:**

When declaring, I will always declare at least the value of my heaviest crate to avoid the -40 penalty flag. This is non-negotiable—the penalty is catastrophic.

I will declare my exact total crate value in all standard cases. This strategy proved reliable across six rounds, kept me competitive, and avoided all penalties. The exact-value declaration is simple, defensible, and reduces decision variance.

I will verify the heaviest crate constraint before every declaration to ensure safety.

**Route Selection:**

I will choose the main route by default. The night route sacrifices my smallest crate to save approximately 15% of declared value in duty.

I will consider switching to night only when:
- My declared value is significantly high (75+), AND
- My smallest crate's value is demonstrably lower than the duty savings (roughly 0.15 × declared value or less)

In practice, this threshold is rarely met. Across six rounds, I chose main consistently and it proved sound. Main route should remain my dominant strategy.

I will not switch to night on marginal arithmetic grounds (e.g., declared 43, saving ~6 in duty but losing ~8–10 in crate value). The trade-off must be clearly favorable.

**Position and Competition:**

I will track my standing relative to other players but will not let it override sound economics. The exact-value declaration strategy compounds to competitive scores over six rounds.

I will not declare unusually high or low values to "draw attention," respond to competitors' declarations, or compete directly. My competitiveness came from consistent, disciplined play.

**Consistency Over Variance:**

I will identify a declaration pattern early based on my crate distribution and stick to it unless circumstances shift dramatically. Exact-value declarations, paired with main-route defaults, form a reliable baseline.

If I fall behind mid-game, I will not abandon exact-value declarations for high bluffs or aggressive night-route gambling. The -40 penalty for a flagged manifest is too severe; the game rewards accuracy, not risk. Catching up happens through steady rounds, not gambles.

If I am tied or ahead late in the game, I will maintain the same disciplined strategy that built my position. Do not become conservative or deviate from proven patterns out of fear of losing.

**Guard Against Overconfidence:**

Exact-value declarations with main-route defaults work reliably. I finished tied for first (252 points) by following this plan. Do not second-guess it based on perceived "room to optimize" in any single round. The strategy is sound.
