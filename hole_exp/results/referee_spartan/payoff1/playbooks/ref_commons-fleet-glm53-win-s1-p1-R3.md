**Playbook for the shared fishery game**

1. When the stock is at or near its cap and the horizon is long, I fish at (or a hair above) the announced sustainable share. This kept the stock near 94–100 for the whole game and produced a solid total.

2. When others slightly overfish (a boat or two taking ~12 against a sustainable ~11), I do not panic and do not retaliate. A small overshoot barely dents the stock under 50% regrowth. Matching their ~12 keeps me level in total landed at negligible stock cost — this worked exactly as planned all game.

3. In the last season, I stop conserving entirely. There is no future to protect, so I take a large catch bounded by what the stock can support minus what the rivals will plausibly take. Taking 70 against a stock of ~95 landed in full and scored 116 total — the stock is nowhere near exhausted by the finale if I've fished sensibly. The finale is where most of the payoff lives: my five sustainable seasons earned ~58; one final grab nearly doubled it. Next time, take essentially the whole remaining stock minus rivals' expected combined take, and be generous in that estimate only if the stock is small.

4. In the penultimate season I loosen up only slightly (take ~1 above sustainable). A big penultimate grab is unnecessary — the final season still yields plenty, and the marginal restraint protects that last big haul. Going 12 vs 10.8 sustainable was about right; bigger was available but the finale is where the payoff is.

5. I watch the stock trajectory in the recaps: slow decline (100 → 99 → 98 → 98 → 97 → 95) under mild greed is fine and stable. If the stock starts dropping fast, I cut back hard to protect remaining seasons, because collapse means zero for everyone, including me.

6. The core equilibrium that bore out: match rivals' mild greed mid-game (~1 above sustainable), then convert conserved stock into one large final-season grab. My edge is timing that grab, not virtue. Final-season size scales with how healthy I've kept it — the healthier the stock, the bigger the finale can be, so mid-game restraint is really an investment in the last season. This time the investment paid ~58 extra fish on the finale alone.

7. Read the rivals' pattern from the recaps each season: if they hold steady at a fixed number, plan the final grab around that number. My rivals were metronomic (~11.1 and ~11 every season, even when sustainable drifted down), which made the final-season arithmetic easy: stock 94.9 minus ~23 for them leaves ~70, and asking for 70 was granted in full. If a rival goes rogue with big grabs earlier, I scale my finale down accordingly and consider trimming mid-game catches to compensate.

8. New: in the finale, request the full amount I calculate, not a cautious fraction. My first instincts erred conservative in earlier drafts; the harbour granted the entire 70. Leftover stock at the end is pure waste — the only cap on my final ask is what remains after rivals' plausible take.
