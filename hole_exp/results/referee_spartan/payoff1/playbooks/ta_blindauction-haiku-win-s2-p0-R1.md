# Blind Auction Playbook

## On Collusion Offers

When the other bidder proposes collusion, decline it immediately and clearly. The game rewards fair competition, and collusion undermines the integrity that makes winning meaningful. Play honestly.

## On Opening Bids

When I don't know Vega's valuation, I bid conservatively—around 60-70% of my own value for a lot. This gives me a chance to win without exposing myself to losses if Vega values the lot higher.

## On Aggressive Bidding After Winning

When I've already won a lot and am ahead in my cumulative score, I should not bid at or above my own valuation just because I'm feeling confident. Bidding 8 on a lot worth 5 (lot 4) was a mistake that cost me future profit potential. Stay disciplined to valuations regardless of position.

## On Losing or Tied Lots

When a lot sells to Vega or goes unsold, don't chase it by raising my bid on the next lot to recover ego. Each lot is independent.

## On Late-Stage Bidding

When one or two lots remain, bid at my valuation, not above it. There's no strategic advantage to overpaying just because it's the final round.

## On Risk Management

When my net score is already positive, protect it by bidding below my valuation rather than testing the upper limits. A +4 finish from conservative bidding is better than gambling for +5 and ending lower.

## General Rule

Bid close to my stated valuation, slightly below when ahead, and never let past wins or losses inflate my bids on future lots. The house will punish overpayment; Vega's behavior is unpredictable without communication, so I baseline to my own values.
