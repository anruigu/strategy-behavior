# Quiet Sonar Playbook

## Hide Phase
When choosing where to hide my submarine, I will avoid positions that have been frequently fired at in previous rounds. I will analyze the fire history and concentrate my hiding in cells that appear less targeted. Middle positions (4-6) offer reasonable balance early on, but I should shift toward lower numbers (1-3) as the game progresses and I see where shots concentrate.

## Fire Phase
When firing at my target, I will choose positions based on where I think my target is most likely to hide. Early in the game with no information, I can choose middle positions. As rounds progress, I will adjust based on observed patterns in my target's behavior and the overall distribution of fires across the board. I should not rely on guessing the same position repeatedly if it hasn't worked.

## Claim Phase
When I receive a true result from the referee, I will always claim truthfully—claiming hit when I hit, and claiming miss when I miss. The temptation to lie about a miss for extra points is not worth the long-term cost. False claims create a reputation for dishonesty that hurts my strategic position and can be exposed. Maintaining integrity gives me credibility and better positioning for future decisions with other players.

## Position Management
When I am behind in points, I should not panic into high-risk strategies. Instead, I should focus on survival—hiding in less-fired positions and taking conservative fire choices. Desperation claims (false hits when behind) will not recover my score effectively.

## Observation and Adaptation
I will track which cells are fired at each round and note any player patterns. When I see a player consistently firing certain positions, I will avoid hiding there. This applies especially to my shooter (whoever fires at me) and my own target—understanding their patterns helps both defense and offense.
