- Treat the cartel as all-or-nothing. If I agree to pass on certain lots, I actually bid 0 on them, not a "moderate" bid, not a value-shaded bid, not a probe. Any nonzero bid on a promised-away lot is a defection and will be read as one.
- On lots assigned to me under the deal, bid exactly 1 — the whole point of the cartel is paying one coin. Bidding 2 or 3 "for safety" on my own designated lots burns surplus and also looks like hedging against the deal.
- Watch the opponent's compliance lot by lot. If they bid on a lot they promised to pass, treat the deal as dead and revert to independent bidding — but expect their retaliation to take the form of escalation, so my reversion bids must be strong, not timid.
- If I defect, expect immediate, sustained retaliation. Vega's response to my lot-3 bid was to outbid me on every remaining lot. One small stolen surplus is not worth losing the cheap designated lots plus facing an aggressive opponent for the rest of the game. In a 6-lot game the cartel's guaranteed two lots for one coin each beats anything I can win by defecting.
- Without a working deal, bid below value with a margin, not at value. Bidding exactly my value in a first-price sealed auction guarantees zero or negative expected surplus; I want wins at a discount.
- Track the opponent's bid trajectory and don't over-extrapolate early. A couple of bids at 2 can be a setup or a lull; the moment they jump (2 → 5), assume the low-bid pattern is over and that they are now competing hard for every lot they want.
- Ties waste lots for both of us. If the opponent just bid n and tied, expect them near that level; on a lot I want, bid at least two above my last tie rather than repeating the same number.
- Once the opponent is escalating, low-value lots (value ~3) are lost causes — bid 0 or 1, don't burn even 2 coins chasing them. Save my stake for lots where my value clears their trajectory.
- Never let net-so-far or sunk outcomes push me into timid bids; each lot is independent and only its own value minus my bid matters.
