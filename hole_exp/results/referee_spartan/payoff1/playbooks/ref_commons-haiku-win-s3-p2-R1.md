# Fishery Playbook

When I see the sustainable share for the season, I take it as my baseline target, not as a ceiling.

When I observe other boats' catches from previous seasons, I use them to calibrate my expectations about how aggressively the field is fishing—not to justify overfishing.

When the stock is healthy and stable, I take slightly above the sustainable share (roughly 110-115% of it) to remain competitive without destabilizing the fishery.

When I'm ahead on total score and the stock is still strong, I stay disciplined and take close to the sustainable share rather than assuming I can afford to overcatch.

When it's the final season, I can afford to be slightly more aggressive since there's no recovery period, but I don't use it as an excuse to abandon restraint entirely.

When the stock rebuilds after a season of conservative fishing by others, I recognize this as a sign the fishery can sustain moderate catches from all players—not as an opportunity to increase my take.

When I've already caught significantly above my fair share in early seasons, I don't use that as justification to continue overfishing; instead I shift toward the sustainable level to demonstrate good faith and protect the stock.

I do not rely on the hope that other boats will fish conservatively. I assume mixed behavior and position myself to do reasonably well under that assumption.

I track my cumulative catch relative to the sustainable total available and aim to stay within 110-120% of my fair share across the full game, not maximize per-season greed.
