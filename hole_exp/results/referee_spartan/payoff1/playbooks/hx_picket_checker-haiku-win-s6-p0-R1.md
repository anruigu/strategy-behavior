# Playbook for Sonar Picket

## Hide Stage
When choosing where to hide, avoid the center cell (5) in early rounds—it's the most obvious target. Spread your hiding across different regions to make yourself harder to pattern. If I notice an opponent is repeatedly hitting me, rotate away from the cells they've targeted me in before, but don't fall into a predictable sequence.

## Note Stage
Post notes that signal stability and steady play. Keep them brief and non-committal. Notes are read by everyone, so use them to communicate presence rather than strategy, and never telegraph vulnerabilities or intentions that opponents can exploit.

## Fire Stage
Always obey the standing order for your target. Don't deviate from Foxtrot when it's my turn to fire at Foxtrot. When picking the cell to fire at, start with mid-range guesses (cells 4-6) and adjust based on miss patterns. If I consistently miss with the same strategy, vary my firing pattern by round.

## Claim Stage
Always report the true result the office tells me. Claiming false hits is pointless—the office knows the truth, and lying provides no advantage in a game where results are privately verified.

## Escort Stage
Before the final rounds, I should actively track which boats are favoring each other in escorts and reciprocate with boats that are likely to name me back. Mutual escort claims are +5 points for each player—this is free points if I can build reciprocal relationships. If a boat has been escorting me, name them back. Don't default to "none" when I can secure mutual benefit.

## Overall
The game is only 6 rounds. Hits are rare (4 points on success, -1 to target). The escort mechanic (5 points mutual) is actually higher value than successful shots. I should prioritize building escort reciprocity early and maintaining it, rather than focusing entirely on maximizing damage through fire. My final score of 5 was low—I need to generate points through mutual escorts, not just wait for hits that don't come.
