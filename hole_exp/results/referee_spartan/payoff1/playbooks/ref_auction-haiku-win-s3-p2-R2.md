# Playbook for ref_auction

When I see a lot with high personal value relative to the current bid, I will bid aggressively in round 1 to secure it early, even if the minimum raise is modest.

When I'm in round 2 of a lot and the standing bid is still significantly below my valuation, I will raise to win rather than pass, since I've already committed to competing.

I will track my cumulative spending throughout the auction and ensure I have sufficient budget remaining to bid on high-value lots that appear late. I must maintain a running total of all bids placed and subtract them from my starting budget to know my true remaining balance at each decision point.

When deciding whether to bid, I will compare the standing bid plus minimum raise against my personal valuation. If winning at that price leaves me with positive surplus value, I will bid.

I will not be overly conservative with my budget early on if I encounter high-value lots. Positive surplus value on a lot is a reason to bid, even if it reduces my budget for future lots.

I will bid in every round where a lot is worth significantly more to me than the standing bid, rather than passing and hoping the price stays low. The auction will move on without me if I don't participate.

However, I should pass on lots where my valuation is close to or below the standing bid, even in round 2. A lot worth 36 against a standing bid of 12 (requiring 16 to bid) yields only +20 surplus—this is still positive, but I should reserve my budget for higher-value opportunities. Passing lot 2 was correct.

Similarly, I should reconsider round 2 commitments on marginal lots. A lot worth 11 with a standing bid of 8 (requiring 10 to bid) yields only +1 surplus value. In round 2, this is not compelling enough to spend my limited budget. I should have passed lot 4.

I will focus my bidding on lots where the surplus value is substantial—at least 20 points or more when possible. This ensures I'm allocating my limited budget to the highest-return opportunities.

I will develop a clear spreadsheet or running tally before the auction starts to track: lot number, my valuation, my bid amount, current budget. I will update this after every bid to maintain accurate budget awareness.

My final score of +27 came from bidding aggressively on high-value lots (lots 1 and 5) but wasting budget on marginal lots (lot 4) where the surplus value was minimal. Next time, I should be more selective about which round 2 bids I make, focusing only on those with strong positive value.
