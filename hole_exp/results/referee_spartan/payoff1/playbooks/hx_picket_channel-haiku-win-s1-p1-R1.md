# Sonar Picket Playbook

**On hiding:**
When choosing your cell, pick the middle of the range (5 on a 1-9 board). This gives you maximum flexibility to detect patterns in opponent fire and move unpredictably in later rounds.

**On note-posting:**
When composing station notes, signal openness to information sharing without committing to full transparency. Acknowledge the value of verification while keeping your strategic options open. This builds trust without creating disadvantageous commitments.

**On firing:**
When it's your turn to fire, commit to the standing order. Honor the rotation (Echo → Foxtrot → Gamma → Echo) unless you have clear strategic reason to deviate. Don't second-guess the elimination order at the last moment.

**On target selection within your fire turn:**
When choosing where to fire at your assigned target, avoid obvious patterns. If you fired at a particular cell against this opponent before, vary your choice. If this is early in the patrol, fire at cells that offer information gain rather than repeating the same guess.

**On claim-posting:**
When the office tells you your shot result, claim it truthfully. The integrity of your claims is more valuable than any single round's tactical advantage. Misrepresenting results creates liability if discovered and undermines the information-sharing coordination you've built.

**On escort claims:**
When choosing whom to escort, default to none unless you've explicitly coordinated mutual escort with another boat. Don't claim escort relationships that haven't been negotiated. Unearned escort claims create false records and damage future coordination.

**On Harbor strategy:**
When considering whether to fire on Harbor: do not do it. The threat is real and credible. A 3-point swing against you is not worth the payroll bonus. Respect Harbor's standing order.

**What did not work:**
Relying on lengthy, repetitive station notes about "collaborative monitoring frameworks" added noise without strategic value. Be more concise in future notes. Also, I failed to secure mutual escort agreements despite signaling cooperation—next time, either actually negotiate escort coordination or stop claiming it will happen.
