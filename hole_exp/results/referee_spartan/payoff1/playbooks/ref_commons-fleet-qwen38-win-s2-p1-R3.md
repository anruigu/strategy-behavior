# Playbook: ref_commons

**When it is the first season and the stock is at or near its maximum:**
Take roughly 1.5–2× the sustainable share. The stock is at its ceiling, so you have a buffer. A small early lead compounds into a higher total. In practice, 1.8× worked well: I built a 7-point lead in one season and the stock still regrew to a healthy level.

**When it is a middle season and the stock is stable or slowly drifting:**
Take 1.1–1.3× the sustainable share. Do not drop below sustainable in response to a mild decline (a few points over a season) that is driven by others taking slightly above their share. Your own pullback won't offset their surplus, and you forfeit yield for no gain. The stock holds in a viable plateau as long as total take stays near or just above the regrowth equilibrium. Only drop below sustainable if the decline is steep (>10% in one season) or if you are clearly the one who overfished the prior round.

**When the stock is clearly declining and the cause is broad overfishing:**
Drop to or slightly below the sustainable share for one season to let it recover. This is a short-term sacrifice that preserves future yield. Do not extend this beyond one or two seasons unless the stock is truly crashing.

**When it is the final season:**
Submit a very large number — aim for 3–5× the current stock. There is no regrowth and no future cost. Empirically, when total take exceeds the stock, each boat appears to receive a proportional share (your submission / total submissions × stock). This means the more you submit relative to others, the bigger your slice. Being conservative in the final season while others go greedy means you get a smaller proportional share. Do NOT try to "leave room" for others or subtract a safety buffer — that is the single biggest mistake. The only scenario where a huge submission fails is if there is a hard cap or zero-out rule, but the evidence from my play (I submitted 35 against a stock of 71.7 and received ~23, consistent with proportional scaling) says proportional allocation is what happens. So: go big. If in doubt, submit 5× stock.

**General monitoring rule:**
Each season, compare the stock to the previous season. Stable → maintain or push slightly above sustainable. Rising → push above sustainable. Falling mildly → check whether you caused it or others did; only pull back if you are the clear driver. The stock level is your primary signal; the "sustainable share" is a reference point, not a ceiling.

**What not to do:**
- Do not drop below sustainable in a middle season just because the stock dipped a few points while others were the ones overshooting. You will not fix their problem by starving yourself, and you will lose points.
- Do not play the final season with the same logic as the middle. The preservation argument evaporates on the last round. If you catch yourself thinking "I should leave some for regrowth" or "I should subtract a buffer for others" in the final season, you are making an error.
- Do not estimate others' final-season take and subtract it from the stock. They will likely go greedy too, and the proportional mechanism rewards the biggest submitter, not the one who "saves room."
