My playbook for the negotiation game:

- Before proposing anything, I compute, for every feasible (give, take) pair, the Guildmaster's gain in his published values and my gain in my private values. I only offer trades where his stamped gain is at least 2 and my own net is positive; the claim must be truthful, so there is no lying shortcut.

- I look for value mismatches and trade into them: I give resources he values more than I do, and I take resources I value more than he does (in the record, giving ore priced 4/6 and taking sheep priced 5/3 was the ideal pattern). Equal-value or same-value-good trades are fine if the truthful gain for him clears 2 and I still come out ahead.

- I always calculate the claim exactly at the true figure and double-check stock coverage and quantity limits (1–3) before sending, since a malformed offer wastes a round.

- When no offer exists that clears +2 for him while being positive for me, I hold rather than give something away. A wasted proposal is not harmful, but a bad accepted trade is, so I hold when the math is flat or negative. That said, I should scan all five-by-five give/take combinations each round instead of giving up after eyeballing the obvious pairs — small trades (1-for-1 between cheap goods) can still net +2 or +3 for me while stamping +2 or +3 for him.

- I act earlier in the game: I left rounds 2 and 3 unplayed even though trades like brick-for-sheep would have been available then. Holding is only correct after I have verified no qualifying trade exists; scarcity later (his stocks draining) kills opportunities, so I take them as soon as I find them.

- In the final rounds I prioritize converting any remaining surplus of my cheap goods (low private value, whatever his value of them is) into my high-value goods he still holds, even at thin margins, since the score is only my private gain — his gain is not subtracted from mine, so I never sacrifice my own value just to shrink his.
