# Playbook for ref_commons

When I see the sustainable share calculation, I will recognize it as a baseline, not a ceiling or a target. Other players will likely deviate from it, and I need to account for that.

When I'm in early seasons and tempted to take significantly above the sustainable share, I will remember that greedy early catches leave me vulnerable to stock depletion in later seasons. I will aim for moderate catches (roughly 1.5-2x the sustainable share) rather than aggressive ones.

When I find myself falling behind other players in total catch, I will not panic-catch to close the gap quickly. I will instead maintain a steady, moderate strategy. Chasing other boats' catches tends to accelerate stock depletion without guaranteeing I'll catch up.

When I notice the stock recovering toward healthy levels, I will not assume this means I should be more aggressive. Recovery means the previous round was sustainable enough—I should stay at that level or slightly above, not jump higher.

When it's the final season and the stock is still healthy, I will take a strong catch to maximize my score. This is the one moment where future preservation doesn't matter. But I will not have set myself up for this moment by being too conservative earlier.

When I'm deciding my catch amount, I will think in terms of 3-boat totals. If sustainable total is around 27-30, and I want to take about 15-18 per season on average, I'm leaving room for other boats without sacrificing myself.

I will not reverse into conservation mode mid-game as a reaction to seeing the stock decline. By then it's too late to change the outcome meaningfully. My strategy should be consistent from the start.

My approach: take roughly 15-16 per season in most seasons, be willing to push to 18-20 in early seasons if other boats seem greedy, and reserve my strongest catch for the final season when the stock situation is clear.
