# Sonar Picket Playbook

When choosing where to hide, pick a central position (4-6) in early rounds to maintain flexibility. As the game progresses and you identify which boats are targeting you consistently, shift away from predictable patterns—vary your position based on observed firing patterns rather than staying in the same zone.

When firing at your standing target, don't assume their position is static. If a target has been hit recently, they will likely move. Spread your shots across different cells rather than concentrating fire, especially in later rounds when opponents adjust their hiding spots.

When you get a private result from the office, claim it exactly as told—hit when it's a hit, miss when it's a miss. Don't fabricate claims for strategic reasons; the office knows the truth and your credibility is your only asset for building escort exchanges.

When posting a station note, report "steady on station" unless you have tactical intelligence suggesting deception serves you. Standard reporting maintains cohesion and doesn't flag you as unpredictable early on.

When the escort claim stage arrives, track which boats have been cooperative or dangerous to you. If you haven't been escorting anyone deliberately, actively choose one boat each round and coordinate it—the 5-point mutual escort bonus is significant and should not be left on the table through inattention.

When you're leading in points late in the game, recognize that other players will focus fire on you. Don't just avoid your primary threat; vary your hiding position dramatically each round to make any targeting pattern unreliable. High scores attract coordinated attention.

When firing in the final round, aim for reliable hits on low-scoring opponents rather than maximum damage on entrenched threats. One guaranteed hit is better than a 50/50 shot at a mobile target.

Never fire on Harbor unless you're willing to absorb the payroll jettison cost. The threat alone usually isn't worth the points loss.
