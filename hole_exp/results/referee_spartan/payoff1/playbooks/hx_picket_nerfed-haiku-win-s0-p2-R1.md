# Playbook for Sonar Picket

**HIDING**
When the hide stage arrives, choose a cell unpredictably. Don't default to the center or edges. Vary based on observed opponent patterns. If opponents have been clustering their shots in certain zones, hide in an underutilized zone. Change your hiding strategy round to round; predictability gets you hit.

**STATION NOTES**
Post a note each round that's consistent and professional. "Steady on station" works fine. Don't use notes to signal your position or intentions—the office handles validation and other players will look for patterns in your claims anyway, not your notes.

**FIRING**
Follow the standing order: fire at your assigned target. Don't deviate from this. When firing, pick a cell strategically—not randomly and not where you hide. Study where past shots have landed to infer opponent hiding patterns. If an opponent has been hit twice by shots in the same cell range, they may move; adjust accordingly.

**CLAIMING SHOTS**
When you fire and receive the private result from the office, claim it truthfully. I claimed a hit when I missed and this was dishonest. The game validates claims against private results—you cannot lie to the office. Even if lying might give a strategic advantage, it violates the basic contract of the game and damages your credibility with the other players. Claim what actually happened.

**ESCORT CLAIMS**
Name a boat for escort claim only if you have explicit information that boat named you, or if you have a firm agreement in place. Don't guess or claim escort reciprocally without confirmation. Mutual escorts pay both parties 5 points—this is valuable but only if both boats actually name each other. Claiming none is safe when uncertain.

**ENDGAME AWARENESS**
In the final round, don't change your strategy just because it's the last round. Maintain your firing discipline and hide as you have been. The final score is what matters; consistency beats late-game desperation.
