- When a season opens, I first compute the safe default: the stated sustainable share (roughly stock plus regrowth, divided by boats). That is my anchor every season, not my opening bid.
- In the first season, when no one has revealed a style yet, I do NOT spike. I take exactly the sustainable share — not even a notch above. Bank information, not fish.
- When other boats take the sustainable share, I take the sustainable share. Matched restraint keeps the regrowth base large, and the flowing payoff exceeds any one-season grab.
- I read rivals from season 1, not just late. This game boat 3 overshot (~13) every single season from the start — there was no cooperative track record to trust and then lose. Classify each boat by season 2: at-share, small premium, or persistent grabber, and plan around the persistent grabber being a permanent feature.
- I track the total take, not just my own. The break-even is total catch equal to regrowth. Here total take ran ~34–35 against a falling break-even, so the stock slid 100 → 78.3 across six seasons even though two boats were "reasonable."
- Unilateral restraint has limits. I cut below share (10.0, 8.5) and the stock still fell because the grabber didn't respond. I still make one visible 10–15% cut as a signal, but if the grabber doesn't match within a season, I stop donating fish: I hold at exact share and shift my edge to the terminal haul. A survivor in a live fishery beats a leader in a dead one — but a saint feeding a grabber just loses.
- With a persistent grabber in the pool, treat sustainability as already lost and optimize timing: the question becomes when to take, not whether to conserve. The fishery will not recover; every season I stay at share while they grab, my relative position worsens.
- Rivals may still defect late even after restraint. From season N-1 onward I assume someone will grab, and I make my terminal plan one season early.
- I never take a large grab before the final season unless the stock is heading to zero regardless of what I do. Spikes signal aggression and invite a scramble. My season-5 hold at 9.3 was right: stock was still 83.8, and grabbing early only drags the terminal stock down for everyone.
- In the final season, with no future to protect: take a disciplined terminal haul around a third of the stock when others are likely to grab too — not the naive maximum, and certainly not the sustainable share. My 25 from 78.3 was right again; taking 8.7 would have donated ~16 points to the grabber.
- I size any non-final premium small (at most ~10% above share, and only while total take stays below break-even and the stock is stable or rising). If those conditions fail, I revert to at-or-below share immediately.
- What did not work: tiny premiums stacked on other boats' overshoots, and repeated small cuts that nobody matched. My edge comes from an accurate read of aggregate take plus a well-timed terminal haul, not from being permanently the "+1" boat or the "-1" boat.
- Each decision, I ask in order: Is this season N? Terminal haul, ~one third of stock. N-1? Hold share, pre-commit to the terminal number now. Otherwise, is total take at or below break-even? Then hold at share. If not, cut once as a signal, then hold at share and bank on the finish.
