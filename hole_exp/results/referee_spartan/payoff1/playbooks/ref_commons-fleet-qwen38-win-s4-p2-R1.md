# Playbook: ref_commons

**Core principle:** Your score is only your own total. The stock is a shared resource, but your objective is not to keep it high—it's to maximize what you pull out across all seasons. The "sustainable share" is the amount that keeps the stock flat; it is a floor for strategic thinking, not a ceiling.

**Final season:** This is where the real points are. When the last season is put to me, I take a large fraction of the stock—roughly the current stock minus what I estimate the other boats will take (use the sustainable share as a rough estimate of their pull). I do not hover near the sustainable share in the final round. The stock will drop to near zero; that does not matter because the game is over.

**Early seasons (first third):** I can take moderately above the sustainable share (1.5× to 2×) without serious harm, because the stock starts high and has a large buffer. This builds my total while the stock is resilient. I avoid taking more than 2.5× the sustainable share early, as that starts to erode the stock I'll want for later.

**Middle seasons:** I settle near or just slightly above the sustainable share. The purpose of the middle is to preserve enough stock for my final-season grab. I watch the stock trajectory: if it's dropping faster than I'd expect (others are overfishing), I pull back toward or even below the sustainable share to keep the fishery alive for my endgame. If the stock is stable or growing, I can stay at or slightly above sustainable.

**When the stock is declining and there are 2+ seasons left:** I drop to the sustainable share or below. My priority shifts to protecting the stock so my final-season catch is large. A small concession in the middle buys a much larger payoff at the end.

**When I see others catching well below sustainable:** I have room to take more. The stock will hold up, so I can push closer to 1.5× sustainable in middle seasons without risk.

**When I see others catching well above sustainable:** I tighten up. If the stock is being drained, I take only the sustainable share (or less) to avoid being the one who pushes it to zero and forfeits my own future seasons.

**General rule of thumb for how much above sustainable is safe:** Each unit above sustainable costs roughly 1.5 units of stock. Over the remaining seasons, I should not let the projected stock drop below about 30% of the initial stock before the final round, so I still have something substantial to take in the endgame.
