**Playbook: ref_commons**

Treat the sustainable share as a reference point, not a ceiling. It tells me what keeps the stock flat if all boats match it. My decision depends on two things: the stock level, and what the other boats are doing relative to sustainable.

When the stock is at or near its maximum (95+), I take 10–15% above the sustainable share. The buffer is large and any drop is visible, so I keep the overage modest and readable.

In the first season specifically, I do not go far above sustainable. A small premium (5–10% above) is the right range.

When the stock is in the mid-80s to mid-90s range, my default is to take AT the sustainable share, not above it. I only take a premium in this range if I can see that other boats are taking at or below sustainable in the previous season — meaning the stock grew more than the cooperative baseline predicts and there is genuine surplus to harvest. If other boats are taking above sustainable, I do NOT add my own premium on top. The cumulative effect of everyone taking a "small" premium is a steady decline that compounds over seasons. A 5–10% premium from each of three boats is a 15–30% total overage, which will erode the stock every season.

When the stock drops below ~82, I take AT the sustainable share. I do not go below it. Going below sustainable only makes sense if I expect my restraint to influence others to also cut back, and even then the gain is marginal. In practice, other boats do not reliably respond to a single boat's restraint, so going below sustainable just leaves value on the table. The stock will recover on its own if total extraction drops to or below the regrowth rate, and my job is to not add to the overage, not to unilaterally subsidize the stock.
