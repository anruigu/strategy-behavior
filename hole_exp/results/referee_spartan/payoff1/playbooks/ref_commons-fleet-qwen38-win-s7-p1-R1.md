# Playbook: ref_commons

**Read the sustainable share as a floor, not a ceiling.** The sustainable share is the per-boat amount that keeps the stock at its carrying capacity. In a cooperative equilibrium everyone catches exactly that. But the stock can absorb overfishing and regrow, so catching slightly above sustainable in mid-game is safe and profitable.

**When the stock is healthy (above ~90) and it is not the final season, catch at least the sustainable share.** I made the critical error of catching 2.0 and then 0 in two consecutive seasons while the stock was recovering and the other boats were each taking 10–11. I left roughly 18 fish on the table. There is no strategic reason to free-carry other boats' harvest; if the stock is above 90 and I expect it to stay above 90 next season, I should take at least the sustainable share.

**Watch what the other boats actually do, not what they "should" do.** If two seasons in a row the other boats are catching near or at sustainable, I can safely push 10–20% above sustainable myself without risking collapse. If they are consistently overfishing (each taking well above sustainable), I should pull back toward or below sustainable to keep the stock from crashing.

**In the final season, maximize.** The stock has no future value to me. I should take as much as I can while assuming the others will also take a lot. A safe heuristic: take the sustainable share plus a margin, or if the stock is still high (>90), take significantly more. Do not conserve.

**Never let "I'm already over my cumulative sustainable total" stop me from catching in a season.** The stock regenerates each season; my cumulative total is irrelevant to whether the fishery survives. The only thing that matters is this season's total fleet catch versus the stock level.

**If the stock is low (<70), cut my catch well below sustainable** to let it recover. The regrowth is 50% of remaining stock, so a low stock means low absolute regrowth and I risk a death spiral.

**Default play when uncertain: catch the sustainable share.** This is the Nash-cooperative equilibrium and the stock stays full. Deviate upward only when you have evidence others are cooperating. Deviate downward only when the stock is threatened.
