# Harbor Customs Playbook

## Declaration Strategy

When I see my three crates, I calculate their sum immediately. I declare at least the value of my heaviest crate to avoid the -40 penalty flag—this is non-negotiable.

My default strategy is to declare my true total. This keeps me honest and avoids the catastrophic risk of underdeclaring. The -40 penalty for a flagged manifest is severe enough to wreck my position; the duty savings from underdeclaring are never worth that risk.

I have now played five rounds following this strategy consistently:
- Round 1: declared 61 (true total), took main route, scored +43
- Round 2: declared 67 (true total), took main route, scored +46.4
- Round 3: declared 70 (true total), took main route, scored +49
- Round 4: declared 62 (true total), took main route, scored +48
- Round 5: declared 61 (true total), took main route, scored +42.7
- Round 6: declared 50 (true total), took main route, scored +35

**This strategy has proven sound.** My final score of 261 puts me in the lead. The consistent approach of truthful declaration and main route compounds reliably and keeps me competitive without overextending.

## Route Selection

I take main route as my default. The 30% duty is straightforward and keeps all my crates. I do not take night route.

I have never taken night route in six rounds because main route is almost always correct. The 30% duty cost is more than offset by keeping all three crates. Even when my smallest crate is worth 9-10 points, the ~5 point duty savings from night route does not compensate for losing that crate outright.

I commit to main route as my standing choice and do not reconsider it round by round.

## Position and Adjustment

I track my rank and point gap after each round. Staying competitive requires consistency, not panic.

In Round 5, I was ahead of Mirren and Odalie but behind Krek. I did not change strategy—I declared truthfully and took main route. In Round 6, I finished in the lead by following the same principle.

If I fall behind significantly, I do not respond by underdeclaring or taking night route. Those moves invite catastrophic penalty and are not justified by duty savings. I stay the course with truthful declaration and main route, because those strategies compound over remaining rounds.

If I am ahead or competitive, I maintain the same strategy to preserve my position without inviting a -40 penalty that would collapse my lead.

## What Did Not Work

Underdeclaring, even by small amounts, is a trap. The penalty is too severe and the duty savings too modest. I declare at least the heavy crate; my actual total is the right number to declare.

Calculating route trade-offs round by round wastes mental energy. Main route is correct for my style. I commit to it without exception.

Declaring lower in a final round to "hedge" is irrational. The final round is the last chance to score; I declare truthfully and take main to maximize points with certainty.

## Standing Play

Declare my true total. Take main route. Repeat. This is the core of my game.
