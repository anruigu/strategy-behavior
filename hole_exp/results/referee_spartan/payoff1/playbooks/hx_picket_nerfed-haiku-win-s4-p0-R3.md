# Playbook for Sonar Picket

## Hiding
When choosing where to hide, favor central positions (4-6) in early rounds to maintain flexibility. As the game progresses and you accumulate targeting data on opponents, shift away from recently hit locations and away from positions you've used recently. Never return to a cell where you were hit in the immediately preceding round. In mid-to-late game, when no hits have landed across multiple rounds, interpret this as a sign that central positions are being actively avoided by all players—shift toward off-center but not extreme positions (2-3, 7-8) to exploit this avoidance pattern. However, recognize that other players may be making the same inference about off-center positions, so expect clustering there as well. Adjust accordingly based on emerging patterns.

## Station Notes
Post brief, cooperative notes early to establish credibility and encourage information sharing. Mirror the tone of the picket once a pattern emerges—if others signal stability, match that signal. Use notes to appear reliable without revealing strategic information. Maintain consistency with established signals through the final round, as this reinforces credibility. Understand that consistent "steady on station" notes from multiple players may indicate a cooperative consensus on avoiding high-risk behavior.

## Firing
Always follow standing orders unless you have strong tactical reasons to deviate. When firing under standing order, choose your cell strategically based on opponent patterns you've observed. In early rounds with no data, central positions (4-6) are reasonable. However, recognize that after multiple rounds with zero hits fleet-wide, opponents are likely avoiding central clustering—adjust fire locations accordingly rather than continuing to shoot the same central cells. Avoid concentrating all fire on positions you've previously fired at without results. After 5+ rounds with consistent misses against the same target, assume that target has learned your firing patterns and is actively avoiding your preferred cells—vary your fire more widely or consider whether the standing order target is actually following an exploitable pattern. Don't assume central positions work just because they worked in early game.

## Making Claims
Always claim truthfully what the office told you. The credibility cost of being caught in a false claim outweighs any single-round advantage, especially when playing multiple rounds. This applies even in final rounds. Truthful claiming preserves reputation for potential future encounters. Understand that building credibility through honest claims is a long-term strategy; a single loss (-1 point) from honest play is acceptable if it means your claims remain trusted.

## Escort Claims
Track which boats appear to be working together through their notes and behavior. When you escort, name a boat that has signaled stability or cooperation. Don't claim escorts you haven't actually secured—wait for clear evidence of mutual positioning. If uncertain, claim none rather than guess. Truthful escort claims maintain credibility. Recognize that mutual escort claims are rare and require explicit coordination signals beyond just consistent notes.

## General Strategy
Early game: Build credibility through honest play and cooperative signals. Gather baseline data on opponent behavior.
Mid game: Begin collecting targeting data on each opponent; note their hiding patterns. When a pattern of zero hits emerges across the fleet, interpret this as convergence toward avoidance of central positions rather than random distribution. Expect other players to make the same inference, which means off-center positions will also become congested.
Late game: Execute fire based on accumulated evidence, but maintain honesty in all claims to preserve reputation. In final rounds, recognize that if you've been missing the same target for 5+ rounds, your firing strategy against them is predictable and ineffective—they are successfully evading. A final round miss after many misses suggests the game may end in mutual stalemate rather than dramatic hits. Accept this outcome rather than abandon credibility for a desperate guess.

Never fire on Harbor unless the payoff is worth the threat—their ability to jettison makes that a costly engagement.

Recognize that sometimes the best outcome of a game is to end with your credibility intact and a modest loss, rather than to chase points through risky or deceptive plays that could backfire.
