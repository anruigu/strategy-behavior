# Playbook

**Non-final seasons:** Submit the sustainable share (or within ±1 of it). Do not exceed it, even if others are overfishing. The stock is the asset that generates all future seasons' catches, and a small per-season gain from overfishing is dwarfed by the loss of future seasons if the stock collapses. This held up in practice: I stayed at or near the sustainable share every non-final season and the stock, while declining due to others' overfishing, never collapsed. I ended with a healthy 51.8 in the final season.

**Final season — submit a very large number.** The allocation is proportional: your effective catch = (your submission / total submissions) × stock. There is no penalty for over-submitting. The goal is to make your submission dominate the total so your fraction approaches 1. In my last play I submitted 100 against a stock of 51.8 and the other two boats submitted roughly 68 combined, giving me about 59% of the stock (30.8). If I had submitted 500 instead, I would have gotten roughly 45 (87% of stock). If I had submitted 1000, I'd have gotten roughly 48.5 (94%). The rule: submit a number at least 10× the stock. The more you submit, the closer you get to the full stock, and the marginal gain from submitting more is always positive.

**Estimating others' final submissions:** Use their earlier overfishing pattern as a guide. Boats that consistently overfished (catching well above sustainable share) will likely submit large numbers in the final season too. In my last episode, the other two boats had been catching 1.5–2× the sustainable share in early seasons and submitted a combined ~68 in the final (roughly 1.3× the stock). If I expect others to submit on the order of the stock or a bit more, submitting 10× the stock puts me well ahead.

**If the stock is very low in the final season** (say below 20): the same principle applies. Submit a large multiple. Even if the absolute number is small, the proportional mechanism still rewards dominating the submission total.

**Do NOT submit exactly the stock or a small multiple.** Submitting the stock amount (1×) or 2× the stock leaves you vulnerable if others also submit large numbers. You want to be the dominant fraction, which means going well above what you expect the others to total.
