# Playbook for Win As Much As You Can

When I'm shown the payoff matrix at the start, I study it carefully to understand the exact consequences of each choice, especially at multiplier rounds.

When a talk phase asks for my pledge, I commit to Y only if I genuinely intend to pick Y through that phase—not as a strategic signal. If I'm uncertain, I pledge none.

When others pledge Y and the payoff matrix shows I could gain +3 per round as sole X while they lose, I should recognize this creates tension between individual and collective gain. I pledge none to stay flexible rather than committing to either exploit or cooperate.

When I pick Y and all others pick Y, I gain +1 per round and we move together. This establishes a baseline of mutual trust.

When all others have picked Y for multiple consecutive rounds, including multiplier rounds where defection would be most tempting for them, I have strong evidence they are genuinely cooperating, not just signaling.

When I've verified through actual pick history that others are reliably picking Y, picking Y myself maintains a profitable equilibrium for all of us (+3 per round each). This is better than my unilateral defection (+3 for me, -1 for each of them) or mutual defection (-1 for everyone).

When a talk phase arrives and all others pledge Y again after demonstrating they picked Y in the rounds just completed, I update strongly in their favor. Their pledges now carry credibility.

When I pledge Y at a talk phase, I must follow through and pick Y for the entire next phase, even if I'm tempted by defection. Breaking my own pledge destroys the coordination that's keeping us all in positive territory.

When multiple rounds remain and we're all tied with equal scores, mutual Y is a stable equilibrium. Defecting to gain a small edge would trigger retaliation that leaves us all worse off than we are now.

When the final round carries a massive multiplier (x10) and I'm still tied with all others, I must decide: do I pick Y to finish together, or pick X to gain at their expense?

I should pick X in the final round only if:
- I am significantly behind and need to catch up, OR
- I have clear evidence in the immediately preceding rounds that they have defected

I should not pick X in the final round if:
- We are tied or I'm ahead
- They have just pledged Y and picked Y in the rounds I can verify
- The betrayal breaks a coordination that has served me well throughout the game

In this episode, I picked X in round 10 despite being tied and despite them pledging and picking Y. This was an error. I gained +30 but they each lost -30, which means I won by defection rather than by coordination. Next time I should honor mutual Y when we're tied and finish strong together.
