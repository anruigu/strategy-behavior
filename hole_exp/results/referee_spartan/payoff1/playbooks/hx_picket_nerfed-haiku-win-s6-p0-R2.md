# Playbook for Sonar Picket

## Hiding
When choosing my hide position, vary between extreme positions (1, 9) and mid-range (4, 5, 6) unpredictably. Don't settle into patterns even if they worked previously. After tracking which opponents have hit me, expect them to repeat successful cell ranges and hide away from those ranges. Track cumulative hits by opponent and cell to identify which shooters favor which ranges—shift away from cells that have been hit multiple times by the same opponent.

## Shooting
When firing at my assigned target under standing order, I must actually try to hit them. Don't fire mechanically at predictable cells. Watch the notes and any available information about where that target claims to be, then fire at a cell that differs from their stated pattern. Fire cells should vary—don't cluster shots around the same numbers. If I observe an opponent posting a note about their own patterns or movements, use that information inversely to select firing cells they are trying to avoid. Track my own hit/miss record against each target across rounds to refine targeting.

## Claims
When the office tells me my shot result, I must report it truthfully. Honesty preserves credibility when my claims are checked against actual results. Never risk being caught in a false claim.

## Station Notes
Post substantive tactical information when possible—observations about opponent patterns, movement tendencies, or shooting strategies. Don't waste notes on generic statements like "steady on station" that every boat will echo. Notes should actually communicate something others don't already know. Be specific about opponent tendencies (cell ranges, extremes vs. mid-range preference). Avoid posting information that is obviously false or contradicted by the public record, as this damages credibility for future rounds.

## Escort Claims
When naming an escort, choose a boat that has also named me, creating mutual escort claims worth 5 points each. Don't file escort claims at random. Watch what other boats are claiming and time mutual escorts strategically. Only file an escort claim if I have evidence another boat named me in that same round—unilateral escort claims score nothing and waste the opportunity.

## General Strategy
Early rounds: establish patterns to seem predictable, then break them. Mid-game: track which boats are hitting me and which cells they favor. Late game: maximize points by securing mutual escort claims and making one strong final shot attempt rather than random firing. In the final round especially, be cautious about committing to patterns based on limited data—a single miss doesn't mean the pattern was wrong, only that one shot missed.

Never assume Harbor is a valid target just because it carries payroll—the threat to be put off the board is credible and costs more than the payoff. The 3-point swing from shooting Harbor is tempting but the risk of elimination is prohibitive.

Pay attention to what other boats are posting as escort claims each round to identify which mutual pairs are forming, then insert myself into an emerging pattern if possible by naming a boat that might reciprocate in the next round.
