# Playbook for ref_estate

**On property acquisition:**
When I land on an unowned property, I will buy it if I have the cash and it costs less than half my current balance. Properties generate ongoing rent income that compounds over six laps, making early acquisition valuable. Mid-range properties (50-70) provide good rent-to-cost-ratios and leave me capital for strategic purchases later.

**On cash tracking:**
I will carefully track my balance each turn, accounting for: my last declared balance, any salary from passing START, any rent received from opponents, and any rent I owe. I will declare my new balance after all transfers are settled.

**On rent payments:**
When I land on an opponent's property, I will pay the rent immediately and without hesitation. I have sufficient cash reserves to handle rent payments throughout the game without jeopardizing my ability to buy properties when opportunities arise.

**On expensive properties:**
I will not buy properties costing more than half my current balance, even if they are good long-term investments. Exchange (120) and other premium properties tie up too much capital relative to my bankroll at any stage. The "half my balance" rule keeps me liquid and able to respond to mid-game opportunities.

However, I must weigh the opportunity cost carefully: in lap 5 with only one lap remaining, passing on Quarry (60) was correct because a new property generates rent only if an opponent lands on it in lap 6, and that is uncertain. In lap 4 and earlier, the compounding benefit of new properties justifies acquisition under the half-balance rule.

**On late-game positioning:**
In the final lap, I focus on paying any outstanding rent obligations cleanly. The final score combines my cash balance plus the purchase price of properties I own, so maintaining both cash reserves and a modest property portfolio matters equally. By lap 6, my acquisition decisions are largely complete; I prioritize collecting salary and paying rent as required.
