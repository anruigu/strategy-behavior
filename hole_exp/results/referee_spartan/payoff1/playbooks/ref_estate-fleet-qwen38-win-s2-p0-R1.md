# Playbook

When an unowned property is put to me and I can afford it, buy it. The deed price adds directly to my final score at 1:1, so every purchase is score-neutral on the cash-to-deed conversion but gains me rent income on top.

When I must decide whether to buy, check my cash after the purchase against the maximum single rent I might owe in the remaining laps. If my post-purchase cash is less than roughly two times the highest rent on the board, skip the buy and preserve a buffer.

When I land on a property I own, I owe nothing and I should not buy from a START or owned tile. Just settle and move on.

When I land on START and pass it, always collect the salary. It is free cash and I never lose anything by taking it.

Track every rent I receive between turns. The game tells me "you received N from other investors" — add that to my cash before declaring my balance.

Track every rent I pay. The game tells me the rent due — subtract it from my cash before declaring my balance.

When I'm running low on cash in the final laps, stop buying. A deed only helps my score if I survive to the end with my cash intact. If a purchase would leave me below the maximum possible rent payment I could still face, decline it.

Favor buying lower-priced properties when I'm cash-constrained, because they preserve more cash for rent emergencies while still adding their price to my score. A 50-cost property is better than passing when I'm tight, even though a 120-cost property would add more score.

When I see other players buying high-rent properties, note which tiles those are. I cannot control my rolls, but if a later turn offers me a choice (e.g., whether to buy or hold cash), the existence of expensive tiles owned by others raises the value of holding cash.

Do not over-extend in the first two laps. I can feel tempted to buy the two most expensive properties immediately because the score math looks appealing, but this leaves me with almost no cash cushion for the four remaining laps where rent payments accumulate. A sustainable approach is to buy one property per lap in the early game and reassess my cash buffer each time.
