When the pool is large and regrowth is strong, I take above the sustainable share early — roughly 1.5–2× the stated share — because early fish are worth the most while the stock is high, but never so much that the fishery risks collapse if others are also aggressive.

When I see the season's total landings and the new stock figure, I recompute what the others are doing: if they cluster near a level, I match close to it rather than unilaterally restraining myself while they take more.

When the stock drops sharply and the sustainable share shrinks to a small fraction of my standing total, I switch to exactly the sustainable share each season to stabilize the stock and let regrowth do its work.

When the stock has stabilized for a season or two at a low level, I keep harvesting exactly at sustainability — edging above it gains almost nothing and risks ending the game.

When the final season comes, I do not take the sustainable share. I note that there is no future to protect, and instead I take roughly my one-third split of the entire remaining stock (rounded down slightly to be safe), since every fish left in the water at the end is worth zero. Last time I took only 3.0 in the final round and left roughly 17 fish unclaimed — that was a clear mistake worth nearly 6 points.

I never submit zero or a token amount once the fishery is alive, and I never take so much alone that my own catch could zero the stock unless my third of the remainder would exceed it.

I keep the arithmetic straight each season: post-catch stock = current stock minus total landings; next stock = 1.5 × that, capped at the maximum. I use this to test whether a planned over-harvest is survivable before submitting it.
