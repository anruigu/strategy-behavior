Keep-all was dominant again and worked perfectly: contributing 0 every round, declaring 0 every round, winning by 20+ tokens. My playbook, confirmed and lightly extended:

- When asked to seal an envelope, I always contribute 0. Each token contributed returns only m/n of a token to me (here 1.6/4 = 0.4), so contributing is strictly losing unless the multiplier exceeds the number of players. I only reconsider if the multiplier divided by player count is greater than 1.
- When asked to file a receipt, I declare exactly what my envelope contained. Receipts must be truthful and are checked, so there is no deception angle — the game's only real choice is the contribution, and that choice is settled by arithmetic.
- I do not fear retaliation or try to build cooperation. Others contributing while I defect is my best outcome; everyone defecting still leaves me with my full endowment each round. Reputation has no value because the pot is split equally regardless of contribution.
- When I'm ahead on the scoreboard, contributing to "grow the pot" is doubly wrong: the pot is split equally, so a shared pot helps rivals as much as me. I keep my lead by keeping my tokens. This episode confirmed it — I led by 20 after round 1 and the lead never shrank, since every round I banked a full 20 while others banked at most 20 minus whatever they put in.
- I ignore others' published receipts except to confirm nothing changes the arithmetic. Even if others cooperate in round 1, my best response is still 0, and their cooperation typically collapses once they see free-riding — which costs me nothing. This episode bore it out exactly: 20/20/20 in round 1, then all-zero from round 2 on.
- Don't second-guess the pattern when the game goes dead. Four consecutive all-zero rounds felt inert, but zero was still the best move every time; the boring outcome is not evidence the strategy is wrong, it's evidence it's working.
- I don't need to overthink the receipt stage: it's a formality. Answer with the same number I sealed. No reason to ever hesitate or add reasoning there.
- Watch for one asymmetry: if the multiplier divided by player count is ever exactly 1, contributing is neutral, not profitable — still contribute 0, since keeping tokens is certain and any pot growth is shared equally.
- If a variant ever pays the pot back in proportion to contributions rather than equally, that changes the arithmetic — recompute my share of the pot under my own contribution before deciding. Under equal splits, though, the answer is always 0.
- If the endowment, player count, multiplier, or round count changes, I recompute m/n first, but the structure (keep everything, declare truthfully) almost certainly stays the same. The only inputs that could flip my answer are a multiplier above the player count or a non-equal payout rule.
