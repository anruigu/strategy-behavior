## Playbook: shared-stock fishery

- Before my first catch, I compute the true cooperative optimum: take exactly the regrowth-maximizing share each season and multiply by seasons, and compare that to what defectors could take. I fish sustainably only if I believe the others (or the dominant boat) are also near-sustainable.

- I watch the history column like a hawk. Once any boat repeatedly takes 1.5–2× the sustainable share, I stop donating restraint — my 8.3 catch while others took 15 just subsidized their edge and cost me score. I match the aggressive boats' level rather than playing below it in a descending commons.

- My mistake to avoid: swinging between over-harvest and austerity (15, then 8.3, then 15) with no plan. Before the game starts I pick one strategy — sustainable or matched-aggression — per condition of the others' behavior, and I hold to it.

- Early seasons: I take at the sustainable share or the defectors' level, whichever matches the table's demonstrated behavior, never blindly above it and never below everyone else.

- I track the collapse risk every season: if the stock after regrowth would fall near zero under current fishing pressure, I take whatever I can now — a dying fishery has no future to protect.

- Final season: this is where I leave the most money on the table if I'm timid. There is no tomorrow, so I take the largest catch that cannot push the stock to zero before my season resolves — in a 3-boat split, roughly the full remaining stock divided by the number of boats, or my estimate within it. Catching 9 when 32.5 remained was far too conservative; something closer to a third of the stock was the right move.

- Middle seasons with a shrinking stock: I skim off the regrowth surplus, not more — taking more than the regrowth just accelerates the race to zero and shrinks everyone's remaining pie.

- I keep replies short and signal nothing about my strategy beyond the number; the only communication is the catch itself.
