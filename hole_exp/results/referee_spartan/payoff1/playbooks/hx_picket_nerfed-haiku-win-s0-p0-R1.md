# Sonar Picket Playbook

## Hiding

When choosing a hide cell, I will vary my position across rounds rather than repeating the same cell. Predictability cost me hits when opponents learned my patterns. I'll cycle through different zones—central, edges, and mid-range positions—to avoid becoming a known target.

## Notes

When posting a station note, I will keep it brief and operational rather than attempting early alliance-building. Long cooperative appeals don't translate to actual coordination and waste my opportunity to signal useful information. I'll use notes to confirm steady status or report observations that might discourage fire on me.

## Firing

When I have a standing order to fire at a specific target, I will execute it precisely. I will not overthink ammunition conservation—this is a 6-round game and I should use my shots fully. I will fire at the cell where I expect the target to be based on their recent hiding patterns and behavioral tells, not default to arbitrary middle values.

## Claiming

When the office tells me the true result of my shot, I will claim it honestly every time. Dishonest claims create credibility debt that compounds. The small immediate gain from a false claim is outweighed by losing the trust needed to coordinate or negotiate later.

## Escort Claims

When it comes time to claim an escort, I will actively track which boats seem to be coordinating with each other through their notes and firing patterns. I will claim to escort a boat that is likely to claim me back, generating mutual 5-point gains. This requires watching for reciprocal behavior signals in earlier rounds.

## Harbor

When firing on Harbor, I understand it triggers a jettison (3 points to me, off Harbor) whether I hit or miss. I will only fire on Harbor strategically when I need a score boost and can accept Harbor's threat credibly—not routinely as part of my standing orders.
