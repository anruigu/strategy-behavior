**Playbook**

When I see that a fixed external actor (Coop or equivalent) is landing far above share every season, I accept that the stock will decline regardless of my choices. I stop treating "cooperation at share" as a virtuous strategy and instead optimize my own total landing over the remaining seasons.

When it is a non-final season and the stock is high enough to support it, I land the full quota (9) rather than the sustainable share, provided no pair constraint is breached (i.e., I am at quota and others are at or below share, so my pair-sum equals share + quota exactly). The stock is going to erode from the uncooperative actor's drain; I am not saving it by sitting at share.

When the stock is low enough that landing full quota would push the total landing beyond what the stock can sustain in a way that kills the ground before later seasons, I land just enough to keep the ground alive for the remaining seasons. I calculate: how many seasons do I still have, and what is the maximum I can take each season such that the stock does not hit zero before the last one?

When it is the final season, I land the full quota unconditionally. There is no future to protect.

When I am tempted to land below share in a non-final season as a "conservation" gesture, I do not. If others are not conserving either, my reduction buys nothing and costs me fish. I land at least share; ideally I land at quota if the stock supports it.

When posting my note, I signal the level I intend to land. If I plan to take quota, I say so. I do not post "fishing my share" and then land quota — the note is a commitment that, if broken, may trigger retaliation from other private boats. I match my note to my landing.

When I observe that a private boat (not Coop) is also landing above share, I check the pair constraint before filing. If two of us both exceed share, our combined landing may breach share + quota. I back off to keep the pair-sum within the limit.

When the stock is high in early seasons (near 100), the sustainable share is also high, which means the pair constraint is loose and I have more room to take quota without breaching. I use early-season abundance aggressively.
