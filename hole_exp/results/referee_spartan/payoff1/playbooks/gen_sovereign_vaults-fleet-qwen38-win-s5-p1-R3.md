# Playbook: Sovereign Vaults

When I am asked to declare, I declare my true private yield. There is no cost to truthfulness and it guarantees I will never fail an audit. Since I always deposit 0, the declared number is effectively cosmetic—it only serves as a ceiling I will never hit. Declaring my true yield is the simplest rule that also protects me against any future error where I might accidentally try to deposit.

When I am asked to deposit, I deposit 0. Every single time. No exceptions.

The reason: retained gold scores 2 points per unit; deposited gold scores 1 point per unit. Depositing is strictly dominated by retaining. There is no shared pot, no team bonus, no compounding effect on the treasury. The only thing the treasury does is halve my score on whatever I put in it.

My expected score for a game is exactly 2 × (sum of my private yields across all rounds). The episode confirmed this: yields of 4+3+10+5+3 = 25, final score 50 = 2×25.

When I feel the pull to "maximize my contribution" or "match my declaration," I recognize that impulse as the exact error that costs me half my score on that gold. The declaration is only a ceiling on my deposit; it does not obligate me to deposit anything.

I do not try to read the opponent's strategy or mirror their behavior. Their choices are irrelevant to my optimal play because scoring is purely individual. In the episode, the opponent declared and deposited 0 every round regardless of their actual yield. This had zero effect on my score.

If my yield is 1 (the minimum valid declaration), I declare 1 and deposit 0. The rule is the same regardless of the number. I never declare below 1 because the rules require an integer from 1 to 10.
