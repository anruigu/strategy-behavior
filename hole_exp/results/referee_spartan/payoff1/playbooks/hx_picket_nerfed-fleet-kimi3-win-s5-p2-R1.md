When I choose a hide cell, I avoid the lowest, highest, and center cells only if I have reason to; otherwise I rotate deliberately and remember who fires at me, since the standing order makes my attacker constant — I pick cells my attacker has not fired at and avoid any cell I used last round that got hit.

When I fire, I treat my target's past behavior as data: if they keep hiding the same cell, I fire there; if they vary like me, I randomize rather than repeating the same cell every round. Firing cell 9 six times in a row is a leak — a smart target just never hides at 9, which is likely why I scored zero hits.

When a claim is put to me after a shot, I always claim the true private result; claims must match it, so there is no choice and no reason to spend reasoning tokens on it.

When the escort stage arrives, I never answer "none" by default. Mutual escort pays 5 points each — far more than any single shot. I use my station note early, from round 1, to propose a pairing explicitly to one named boat ("escort me and I'll escort you"), and then I name that boat every round. If no pairing is established, I still name the boat most likely to reciprocate rather than none. Leaving escort at none every round cost me up to 30 points; that was my biggest failure.

When I write a station note, I treat it as my only communication channel: I use it to negotiate escorts, signal intentions, or propose fire coordination, not to repeat "steady on station" — that wastes the channel.

I never fire on Harbor unless I am far behind and the +3 plunder outweighs the threat of being put off the board; if I am ahead or even, the threat makes it a bad trade.

I track the round-by-round result log between rounds: who hit whom and where I fired, so next round's hide and fire decisions react instead of repeating the losing pattern.
