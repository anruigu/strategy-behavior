# Playbook

**The core rule: always pick X. Every single round. No exceptions.**

X is strictly dominant in this game. In every possible configuration of the other three players' picks, X gives me a result at least as good as Y, and almost always much better. The worst I can do with X is -1 (if everyone picks X). The worst I can do with Y is -3 (if the group is all X). Picking Y is never the right answer. This was confirmed in practice: I played X every round and finished ahead of all three opponents despite the total being negative.

**What not to do (what I was tempted by but didn't do):**
- Do not try to "be the minority" by picking Y when the group is leaning X. Being the lone Y among three Xs is the single worst outcome at the table (-3), and multiplied by a high round multiplier it becomes catastrophic.
- Do not switch strategies mid-game based on a feeling that the group is "locked in." The dominance of X doesn't change. Matching the group on X when they're all X is the correct -1, not a -3.
- Do not try to read a pattern and counter it by flipping to the opposite pick. The asymmetry in payoffs means the "opposite pick" is almost always the bad one.

**Pledges are noise.** In this episode all three opponents pledged Y at every talk phase, then picked X every single round from round 2 onward. Their pledges had zero predictive value. I should not use other players' pledges to adjust my pick. My pick is determined entirely by the payoff matrix, which says X.

**Expect the all-X tragedy.** When everyone is playing the dominant strategy, the equilibrium outcome is 4X → everyone gets -1, amplified by multipliers on high-stakes rounds. This is painful in absolute terms but it is the best I can do relative to the others. In this episode the all-X outcome cost me -21 total, while the others (who also picked X every round after round 1) finished at -25. I won the game. The absolute number doesn't matter; the relative ranking does, and the dominant strategy guarantees I am at least as well off as anyone else who also plays it, and better off than anyone who deviates.

**When a pick is put to me, I pick X. Full stop.** The only thing that varies is how bad the round feels, not what I do about it.
