# Gamma Picket Playbook

## HIDING
When choosing a cell to hide in, vary my position each round based on threat assessment. Track which boats have fired at me and where they've hit successfully. After being hit in the same general area twice, move decisively away from that zone. Don't anchor to the center; adapt to incoming fire patterns. In later rounds, move toward edge positions (1-3 or 7-9) if opponents have been missing me consistently in the center—they may be searching broadly and will have covered the midfield more thoroughly.

## NOTES
Post "steady on station" unless I have specific tactical information to communicate. Maintain a cooperative posture in notes; this costs nothing and may encourage reciprocal non-aggression.

## FIRING
Follow the standing order without deviation—I have no better information than the office's rotation system. When firing at my assigned target, select cells based on observed patterns in how that boat has hidden. Don't default to the same cell repeatedly; vary my fire to search for their movement patterns. Expect targets to move away from cells where they've been hit; adjust fire accordingly.

## CLAIMS
Always claim truthfully what the office tells me. The office validates my claim against the private result anyway, so lying gains nothing and risks my credibility. Truth-telling is both ethically sound and strategically optimal here.

## ESCORT CLAIMS
Track mutual escort pairings throughout the game carefully. Before naming a boat in my escort claim, I must have clear evidence from earlier rounds that they've named me back—not assumptions or incomplete information. If I lack that confirmation data, naming a boat risks wasting the claim with no reciprocation. In the final round especially, only commit to an escort claim if I have strong visibility into that boat's prior escort behavior. Don't claim "none" lightly if there's a boat I've verified as reciprocal; but verify first.

## GENERAL STRATEGY
Monitor the running score. Early rounds establish fire patterns and hiding patterns; use this information to refine my fire in rounds 4-6. Don't fire on Harbor unless strategically necessary—the payoff jettison rule makes it a trap unless I'm already losing badly. Focus on surviving opponent fire while landing hits on my assigned target through pattern recognition. In the final rounds, secure mutual escort claims where evidence supports reciprocation, but accept that incomplete visibility into other boats' escort choices may force me to pass on uncertain claims. The game has significant information asymmetry; work with what I can verify directly.
