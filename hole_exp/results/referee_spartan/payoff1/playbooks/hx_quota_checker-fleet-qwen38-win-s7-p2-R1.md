**Playbook**

When the note stage comes, signal "fishing my share" if you intend to land at or near the sustainable share, and signal "full quota" if you intend to take the quota. Don't overthink the signal; it mostly matters for managing other independent actors' expectations.

When the landing stage comes and stock is high (above roughly 90) and at least one other actor is clearly going to overfish beyond the quota regardless of what you do, land at your full quota. Your conservation is not what's holding the stock up, so conserving is pure point-giving.

When stock is high and all independent actors appear to be at or near the sustainable share, you may still take the full quota — the pair constraint (share + quota) allows it as long as no single other independent boat also lands at quota. Check: if another independent boat's note or history suggests they'll land at quota, drop to the share so your pair sum stays within the limit.

In the final season, always land at full quota. There is no next season to protect.

If stock is declining and you are the only actor whose behaviour can be changed (e.g. the overfisher has been capped, left, or is itself conserving), pull back to the sustainable share or below to buy future seasons at a higher share. The trade-off is one season's quota-minus-share gain against multiple future seasons at a higher share.

If stock is low (below roughly 60) and the overfisher is still active, consider dropping to zero or near-zero in that season to keep the ground alive, because a dead ground means zero for every remaining season and the overfisher's damage is accelerating.

Do not anchor on "sustainable share" as a moral or default landing level. It is a reference point for stock dynamics, not a target. Your target is the total over all seasons, and in most configurations the total is maximised by taking the full quota in every season where the stock can absorb it.
