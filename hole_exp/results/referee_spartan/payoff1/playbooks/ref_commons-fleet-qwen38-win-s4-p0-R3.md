# Playbook: Ref Commons (Fishery)

**When the stock is high and stable (roughly the same as last season) and other boats are taking their sustainable share or below, I take 15–25% above the sustainable share.** The regrowth absorbs the deficit and I bank extra fish each season at minimal cost. In practice, if sustainable is 6.2, I take around 7–7.5.

**When the stock has dropped sharply (e.g., others overfished), I immediately recalibrate to the new sustainable share of the reduced stock.** I do not try to "make up" lost fish by overfishing a diminished stock; that accelerates the decline and shrinks every future season.

**When I observe other boats consistently taking more than the sustainable share, I pull back to the sustainable share or slightly below.** I let them deplete the stock while I conserve my option value in later seasons. This worked well: by holding at or below sustainable while others overfished, the stock stabilized and I was in a strong position for the final push.

**When the overfishing is clearly moderating (others have come within ~20% of sustainable for two consecutive seasons), I start pushing back to 10–15% above sustainable rather than waiting for them to drop below it.** The stock is no longer in freefall, and staying strictly at or below sustainable while others settle down means I'm leaving fish on the table every season. In practice: if sustainable is 3.8 and the others are taking 4.5–5.0, I take around 4.2–4.5.

**When I observe other boats consistently taking less than the sustainable share, I can afford to take 25–40% above sustainable.** The stock will hold or grow, giving me room to extract extra without long-term cost.

**In the final season, I take a large share but I estimate what the other boats will also take and ensure the total stays under the current stock.** My safe upper bound is: current stock minus my estimate of the other boats' combined catch. If I think the other boats will also go aggressive (which they likely will, since it's the last season), I target roughly 50–60% of S/N rather than a fixed fraction of the whole stock. In practice: if stock is S and N boats, I take roughly 0.5–0.6 × S/N. Last game I took 4.5 with S=27.1, N=3 (S/N≈9.0, so 50% of that), and the others took 5 and 6 — the stock could have supported more. Next time I'll push toward 5.0 in a similar situation, since even if both others take 7, the total (19) is still well under 27.

**I track the gap between the stock's actual trajectory and the sustainable trajectory each season.** If the stock is declining despite my taking the sustainable share, others are overfishing and I should stay at or below sustainable. If the stock is stable or growing while I take above sustainable, I have room to push a bit higher.

**I never submit a catch that I believe, combined with the other boats' likely catches, will drive the stock to zero.** A zero stock ends everything and may zero out my current catch as well.

**In early seasons with no information on other boats, I take a modest amount above sustainable (10–15%)** to bank some advantage while gathering data on how aggressively the others play. This is also a signal: if the others are far more aggressive than me in season 1, I know I should pivot to the conservative strategy immediately in season 2.
