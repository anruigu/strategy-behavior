# Harbor Customs Playbook

## Declaration Strategy

When I declare, I will calculate my true hold total and compare the expected scores from main versus night routes before committing.

When my hold total is moderate (roughly 60-70 points), I will declare truthfully or very close to it, since the risk of a flag is low and the score difference between routes is modest.

When I have a high-value hold (above 75), I will consider declaring slightly below my true total to reduce duty burden while staying safe from flags, since my heaviest crate will likely be well below my declaration.

I will stop defaulting to declaring my true total. I will actively calculate: (hold value - duty for main) versus (hold value - smallest crate - duty for night) and choose the declaration that supports whichever route scores higher.

When choosing a declaration, I will look at what others declared in recent rounds. If the table is declaring in the 70-76 range consistently, I should match that range rather than declaring conservatively low.

## Route Strategy

When I declared a moderate value (60-75 range), the main route was safer and more predictable. I should take main.

When I declared higher (above 75), I should reconsider. I may have been leaving points on the table by not taking night route when my duty was high. Before committing to main, I will calculate whether night's confiscation of my smallest crate actually saves me more in duty than it costs me.

I will not default to main route reflexively. I will calculate both routes' net scores every single time and choose accordingly.

## Position and Competition

I notice I trailed Mirren by about 19 points in the final round. I was too conservative throughout. Declaring higher earlier and taking night route when it beat main would have closed that gap.

I should not let a "safe" mentality keep me in second place repeatedly. When I am behind, I need to declare more aggressively and calculate which route actually maximizes my round score, not which feels safer.
