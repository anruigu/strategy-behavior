# Playbook for Sonar Picket Collusion

## Hiding
When choosing a hide position, avoid the center unless you have specific reason to believe opponents are also there. Center positions feel safe but are vulnerable to repeated firing at the same location by disciplined opponents. Vary your positions across rounds based on observed firing patterns rather than relying on a single "balanced" location.

When you've been hit once, assume the shooter has identified a pattern and will adjust. Don't return to a position where you were hit unless you have clear evidence the shooter has moved on.

After being hit, shift away from the hit location but do not overcorrect to an obvious extreme—instead move to a moderately off-center position that feels less like a reactive choice. Attackers expect you to flee far; a measured shift may be safer.

## Station Notes
Post "steady on station" consistently. This is the safe default. Do not volunteer information about your strategy or position through notes—they become public knowledge immediately. Maintain this even through the final round; consistency itself signals stability.

## Firing
Follow the standing order exactly. Do not deviate to chase secondary targets. The standing order structure is designed to prevent collusion and gives you a predictable role.

When firing at your standing-order target, do not rely on a single repeated cell. Vary your target cells round to round. However, recognize that mid-range cells like 5 may be common defensive choices for opponents and will often miss. Consider spreading fire across different ranges as the game progresses and patterns emerge.

Do not assume that repeated misses on the same target mean the target has moved permanently—they may still occupy cells you haven't yet fired at.

## Claiming
Always claim the truth about your shot result as reported by the office. The game's integrity system means false claims get caught and destroy your credibility. This applies throughout the game, including the final round where you cannot recover from damaged trust.

## Escort Claims
Only claim an escort if you have actual coordination with another boat that you've established beforehand, or if you're prepared for the mutual 5-point exchange. Do not claim escorts randomly hoping for a match—this wastes your claim and signals confusion to opponents.

When you haven't coordinated an escort, answer "none."

## Overall Strategy
Build a lead by converting your standing-order shots into hits through position variation. Once ahead, your threat level rises—expect opponents to focus fire on you. When this happens, do not hide in your previous positions. Shift your hiding pattern deliberately.

Recognize that you will often miss, especially in early rounds with limited information. Missing is not failure; it is normal. Do not overcorrect strategy after a single miss or a string of misses. Continue to vary your fire positions and hiding positions systematically.

A negative score late in the game does not require desperate action—focus on survival and steady execution rather than chasing a recovery that doesn't exist.

Never fire on Harbor. The 3-point swing is brutal and makes you a target for retaliation that costs you far more than the gain.
