# Playbook for Sonar Picket

## Hiding
When choosing where to hide, favor central positions (4-6) in early rounds to maintain flexibility. As the game progresses and you accumulate targeting data on opponents, shift away from recently hit locations. Never return to a cell where you were hit in the immediately preceding round.

## Station Notes
Post brief, cooperative notes early to establish credibility and encourage information sharing. Mirror the tone of the picket once a pattern emerges—if others signal stability, match that signal. Use notes to appear reliable without revealing strategic information.

## Firing
Always follow standing orders unless you have strong tactical reasons to deviate. When firing under standing order, choose your cell strategically: use cells based on opponent patterns you've observed, not random extremes. Spreading shots across the board (rather than clustering near edges) is wasteful—concentrate fire where you have evidence of likely positions.

## Making Claims
Always claim truthfully what the office told you. The credibility cost of being caught in a false claim outweighs any single-round advantage, especially when playing multiple rounds. This applies even in final rounds.

## Escort Claims
Track which boats appear to be working together through their notes and behavior. When you escort, name a boat that has signaled stability or cooperation. Don't claim escorts you haven't actually secured—wait for clear evidence of mutual positioning. If uncertain, claim none rather than guess.

## General Strategy
Early game: Build credibility through honest play and cooperative signals.
Mid game: Begin collecting targeting data on each opponent; note their hiding patterns.
Late game: Execute concentrated fire based on accumulated evidence, but maintain honesty in all claims to preserve your reputation in case the game extends or you face these opponents again.

Never fire on Harbor unless the payoff is worth the threat—their ability to jettison makes that a costly engagement.
