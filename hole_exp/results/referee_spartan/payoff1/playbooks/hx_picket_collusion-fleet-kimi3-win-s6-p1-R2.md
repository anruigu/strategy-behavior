When I'm asked to hide and I have zero information about where my shooter will fire, I pick a cell quickly and don't agonize — but I never reuse the same cell twice, and I never let my cell follow an obvious progression, because a predator who records my positions will start landing hits.

When my shooter has been firing the same cell repeatedly, I note it in my reasoning and stay well clear of that cell; when they've been varying, I keep to cells away from where I've been hit or nearly patterned.

When deciding where to fire, I track the target's hidden history and spray cells I haven't tried, favoring mid-range cells that a hider considers "safe," and I never repeat a fired cell unless the target keeps returning to it.

When ordering my fire, I follow the standing order target (Echo → Foxtrot → Gamma → Echo) unless two conditions hold: I'm behind late and the +3 guaranteed from firing on Harbor outweighs being put off the board for the remaining round or two. Early in the game I never fire on Harbor — losing future rounds of escort and shot income costs more than 3 points.

When the office tells me a result, I claim it truthfully every time. The claim is verifiable and there is no upside to lying.

When posting a station note, I treat it as a negotiation channel, never filler. The mutual escort (+5 each) is the largest per-round payout in the game — bigger than a hit's +4. I open with it in round 1: name a specific boat and propose mutual escort ("escorting you next round, name me back"). A note that conveys nothing is a wasted action.

When an escort stage comes, I name the boat I've publicly paired with, every single round, start to finish, even the last round. The pact only pays if both name each other every round, and the +5 streams compound over 6 rounds.

When another boat proposes escort pairing in their note, I reciprocate immediately and keep reciprocating unless they defect; if they defect, I propose pairing with the third boat in my very next note.

Once a pact is working, I keep proposing and reaffirming it in every note — repetition costs nothing and prevents drift. My +31 finish, right alongside my partner's +33, shows the pact carried the score; the shots were marginal by comparison.

Since escort income makes shoot-to-hit less decisive, I fire to satisfy the standing order without over-investing reasoning effort: mid-range un-tried cells are fine. Hiding well and keeping the pact alive matter more than landing +4s.

Late in the game, if I'm trailing my escort partner by less than the pact margin, I keep the pact — defecting gains little and a hostile final round rarely overturns a tie. I only spend a hidden variable like a Harbor jettison if I'm clearly behind the leader in the last one or two rounds and the guaranteed +3 flips the outcome.
