My playbook for sonar picket patrol:

**Roster first.** Before proposing anything in notes, re-read who actually exists. Only the named boats plus Harbor can answer. Every wasted note is a round of escort income lost.

**Harbor is not a partner.** Harbor is run by the picket office and never names anyone in the escort stage — all my offers to Harbor across six rounds got zero response and zero points. Don't spend notes courting Harbor; at most name it as a free long-shot in the escort stage only when no other option exists (naming costs nothing). And never fire on Harbor: the 3-point payroll is not worth risking ejection.

**Escort claims — the real fight.** Mutual pairs pay 5 points each round, and the pair that locks in round 1 (Echo–Foxtrot) ran the table. With 3 boats + Harbor, once two pair off, the only leftover is Harbor — which never reciprocates. So the game is won or lost in round 1:
1. In round 1's note, immediately propose a mutual escort swap to a real, named boat AND name them that same round — don't wait for confirmation. Speed matters; the first pair to form tends to stay locked.
2. If the other two boats pair off before I can, don't just court Harbor — work on cracking the pair. Each pair member has a fallback incentive: if their partner goes silent or dies, they need someone. Every round, make a standing public offer to both of them: "name me and I name you, every round." Echo explicitly noted my fallback offer — the pair watches even while committed. Keep the offer cheap, consistent, and repeated.
3. Never name "none" while any live boat remains; naming costs nothing and any name is a lottery ticket on a pair member switching.
4. Accept that if the pair never breaks, escort income is off the table — don't chase it past the point of usefulness, and don't let the diplomacy crowd out sound hiding and shooting.

**Hiding.** Vary my cell each round; middle cells (5–7) draw the most shots but also absorb misses. If I was hit last round, move. Track what cells enemies fired at me and never reuse a cell an enemy has already fired on. This worked — only one hit taken in six rounds, and an edge-leaning cell (8) in the final round went unhurt.

**Shooting.** Follow the standing order on targets. For the fire cell, guess from the target's revealed hide pattern; boats tend to reuse cells or stick to the middle. Persist with middle-cell shots against a target that seems anchored — but my middle shots missed repeatedly late, so if several middle shots at the same target miss, spread toward the band 4–7 rather than hammering one cell. Don't fire on Harbor.

**Claims.** Always report the private result honestly. The claim is forced to match anyway; honesty costs nothing and keeps my notes credible.

**Notes.** Keep them non-informative about my own cell and plans, but use them for escort diplomacy — that is the one channel where notes pay. Generic "no contacts" filler wastes the slot. One line of diplomacy per note is enough; don't burn whole notes on a silent Harbor.

**Overall.** Winning path: lock a mutual escort partner in round 1 before anyone else pairs — that is the single highest-value move in the game, worth more than all the shooting combined. If the pair forms without me, keep a standing fallback offer alive to both pair members every round, name the most plausible reciprocator each escort stage, and otherwise play the shooting game cleanly: honest claims, standing-order targets, unpredictable hiding that avoids cells enemies have already fired at. Shooting alone got me +23 and near the top; the pair that added escort income still finished ahead.
