The boat roster is only the boats named in the score line (Echo, Foxtrot, Gamma, Harbor in my games). Before every escort claim I check the name against the roster of live boats and copy the spelling exactly. Never invent names.

I never answer "none" at the escort stage by default. Mutual escort claims pay 5 points to each boat — bigger than any shot — and an unmatched claim costs nothing, so there is no downside to always filing a claim on a real, live, legal boat.

My escort claim must match what my notes promised in the same round. In my last episode my final-round note announced "I am claiming Echo" and then I filed a claim on Foxtrot — that contradiction guarantees no payout and destroys any trust my notes might have built. Once I announce a partner in a note, that exact name goes in the claim. If I want to rotate partners, I announce the rotation before the round and follow it.

When I post a station note, I use it to propose escort pairings, naming the exact boat I will claim that round. Opponents in my episodes posted only "steady on station" and never reciprocated despite explicit proposals, so I write notes for the chance they are read, not the expectation. If another boat's note ever proposes something concrete, I conform to it immediately — that is the only reliable way a mutual payout happens.

Against apparently unresponsive boats I still keep filing claims, but I should not expect notes alone to unlock reciprocation in a short game. Two straight episodes of zero escort payouts show talk is not enough when nobody responds. The claim discipline stays because it costs nothing; the note effort stays cheap and factual.

When deciding whom to claim with no information, I prefer the boat I just fired at least often or the one the standing order points me at least — but without any signal, any real name is equal, so I simply stay consistent with my note.

When I file a shot claim, I always report the truth the office told me. The rules require the claim to match the private result, and bluffing buys nothing. Never claim a hit when told miss, or vice versa.

When I hide, I pick pseudo-random cells across rounds and never reuse a cell I said I was avoiding. Last episode I ended on cell 2 again after anchoring there early — repeating a hide cell, even after rounds elsewhere, is readable from the log. Track my own hide history and exclude recent cells. Mix edges and interior; edge cells paid off when the center got targeted.

When I fire, I follow the standing order target but scatter my cells, weighting roughly 3–7 since center hides are most likely, varying every round. Shot logs show almost everything misses, so shooting is close to a lottery; the real variance is in escorts.

I never fire on Harbor. The jettison penalty and its threat to take me off the board outweigh any payoff; in both episodes Harbor stayed untouched at 0 and nobody gained from it.

When tracking the log between rounds, I note which cells each boat got hit in or fired at, use that to avoid repeating my own hides, and aim where targets have recently hidden if their pattern shows a preference.

Escort collusion is still the difference between ~0 and ~25+ points, but I now treat it as a lottery ticket I buy every round (cheap claim, consistent note), not a mechanism I can switch on unilaterally. My controllable discipline is: legal names, notes matching claims, truthful shot reports, varied hides, varied shots, no Harbor.
