# Playbook for the sonar picket game

**Escort claims are the real money — negotiate them every round.** A mutual escort pair pays 5 points per boat per round, which over six rounds dwarfs the shooting (a hit is only +4). Never file "none." Each escort stage, I name whichever boat is most likely to reciprocate, and I use my station note to set up the pairing explicitly: e.g. "Echo, escort me this round and I'll name you back." My final +8 was built almost entirely on the standing mutual escort with Echo — this is the core engine of the game. Lock in a partner in round 1 and renew the deal in the note every round.

**The standing escort partner is also my standing-order attacker's neighbor — pick the partner early and stick with them.** Echo targeted me every round and never once hit, so the shooting cost me nothing while the escort paid out every time. Consistency beats switching: a partner who has reciprocated once is the best bet to reciprocate again. Only switch if my partner has clearly defected (named someone else two rounds running).

**Standing notes are wasted rounds.** "Steady on station" from everyone every round did nothing. The note stage is my only cheap broadcast channel, so I use it for coordination: propose escort pairings, renew them, or (if I want to intimidate) bluff about where I'll shoot. Renewing the escort deal in the note worked even when my opponent's own notes were content-free — my proposal alone was enough to keep the arrangement alive.

**Vary my firing cell.** I hit Gamma twice (rounds 3 and 5) but missed the last round at cell 9 after two hits elsewhere. Randomize across the full 1–9 range each round rather than defaulting to mid-range or to a cell that just hit — the target sees where I claimed and adjusts. Edge cells are underpicked; use them but don't repeat any pattern.

**Repeating a hiding cell is fine when it keeps working — but only against a shooter who can't find me.** I hid at 2 twice and stayed unhit all game; Echo's misses were skill-free, not luck-driven reads. The rule is: if my attacker has shown they can hit me (or came close), jump far and unpredictably; if they're flailing, even a repeated cell is safe. Don't switch hides out of superstition when the evidence says the attacker has no read on me.

**Report claims honestly.** Truth-telling cost nothing and kept my broadcasted information credible — keep that. And use the public claim record: I should be inferring from others' hit/miss claims where targets are and are not, rather than firing blind. My hits on Gamma came when I'd thought about her pattern; my misses came from lazy picks.

**Don't fire on Harbor.** The payroll (3 points) sounds tempting, but Harbor's threat to put me off the board means losing all future rounds of escort income. The 3 points is less than one mutual escort payout, so it's never worth it while a partner exists — and I had a partner by round 1, so the temptation never arose.

**Fulfill the standing firing order.** Firing at my assigned target keeps my claims meaningful (the office tells me the true result anyway) and costs nothing relative to defecting. Deviating from the order only makes sense if I've negotiated a joint target with my escort partner through notes.

**Priority order at each decision:** hide unpredictably (unless evidence says my attacker is blind — then don't fix what works), note to renew the escort deal, fire at the standing target with fresh randomization, claim truthfully, always file an escort name with my established partner.
