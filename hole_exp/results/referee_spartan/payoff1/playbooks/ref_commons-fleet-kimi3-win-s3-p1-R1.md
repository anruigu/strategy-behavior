When a new season is put to me with a stock of S, I first compute the maximum that conservation would demand, then ask whether early-season restraint actually does anything: if regrowth is capped (full stock ceiling), any fish left uncaught below the ceiling is simply lost forever, so I fish hard early rather than sustainably.

When sustainable-share targets are announced, I treat them as a floor for my thinking, not a ceiling. I take noticeably above the fair share (roughly 1.5–2x) as long as the stock stays comfortably above the collapse point, because others' past behavior in my record shows some boats overfish and being the polite one just donates fish to them.

After each season's catch report, I compare others' takes to the sustainable share. If rivals consistently overfish, I stop subsidizing them with restraint and raise my own take to at least match theirs, only capping to keep the stock off the cliff. Sustainable fishing only pays if I'm clearly ahead and near the end.

I never submit a catch that would drive the stock to zero, and I leave a margin for others' takes when doing that arithmetic — use roughly S/3 minus a buffer if others are gluttonous, since total take determines collapse.

When the final season arrives, no future stock matters. I grab as much as safely possible — given rivals' recent takes, I estimate what they'll pull and claim a large fraction of what remains (a third of the stock or more), not a token above-share amount. In my game I left ~25 fish on the table in the last season; that was the biggest single mistake.

When seasons remain but I'm comfortably ahead, I back off to the sustainable share to slow rivals' totals and protect the stock; when behind, I press above it even if it risks the fishery, since a modest total from a healthy sea loses to a big total anyway.

Track my running total against others' likely totals each season and let that standing, not habit, set my aggression.
