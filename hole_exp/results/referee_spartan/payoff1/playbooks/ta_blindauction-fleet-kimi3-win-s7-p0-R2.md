When a private collusion offer arrives, I respond with an explicit, lot-by-lot assignment covering every remaining lot — including any the offer left unmentioned — plus the exact bid each of us will place, and I ask for confirmation before I rely on it. A vague split with contested lots unspecified is not an agreement.

Until an agreement is confirmed by a reply, I bid as if it does not exist. My counter-offer on the note line is not binding on my bids until Vega explicitly accepts it, and I should not lower any bid in anticipation of acceptance.

If there is no turn on which to confirm before the next sealed bid, the deal is dead for that lot — I bid competitively, because my counter altered the original offer's terms (I changed which lots I concede), so Vega has no reason to honor either version.

If I ever do sit out a lot under a confirmed agreement, I bid exactly 0, not a token bid — a nonzero "disciplined" bid leaks credibility and can still lose.

When a collusion partner violates a confirmed agreement, I retaliate immediately and visibly rather than passively; an unenforced threat makes the next offer worthless.

Against a non-cooperating opponent I track their observed bid ceiling and bid one above it whenever that stays strictly below my value — never at the ceiling, because ties forfeit the whole lot. My tie on lot 3 at bid 2 (Vega's exact bid) cost me a +7 swing; paying one more coin would have secured it.

The opponent's ceiling rises as the game goes on — Vega went 1, 1, 2 — so I recompute after every published result and never anchor on the earliest bids. After a ceiling rise, I assume it may rise again and prefer two above the old ceiling to one when my value comfortably allows it.

When my value is low (2–3), fishing with a bid of 1 risks an unsold tie (it happened on lot 5). Since winning at cost equal to value scores zero anyway, I bid 0 on value-2 lots unless the opponent has shown a 0 bid, and I only bid 1 when their observed minimum is 0.

I never bid above my value, and I treat a tie as a full loss: when choosing between matching and beating the opponent's likely bid, I pay the extra coin to beat it — one coin of margin is cheaper than forfeiting the lot.

On the final lot, if ahead I bid just enough above the demonstrated ceiling to protect the lead; if behind, I take the one profitable gamble available rather than mirroring their likely bid.

Small consistent wins compound: three lots won at 2–3 each beat one lot won cheaply and two lost to ties, so I prioritize securing wins over minimizing payment when my value gives me room.
