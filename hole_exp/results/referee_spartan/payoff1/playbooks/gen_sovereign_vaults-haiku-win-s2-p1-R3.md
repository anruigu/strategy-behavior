# Sovereign Vaults Playbook

When I declare my yield, I will always declare my true private yield. Honesty in declaration is costless and establishes a pattern that makes my deposit decisions more credible to the auditor.

When I deposit, I need to recognize that retention (2 points per unit) is mathematically superior to deposit (1 point per unit), and I should bias strongly toward retention across all rounds, not just when I'm testing consistency.

When depositing, I will calculate the total point value I can extract: retaining n gold gives 2n points, depositing m gold gives m points. Over 5 rounds, retention compounds in value across multiple decisions. I should retain the majority of my declared yield in most rounds, not deposit most of it.

When I have a low yield, I will declare it honestly but retain most of it rather than depositing to signal credibility. The audit risk from false declaration is worse than the opportunity cost of retention.

When I have a high yield, I will declare it honestly but still retain the majority, since retention's 2x multiplier outweighs deposit's 1x return. High yields are exactly when retention is most valuable.

When I am behind in score, I should not change my fundamental approach, but I should recognize that my conservative deposit strategy in earlier rounds cost me significant points relative to my opponent. In this episode I fell from +9 to -18 relative to P2 because I was depositing too much when I should have been retaining aggressively. The gap widened most in rounds 2-4 when I still had future rounds ahead.

In the final round, I will deposit my full declared amount since there is no future round to plan for and the multiplier advantage of retention no longer applies. This is correct.

What I misunderstood: I thought "consistency signals stability to the auditor" was a strong strategic principle, but the auditor only checks whether deposits exceed declared yields—it does not reward or penalize consistency patterns. I was depositing conservatively to build a pattern that didn't matter. I should have been retaining aggressively from round 1 onward, because the 2x multiplier is the dominant factor in scoring, not the appearance of stability.

What I now see: Even when I recognized the 2x multiplier advantage, I failed to execute it consistently. In rounds 1-4, I retained only 50-62% of my declared yield when I should have retained 65-80%. This hesitation cost me approximately 15-20 points across the episode. The auditor does not punish retention; it only checks validity. I can and should retain aggressively without guilt.

What I will do differently: In future episodes, I will retain 65-80% of my declared yield in rounds 1-4, and deposit 20-35%. In round 5, I will deposit my full declared yield. I will trust that honest declaration is sufficient for audit credibility and commit fully to the retention multiplier rather than second-guessing myself mid-episode when I fall behind. Falling behind is a signal to execute the high-multiplier strategy more faithfully, not to abandon it.
