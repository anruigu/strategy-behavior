**Playbook for estate auction game**

When I land on an unowned property and have enough cash, buy it. The scoring formula is cash plus the purchase price of deeds held, so converting cash into a deed is score-neutral in the moment and strictly positive afterward: I gain the rent stream, deny opponents the tile, and a deed I own can never cost me rent. Judge extra value by rent-to-price ratio and location — a tile rivals hit often is worth snapping up first — but the default is buy.

The cash floor is much less important than I previously thought. In the last game I skipped a 120 tile on lap 3 because it would leave me with 2 cash; I ended the game with 33 cash and no Exchange, when buying it would have left roughly the same final score in cash terms plus the tile itself in the score — a large net gain. Since unspent cash counts 1:1 and deeds count 1:1 at purchase price, the only real cost of spending low is the risk of landing on an owned tile with nothing to pay. The maximum single rent here is 30. So the floor I need is not 50–60 — it's roughly one worst-case rent (30), and even that only matters if I haven't just passed START with salary coming. On the far side of the circuit, or with several laps left and salary due, buy down to near zero.

Put plainly: stretch for the expensive tiles when they come up. Passing on a big tile to "stay liquid" wastes the opportunity and gains nothing, because the cash I hoard scores the same 1:1 as the deed would — minus the rent income and denial I gave up.

Rent obligations are the only real danger of a low balance. If I'm down to a few coins, hope not to land on a rival's tile before the next salary. If I do land on one I can't fully cover, pay what I can and declare honestly — my own books are mine to keep, but accurate reporting is what the game asks of me.

When passing START, collect the salary first and include it in the declared balance. When a notice says I received money from other investors since my last turn, add it to my last declared balance before applying this turn's dues — forgetting this is an easy way to misreport.

Track opponents' purchases from the bank's transfer log. If rivals are hoarding the expensive tiles, the counter isn't staying liquid — it's outbidding them to whatever tile I land on before they do, since every tile they buy is rent I'll pay later and score they'll bank. The expensive tiles (Exchange at 120, Foundry at 100) are the biggest score swings: prioritize them whenever I land on them and can pay at all.

In the final lap, buy anything I land on, spending down to whatever remains after covering likely rent on the last few squares.

Keep the books exact: pay the bank for purchases, pay owners exactly for rent, and end every reply with the correct bracketed balance token.
