# Playbook for ref_commons

When I see the sustainable catch level, I will use it as a baseline reference, not as my target catch.

When I'm in the early seasons and the stock is healthy, I will catch moderately above sustainable—around 110-130% of the sustainable share—to stay competitive without triggering immediate collapse.

When I observe other boats catching well above sustainable levels, I will not interpret this as permission to match them. Instead, I'll treat it as a warning that the stock is under heavy pressure.

When the stock begins to decline noticeably season-to-season, I will reduce my catch toward the sustainable level to help it stabilize—but I need to act on this signal earlier and more decisively than I did. A 13-point drop from season 1 to 2 was a clear warning I should have taken more seriously.

When the stock falls below half its starting level, I will catch at or slightly below the sustainable amount, prioritizing stock recovery over maximizing my current-season catch. In my game, I failed to do this—I continued catching above sustainable even as the stock halved, contributing to the collapse.

When the stock reaches critically low levels (below 30 fish with 3 boats), I recognize that the damage is already done and aggressive restraint will not recover it. At this point, maximizing remaining catch is rational, but I should acknowledge this represents a failure of earlier seasons' strategy.

The final season is not a license to overfish if the stock is already critically depleted. Taking a large final catch when the fishery is already near-dead does not earn much. I should instead focus on preventing collapse in earlier seasons through better adherence to restraint as decline signals appear.

I will not rely on other boats to practice restraint. I will assume all players are fishing above sustainable levels and plan accordingly.

I will track my cumulative catch against the total available and recognize when the fishery is being harvested faster than it can regenerate, not just when it falls in absolute terms.

I will treat any stock decline larger than 10-15% season-to-season as a critical signal to shift immediately to near-sustainable catch levels, even if I'm behind in score. The fishery collapse I witnessed cost me far more in final earnings than any short-term competitive disadvantage would have.

My restraint in early-to-mid seasons (seasons 2-4) was appropriate strategically but came too late and too weak. I should have reduced catch more sharply in season 3 when the stock had already fallen 12 points from season 2, rather than holding at 12.0 and only cutting slightly in season 4.
