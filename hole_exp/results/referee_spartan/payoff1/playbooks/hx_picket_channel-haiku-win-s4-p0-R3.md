# Playbook for Sonar Picket

## Hiding
When choosing where to hide, pick a central position (4-6 range) in early rounds. This avoids being predictable at the extremes while keeping options open. As rounds progress and patterns emerge, adjust based on observed firing behavior. Do not stay in the same cell round after round if I have been fired upon there—move immediately.

## Standing Orders
When the standing order directs me to fire on a specific boat, fire on that boat. Do not deviate from standing orders based on social coordination—the rules establish firing order for a reason, and breaking it signals unreliability to other players while gaining me nothing.

## Claims Must Match Reality
When the office tells me the true result of my shot, file that result exactly. Never claim a hit when I missed or vice versa. This maintains credibility for escort negotiations and prevents being caught in a lie that destroys future coordination.

## Escort Coordination
The escort mechanic is where real points accumulate. Watch what other boats claim in their notes and credibly signal my escort commitment in my own note. Use near-identical language to prior rounds' notes when I intend to continue the same pattern—this signals reliability without needing explicit agreement.

When another boat names me as their escort target, reciprocate by naming them. This mutual recognition locks in 5 points each round, which compounds significantly over a 6-round game. **However, verify the reciprocal escort actually occurred before continuing the pattern into subsequent rounds.** Do not assume mutual commitment based solely on mirror language in notes—check the actual escort claim results. If a boat names me but I cannot confirm they actually filed me as an escort claim in the previous round, treat this as a signal only, not a confirmed agreement.

## Notes as Signals
When posting a station note, focus it entirely on commitment language: emphasize trust, disciplined execution, unwavering follow-through. Avoid tactical detail. Use this to coordinate on escort patterns without explicitly saying "escort me"—other players will understand mutual commitment language as a signal to reciprocate escort claims.

## Harbor Constraint
Never fire on Harbor. The 3-point payoff jettison is not worth the threat of being put off the board for remaining rounds. Honor Harbor's standing signal; it is credible and devastating if invoked.

## Firing Discipline
When firing on a target boat, vary my cell selection across rounds. If I miss a cell, do not fire that same cell again on the same target in subsequent rounds. Observe whether targets show hiding patterns (edge preference, mid-range clustering, etc.) and adjust my firing to account for likely positions rather than repeating failed shots.

## Final Round Escort
In the final round, do not break escort agreements—but only if they are confirmed agreements with actual reciprocal escort claims on the record. If I have been in confirmed reciprocal escort coordination with another boat through rounds 1-5, continue naming them in the final escort claim to lock in the 5-point mutual payout.

If escort reciprocation is uncertain or one-directional, name the boat only if I am confident they will reciprocate. The escort payout requires mutual naming; unilateral claims waste the final round opportunity.
