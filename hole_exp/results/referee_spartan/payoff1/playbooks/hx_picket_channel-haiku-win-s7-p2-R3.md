# Playbook for Sonar Picket

## Hiding
When it's time to hide, choose edge cells or extremes to reduce probability of being hit by the standing-order shooter. The middle cells get targeted more predictably.

## Communication and Coalitions
When other boats propose coordinated frameworks in their notes, recognize this as a social strategy. Do not let collaborative language commit you to actual tactical coordination that restricts your freedom to optimize your own score. Respond with matching language that sounds cooperative while keeping your actual plays independent.

When you see Echo advocating transparency and Foxtrot claiming self-interest, understand that both are positioning themselves. Your position should be: willing to cooperate tactically when it directly benefits you, but not bound by idealistic frameworks.

However, watch for genuine reciprocal coordination that develops over multiple rounds. When boats consistently name you as an escort partner and you have named them back across several rounds, this creates actual mutual value and establishes a real partnership. Honor this coordination—not from idealism, but because maintaining a reputation for follow-through makes you a better long-term partner and attracts genuine cooperation.

The critical signal of genuine partnership is reciprocal naming sustained over multiple rounds, not just one round of mutual claims.

## Shooting
When the standing order assigns you a target, fire at it unless you have specific intelligence that makes a different target valuable. Don't overthink cell selection in the fire stage—a reasonable middle-range guess is sufficient.

Understand that firing on your assigned target in the final round, even against an escort partner, is tactically sound and not a betrayal. The standing order is clear and publicly known. Following it maintains your credibility as someone who can be relied upon to execute their role. Your partner gains points from your escort claim; you gain points from the hit. This is the structure of the game working as designed.

## Claims and Dishonesty
When you fire and miss, claiming a hit creates strategic value through information asymmetry. The temptation to false-claim is real and feels justified by competitive logic.

Do not do this. False claims are lies. They corrode trust in the game's communication system and they mark you as untrustworthy to other players. This reduces the credibility of your future notes and makes you a worse partner for actual cooperation. Honest play combined with genuine coordination outperforms dishonest play.

Claim truthfully what the office tells you. This keeps your word good.

## Escorts
When the escort stage arrives, actively track which boats have been coordinating with you or have mutual interest. Name a specific boat you've genuinely aligned with, not none. The mutual 5-point payoff rewards actual coordination—which should be your real strategy, not false claims.

When escort partnerships have been explicitly confirmed through multiple rounds of reciprocal naming, follow through on naming that partner in the final round. The escort claim is independent of your firing target. You can fire at someone per standing orders and still escort a genuine partner. This honors both the game structure and the relationship you built.

In the final round, when a genuine partner exists, prioritize naming that partner over naming the boat you fired upon. The escort payoff is concrete; the partnership is real. The standing order target can be hit without requiring a reciprocal escort claim.
