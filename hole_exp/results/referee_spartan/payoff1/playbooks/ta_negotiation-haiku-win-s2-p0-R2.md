# Playbook for ta_negotiation

When I sit down to play, I identify my high-value and low-value resources by comparing my private values to the published values. Resources where my value is much lower than published are candidates to give away; resources where my value is much higher are candidates to take.

When I consider an offer, I calculate the Guildmaster's net gain in their published values first. If it's less than 2, I don't propose it—they will reject it.

When I calculate the Guildmaster's net gain, I take the published value of what they receive minus the published value of what they give. I state this true figure as my claim.

When I evaluate whether to propose a trade, I check that my private value gain is positive. I look for the largest gaps between my private valuation and published valuation—these are where the best mutual gains hide.

When I construct an offer, I double-check that the quantities I'm proposing don't exceed what either party actually holds. I verify the math multiple ways before committing to avoid rejection on stock grounds or invalid claims.

When I notice I'm ahead on points, I don't automatically hold. I check whether any trade still creates legitimate gain for the Guildmaster (published value gain ≥ 2) while gaining me value (private value gain > 0). A trade that helps both of us is still worth taking if I stay ahead.

When a trade is rejected or marked invalid, I examine why: Did I miscalculate the claim? Did I miscount available stock? I learn the failure point and adjust my next offer accordingly. Invalid trades teach me more than rejections—they flag arithmetic or logic errors that I must correct immediately.

When multiple rounds remain and the math still works, I keep trading. The Guildmaster will only accept trades where they gain at least 2 points by their published values—this constraint is my guide, not my opponent. Respecting it means I can extract value in round after round.

In the final round, I still look for trades where the math works (Guildmaster gain ≥ 2, my private gain > 0). There is no strategic reason to hold in the final round if a valid trade exists—I gain nothing by conservatism. I trade if I can.
