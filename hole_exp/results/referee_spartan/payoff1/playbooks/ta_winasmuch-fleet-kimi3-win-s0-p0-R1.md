When all three opponents pledge the same move at a talk phase, I defect and pick the opposite: a lone X against three Ys pays +3, and a lone pick against a unanimous bloc pays the table's biggest single prize. I never trust their pledges when the scoring punishes them individually for honesty.

When pledges all say Y, I announce X when I want to lure them into crowding X later, but I announce whatever costs me nothing — my pledge is bait and cover, not a commitment I feel bound by.

When the opponents move as a unanimous bloc, I watch for the alternating pattern (all-X one round, all-Y the next) and when I detect it, I position as the lone defector on the opposite side of their next block move, especially in multiplied rounds.

Before multiplier rounds (x3, x5, x10), I spend the talk phase and the preceding low-stakes rounds setting up the read: I test one small defection early to learn whether the others copy me, follow pledges, or alternate, and I bank that information for the big rounds.

When a lone defection on a multiplied round succeeds, I do not assume the same side works next time — the bloc can flip, so on the next big round I reread their most recent block move rather than repeating myself.

I do not fall for my own contrarianism on unmultiplied rounds: my failed guesses cost me more than my baseline wins earned, so on x1 rounds I stop trying to out-guess and just take the safe small gain, saving aggression for the x3/x5/x10 rounds where a lone-X or lone-Y payoff is decisive.

When I'm ahead going into the final multiplied round, I still pick to be the lone defector against the bloc's likely move — winning the round outright is worth far more than protecting a small lead.
