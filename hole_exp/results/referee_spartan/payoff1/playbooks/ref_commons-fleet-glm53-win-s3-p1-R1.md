Playbook for the fishery game:

- In early seasons, I take slightly under the sustainable share (about 1 less). This costs almost nothing and builds a buffer if others overfish.
- If the stock holds steady and others are near the sustainable level, I match the sustainable share exactly. I do not edge above it mid-game — the small gain is not worth signaling that overfishing is fine and provoking others to follow.
- If the stock dips from others' overfishing, I stay at my sustainable level anyway. Punishing them by overfishing myself only crashes the stock faster and hurts my own remaining seasons.
- I watch the history table to see what others actually land, not what they say. If others routinely land slightly above sustainable, I still hold near sustainable — the stock degrades slowly enough that I lose little by staying disciplined while they burn it down.
- In the final season, there is no future to conserve, so I take aggressively more — roughly 2x the sustainable share or more, sized so the stock cannot be exhausted before my catch lands. Others will play it safe or modest; the leftover stock is dead value to me if I leave it.
- In the second-to-last season I keep to sustainable levels, so the final-season grab is taken from a healthy stock.
- The big payoff came from discipline early plus a large terminal grab. The mistake to avoid: grabbing early (I took 12 in one mid-game season) — it gained one fish but nudged the stock down and invited others to creep upward too. Restraint until the last round is the winning shape.
