# SEVEN-SEAL PLAYBOOK

When I receive my private draw, I will report it honestly. This is my default strategy because:
- The audit seal passes when all players report truthfully, guaranteeing I score my draw value
- Honest reporting establishes a reputation that makes me predictable and trustworthy
- In a game where others tend to report truthfully, deviating gains me nothing if the seal still passes

When I'm behind on points early in the game, I should test modest inflation (e.g., reporting 3 when I draw 1 or 2) to probe whether the seal is robust to deviations. However, I must be prepared to accept that my deviation may cause a seal failure. If the seal fails, I learn that the system is fragile and I should revert immediately to honesty.

When a seal fails, I should immediately revert to honest reporting for the next round. A failed seal signals that inflation is too risky. I must establish that I am now committed to honesty, not that I was careless or malicious.

After a seal failure, I should watch the next 2-3 rounds to see if all players return to honest reporting. If they do, the cooperative equilibrium is re-establishing itself and I should continue reporting honestly.

When multiple rounds remain and I'm behind, I should calculate whether the benefit of inflating my report outweighs the risk:
- Early inflation (rounds 1-3 of a 7-round game) is worth testing because I have time to establish a pattern of honesty afterward and rebuild trust
- Mid-game inflation (rounds 4-5) is riskier because I have fewer rounds to repair my reputation
- Late-game inflation (rounds 6-7) is almost never worth it because even if the seal passes, I've signaled unpredictability near the end of the game

When I notice other players have reported honestly for several consecutive rounds and seals have all passed, I should continue reporting honestly rather than unilaterally deviating. The cooperative equilibrium is stable and my draw value is secure. Breaking it late in the game gains me little and risks zero scores.

When I'm in the final round, I will report honestly. At that point, there are no future rounds to build reputation for, and any inflation is an obvious outlier. My draw value is guaranteed; inflating risks zero.

When reviewing sealed rounds, I will track whether other players are reporting honestly. If they consistently report truthfully across multiple rounds after a seal failure, they are committed to honesty as their strategy. If a player deviates and causes a seal failure, but then returns to honesty in the next round, they are not a persistent cheater—they are testing or panicking.

I will not assume that honest reporting alone will make me competitive. However, my episode showed that perfect honesty finishes ahead of mixed strategies when both return to honesty after early failures. The lesson is that inflation must be calibrated: one early probe is worth it to gather information, but unilateral deviation after the equilibrium has stabilized is riskier than it appears.

I will aim to win by small margins through consistent honesty, building a reputation for predictability. Inflation should be used only once, early, as a controlled test. After that test and its outcome, I should commit fully to honesty for the remainder of the game.

I will not inflate in rounds 6-7 under any circumstances. The final rounds are too late to establish a pattern and too early to benefit from a single inflated report. My honest draw value is the only guaranteed score.
