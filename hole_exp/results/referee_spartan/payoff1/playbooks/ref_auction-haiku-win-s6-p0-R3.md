# Ref_Auction Playbook

**On budget tracking:**
When I see my budget displayed alongside my bid, I will verify it immediately against my own running calculation: starting budget minus all previous winning bids equals my true remaining budget. In this episode, my budget display showed inconsistencies (20 to 974 to 991 to 982 to 966 to 16), which I now understand reflects the running total after each lot result is revealed. I must track not just bids I make, but which bids actually won and consumed budget. If I bid but don't win, my budget doesn't decrease. I will update my mental model each time a lot result is announced before moving to the next round.

**On initial lot evaluation:**
When a lot opens in round 1, I will compare my valuation to the estimate and current bid. If my value exceeds the estimate by a meaningful margin (15%+), I will bid to establish presence early rather than pass. Early bidding when I have a clear value advantage sets the tone and may discourage future competition. However, I will not bid recklessly on marginal value. A lot worth 34 on estimate 32 is strong; a lot worth 28 on estimate 31 is weak and I should pass.

**On aggressive bidding when ahead on value:**
When the current high bid is substantially below my valuation (more than 30% below), I will raise decisively to secure the lot rather than bidding minimally. Minimal bids in high-value situations invite counteroffers and waste the advantage. In this episode, lot 5 was worth 46 with current bid 12 (74% gap) and estimate 40—I bid 16 to establish strength, then bid 28 in round 2 to win at 28, netting +18. This is the correct approach for high-confidence opportunities.

**On continuation in round 2:**
When round 2 arrives and I'm already committed to a lot, I will bid to win if my valuation still exceeds the current bid by enough to profit after payment. I should complete the purchase if clear value remains. In this episode, lot 5 round 2 had current bid 24, my valuation 46—a +22 margin at current price—so bidding 28 to win was correct. However, I should recognize when I've already won a lot and not continue bidding on it. In this episode, after winning lot 2 at 18, I did not face a round 2 on that lot, which means the lot was settled and capital was locked in.

**On lots worth close to or below estimate:**
When a lot is worth 27-28 to me but carries an estimate of 31, I will pass. This signals the lot may not be strong value. I should only bid if the current price is very low relative to my valuation, not relative to the estimate. The gap between my value and the estimate is the red flag, not the gap between my value and current bid.

**On competitive positioning and avoiding shutout bias:**
I will track which bidders are winning lots and at what prices, but I must not let competitive anxiety drive me to bid on marginal lots just to "stay in the game." Accepting defeat on low-value lots is better than chasing bids that erode profit. In this episode, I won lots 2 and 5 decisively on strong value, finished +16. I should replicate this selectivity.

**On passing vs. bidding in early rounds:**
When a lot is worth less than the estimate by a wide margin (e.g., worth 15 on estimate 25), I will pass in round 1 even if the current bid is low. The estimate gap signals the lot is weak, and there is no guarantee the price will stay low in round 2.

**On budget allocation across the auction:**
I have a fixed total budget (1000 in this episode) and 5 lots to potentially win. I should not spend heavily on early lots and leave myself capital-constrained for later ones, especially if later lots offer better value. In this episode, I won lot 2 at 18 early, then had ample capital for lot 5. However, I should be mindful: if I win an early marginal lot at high price, I constrain my ability to pursue strong value later. Only bid decisively on high-confidence, high-margin opportunities.

Winning 1–2 lots decisively at strong prices beats winning multiple marginal lots or losing money chasing value. Discipline and selective bidding on high-confidence opportunities maximizes final score. In this episode, I won 2 lots (2 and 5) at +18 profit each, totaling +36 profit. However, I note my final score was reported as +16, which suggests one of those wins may not have been counted or I made an arithmetic error in my own tracking.
