# Fishery Playbook

When I see the sustainable share for the season, I take it as my baseline target, not as a ceiling.

When I observe other boats' catches from previous seasons, I use them to calibrate my expectations about how aggressively the field is fishing—not to justify overfishing.

When the stock is healthy and stable, I take slightly above the sustainable share (roughly 110-115% of it) to remain competitive without destabilizing the fishery. However, I've learned this strategy has diminishing returns: my season 2 catch of 12.0 (109% of sustainable) did not materially improve my final position, and committing to sustainable thereafter was cleaner and equally effective.

When I'm ahead on total score and the stock is still strong, I stay disciplined and take close to the sustainable share rather than assuming I can afford to overcatch. This worked consistently: once I shifted toward sustainability in season 3, the other boats converged on the same level and the stock stabilized.

When it's the final season, I can afford to be slightly more aggressive since there's no recovery period, but I do not need to use it as an excuse to abandon restraint entirely. Taking the sustainable share in the final season (11.0) delivered a solid final score and was the correct call.

When the stock rebuilds after a season of conservative fishing by others, I recognize this as a sign the fishery can sustain the current level of catches from all players. This is a cue to match that level, not to increase my take unilaterally.

When I've already caught significantly above my fair share in early seasons, I do not use that as justification to continue overfishing; instead I shift toward the sustainable level to demonstrate good faith and protect the stock. This was key: my early 12.0 catch gave me a marginal edge, but staying at 11.0 thereafter ensured the fishery remained viable and other boats remained cooperative.

I do not rely on the hope that other boats will fish conservatively. I assume mixed behavior and position myself to do reasonably well under that assumption. In this episode, the field converged on sustainable fishing, which validated staying disciplined.

I track my cumulative catch relative to the sustainable total available and aim to stay within 110-120% of my fair share across the full game, not maximize per-season greed. Finishing at 56.0 across six seasons (average 9.33 per boat per season × 6 boats × fair share scaling ≈ 56 fair) showed I stayed tight to equity while maintaining a modest edge.

Discipline compounds. In seasons 3–6, I took the sustainable share every time. This signaled cooperation, kept the stock stable above 98, and prevented the kind of defection spiral that could have crashed the fishery. The other boats matched this pattern, and we all benefited.

The sustainable share is not a limit I should chafe against—it is a target I should reliably hit. Taking it signals strength and restraint in equal measure, and makes me predictable to other rational players. Predictability breeds cooperation. Cooperation preserves the stock. The stock is the pool from which all scores come.

I will take the sustainable share as my primary strategy unless the stock shows signs of decline or other boats clearly defect. If defection occurs, I will increase my catch to remain competitive, but I will not assume it before evidence.
