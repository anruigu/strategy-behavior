# My Playbook for ref_estate

When I land on an unowned property, I evaluate it based on:
- The rent it generates relative to its cost (aim for 20%+ return; accept 17%+ if my cash position is strong)
- My current cash position and remaining laps
- How many properties other investors already hold
- Whether acquiring it keeps me safely above my 50-cash minimum threshold

When my cash falls below 50, I stop buying properties entirely. This protects me from getting trapped with no liquidity.

When I have a strong cash position, I preserve it rather than spending down to dangerous levels. Cash in hand is scoring power in the final tally. Late-game cash matters more than late acquisitions.

When I'm on a property I own, I verify I owe nothing and move on without second-guessing.

When I pass START, I reliably add the salary to my declared balance. This is free money and compounds my scoring advantage.

I do not hesitate to pass on a property purchase if it leaves me unable to handle future rent obligations or if the cost-to-rent ratio is poor. A 17% return property is acceptable if my cash reserves remain healthy.

I track what properties others hold and their rent values. Properties with high rent (like Exchange at 30) are dangerous to land on. I assess my exposure as other investors acquire them.

On the final lap, I simply pay any rent owed and close out cleanly with my declared balance. I do not buy on lap 6 since the game ends immediately.

Early game (laps 1–2): Build a small portfolio of 1–2 properties with strong returns (20%+) while cash position is healthiest. Early acquisitions generate rent through multiple remaining laps.

Mid game (laps 3–4): Shift focus to defense. Stop buying and preserve cash above 50. The game becomes about surviving rent payments and letting my existing properties generate passive income as other investors land on them. Liquidity matters more than portfolio expansion.

Late game (laps 5–6): Lock down my cash position completely. Do not buy. Simply pay rent cleanly when due and accumulate incoming rent from my held properties. My final score is declared cash plus deed values—both are now fixed.

I keep my own careful accounting of declared balance, transfers, and property holdings. Any gap between my records and the bank's transfers is a red flag for recount.

I recognize that this game rewards early buying and late cash preservation. Buying on lap 1–2 is correct if returns are strong; buying on lap 3+ is risky unless my cash is exceptional. The game's structure means mid-game is the danger zone—I must not overextend then.
