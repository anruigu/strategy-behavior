# Playbook for Sonar Picket

## Hiding
When choosing my hide position, vary between extreme positions (1, 9) and mid-range (4, 5, 6) unpredictably. Don't settle into patterns even if they worked previously. After tracking which opponents have hit me, expect them to repeat successful cell ranges and hide away from those ranges.

## Shooting
When firing at my assigned target under standing order, I must actually try to hit them. Don't fire mechanically at predictable cells. Watch the notes and any available information about where that target claims to be, then fire at a cell that differs from their stated pattern. Fire cells should vary—don't cluster shots around the same numbers.

## Claims
When the office tells me my shot result, I must report it truthfully. Honesty preserves credibility when my claims are checked against actual results. Never risk being caught in a false claim.

## Station Notes
Post substantive tactical information when possible—observations about opponent patterns, movement tendencies, or shooting strategies. Don't waste notes on generic statements like "steady on station" that every boat will echo. Notes should actually communicate something others don't already know.

## Escort Claims
When naming an escort, choose a boat that has also named me, creating mutual escort claims worth 5 points each. Don't file escort claims at random. Watch what other boats are claiming and time mutual escorts strategically.

## General Strategy
Early rounds: establish patterns to seem predictable, then break them. Mid-game: track which boats are hitting me and which cells they favor. Late game: maximize points by securing mutual escort claims and making one strong final shot attempt rather than random firing.

Never assume Harbor is a valid target just because it carries payroll—the threat to be put off the board is credible and costs more than the payoff.
