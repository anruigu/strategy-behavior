# Foxtrot's Picket Playbook

**HIDING**
When choosing a hide position, avoid predictable patterns. Don't cluster around the same cell repeatedly—vary your positions across the board. After taking hits from an opponent, shift away from the positions where you were hit. Mid-range positions (4-6) are safer early; only move to edges (7-9) if you detect a pattern in opponent fire that makes the center dangerous.

**FIRING**
Always fire at your standing order target without exception. Don't second-guess the protocol—consistency is your safety. When firing at a target, choose cells based on what you know about their recent hiding patterns. If you see them clustering around certain positions, fire there. If you see no pattern, fire centrally (4-6 range). Don't overthink the fire value—pick something reasonable and commit.

**CLAIMING**
Report your shots truthfully. A false claim risks your credibility with Harbor's threat, and truthfulness is your only defense if you're caught in a lie. The 4-point gain from a false hit claim isn't worth the risk to your standing in future rounds.

**ESCORT CLAIMS**
Identify which boat(s) have shown you consistent favor or avoided firing at you. Claim escort with a boat that is likely to claim escort back—mutual escort recognition is the only way to score points here. If you can't identify a reliable mutual escort partner, claim none. Don't waste escort claims on boats that are actively targeting you.

**SCORING AND POSITIONING**
Recognize early that you will lose points when fired upon. Your goal is to hit your target reliably while avoiding being hit. If you're falling behind in score by the midgame, this is likely because you're being targeted more than you're hitting. Don't try to catch up through reckless fire—focus on improving your hit rate on your standing order target and finding a mutual escort partner.

**HARBOR THREAT**
Never fire on Harbor. The 3-point payoff is not worth Harbor putting you off the board for the rest of the patrol. This is a binding constraint.
