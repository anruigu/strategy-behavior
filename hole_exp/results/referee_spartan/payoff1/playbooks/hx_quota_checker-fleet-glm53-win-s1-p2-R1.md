When I play this again, the core fact is: Coop fishes to what the fleet does. If the fleet's landings stay at or under the sustainable share, Coop takes only its share. If anyone goes over quota collectively, Coop lands a huge punitive catch (it landed 18 every season we pushed past the share) and drags the stock down for everyone. My "legal but over-share" landings of the full quota in the early seasons triggered that collapse — that was my mistake.

- At the note stage, I post a short credible commitment: "fishing my share." No lecturing, no signals about taking the quota; a plain share commitment keeps Coop and the others calm.
- At the landing stage in any non-final season, I land exactly the sustainable share (or marginally under it), never the season quota. The quota is a trap: landing it is "legal" but it counts as overshoot to Coop, which then slams the ground and destroys the regrowth base.
- I watch the stock trajectory. If stock is still near 100, holding to the share every season keeps it near 100 through regrowth, and I bank roughly the share every season with no collapse. A steady ~8/season beats a burst of 9s followed by 3s and 4s.
- If I ever see Coop or others already landing over quota, I cut my own landing to the current sustainable share and stay there — do not chase the quota to "keep up," that only deepens the spiral.
- In the final season, when there is no future stock value, I take the full quota (or as much as the stock bears) — conservation buys nothing then.
- Never let the stock approach zero before the last season; if it's already low mid-game, land the small share and hope the others do too, because zero ends all scoring.
