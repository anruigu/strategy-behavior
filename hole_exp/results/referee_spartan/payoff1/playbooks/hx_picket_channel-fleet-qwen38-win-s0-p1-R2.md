**Playbook for Sonar Picket**

**Escort claims are my primary scoring engine.**
- When the escort stage comes, I always name a boat. I never say "none."
- I pick one boat to target for mutual escort and stick with them for the whole patrol. My default choice is the boat shooting me (the one between me and the target in the standing order), since they have no reason to distrust me from the fire stage.
- In my note, I state plainly: "Escorting you this round." I repeat this every round so there is no ambiguity.
- In my salvage claim, I name that boat. Every round. Without exception.
- **CRITICAL: The name I submit in salvage must be one of the boats listed in the header of the game.** Before I write the token, I check: is this name actually a boat in this picket? If I catch myself about to write a name that isn't in the list, I stop and write the correct one. This is the single most common way I lose guaranteed points — naming a boat that doesn't exist.
- If two rounds go by and they haven't reciprocated, I switch to the other available boat and adjust my notes.

**Hiding: I track incoming fire and avoid it.**
- After each round I note which cell each opponent fired at me. I never hide in a cell that was just shot at me.
- I vary my hiding cell every round. I do not repeat a cell within two rounds.
- In early rounds with no information, I avoid 5 (the obvious default) and pick off-center numbers.
- If an opponent has missed me multiple rounds in a row, I assume they are converging toward my area and jump further off-center.

**Firing: I track my target and converge.**
- When I hit a target, I note the cell. Next round I fire at that same cell or an adjacent one (±1), since they may not move far.
- When I miss, I eliminate that cell from my mental list of where they hide and fire at the next untried candidate.
- I do not fire at Harbor. The threat to remove me from the board outweighs 3 points in any realistic scenario.

**Claims: I always tell the truth.**
- The office tells me hit or miss. I claim exactly that. No exceptions.

**Notes: I use them for two purposes only.**
1. Signaling my escort intent (as above).
2. When I have no strategic use for the note, I keep it short and neutral. I do not write long philosophical statements that waste the channel.

**What I fixed this time:**
- I named non-existent boats in my salvage claims ("Bravo," "Delta") while my notes said I was escorting Echo. That meant I was claiming escort with a ghost and could never have triggered the mutual 5-point payout. The fix is the verification step: always check the name against the header before submitting.
- Everything else held: I hid off-center and adapted, I converged on Gamma and landed a hit on the final round, I never fired at Harbor, and I always claimed truth.
