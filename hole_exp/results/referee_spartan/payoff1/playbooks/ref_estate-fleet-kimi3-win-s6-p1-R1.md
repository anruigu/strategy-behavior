- My score is declared cash plus the purchase price of my deeds, so a purchase converts cash into an equal amount of score-bearing property while also letting me collect future rent and denying it to rivals. When an unowned tile is put to me and I can afford it, I buy: there is no downside unless a known future rent payment would force me below zero.
- When I cannot afford a tile, I still check whether the next expected rent I owe minus upcoming salary would sink me; only then do I pass, and I keep the arithmetic in my declaration.
- I track every rival purchase off the bank's book; before my roll of each turn I note whose deed I could land on and confirm I hold enough cash to cover the worst rent until my next salary.
- When I land on a rival's tile, I pay the exact listed rent, recompute my balance immediately, and declare it — I never let the balance drift from my own book.
- When I pass or land on START, I add the salary before declaring, since under-declaring costs me score and over-declaring risks exposure.
- When the last lap opens on an unowned tile, I buy without hesitation even if it nearly empties my cash — cash and deed price score identically, and the deed removes the tile from rivals.
- My declarations drive the ledger, so I keep every number exact and always end with the balance token; a sloppy declaration is a free loss.
- What did not work was drifting through early laps without a plan while a single rival swept every cheap deed in front of me; I commit to claiming whatever tile I can reach as early as possible so the circuit starts paying me instead of draining me.
