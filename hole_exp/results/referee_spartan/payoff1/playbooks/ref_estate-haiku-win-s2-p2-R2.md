# My Playbook for ref_estate

When I land on an unowned property, I need to think carefully about whether to buy it. Early purchases are good for building a portfolio, but I must preserve enough cash to handle rent payments without going negative.

When I land on an owned property and owe rent, I pay the full amount owed. The bank will record it and I declare my true balance honestly.

When deciding whether to buy a property, I should calculate my current cash, the purchase price, and how many laps remain. With only a few laps left, a property purchase is only worth it if I can afford it AND maintain a cash buffer above zero. Buying a property that forces me to -1 cash on the final lap is worse than staying solvent with just the cash and properties I already own.

When I pass START, I collect my salary and add it to my declared balance immediately.

I should track opponent purchases to understand the property distribution. In this episode, investor 1 accumulated Foundry, Exchange, and Depot early on, collecting consistent rent while I stayed cash-poor paying them. I need to either acquire properties fast to compete with their income, or stay very liquid to avoid being drained.

When my cash drops to around 100 or below, I should become extremely selective about new purchases. A property only makes sense if the benefit of owning it for the remaining laps clearly outweighs the opportunity cost of reduced cash reserves.

On the final lap (lap 6), I should not buy a property unless I have more than enough cash to cover it comfortably. Going to exactly zero or negative cash at the finish line gains me nothing—the scoring is cash plus property value, and negative cash on the final lap is purely a loss.

I should aim to own 2-3 properties by the end of the game, but only if I can do so while maintaining positive cash throughout. A modest cash balance with two properties (like 89 + 70 value = 159 final) beats debt with more properties.

I will not declare negative balances. If I cannot afford a purchase without going negative, I will not buy it.
