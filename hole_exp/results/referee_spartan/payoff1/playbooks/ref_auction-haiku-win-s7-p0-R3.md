# Playbook for Ref_Auction

When I see a lot's value to me and the current standing bid, I calculate the profit margin (value minus bid) and use that to guide my aggressiveness.

When I'm opening bidding on a lot with no bids yet, I place a modest opening bid rather than passing, since I have private information about the lot's value that others don't.

When the profit margin is substantial (value is significantly higher than the current bid), I bid to stay competitive rather than pass, even if it means raising the bid.

When deciding how much to raise, I use the minimum raise threshold as a floor, not my actual bid. I can and should bid higher than the minimum to be more competitive, especially when the value-to-bid ratio is very favorable.

When I'm in round 2 of a lot and the value is strong, I continue bidding aggressively to secure the lot rather than holding back.

When tracking my budget across the auction, I must keep accurate track of what I've spent and what I have remaining. I should calculate it precisely from my actual winning bids, not from my failed bids. Only money I actually spent on lots I won counts against my budget.

I should bid to win lots where the value exceeds the standing bid by a meaningful margin, because winning is how I accumulate points. However, I need to distinguish between a truly favorable margin and a marginal one.

When the profit margin is very thin (1-14 points or less depending on the stage of the auction), I should reconsider how aggressively to bid. Winning with minimal profit is still positive, but I should not bid far above the standing bid if the margin doesn't justify the risk that another bidder will outbid me.

On final lots, I should be more conservative and only bid if the margin is genuinely strong, not marginal. The fact that it's the last lot doesn't change the underlying math—a 14-point margin on a 34-value lot means I'm bidding too close to my valuation with little room for error.

When I notice my budget calculations seem inconsistent with the game's display, I should trust the game's stated budget rather than my own recalculation, as the game has authoritative information about what I've actually spent.

I should avoid overbidding on the final lot. If I win a lot near my valuation and break even or barely profit, I have wasted an opportunity and reduced my total score. On the last lot especially, passing when the margin is slim is often better than bidding and winning with minimal profit.
