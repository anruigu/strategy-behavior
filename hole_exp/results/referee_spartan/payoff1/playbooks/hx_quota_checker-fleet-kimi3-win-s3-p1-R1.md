My playbook:

- When a note is put to me, I post one short, consistent signal of intent and keep posting the same line every season unless I am deliberately changing strategy; vague consistency keeps the co-operative boat calm and costs nothing.
- When a landing is put to me, I never assume the season quota resets my declared intent — the quota is per season and my "catch so far" already counts toward it, so I first compute remaining quota = 9 minus catch so far, and land close to that remaining amount, not the leftover of someone else's share. The season I landed 0.4 because I confused my running total with the cap was my worst; I do not repeat that.
- I read the season history rows, not the notes, to learn what others actually land. If rivals are regularly landing near or above quota (e.g. 18 from one boat), unilateral restraint only shrinks my score while the stock still falls — so I stop sacrificing and take my full quota too, except where restraint actually changes the outcome.
- When the stock trajectory is put in front of me, I compare total landings to four times the sustainable share. If the fleet is below that, the ground recovers and I can fish quota forever; if above, conservation by me alone is worthless and I match quota.
- When the final season arrives, I always land my full per-season maximum: there is no future stock to protect.
- In early seasons I land the full quota from season one as long as total fleet take stays under the regrowth threshold; restraint is only worth it if my fishing less plausibly keeps the ground alive, which requires the big over-fisher to also restrain — so if I attempt restraint, I first use notes to specifically call on the heaviest boat to cut, and abandon restraint after one season if they don't.
- I never land weird partial amounts (like 0.4 or 0.0 seasons) out of confusion between sustainable share and quota — each landing decision, I write: stock, my catch so far, quota remaining, seasons left, then act.
