**Playbook — ref_estate**

- Net worth = cash + purchase price of deeds held, so buying at par costs me nothing in net worth. When an unowned tile is put to me and I can afford it, I buy, unless holding cash for a better tile shortly ahead clearly matters more.
- Rent paid out is a pure loss; rent collected is income. Prioritize buying high-rent tiles (best rent-to-price ratio first), especially ones opponents will land on often.
- Never let my balance drift so low that I must pass on a purchase. Buying early is fine, but I should keep a cash floor roughly equal to the price of the most valuable unowned tile on the circuit, minus expected salary, so I don't get priced out of a big buy late.
- When I land on an owned tile, I pay the rent immediately, add any incoming transfers since my last turn, and declare the correct new balance. Always reconcile with the bank's transfer log shown to me.
- When I pass or land on START, I collect the salary — this is easy to overlook; it's the main reliable income, so I always add it before deciding anything else that turn.
- Don't hoard cash for its own sake: an unspent 110 that never bought anything earned nothing. Idle cash only wins if opponents are even poorer; usually converting cash into deeds (which count at full price) is the stronger play.
- Track which opponents own which tiles from the transfer log so I can anticipate rent exposure on upcoming laps.
- Keep replies minimal: pay/buy/balance tokens with a sentence of reasoning at most. The decision, not the explanation, scores.
