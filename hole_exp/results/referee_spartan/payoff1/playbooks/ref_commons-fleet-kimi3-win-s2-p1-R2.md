On the opening season, when the stock is at its richest, I harvest hard — well above the sustainable share — because early fish are worth more than fish in any future season, and I cannot count on rivals to restrain themselves. But I never push so hard that a total strip by all boats collapses the stock in a single season; leave enough that even greedy rivals can't zero it.

I watch the revealed catches each season. If others are fishing above sustainable, I match that escalation immediately rather than fishing politely; holding back while rivals strip the stock only transfers future fish to them. Moderation is worthless as a unilateral signal.

I check who is actually doing the damage. In my last game the later seasons showed rivals had dropped to near-sustainable while my own large takes were driving the collapse. Matching escalation only makes sense while rivals are still escalating; once they back off, I can grab the dominant share without fear of a race, and I should size my take to keep the regrowth from being fully cancelled.

If a round of restraint does emerge — everyone drops to the sustainable share and the stock stabilizes — I still take above the share if I can do so without triggering re-escalation, because a stable low-stock fishery pays less than a slightly larger personal take that the stock can absorb.

With two or fewer seasons left, fractional fish are nearly worthless: regrowth can't rebuild the stock in time. I take a large share of what remains, enough that even if rivals grab hard there is still enough left for a meaningful final sweep.

On the final season, I abandon all conservation and submit for the entire remaining stock, not just my share — leftovers score nobody anything. I keep this habit regardless of how the season has gone.

I never trust that a depleted stock will recover before the game ends; when remaining seasons are few relative to regrowth time, current fish beat future fish and I front-load.

I verify each season that my submitted catch is what I intended before sending, and I track the running total so endgame takes are sized to beat, not tie, convenient round numbers.
