My playbook for the fishery game:

- Early seasons: when the stock is at or near full and others are taking the sustainable share, I match the sustainable share exactly. This keeps the stock at maximum and preserves cooperation. This worked perfectly for five straight seasons in the episode just played.
- Cooperation can be stable: if every boat keeps to the sustainable share and the stock stays at max, do not "pre-emptively" defect mid-game. Any early deviation risks triggering a cycle of overfishing that costs more than the one-season gain.
- When a rival takes above sustainable and the stock dips slightly, I do not chase them for one season's marginal gain — it barely moves my total but risks triggering escalation. Revert to the sustainable share the following season while the stock is still healthy.
- Final season: since regrowth no longer matters, extract fully — take roughly the whole stock divided by the number of boats (or as much as I can get). Taking 33.3 of a 100 stock was right; anything less than my full share of what remains is fish left on the dock. This paid off: the final score of 82.8 exceeded the pure-cooperation total of 66.6.
- Second-to-last season: consider whether a modest overshoot is worth it if the stock is high and the last-season regrowth from a slightly reduced stock is small — but only if it won't cause rivals to punish me in the final round. With a short horizon and stable cooperation, staying at sustainable until the final round was the safer, near-optimal play. Note: overshooting in the last non-final season also doesn't tip off rivals before they can react in time — worth re-examining next episode, since rivals may also defect in the final round and a second-to-last overshoot may go unpunished.
- If others overfish repeatedly, I still keep to sustainable while the stock is high, because the fishery only dies at zero; small over-extraction by one boat is survivable across a short horizon.
- In the final season, if others defect too, the stock simply splits among the boats that ask for it — requesting more than stock/boats is wasted, so cap my final ask at remaining stock divided by number of boats.
- General rule: discount the last round (extract fully), cooperate on all earlier rounds, and only deviate if a single-round gain clearly outweighs the compounding loss of stock across remaining seasons.
- My pattern of full cooperation followed by a final-round defection yielded a strong score; repeat this shape next episode unless rivals' behavior forces an earlier adjustment.
