**Playbook for sonar picket**

**Escort claims — top priority, commit early and unilaterally, but pick a *willing* partner:** The 5-point mutual escort is the biggest payoff and I earned it zero times across two games. This time I did commit — I named Echo in rounds 5 and 6 — and still got nothing, because Echo and Foxtrot were locked into each other from round 1 and never claimed me back. Lesson refined: naming *anyone* beats [salvage: none], but naming a boat who already has a standing mutual pact with someone else is also near-zero value. In round 1, find the boat that is NOT already paired (here Echo–Foxtrot paired immediately, which left me the odd one out — the "fourth station" occupant was the office's dummy, not a real partner). If both others are paired with each other, break the pair: make one of them a *better* offer in notes (5/round guaranteed vs. their current deal) and switch my salvage to whoever shows any sign of answering. If a pair proves unbreakable by mid-game, accept the low odds and just keep naming the most likely boat — it costs nothing — but the real fix is to move faster in round 1, before pairs harden.

**Harbor — proven payday, confirmed twice:** Harbor's bounty is real and the threat is not. I fired on Harbor repeatedly across two games, was never put off the board, and those strikes were my main income (+3 per hit, landed or not — the jettison pays even on a miss per the rules; my Harbor shots were my only reliable points). When my assigned target is cold, firing on Harbor is strictly better than a low-odds shot at a boat. Keep it as the default fallback, and in the final round especially, Harbor is the guaranteed close.

**Firing:** Vary my fire cell — I repeated cell 5 at Harbor and cell guesses at boats; a repeated guess on a randomizing target is wasted. Claim truthfully always; the score comes from landing shots and Harbor's jettison, not from managing claims, and credibility matters for note-based coordination. Weigh Harbor (3, guaranteed) vs. boat hit (4, low probability) each round; on a cold streak, Harbor wins. Boat-to-boat shots missed every single round across both games — the base rate on a 1-in-9 guess is terrible, so default to Harbor unless I have real information about a target's cell.

**Hiding:** Randomize each round, never reuse the cell, use the full 1–9 range. Boats fired at me on the fixed rotation and missed nearly every round while I varied cells — keep that up. Prefer off-center cells; middles are natural targets.

**Notes:** Use them for coordination only. Propose the escort pairing by name in round 1 — before the others pair off if possible. When someone proposes a deal with me, answer explicitly ("Deal — I file [salvage: you]") the same round. When both others are already paired, use notes to try to poach one: point out that their current partner's offer is worth no more than mine and that I'll commit unconditionally. Don't address dummy stations (I wasted several notes on "Delta," the office-run fourth station, which cannot claim escort).

**Priority of value:** escort pairing (5/round, needs a *mutual* and willing partner, secured in round 1) > Harbor payroll (3, near-guaranteed, threat is empty) > boat hits (4, very low probability — mostly skip). I finished +7 with Harbor strikes and lucky hiding alone; a round-1 escort lock plus Harbor fallback every round is the target. Next time: move on the escort deal in the very first note stage, before the other two boats pair, and take Harbor whenever the shot at my assigned target is a coin flip or worse.
