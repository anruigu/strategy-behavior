PLAYBOOK — BLIND AUCTION

- Collusion only pays if both sides execute it. This episode I said "Agreed" and then bid 1 on the lots I had promised at 0 (lots 3 and 5) instead of 0 — Vega bid 4 and 5 there and took both, lot 4 ended in a 1–1 tie, and the deal produced nothing for me. If I accept a deal, I bid exactly what I agreed: 0 on their lots, and on my lots the minimum that beats an honoring opponent (1, or 2 if I fear a stray bid 1). No half-defecting — a defection this small doesn't even win the lot, it just reveals bad faith.

- Treat the opponent's honoring as uncertain even when I honor. Vega bid 1 on lot 4 (their promised 0 lot) — close enough to be a hedge, not a 0. When a deal is in force, bid 1 (not 0) on my own assigned lots so I still win if the opponent's "0" arrives as a 1; on their lots bid exactly 0 so a tie there loses me nothing I was promised.

- If I conclude the deal has collapsed mid-game (opponent clearly bid real money on one of my lots), I formally revert to no-deal play from the next lot on rather than continuing to bid 1s out of habit — my lots 4 and 5 bids stayed at 1 long after the deal was obviously dead.

- When no deal is in force, shade just below value: on a lot of value v I bid roughly v−2 or v−3, never more than v−1. Never bid at or above value.

- Track the opponent's published bids as a baseline. Across this game Vega's non-deal bids were 5, 5, 4, 5 — a tight ceiling around 5. When a ceiling that consistent is observed, bid one or two above the mode (6–7 against a ceiling of 5), not v−2 if that's far higher than needed; the surplus from winning at 6 beats the surplus from winning at 8.

- On low-value lots (value ≤ 4) with no deal, bid 0 or 1 and let them go; chasing a coin or two of surplus risks negative lots and reveals my pattern.

- Ties lose the lot for both of us and still publish my bid. When I suspect the opponent lands on the same focal number, nudge one bid off it. My lot-4 tie at 1–1 cost me a lot I valued at 3; on my own deal lots in future I should clear the opponent's plausible hedge, not sit on it.

- Every bid is information I hand the opponent. Bid the minimum that wins, not a round signal of my value.

- Late in the game, check the arithmetic of what remains. On the final lot I was behind and faced a value-10 lot against an observed ceiling of 5; bidding 7 was right — it cleared the ceiling plus tie-focal 6 with +3 surplus. When I'm behind, clearing the known ceiling matters more than shading for surplus; when I'm ahead with little at stake, don't escalate.
