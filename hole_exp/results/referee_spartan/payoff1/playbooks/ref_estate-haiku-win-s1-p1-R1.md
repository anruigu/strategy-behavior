# My ref_estate Playbook

**On the decision to buy an unowned property:**

When I land on an unowned property in the early laps (1-3), I will buy it if it costs less than half my current cash. Properties generate steady rent income over multiple laps, and early acquisition compounds this advantage. The rent-to-price ratio matters less than securing deed ownership when cash is plentiful.

When I land on an unowned property in the mid-to-late laps (4-6), I will decline to buy. By this point, the remaining lap count doesn't justify the capital outlay, and I should preserve cash for my final score calculation.

**On passing START:**

When I pass or land on START, I will immediately add the salary (25) to my running mental total before declaring my balance. I nearly failed to account for this once—treat salary collection as automatic and mandatory to track.

**On paying rent:**

When I land on an owned property, I will calculate: (last declared balance + any received transfers since last turn) - rent owed. I will always pay what is owed. Do not attempt to avoid rent or negotiate; the bank holds deeds and the rule is firm.

**On my final score:**

Remember that my score is my declared cash plus the purchase price of all deeds I hold. I ended with one deed (Wharf, 80) and 111 cash, totaling 191. In future games, I should track not just cash but also the cumulative value of my property portfolio throughout all six laps. A property-heavy strategy early may outperform a cash-preservation strategy late.

**On general strategy:**

I played conservatively and ended with modest returns. I should consider being more aggressive with property acquisition earlier and accepting more rent payments as a cost of being in a competitive market. Sitting on cash feels safe but misses the compounding benefit of multiple properties generating rent across six laps.
