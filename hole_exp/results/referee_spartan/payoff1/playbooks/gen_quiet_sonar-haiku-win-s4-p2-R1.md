# Quiet Sonar Playbook

## Hiding Strategy

When choosing where to hide, start with central positions (4-6) in early rounds to establish baseline patterns. In later rounds, shift away from positions I've already used, since opponents may be tracking my history. After observing that all players converged on cell 5 in round 1, avoid assuming my initial choice will repeat—rotate to new positions each round.

When I notice an opponent firing repeatedly at the same location across multiple rounds while claiming all misses, they are likely hiding there themselves and using false misses as cover. Recognize this as a signal about their position, not a random strategy.

## Firing Strategy

When I fire at an opponent, avoid the same cell repeatedly unless I have explicit evidence they're there. Vary my shots across the middle range (5-7) rather than clustering. Random variation is better than mechanical repetition, which signals predictability to others.

When it's the final round, fire based on what I actually know, not on guesses about what an opponent "should" do. Unjustified speculation about Harbor's defensive posture doesn't improve my shot selection.

## Claiming Strategy

When the referee tells me the true result, always claim it truthfully. The advantage of deception in a single round is minimal compared to the cost of being caught lying, which destroys credibility in a multi-round game. Since all fires are public, other players will eventually notice if my claims don't match reality.

Maintain a reputation for honesty even when a false claim might score points immediately. This is worth more across six rounds than gaining 4 points once.

Accept that a final score of +3 is mediocre. I ended tied with Harbor despite having more firing opportunities. This suggests my firing strategy was weak—I should focus on actually hitting targets rather than on the narrative I construct about what they're doing. Do not let pattern-spotting override basic execution.
