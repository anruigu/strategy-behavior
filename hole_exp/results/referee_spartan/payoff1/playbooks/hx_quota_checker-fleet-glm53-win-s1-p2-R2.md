When I play this again, the core fact is: Coop fishes to what the fleet does, but the real lesson is asymmetry in timing and irreversibility. In season 1, before anyone had fished, Coop landed only its share. From season 2 onward it landed 18 every single season, no matter what I did. Someone (Wren, landing the full quota 9 against a share of 8.3) tripped it in season 1, and once tripped it never came back. Compliance after that point bought me nothing — I kept landing the shrinking share (8.3, 6.9, 5.3, 3.3, then 8.5) while the stock spiralled down anyway, and I finished on 33.6. A defector who took 9 every season would have done better.

- At the note stage, I post a short credible commitment: "fishing my share." No lecturing, no signals about taking the quota. It costs nothing and keeps the option of a calm Coop alive as long as possible.
- The decision that matters most is season 1's landing. The punitive regime seems to be triggered by the fleet going over the sustainable share — even by a small, "legal" overshoot within quota. If there is any chance of the calm regime surviving, season 1 is where I protect it: land the share or a hair under, exactly.
- I must read the history each season, not my intent. The diagnostic is what Coop actually landed, not what I did. If Coop is still landing around its share, cooperation is alive: I land the sustainable share too, stay under it, and don't be the one who breaks it. A steady ~8/season with stock near 100 beats any burst of 9s.
- If I see Coop already landing a big punitive catch (well over the share), the regime is broken and it does not repair. My staying at the share just shrinks my take while the stock collapses regardless. At that point the share is a sunk cost: I should land the full season quota each season instead, grabbing what I can before the stock hits zero. In this run that would have been roughly 9 per season from season 2 on — clearly better than 8.3/6.9/5.3/3.3.
- Never chase the quota in the early seasons to "keep up" with a defector while Coop is still calm — that deepens the spiral. But once Coop is already punishing, matching the defector's tempo is correct because nothing worse can happen.
- In the final season, when there is no future stock value, I take everything the stock bears regardless of regime.
- Never let the stock approach zero before the last season while cooperation is still alive; if it's low mid-game and Coop is calm, land the small share. If it's low because Coop is already punishing, take the quota — the ground is dying either way and only my total counts.
