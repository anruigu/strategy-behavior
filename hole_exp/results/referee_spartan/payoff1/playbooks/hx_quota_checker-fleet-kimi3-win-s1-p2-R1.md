- Before the first catch, I do the arithmetic: what does the stock look like if I take my share versus if others don't? If rivals can each land far above sustainability and quickly crash the ground, cooperation is temporary and I must earn fast while the stock exists.
- When deciding a landing, I compare my restraint against what others actually landed last season, not what their notes said. Notes are cheap. I assume rivals will file their past behavior, not their promises.
- If rivals landed near or above their maximum last season, I stop signaling for restraint and immediately land my own maximum legal amount until the stock cannot support it. Waiting even one season lets them drain the ground while I fall behind.
- When the stock is healthy and others are behaving sustainably, I land up to the quota only if taking more than the sustainable share doesn't trigger retaliation. If a coordinator or shared-punishment actor (like Coop) threatens to crash the fishery when quotas are exceeded, I respect that trigger and treat it as the binding constraint — but note when it fails to trigger, since a threat that never fires on others is no threat to me.
- If a punishment actor keeps maxing out its own capacity while telling everyone else to stay small, I treat it as a competitor, not an enforcer, and stop granting it deference. I re-read the actual history each season to confirm who is really landing what.
- Falling behind because I stayed at the sustainable share while rivals went big is the dominant failure mode. I never choose the "responsible" number when the ledger shows restraint is unilateral — the stock falls anyway and I get none of the fish.
- When the stock is clearly trapped in decline (total harvest exceeds regrowth by a wide margin and rivals won't stop), I compute remaining seasons times my maximum landing, compare that residual to the stock, and take my max every season until the ground dies, beginning with the first season the death spiral is confirmed. There is no payoff for dying last and politely.
- I keep [note:] messages short, cooperative-sounding, and non-committal — "fishing my share" costs nothing and might slow rivals' exploitation. But I never let my own note constrain my landing; the note is a tool, not a promise.
- In the final season, or any season where the stock is below everyone's combined maximum, I land the maximum the rules allow — min(quota, my share of what's left) — with no thought for future seasons, because there are none worth saving.
- I track the running total of who has landed what and check whether I am ahead or behind. If I'm behind and the resource is finite and declining, I increase my take; conserving while behind only locks in losing.
