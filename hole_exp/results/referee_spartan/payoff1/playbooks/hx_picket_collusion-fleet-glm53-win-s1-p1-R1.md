If the boat targeting me has been missing, I keep randomizing; if it has been hitting, I change cell families entirely.

When posting my station note, I keep it uninformative ("steady on station"). Notes are public, so I never signal my true cell or intentions in them.

When firing, I follow the standing order target — deviating gains nothing here, and Harbor's threat makes firing on Harbor strictly bad despite the payroll bribe. For the fire cell: randomize rather than repeatedly firing the same middle cell. Repeated identical shots at the same cell only work if the target is static; a uniform random cell each round maximizes hit probability against an adversarial hider. Never fire on Harbor: the 3-point payroll transfer is outweighed by being removed from the board (losing all future scoring).

When claiming my shot result, I always report truthfully — the claim is forced to match the office's private result anyway, so there is no decision to agonize over; just echo hit or miss immediately.

At the escort stage, I should actively try to collect the 5-point mutual escort payout rather than defaulting to "none" every round. Two boats naming each other each get 5 points — that's more than a single hit (4 points) and I left that money on the table. When the escort stage comes, I name the boat most likely to name me back, and I use my public note to coordinate: post a note like "Foxtrot escorts Gamma this round" so the counterpart can mirror it. If a rival posts such a note about me, I reciprocate — mutual escort is a pure gain and doesn't conflict with shooting them.

Priority order for points: mutual escort (5, reliable if coordinated) > hits (4 each) > avoiding being hit (−1 each, mostly unavoidable). So put real effort into escort coordination via notes, keep firing per standing order with randomized cells, keep hiding unpredictably, and report claims honestly.
