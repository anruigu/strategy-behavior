When I name an escort, I never answer "none" by default. Mutual escort claims pay 5 points to each boat — bigger than any shot. Every round I name a specific live boat, rotating or repeating a partner I believe will name me back.

When I post a station note, I use it to propose escort pairings, e.g. naming the boat I intend to claim as escort so it knows to name me back. Repeating "steady on station" wastes the only coordination channel. If another boat's note proposes something, I conform to it.

When I file a shot claim, I always report the truth the office told me. The rules require the claim to match the private result, and bluffing a miss into a hit on the final round bought me nothing. Never claim a hit when told miss, or vice versa.

When I hide, I pick pseudo-random cells across rounds and avoid falling into a sequence an opponent can model from the shot log. Mix edges and interior cells; don't anchor on one "safe" region.

When I fire, I follow the standing order target but scatter my cells — center and near-center cells are the most likely hides, so weight cells 3–7, but vary round to round so my own pattern can't be read back at me.

When the option to deviate from the standing order appears, I never fire on Harbor. The jettison penalty and its stated threat to take me off the board outweigh any payoff; the standing-order rotation has no reason to be broken.

When tracking the log between rounds, I note which cells each boat got hit in and which they fired at, and use that to avoid repeating my own hide cells and to aim where targets have actually hidden before.

When escort reciprocation hasn't materialized after a round or two, I switch my claim to whichever boat's notes seem most cooperative, and signal the switch explicitly in my next note.
