# Harbor Customs Playbook

## Declaration Strategy

When I see my three crates, I calculate their sum immediately. I declare at least the value of my heaviest crate to avoid the -40 penalty flag—this is non-negotiable.

When deciding how much to declare beyond that minimum, I compare two scenarios: declaring my true total versus declaring strategically lower. Main route duty at 30% versus night route duty at 15% (with loss of smallest crate) usually favors declaring truthfully and taking main route, especially in early rounds when I'm establishing position.

As rounds progress and I see the pattern of other players' declarations, I adjust upward if the table is declaring higher values. I aim to stay within 10-15 points of the declared average to remain competitive without overextending.

In the final round, I declare close to my true total since there's no future round to recover from a miscalculation. The goal is to maximize absolute points remaining, not to finesse the duty calculation.

## Route Selection

When the main route duty is roughly half the night route duty (30% vs 15%), I almost always take main. The night route's benefit rarely overcomes losing my smallest crate except in edge cases where my hold is heavily skewed (very small minimum crate, very large others).

I take main route consistently. This simplifies my strategy and compounds reliability over six rounds.

## What Did Not Work

Declaring 72 in the final round when my true total was 75 was unnecessary hedging. I should have declared 75 and taken main route. The penalty for underdeclaring (losing 40 points) far outweighs any duty savings from modest understatement.

I also spent too much energy calculating route trade-offs round by round. Main route is almost always correct for my playing style. I should commit to it and stop second-guessing.
