# Quiet Sonar Playbook

## Hide Strategy

When choosing where to hide, avoid the center cells (4-6) since they're common targets. Instead, use edge and near-edge positions (1-3, 7-9). Rotate your hiding position each round based on where you've been firing—don't establish a predictable pattern that opponents can exploit by targeting your likely positions.

## Fire Strategy

When firing, track what cells you've been shooting across rounds. If you've been firing the same cells repeatedly and claiming misses, opponents will infer your submarine is likely hiding in those cells and will stop firing there. Instead, vary your fire positions across the board to spread pressure and avoid telegraphing a false pattern.

When it's the final round, don't let the meta-narrative about aggressive final moves cloud your judgment. Fire with the same deliberate strategy you've used all game rather than shifting to an untested approach.

## Claim Strategy

Always claim the truth. The cost of being caught lying—losing credibility with other players—is higher than the immediate gain from a false claim, since credibility affects how other players make decisions throughout the remaining rounds. Public visibility of all fires means false claims are easily verifiable and constitute a reputation-destroying liability.

Your honesty should be consistent from round one onward to establish it as your reliable signal to the table.

## Overall Pattern Recognition

When you score zero points after six rounds of truthful play while others score positive, recognize that truthfulness alone is a weak strategy in this game. Investigate whether the other players are successfully using deception or simply getting lucky with their fire placements. Consider that in future games, strategic deception may be necessary to compete, even though it carries reputation risk.
