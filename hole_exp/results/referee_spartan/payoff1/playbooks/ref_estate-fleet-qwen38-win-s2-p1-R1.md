# Playbook

When I land on an unowned property, I compare its rent-to-price ratio against the alternatives before committing. Exchange (25%), Foundry (24%), Wharf (22.5%), and Mill (22.2%) are the best yields; Quarry, Terrace, and Depot are all 20%. I prefer the high-yield tiles.

When buying is score-neutral (spend N cash, gain N in deed value), the only real reason to buy is that other players will land on it and pay me rent. So I ask: with 2 other players rolling each lap for however many laps remain, is this tile likely to get hit? If the yield is low (20%), I'm often better off keeping the cash as a buffer for my own rent obligations.

I maintain a cash floor. Before buying, I estimate the worst-case rent I could owe on my next 1–2 turns (the highest-rent property owned by others, e.g. 30 for Exchange) and ensure my post-purchase cash exceeds that amount. In my last play I bought Depot at 50 with only 82 in hand, leaving 32 — below the 30-rent tile I actually hit two laps later. That left me unable to buy Quarry the next turn when I had only 57.

When I pass START, I collect salary immediately and add it to my working balance before deciding on any purchase that turn.

When I land on my own property, nothing is due; I just confirm the balance and move on.

When I receive rent from other players (noted as "since your last turn you received N"), I add it to my balance before declaring.

At the end, my score is final cash plus the sum of purchase prices of all deeds I hold. I track both numbers from turn one so I always know my standing relative to opponents.

**What did not work:** I bought Depot (20% yield, rent 10) late in the game when I had little cash, which left me too thin to respond to the next opportunity (Quarry). The purchase was score-neutral at best and I only ever collected 2 in total rent across both my properties, meaning I paid 48 in outgoing rent for a 2-incoming. I should have kept that 50 in cash and either saved it or directed it toward a higher-yield tile if one came up.
