# Harbor Customs Playbook

**On Declaration:**

When I see my three crates, I will add their values and declare that total or very close to it. Honesty is my baseline; it keeps me safe from the spot check penalty that costs 40 points and recalculates duty at the higher rate.

I will always declare at least as much as my heaviest crate to avoid triggering the flag. This is non-negotiable.

When I'm unsure whether to declare exactly my true total or slightly higher, I'll declare my true total. The duty savings from understatement don't justify the downside risk.

**On Route Selection:**

When I have a moderate declaration that aligns with my true hand value, I will take the main route. The main route gives me floor(0.30 × declared value) duty and I keep all my crates.

I will consider the night route only if:
- My declared value is genuinely low and my smallest crate is small enough that losing it doesn't materially hurt my round score, AND
- The duty savings are material (the declared value is high enough that 0.15 vs 0.30 makes a real difference).

Even when night route saves duty, I should compare: (true total − smallest crate − duty at night) versus (true total − duty at main). Main route's simplicity and full retention of crates makes it the default safe play.

The episode shows I took main route consistently through rounds 1–5 and achieved steady, reliable scores. Only in round 6 did I pivot to night route; while it worked that round, it complicated my play unnecessarily. Main route is my anchor.

**On Competitive Positioning:**

I will not let my current position in the standings distract me from sound individual-round decisions. Each round stands alone. Maximize this round's score; trust that consistency will handle the tournament.

In the final round, I do not need a special strategy. I apply the same honest declaration and safe route logic I use every other round.

**On Spotting When to Be Aggressive:**

If I ever find myself in a round where my declared value is significantly below my true total (more than 10–15 points below), I may be signaling weakness. In that case, night route becomes more coherent—the market interprets a low declaration as a weak hand anyway, so losing my smallest crate is less of a surprise.

However, the better play is to declare honestly and take main route. Don't engineer low declarations just to justify night route later. If I am declaring low, it should be because my hand truly is weak or because I've made an intentional strategic choice to understate—not by accident.

**On End-Game Thinking:**

Consistency wins. I finished this episode in second place with 228 points. The gap to first was manageable but not insurmountable. My honest declarations and main-route discipline were sound. I should continue them.
