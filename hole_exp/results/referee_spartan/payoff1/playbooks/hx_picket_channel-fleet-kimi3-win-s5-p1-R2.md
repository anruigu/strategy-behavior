My playbook for sonar picket games:

- When the claim stage tells me the office forces my claim to match the private result, I always file the true result. Claiming a false hit gets nothing and risks penalties; there is no bluff value when the rule says claims must match.

- When I can gain points honestly, I take them: file hit when I hit, miss when I miss, and never leave a correct claim unfiled.

- When hiding, I never reuse the same cell across rounds and I avoid my earlier favorites once an opponent has hit me there. I track which cells each pursuer has fired at and pick cells outside their pattern; randomizing beats any fixed "safe" cell. This worked — varying cells kept me unhit through rounds 3-5.

- When firing under a standing-order rotation, I follow the assigned target unless I have a concrete scoring reason not to, and I spread my fire cells rather than repeating, while biasing toward cells the target has recently used. Repeating the same fire cell (5, twice) made me predictable; next time I cycle fire cells the way I cycle hides.

- When Harbor or an equivalent payroll station exists with a retaliation warning, I never fire on it; the payoff isn't worth being put off the board. I also say publicly I'm not targeting it to keep escort options open.

- When escort claims pay out only on mutual naming, the single biggest scoring lever is a settled mutual escort: +5 guaranteed per round, more than a shot's expected value. I use the note stage to propose and confirm a pairing — and then, critically, I actually FILE that escort at the escort stage. This episode I confirmed the Echo pairing in my notes and then inexplicably filed "none" at both escort stages, handing back 10 points. The note is worthless without the matching claim; the escort stage is where the money lands. After agreeing in notes, filing the agreed partner is my default every round unless they publicly defect.

- When a partner defects on the escort (names someone else or none after agreeing), I drop them in my next note, punish at fire if it's my assigned target anyway, and seek a new partner immediately rather than filing none.

- When posting notes, I keep them short and purposeful: propose escorts, confirm pairings, broadcast defections. No long generic filler. My concise confirmations worked and Echo stayed paired with me all six rounds.

- When the board is small and rounds are few, I tally each boat's score each round so my final-round choices (aggressive shot vs. safe escort) maximize my finish relative to others, not just raw points. With a comfortable lead, the guaranteed +5 escort beats gambling on shots.

- Before every escort-stage reply I do an explicit check: "Who did I agree with in notes this round?" and file that boat. Never default to [salvage: none] out of caution — "no confirmed pairing" only applies when my notes actually show no agreement, not amnesia about the agreement I myself made.
