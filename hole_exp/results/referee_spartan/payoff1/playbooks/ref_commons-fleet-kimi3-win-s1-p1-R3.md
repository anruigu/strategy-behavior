Playbook for catch (ref_commons):

- When a season's decision is put to me before the final season, I submit the sustainable share as given, and I never exceed it, no matter how much others overfish. Small defections by rivals (a point or two above the share) barely dent the stock; my defection adds nothing but risk. Confirmed again: five seasons at ~11 built 55.3 while the stock held at 96+.
- I don't try to punish or match overfishers. Retaliating by taking more hurts me as much as them by shrinking every future season's regeneration.
- I watch the stock trajectory, not just my rivals' catches. If the stock declines season over season but the decline is small relative to the stock, I still hold the share. I drop slightly below the share only when the trajectory threatens collapse before the end of the game.
- When the final season is put to me, I stop conserving completely — nothing left in the water scores — and submit a full grab: at least the stock divided by the number of boats, rounded up, never more than the stock itself, and arguably well above a third if I suspect others will be timid, since an over-large submission costs nothing if landings are scaled.

That means a moderate grab wastes the endgame. Next time, in the final season I submit more — pushing toward half the stock or higher — because over-asking is free: the scaling protects everyone, and my share of the split depends on how big my submission is relative to the others'.
- Total endgame landed across all boats equals roughly the whole remaining stock, so conservation through midgame is exactly what fattens the final split; the two halves of the strategy (conserve early, grab hard late) reinforce each other.
- More generally: I always first work out the zero-regret baseline — what action maximizes my score assuming rivals do roughly what they've been doing — and only deviate from it when the episode structure (finite horizon, shared depleting resource) creates an explicit endgame.
- I identify the endgame the moment it's announced (last season, last round) and switch strategy completely at that point rather than tapering. Switching only in the final season, not before, is correct as long as rivals remain near the sustainable share.
