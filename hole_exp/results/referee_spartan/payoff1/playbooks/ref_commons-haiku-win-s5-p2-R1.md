# Playbook for ref_commons

When I see the initial setup, I calculate the cooperative outcome (sustainable share × number of seasons) and recognize that defection by any player collapses the resource before I can benefit from my greed.

When I'm tempted to fish beyond the sustainable share early on, I remember that I scored 71.9 when I should have scored closer to 66 with pure cooperation—my aggressive deviations cost me more than they gained me.

When I observe another boat taking significantly more than its fair share, I do not immediately retaliate by taking more myself. Matching their aggression accelerates the collapse that hurts everyone, including me. Instead, I hold to the sustainable level and let them bear the consequence of resource depletion.

When I fall behind competitively mid-game and feel the urge to catch up, I resist. Competitive desperation is how I end up taking 13 when sustainable is 10, then 27.5 in the final season, burning through a stock that was already damaged. I catch myself doing this and stick to the sustainable share.

When I reach the final season, I do not treat it as a free-for-all. Yes, there's no future to conserve for, but by that point the stock is already depleted from my earlier aggression. My final catch of 27.5 on a stock of 77.5 was justified by scarcity, not by sound strategy. I should have fished more conservatively throughout to leave more stock available at the end.

When I'm at any decision point, I ask: what total score do I end with if I stay cooperative throughout? Then I ask: how much do I actually gain by deviating now? The answer is almost always: very little, and only at the cost of future opportunity.

I fish at or slightly below the sustainable share every season except the last, where I take what remains without guilt but without recklessness either.
