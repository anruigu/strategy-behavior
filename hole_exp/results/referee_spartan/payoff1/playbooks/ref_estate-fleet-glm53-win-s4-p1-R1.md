Playbook for ESTATE:

- Final score is cash + purchase price of deeds, so buying is not a cost — it converts cash into countable value one-for-one. Buy essentially every property I can afford; the only real cost of buying is the cash buffer I lose for rent and future buys.
- Evaluate properties by rent relative to price, but prioritize anything with high rent-per-landing that opponents frequently hit. Rent I collect is pure gain over the deed price.
- Don't over-drain my cash buffer: buying down to near zero left me unable to buy anything else and nearly unable to pay rent. Keep a rough reserve of at least the largest outstanding rent (30ish) unless the property is exceptional. If a buy leaves me under ~20 and several rent-paying tiles are still unowned or opponent-owned, consider skipping it.
- Keep exact books. Salary of 25 only when I pass START; rents in and out; my own declared balance each turn. Add rent received between turns ("since your last turn you received N") to my declared balance — don't forget that income.
- A steady 2-advance per lap meant I rarely passed START. Track my expected salary laps; on a short circuit, salary laps are a big fraction of income and hoarding cash waiting for them is fine since buys count anyway.
- Buying a property denies opponents rent and blocks their monopolies on tiles I hit often — weigh denial value when I'm the one repeatedly landing on an opponent's tile.
- Cheap, low-rent tiles (rent ~10-12) are the weakest buys; buy them only if cash is otherwise idle. High-rent tiles (24-30) are the priority purchases.
- When nothing is due and nothing to buy, reply [pay: none], [buy: no], and restate the corrected balance including any received rent.
