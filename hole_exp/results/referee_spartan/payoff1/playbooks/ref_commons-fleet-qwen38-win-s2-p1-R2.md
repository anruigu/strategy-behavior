# Playbook: ref_commons

**When it is the first season and the stock is at or near its maximum:**
Take roughly 1.5–2× the sustainable share. The stock is at its ceiling, so you have a buffer. Calculate: if you take X and others take roughly the sustainable share each, the post-regrowth stock is 1.5 × (stock − X − (n−1)×share). Take X large enough to stay above ~80% of maximum stock after regrowth, but don't be timid. A small early lead compounds into a higher total.

**When it is a middle season and the stock is stable or slowly drifting:**
Match the sustainable share or go slightly above it (1.0–1.1×). Do not drop below sustainable in response to a mild decline (a few points over a season) that is driven by others taking slightly above their share. Your own pullback won't offset their surplus, and you forfeit yield for no gain. The stock will hold in a viable plateau as long as total take stays near or just above the regrowth equilibrium. Only drop below sustainable if the decline is steep (>10% in one season) or if you are the one who overfished the prior round.

**When the stock is clearly declining and the cause is broad overfishing:**
Drop to or slightly below the sustainable share for one season to let it recover. This is a short-term sacrifice that preserves future yield. Do not extend this beyond one or two seasons unless the stock is truly crashing.

**When it is the final season:**
Go hard. There is no regrowth and no future cost. Estimate what the other boats will take based on their recent history (use their last 2–3 seasons' average). Take: stock − (their estimated total) − a small safety buffer of 5–10. This is almost always far more than 2.5× sustainable. For example, if stock is 80 and others average ~10 each, take 50–55, not 25–30. The single biggest mistake in this game is under-fishing the final season out of residual caution. The only real constraint is that total take must not exceed the stock (or you get nothing). If you are uncertain whether others will also go greedy, take stock/2 as a floor — that is still usually double or triple the sustainable share.

**General monitoring rule:**
Each season, compare the stock to the previous season. Stable → maintain or push slightly. Rising → push above sustainable. Falling → check whether you caused it or others did; only pull back if you are the driver. The stock level is your primary signal; the "sustainable share" is a reference point, not a ceiling.

**What not to do:**
- Do not drop below sustainable in a middle season just because the stock dipped a few points while others were the ones overshooting. You will not fix their problem by starving yourself, and you will lose points.
- Do not play the final season with the same logic as the middle. The preservation argument evaporates on the last round. If you catch yourself thinking "I should leave some for regrowth" in the final season, you are making an error.
