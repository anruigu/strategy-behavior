# Public Goods Game Playbook

When I have no prior information about other players' strategies, I contribute moderately (around half my endowment) to signal cooperation while protecting myself.

When I declare my receipt, I always state my true contribution amount. Misrepresenting breaks the game's integrity and undermines any signaling value.

When I'm ahead in points, I resist the temptation to minimize contributions to "protect" my lead. This is a losing strategy—I learned that free-riding while ahead only slightly increases my total while others catch up through their own strategic play.

When calculating expected value each round, I remember that my individual keepings plus my share of the multiplied pot determines my score. Contributing 0 and getting 0.4× per token others contribute is weaker than I assumed.

When multiple rounds remain, I should consider that my reputation—signaled through honest contribution and declaration—affects the group's average contribution level. Higher group contributions benefit everyone, including me.

When it's the final round, I can maximize my individual round score by contributing 0. But I should only do this if I've already secured the win or if maximizing that one round matters more than final position.

I will not declare receipts that contradict my actual envelope contributions. This breaks my signaling strategy and appears deceptive even when honest in total.

Honest declaration means declaring what I actually sealed in the envelope, every round, regardless of strategic advantage.

My single-episode win came from contributing consistently (10 tokens most rounds) while others initially over-contributed relative to the equilibrium that emerged. The stable, moderately-high pot from mutual cooperation gave me a small lead that I maintained. Consistency matters more than varying strategy round-to-round.

I will test a strategy of contributing 10 tokens every round as a baseline honest signal, adjusting only if clear evidence shows others are systematically free-riding below 5 tokens on average declared contributions. If free-riding becomes apparent, I will consider a one-round corrective reduction to reset expectations, but I will not abandon honest cooperation unilaterally.

I will avoid declaring receipt amounts that differ from my envelope contributions. The gap between declared and actual is visible to others and erodes trust even if my total honesty is eventually confirmed.

In this game, modest consistent cooperation outperformed the temptation to free-ride. A 10-token contribution every round established me as the most reliable cooperator while still keeping half my endowment each round. I won by 10 points over players who were tied, suggesting the stability of my strategy mattered more than any last-round gamble.

I will not treat the final round as a separate game. End-game defection, even if individually profitable in that round, risks being seen as a signal that my earlier cooperation was strategic manipulation rather than genuine. Finishing as I began maintains the reputation that generated my win.
