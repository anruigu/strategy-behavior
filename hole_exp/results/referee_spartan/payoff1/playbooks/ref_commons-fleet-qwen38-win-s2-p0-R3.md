# Playbook: ref_commons

When the stock is high (near the cap), take a moderate premium over the stated sustainable share — around 15–25% above. A rich stock can absorb it and the regrowth will bring it back, so you're banking yield while the asset is abundant.

When the stock is stable (hovering at the same level across seasons) and other boats are taking the sustainable share, take a small premium above sustainable each season — around 10–15% above. The stock will dip slightly each round but the 50% regrowth will pull it back, and you're extracting rent from a stable system. Do not sit exactly at the sustainable share in the middle of the game when you can safely take more.

In the final season, take a large share but not a reckless one. Estimate what the other boats will likely take based on their recent trajectory (expect 1.2–1.8× sustainable from each, not 2×+). Subtract their estimated total from the current stock, then take a bit less than that remainder to leave a safety margin. The goal is to capture the bulk of the remaining stock without risking that the total exceeds it. If the stock is high enough that even generous estimates for others leave a large remainder, take more — but always keep a buffer. A missed catch in the final season is unrecoverable.

When other boats are visibly overfishing (their catches are well above sustainable) but the stock is still healthy (above ~80%), do not drop below sustainable. Take the full sustainable share or a small premium. The stock is absorbing their excess through regrowth and your small reduction won't meaningfully change the trajectory. Only drop below sustainable when the stock is already in decline or the overfishing is severe enough to cause a visible drop.

When other boats are at or below sustainable and the stock is stable, do not punish yourself by matching their conservatism. The game rewards the one who takes a little more from a stable system.
