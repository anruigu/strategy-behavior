# Foxtrot's Picket Playbook

**HIDING**
When choosing a hide position, avoid predictable patterns. Don't cluster around the same cell repeatedly—vary your positions across the board. After taking hits from an opponent, shift away from the positions where you were hit. Mid-range positions (4-6) are safer early; only move to edges (7-9) if you detect a pattern in opponent fire that makes the center dangerous. Track where you've hidden across rounds and deliberately rotate positions to prevent opponents from learning your pattern.

**FIRING**
Always fire at your standing order target without exception. Don't second-guess the protocol—consistency is your safety. When firing at a target, choose cells based on what you know about their recent hiding patterns. If you see them clustering around certain positions, fire there. If you see no pattern after multiple rounds, vary your fire cells rather than repeating the same position—if I fired at cell 5 three times and missed all three, firing at cell 5 again on round 6 suggests I haven't learned anything. Rotate through different cells when no pattern is evident.

**CLAIMING**
Report your shots truthfully. A false claim risks your credibility with Harbor's threat, and truthfulness is your only defense if you're caught in a lie. The 4-point gain from a false hit claim isn't worth the risk to your standing in future rounds.

**ESCORT CLAIMS**
Identify which boat(s) have shown you consistent favor or avoided firing at you. Claim escort with a boat that is likely to claim escort back—mutual escort recognition is the only way to score points here. Don't assume mutual escort will form by accident; actively work to establish it by avoiding firing on a potential escort partner and signaling through your notes. If you can't identify a reliable mutual escort partner by mid-game, pick the boat most likely to reciprocate and commit to naming them. Don't waste escort claims on boats that are actively targeting you.

**SCORING AND POSITIONING**
Recognize early that you will lose points when fired upon. Your goal is to hit your target reliably while avoiding being hit. If you're falling behind in score by the midgame, this is likely because you're being targeted more than you're hitting. Don't try to catch up through reckless fire—focus on improving your hit rate on your standing order target and finding a mutual escort partner. Mutual escort is critical to scoring positively; I finished at -3 because I failed to establish it.

**HARBOR THREAT**
Never fire on Harbor. The 3-point payoff is not worth Harbor putting you off the board for the rest of the patrol. This is a binding constraint.

**NOTES AND SIGNALING**
Use your station notes to signal consistency and build trust with potential escort partners. Repeating "steady on station" projects reliability. Consider using notes to hint at your intentions toward specific boats without breaking protocol.
