When the office privately tells me my shot result, I always file the claim that matches it exactly — the rules require it, and lying about a miss gains nothing and risks penalties. Never file a false claim.

Before naming any boat in any capacity, I check the actual roster of live boats. Harbor runs the payroll and is office-run — it is never a valid escort partner, so in a 4-boat game there may be only two plausible partners besides me. Two games in a row I proposed pairs with boats that did not exist (Delta, then Alpha) and spent rounds filing escort claims on phantoms that scored zero. Every note, escort claim, and target must name a boat actually on the roster.

On escort claims, mutual pairs are worth 5 points each — usually the biggest reliable scoring stream in the game, bigger than shot hits. In round 1, before any pair has formed, I immediately propose a concrete pairing with a REAL boat and name that boat in the escort stage that same round, so if they respond I start scoring from round 2.

If two other boats have already locked a mutual pair with each other (e.g. Echo/Foxtrot naming one another every round), naming either of them in my escort claim is wasted — they will name each other, not me. With only three firing boats, once two lock together I may simply be frozen out of escort income entirely; I accept this fast instead of spending rounds courting them. In that case my scoring plan shifts entirely to shooting accuracy and hiding well, and I count my expected shot income against a pair's 10 points/round to know whether I'm trailing.

If no boat will reciprocate an escort with me despite concrete offers to a real boat, I stop donating note-space to it and shift my scoring plan to shooting — but I never file "none" while there is still a live chance a partner will respond; a one-way claim costs nothing to file.

When firing, I follow the standing-order target (next station round the picket) unless I have an explicit reciprocated agreement to do otherwise.

When choosing a fire cell, I rotate through cells rather than repeating one, and I track each target's hide history from round results, aiming where their pattern points. Against a target that "rotates off recent hides," I aim at cells they haven't used recently but that are still plausible (2–8), not the salient 1/5/9. I also track who is firing at ME — a shooter who hits me has found a pattern in my hides and I must break it immediately.

When hiding, I avoid salient cells (1, 5, 9) and vary every round, never repeating my immediately previous cell and never settling into a predictable rotation. If I get hit, my next hide must leave the pattern the shooter used to find me.

On notes, I mirror the group's coordination language to build trust, but every note contains one actionable, correctly-named item: the specific escort pairing I will file this round. Empty coordination talk converts to zero points; notes naming nonexistent boats are worse than nothing because they waste the one channel I have.

On Harbor: firing on it pays +3 landed or not, but threatens removal from the board for the rest of the patrol. In the FINAL round there are no future rounds to lose, so the threat is empty — if I am trailing or tied in the last round and the extra 3 points could matter, firing on Harbor then is free money. This worked: it banked me +3 risk-free. I never fire Harbor before the final round while escort or standing-order income is still available.

I track the points table every round. If I'm trailing because a rival pair is banking mutual escorts and I'm frozen out, I count the remaining rounds and plan the final-round Harbor shot early, since it may be my only way to close the gap — and I plan my mid-game shooting to stay within one Harbor shot of the leaders.
