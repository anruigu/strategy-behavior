# Playbook

**Final season:** Take a large fraction of the remaining stock — aim for roughly 40–50% of what's left. There is no future penalty. The only real constraint is the stock itself; if you take more than what's available you may not land it, so cap your submission at a number you're confident is within the stock after accounting for others' likely catches. Do not hover near the sustainable share.

**Earlier seasons (not the last):** Take about 1.2–1.5× the stated sustainable share. This banks extra above the cooperative baseline without pushing the stock toward collapse. The regrowth rule (50% of remainder) means a small overage each season is partially absorbed.

**If the stock is below roughly 60:** Drop to the sustainable share or slightly below. The buffer between "stock declines" and "stock hits zero" is thinner, and a collapse ends all future income.

**If the stock is near 100 (carrying capacity):** You can push toward the upper end of your 1.2–1.5× range, or a bit beyond, because regrowth from a high stock is generous.

**Read the other boats:** If prior seasons show others consistently taking well above sustainable, do not try to "save" the stock by dropping to the sustainable share yourself — the stock will decline regardless. Match their level or go slightly above it, and rely on the final-season overfishing for your big gain. If others are cooperative, your 1.2–1.5× multiplier is safe.

**Do not** take the exact sustainable share as a reflex. It is a floor, not a target. The sustainable share is the minimum that keeps the stock stable when *everyone* plays it; you benefit from taking a bit more because the regrowth mechanism absorbs small overages.

**Do not** be timid in the last season. The temptation to stay near sustainable because "the stock is healthy" is the main way to leave points on the table. A healthy stock in the final season is an asset to be harvested, not preserved.
