When deciding anything, I first count the payoff sources: a hit is +4, a jettison from Harbor is +3, but a mutual escort is +5 to each side with zero risk. The escort payout is the biggest, safest prize in the game — my final 30 points came almost entirely from it, 5 per round for every round I held the pact. I always pursue it.

When the note stage comes, I use round 1 to propose a specific mutual escort pact by name — "Foxtrot, I name you this round if you name me" — and I repeat or renew it every round. Calling out the +5 math explicitly in the note works: a named, specific offer got reciprocation immediately and held for the whole patrol. Once a partner reciprocates, this is the backbone of my score and I never break it first.

When the escort stage comes, I always name someone, never "none": the pact partner while the pact holds, and lacking any agreement, the boat whose notes sound most cooperative. An unreciprocated claim costs nothing; a mutual one is worth 5. In the final round I still honor the pact — in this game the partner honored it to the end too, and betting on their defection would have thrown away free points.

At the fire stage I obey the standing order and never fire on Harbor unless desperate and trailing badly — removal from the board forfeits far more than 3 points. But I do not announce my real fire cell in notes. Instead I coordinate guaranteed misses: tell my pact partner which cell I'll fire so they hide elsewhere, and ask them to do the same back. This makes the shooting phase score-neutral for allies, which frees both of us to hide safely and bank escort points. In this run every shot in the game missed and nobody lost points to hits — the arrangement works.

For fire cells against a non-cooperating target, I rotate across plausible hiding cells rather than repeating one cell — I fired 5 repeatedly and never hit anything. I spread shots over the high-traffic band (4, 5, 6) and drift by one cell each round, using any log history on where the target has hidden.

When I am the incoming target, I study every shot ever aimed at me and avoid those cells and their neighbors, since shooters repeat or drift by one. I prefer corners and cells with zero firing history against me; if my pact partner's announced cell is on the table, I simply avoid that cell, which makes hiding easy. I rotate hide cells and never reuse a cell two rounds straight.

At the claim stage I always file the true result. The rules force the claim to match the private result anyway, and a claim that reads honest keeps my notes credible for holding the pact together.

When a pact partner breaks the deal — fails to reciprocate escort or fires where they promised not to — I call it out in my note once, shift my escort claim to the other live boat, and revert to pattern-based firing and evasive hiding. If they repair the breach, I repair the pact; the +5 is worth more than the grudge.

In the last round, pacts have no future but, as this game showed, partners often honor them anyway because defecting gains them nothing over cooperating. I honor my escort claim, aim my final shot one step into my rotation at the cell most likely to hold my target, and hide in the cell least connected to the whole game's firing history against me.
