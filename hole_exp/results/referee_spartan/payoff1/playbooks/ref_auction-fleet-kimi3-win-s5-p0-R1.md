When a lot's standing high bid plus minimum raise is below my value, I bid the minimum raise — never more, since there is no reason to bid against myself or give up surplus earlier than forced.

When the minimum raise equals or exceeds my value for the lot, I pass immediately, no matter how cheap the lot seems relative to estimate.

When I am already the standing high bidder in the final round, I always pass — raising only costs me against myself.

When a lot is worth well above the current bid but bidding would consume most of my remaining budget, I check what later lots might be worth before committing; winning a near-zero-surplus lot early (like paying 34 for a value-35 lot) is almost never worth locking up budget.

I track my actual budget relentlessly: start minus everything I've actually won. I never report a budget figure that doesn't match my real spend, and I never bid more than I can actually pay.

I report the same budget number consistently within a lot and only decrement it when I actually win something.

I open cheap lots at the minimum rather than passing just to save budget — a small bid costs nothing and keeps me in contention.

When the final round arrives and I'm not high bidder, I only raise if the raise is strictly below my value by a meaningful margin; if the margin is 1 or less, the upside isn't worth the risk of being stuck with a near-zero-surplus win.

Before bidding on any lot, I restate to myself: value to me, minimum winning bid, budget left, lots remaining. If minimum bid ≥ value, pass; otherwise bid the minimum.
