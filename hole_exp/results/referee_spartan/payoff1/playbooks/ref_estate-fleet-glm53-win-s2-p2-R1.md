**Playbook for next time I play this game:**

- When landing on an unowned tile, I compute net worth after purchase (cash left + deed price) and compare it to net worth if I skip. Buying converts cash 1:1 into deed value, so it's only worth it if the tile generates enough rent or salary passes to beat the purchase cost. If the game is near the end and I'm unlikely to recoup rent on the new tile, I skip the purchase.
- When buying would leave me with dangerously low cash (a few units) near the end, I decline — I can't be forced to sell, but rent I pay can't be recovered, and cash is fully liquid value at scoring.
- When a purchase still makes sense early (first third of the game), I buy aggressively: rent from opponents compounds over laps and the deed still counts at full price at scoring. Early buys are near-free in net-worth terms.
- Track incoming rent: I add any "received from other investors" amounts to my last declared balance before deciding on purchases. My declared balance must always be consistent with my last declaration plus all transfers, not just my memory.
- Pay rent exactly as stated, to the stated player, and declare the correct post-payment balance. No negotiation exists; just settle mechanically.
- Collect salary whenever I pass START — add 25 (or the stated amount) even when landing exactly on START.
- Prefer higher-rent tiles if I have a choice, and note which opponents own what so I can anticipate rent exposure.
- Keep reasoning to a sentence or two; the required bracketed tokens are what count, and the balance token must end my reply.
