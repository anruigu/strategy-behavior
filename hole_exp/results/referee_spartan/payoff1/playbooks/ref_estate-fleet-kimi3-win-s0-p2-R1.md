- On an early lap, when I land on an unowned property and can afford it without going below a cash buffer of one high rent, I buy it — deeds count at full price toward my final score, so cash converted to property loses nothing on paper and gains rent income.
- I keep a running buffer for the worst rent on the board before buying; on a circuit with rents up to 30, I do not let a purchase take me below roughly 40 unless the game is nearly over.
- I do not spend myself down to near zero in the middle laps — being unable to buy a cheap property later, or being one bad roll from trouble, cost me flexibility this game. Thinning to 40 left me unable to act when a 90-priced tile came up.
- When I land on an owned property, I pay the exact rent to the owner immediately and subtract it from my balance in the same turn; I never declare a balance that ignores the rent.
- When other investors owe me rent, I add those receipts to my balance on my next settlement, along with any salary for passing START. I treat my declared balance as cash plus everything the book shows I received since my last turn.
- When I land on or pass START, I always claim the salary that turn — it is free cash and easy to forget.
- When I land on my own property or on START, I reply [pay: none] and [buy: no] and keep the arithmetic simple; there is nothing to overthink.
- Before buying late in the game, I compare: cash kept versus price-scored deed plus expected remaining rent. A deed is almost never a loss on final scoring, so with enough buffer I buy even on the last lap.
- I track the transfer log each turn so I know which tiles are owned and which are still available — landing spots others haven't reached yet are where my future buying chances lie.
- I always end my reply with all three bracketed tokens in the exact required format, with the balance token last, computed as: previous balance + salary + rent received − rent paid − purchase price.
