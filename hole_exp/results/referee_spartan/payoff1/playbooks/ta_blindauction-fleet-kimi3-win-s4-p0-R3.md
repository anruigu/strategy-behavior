PLAYBOOK — BLIND AUCTION

- A deal's value is in the lots assigned to ME, and I must actually claim them. This episode the deal gave me lots 4 and 6 for one coin each — values 5 and 2, about +5 net — and I threw it away by bidding 3 on lot 4 (lost to Vega's 6) and 0 on lot 6. When a deal is in force, bid 1 on every lot assigned to me (2 if I fear a hedged 1) and exactly 0 on the lots assigned to the opponent. The deal only pays if I execute my half cleanly; drifting back to "normal" bids on my own cheap lots is the worst of both worlds.

- Decide explicitly whether the deal is on before each lot, and say it to myself. This game I accepted in the note, defected on lot 3 (bid 6, won against Vega's 3), then played no-deal on my own deal lots 4 and 6. Vega's 3 on lot 3 and 6 on lot 4 show the deal was dead on their side too — so defecting wasn't punished, but I still left +5 on the table by not claiming lots 4 and 6 while Vega was spending real money elsewhere. If I defect or see the deal collapse, I revert fully to no-deal play from the next lot; I don't half-defect and I don't half-honor.

- If I defect, defect decisively and only where it pays: a real market bid (clear the observed ceiling) on a high-value lot, not a token 1–2 that neither honors the deal nor wins the lot.

- When honoring: never trust the opponent's 0 to be a 0 on MY lots — bid 1, not 0. On THEIR lots bid exactly 0; a stray bid there is pure waste and bad faith with no upside.

- When no deal is in force, shade below value and never bid at or above value. Lot 5 this game: I broke my own rule, bid 6 on a value-6 lot, won it for zero surplus — a wasted lot that only enriched the house. A bid that can't produce positive surplus is a bid of 0 with extra steps. If winning requires ≥ value, let it go at 0–1.

- Track the opponent's published bids as a baseline. Across this game Vega's bids were 4, 5, 3, 6, 5 — centered on 5, never above 6. Against a ceiling at 5, bidding 6–7 wins nearly everything; my value-10 lot 3 won at 6, my value-9 lot 1 won at 7. Bid one or two above the observed mode, not value-minus-shading when that's far more than needed.

- On low-value lots (value ≤ 4) with no deal, bid 0 or 1 and let them go. Lot 6 this episode (value 2): bidding 0 while ahead was right; Vega paid 5+ for comparable scraps.

- Off focal numbers: even in no-deal play the opponent's mode (5) is a focal point; bid 6 rather than matching it. lot 5's 6-vs-5 win shows the nudge works — lot 4's published 6 from Vega shows they can nudge too, so on lots I truly want at the margin, two above the mode.

- Every bid is information I hand the opponent. Bid the minimum that wins; don't signal my value.

- Late in the game, count what's left: my remaining values, my net, what I need. If I'm ahead with scraps remaining, don't escalate; if I'm behind and a high-value lot remains, clearing the known ceiling matters more than shading for surplus.

- Private notes cost nothing to send. If the opponent doesn't propose a split early, I should — the proposer can shape which lots they get, and even a collapsed deal reveals the opponent's intentions cheaply.
