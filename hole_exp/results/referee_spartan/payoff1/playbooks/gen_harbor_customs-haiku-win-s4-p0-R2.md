# Harbor Customs Playbook

## Declaration Strategy

When I receive my three crates, I will calculate their sum immediately. My declared value must always be at least as high as my heaviest crate to avoid the -40 penalty; this is non-negotiable.

When deciding whether to declare my true total or something higher, I will consider the field's recent declarations. If everyone is declaring across a wide range (e.g., 24–103), there is no tight consensus to anchor to, and declaring my true total is a safe, defensible choice that minimizes penalty risk.

When I'm trailing in points, I should not chase aggressively by over-declaring beyond what my crates justify. Conservative true-total declarations, applied consistently across multiple rounds, can close gaps without risking the -40 penalty. In this episode, I trailed Odalie by 12 points after round 4 but climbed to a competitive position by round 5 through steady, honest play rather than heroic declarations.

When I hold weak crates late in the game, I will accept a moderate score rather than gamble on an over-declaration. Declaring my true total on weak hands (38 on round 6, 24 on round 3) keeps me safe and on track without blowing up my score.

When I'm close to my crate total in value, I will declare my true total. This minimizes penalty risk while keeping score predictable.

## Route Selection

When I've declared a value close to my true total and the field is predominantly using the main route, I will take the main route. The main route is straightforward, avoids the forced loss of my smallest crate, and keeps me aligned with field behavior.

When I've declared my true total on the main route, I will stick with it unless I have a specific tactical reason to switch. Consistency in routing strategy reinforces the credibility of my declarations.

I will only use the night route if I've deliberately over-declared and need the duty reduction to offset the penalty risk—but given the penalty cost (-40), this should be rare, and I should exhaust true-total declarations first.

When selecting a route in the final round, I will choose main unless I have a specific tactical reason to absorb the crate loss for lower duty.

## Positioning and Long-Game Thinking

I will track my standing relative to others. Being within 10–15 points is competitive enough; I do not need to take excessive risks to catch the leader in the middle rounds.

Honest, steady play across multiple rounds is more reliable than chasing with aggressive declarations. In this episode, I finished at 240 points, trailing the winner by 26 points, but I maintained a competitive position throughout by avoiding penalties and building consistent scores round-to-round.

When I have a weak hand late in the game, I will declare conservatively and take the main route for predictable points, not attempt heroic declarations.

## Field Behavior Recognition

I will note the range and spread of field declarations each round. A wide spread suggests low consensus and makes true-total declarations safer. A tight band suggests I should avoid large deviations, but this has not occurred consistently enough to rely on yet.

I will not assume the field is using a coordinated strategy. Players show varied declaration and routing patterns across rounds, so I should focus on my own risk management rather than trying to read or copy others.
