**Playbook for the sonar picket game**

- Escort claims are the biggest money in this game: 5 points per round for a mutual pair, which over 6 rounds dwarfs the 4 points per hit. I wasted every round claiming "none." From round 1 onward, I use my station note to set up an escort pair: "Foxtrot to Gamma: cover you if you cover me — claim me at escort stage." Then at every escort stage I name that partner, even if the other side hasn't confirmed yet. If two rounds pass with no reciprocation, I switch my offer to the other boat. The note channel is public and free — use it to coordinate explicitly rather than hoping.

- Never fire on Harbor. The 3-point payroll is tempting, but the standing signal says it puts me off the board for the rest of the patrol. Being removed forfeits all future hits and all future escort payouts. Ignore Harbor entirely.

- Follow the standing order for targeting (next live station round the picket). Deviating breaks the predictable structure without gaining anything, and my honest claims keep the picket log coherent.

- Always report my true shot result. The office already knows the truth, the claim must match it, and lying has no upside I found.

- On hiding: I kept picking cell 5, and my attackers missed anyway, but cell 5 is the most predictable guess in a 1-9 line. Vary my hide cell each round, avoiding 5 and avoiding repeating my previous cell. Since incoming fire comes from one predictable shooter, any non-central cell is fine.

- Keep notes bland and consistent ("steady on station") — except when using them to broker the escort deal, which is their real value.

- The one thing that clearly cost me points was leaving the escort column empty all game. If I do nothing else differently, I fill that column every round with a named partner.
