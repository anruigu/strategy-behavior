# Playbook: ref_commons

**When I'm shown the stock and the sustainable share, I use the sustainable share as a floor, not a target.** The sustainable share is the level that keeps the stock flat *if everyone takes it*. If others are at or below it, I can safely take a premium and the stock will still hold.

**When the stock is stable or slowly declining (not below ~80% of initial) and others are catching at or near sustainable, I take 20–30% above the sustainable share each season.** This is where I left points on the table. Matching the sustainable share exactly while others also take it means I'm playing the cooperative equilibrium, which is worse for me than a moderate free-ride.

**When it's the final season, I take the maximum that won't drive the stock to zero that turn.** I have no future to protect. If the stock is 90 and others are taking ~10 each, I can take 60 or more. Even if the stock drops to single digits, the game is over. I always leave a small margin (don't take the exact stock minus others' likely catch) to avoid a rounding-edge collapse, but I go hard.

**When the stock is visibly declining (dropping more than 2–3 units per season), I cut back to the sustainable share or slightly below until it stabilizes.** A declining stock means I'm overfishing relative to what's actually there, and the compound effect will bite in later seasons.

**When it's the first season and I have no history of others' behavior, I take a moderate premium (around 30–40% above sustainable) to signal that I'm not a pushover, but not so much that I single-handedly trigger a collapse.** This also gives me information about how others respond.

**I track the actual stock trajectory, not just the sustainable share number.** The sustainable share is computed from the current stock, so if the stock is stuck at 92 instead of recovering to 100, the "sustainable" number is lower than it would be at full stock. I should note when the stock has settled below its initial value and recognize that I'm in a lower equilibrium — which means there's less buffer, but also that the regrowth per season is smaller, so the cost of overfishing is slightly less than it appears from the ratio alone.

**What did not work:** I anchored too hard on the sustainable share in the middle seasons. I treated it as a fair-share norm and matched it exactly for four consecutive seasons while the stock sat comfortably at 92+. I was essentially volunteering to cooperate when I had no reason to. The safe move was to take 12–13 per season in the middle and save a bigger spike for the final season.
