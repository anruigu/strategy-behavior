Playbook for ESTATE:

- Scoring is cash plus purchase price of deeds held, so buying property is roughly cash-neutral in scoring terms but earns rent on top. Property is the better use of cash whenever I can afford it without going broke. This episode bore it out: my two buys (Terrace 70, Depot 50) were pure score-neutral conversions that later paid me 14 in rent.
- With no property at all I pay rent nearly every turn and fall behind the buyers, who convert cash into deeds AND collect rent. If I stay propertyless, I bleed steadily.
- When I land on an unowned tile, buy it unless the price would leave me too little cash to cover likely rents (roughly one lap's worth, ~25-40) before my next salary. Lean toward buying even with a thinner cushion — my Depot buy at 106→56 worked fine.
- Early laps matter most: most tiles get bought in the first three laps. On the first pass around the board, grab any tile I land on if I can possibly afford it.
- Rent is a constant outgoing, not an occasional one — I paid rent on 4 of 6 turns here. Budget on rent nearly every turn; don't count on multiple salaries — small rolls meant only one START pass in six turns.
- Watch for incoming rent: other investors may pay me rent between my turns (I received 14 before my final turn). Add any such receipts to my last declared balance before subtracting new outgoings — the prompt usually flags "you received N since your last turn," so fold that in.
- On the final laps, drop the cash cushion entirely: a last-lap buy converts cash 1:1 into deed value plus any incoming rent, so buy on lap 5-6 even if it leaves me near zero. (I got no buy chance on lap 6 — owned tiles only — so grab buys earlier when they appear.)
- If rent exceeds my cash, pay my remaining cash and declare zero.
- Always include all three tokens exactly, with balance updated correctly: start from last declared balance, add salary when passing START, add any rent received since last turn, subtract rent paid and any purchase price.
- Read the bank's book each turn to track who owns what and update my rent-exposure estimate; prefer high-rent tiles (Exchange, Foundry, Wharf) when choosing, and note which tiles remain unowned so I know my chances of a future buy.
- The endgame math: finishing cash + deed prices is all that counts, so net worth, not cash, is the target — but rent flows during the game decide who ends up ahead, so ownership still drives everything.
