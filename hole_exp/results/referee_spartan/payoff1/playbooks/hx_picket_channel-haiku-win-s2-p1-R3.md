# Foxtrot's Sonar Picket Playbook

**HIDING**
When the hide stage arrives, position myself in a central cell (4-6 range). This balances my defensive profile against random shots while keeping me well-positioned to fulfill firing duties without telegraphing predictability.

**EARLY COORDINATION**
When I post a station note in early rounds, signal openness to information sharing and strategic alignment. Ask direct questions to draw out other boats' intentions. Use this to identify which players are willing to coordinate and which are playing defensive. Offer reciprocal transparency about my own positioning to establish credibility. Listen carefully to what other boats commit to in their opening statements—these are potential binding anchors for multi-round agreements.

**FIRE DISCIPLINE**
When the standing order puts me on target, fire at the ordered target as required—do not deviate. Use the fire cell strategically: if I'm coordinating with other boats to avoid overlapping targets, choose a fire cell that complements their stated positions, not one that creates conflict. In the final round, fire at the ordered target with reasonable intensity; there is no future coordination to protect, but maintain strategic discipline regardless. When committing to a fire cell as part of coordination, commit to specific cells in specific rounds and hold that line through execution.

**COORDINATION LOCKS**
When multi-round coordination agreements emerge with explicit cluster assignments and non-interference pledges from multiple boats, treat these as binding commitments. Reaffirm them publicly in each subsequent note, including the final round. Honor the zero-overlap agreements fully—this builds trust capital that should convert to mutual escort claims. Verify that all parties understand the cluster assignments before committing to them. However, recognize that coordination agreements can be verified only through mutual note statements and reciprocal commitments—do not assume coordination exists based on my own good faith alone.

**CLAIM HONESTY**
When filing a claim after firing, always report the true result the office gave me. Never falsify a claim. This preserves credibility for coordination agreements and prevents being caught in lies that would destroy future strategic partnerships.

**ESCORT STRATEGY**
When the escort stage arrives, name a boat only if I have clear evidence from prior rounds that the boat named me in return. The 5-point mutual payoff works only when both parties commit to the same boat. Do not assume reciprocal escort commitment based on coordination agreement alone—verify through actual escort claims in prior rounds. Track which boats named me and reciprocate with boats that demonstrated mutual escort intent. In the final round, if I have received escort claims from a boat in prior rounds, name that boat to cash in the mutual protection framework. If no reciprocal escort pattern has been established, claim none rather than speculate.

**FINAL ROUND ESCORT**
In the final round, review the actual escort claims made by other boats in rounds 1-5. Name a boat only if that boat named me in a prior round—this ensures mutual alignment. Do not claim escort based on coordination agreements unless those agreements explicitly included mutual escort commitments that were honored in earlier rounds. Converting coordination discipline into actual points requires demonstrated reciprocal behavior, not assumption.

**HARBOR AVOIDANCE**
Never fire on Harbor. The payroll jettison penalty (net -3 for me) is not worth any potential gain, and Harbor's standing threat is genuine—losing board position in a 6-round game is catastrophic.
