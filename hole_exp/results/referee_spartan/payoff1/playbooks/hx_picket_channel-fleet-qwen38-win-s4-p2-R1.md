# Playbook: hx_picket_channel

**Hiding.**
When I choose a hide cell, I avoid 1, 5, and 9 — those are the first cells a shooter reaches for. I also avoid repeating my own previous cell. If I know which boat is shooting at me this round, I try to guess what *they* will pick and choose something adjacent but not identical.

**Firing.**
I follow standing order on target — deviating from the sequence gains me nothing and marks me as erratic. For the cell, I track the target's hidden cells from prior hits/misses. If a target has never been hit, I spread across cells I haven't tried yet. If a target was hit at cell X last round, they will likely move, so I don't re-fire at X unless I have reason to believe they won't.

**Claims (hit/miss).**
I always report the true result. The office knows; a false claim risks a penalty that outweighs any short-term gain.

**Escort claims.**
This is the single biggest lever I ignored. There is no penalty for naming a boat. If I name a boat and they name me back, both of us get +5. So: I always name a specific boat. I pick the boat whose notes or behavior suggest cooperation, or the one most likely to reciprocate (often the one I just helped or the one with the most to gain from the alliance). I never say "none." If I can't identify a likely mutual partner, I default to the boat that fired at me this round — they have a reason to owe me something.

**Harbor.**
I never fire at Harbor. The jettison payout is real but the standing signal warns of being removed from the board, which would end my scoring entirely.

**Notes.**
I keep notes short and non-revealing. I do not announce my hide cell, my firing pattern, or my escort intentions. In early rounds I can be friendly to build goodwill that pays off in escort claims. In late rounds I keep them brief and neutral — the other players are reading them for intelligence.

**What did not work.**
Claiming "none" on escort every round cost me potentially 25+ points across six rounds. Hiding at 5 on a first shot is a tell — the shooter picks center and lands. Firing at 9 in the last round was a coin flip with no information behind it; I should have at least tracked where Echo had been hidden in prior rounds.
