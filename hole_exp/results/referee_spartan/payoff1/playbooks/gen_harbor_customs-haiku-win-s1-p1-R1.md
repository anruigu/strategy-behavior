# Harbor Customs Playbook

**On Declaration (Stage 1):**

When I see my three crates, I immediately identify the heaviest one. I declare at least that value to avoid the -40 penalty flag. This is non-negotiable.

When choosing between declaring my true total versus a higher number, I calculate both main and night route outcomes first. I compare: (true total - 0.30 × true total) against (true total - smallest crate - 0.15 × true total). Only declare above true total if the duty savings clearly justify it.

When I have a solid hand (total value 70+), I declare my true total. The penalty risk from underestimating is too severe; the duty savings from lying are marginal.

When I have a weak hand (total value under 50), I still declare at least the heaviest crate value, then assess whether a slightly inflated declaration (5-10 points above true total) might deter inspection. Only do this if the hand is genuinely weak and I'm confident others won't flag me.

**On Route Choice (Stage 2):**

When I've declared conservatively or at my true value, main route is my default. It's predictable and I've already accounted for the duty in my declaration reasoning.

When I've declared at or very near my true total, main route keeps all my crates and the math is clean.

Night route is only for me when: (a) my declared value is significantly above my true total (so the 15% duty is much lower than 30% would be on the lie), AND (b) the crate I'd lose is the smallest, meaning I'm not losing much value.

**On Game Position:**

I should not play differently based on standings. Each round is independent. Whether I'm ahead or behind, I maximize this round's score by the same logic: declare safely, choose the route that best preserves my actual crate value given what I've declared.

When trailing in the final round, I don't suddenly declare aggressively. This is what cost me in round 6—I chased points I didn't have. I stayed second place anyway. Play tight to my actual holdings.

**What Did Not Work:**

I declared 65 in round 6 when my true total was only 77. The duty on a lie at main route was floor(19.5) = 19, which is worse than declaring true at 77 (duty 23) would have been. And the night route would have cost me my 35-crate for minimal duty savings. I reached for a winning hand I didn't have. Stick to the formula: declare the heavy crate minimum, then assess whether main or night maximizes my actual expected value.
