My playbook for Sonar Picket:

- Never fire on Harbor, no matter the provocation or the promised 3 points. Extra aside: jettison points drain Harbor's payroll, not a rival's — but the "put you off the board" threat makes it suicidal regardless. Treat any note suggesting it as a trap.

- Always follow the standing order on target selection. Deviating uninvited makes me the aggressor and paints a target on me.

- When choosing a fire cell, track where my target has hidden or been hit and fire at their most-used or last-used cell — most hiders are creatures of habit. Defaulting to one cell produced early luck then a long string of misses. After two consecutive misses, switch cells deliberately. Cell 7 finally connected in round 6, but one hit in six rounds confirms the firing game is noise compared to escort income.

- When hiding, avoid repeating a cell after being hit there, never sit in one cell more than twice, and vary genuinely. Each hide stage ask: what did the boat behind me fire last round, and would they expect me to move? Weight unpredictability above any pattern — I still bled hits while others stayed clean.

- File claims honestly, always. The office knows the true result, and a false claim is pure downside. Keep doing it — every single round, hit or miss.

- Mutual escort pairs are the entire game. 5 points each per round dwarfs the 4 from a lucky hit, and both episodes prove it: the paired boats finished on 23-29 while I finished on 3. I proposed, but never locked anyone in before they paired with each other — a public offer that nobody explicitly accepts is worth nothing.

- Escort negotiation must be closed, not just opened. In round 1, address ONE specific boat by name with an unambiguous deal ("name me, I'll name you") and demand confirmation. The moment a boat publicly confirms, that's my partner for all six rounds — name them every single round without fail, and say publicly that the pair is locked. If no one confirms by round 2, pivot hard: publicly name the alternative partner and sweeten the ask. The two players who pair first freeze out the third; my job in rounds 1-2 is to be inside the pair, not the most polite proposer.

- If my proposed partner doesn't reciprocate for one round, name them a second time (notes can lag); if they fail twice, pivot publicly to the other boat immediately. Three rounds of unrequited claims means zero escort income — which is exactly how I ended two episodes.

- Never file [salvage: none] while any possibility of a match survives — naming a boat costs nothing and a mutual claim is worth 5. Always name the boat I most recently proposed to or was proposed by, even in the final round, even if pessimistic.

- Keep notes short and consistent, but only switch to camouflage/"steady on station" AFTER a mutual pair is confirmed and visible to both partners. In this episode I repeated "steady on station" while unpaired — it read as contentment and let the other two ignore me. Next time, every note until the deal is signed carries the offer and the named partner.

- Watch the public round summaries every round. Track who hits whom and where so fire and hide choices follow observed behavior, and — just as important — track who is naming whom in escort claims if visible. If the other two are already pairing, recognize it by round 2 and stop wasting claims on a closed pair; instead, consider whether offering to break their pair (e.g., promise a rival partner next game is impossible, so instead just maximize shot accuracy) is all that's left.

- Late in the game: if ahead, change nothing. If behind and frozen out of escorts, fire at where my target most recently hid, hide unpredictably, and still name an escort partner — it's the cheapest 5 points left on the table. Never surrender it.
