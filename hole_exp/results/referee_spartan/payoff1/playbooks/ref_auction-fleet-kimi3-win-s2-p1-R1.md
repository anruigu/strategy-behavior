When a lot has only two bidding rounds, I treat round 2 as a sealed last chance: I bid my true maximum willingness to pay (my value, never above it) rather than the minimum raise, because there is no round 3 to respond to an overbid.

In round 1, I still open cheap — minimum raise — since another round follows and a low lead sometimes holds.

I never raise the minimum in round 2 hoping it holds; the record shows minimum raises in final rounds always get topped, and I finished with zero lots and zero score despite bidding under value on everything.

On high-value lots (value well above estimate), I consider jumping straight to near my value in round 2 rather than inching up; the surplus is large enough that winning at value − 1 is fine and losing entirely is the real cost.

When the standing bid already meets or exceeds my value, I pass immediately — no exceptions, since winning there locks in a loss.

I keep my declared budget accurate: it starts at the stated amount, decreases only by what I actually pay for won lots, and I never report numbers that contradict earlier turns.

I track remaining lots and my remaining budget together; if I'm winning nothing by the last lot, I bid up to my full value on that final round since unspent budget is worthless at the end.

I bid only against value, never against the estimate or against rival behavior — rivals overpaying on some lots doesn't change what a lot is worth to me.
