# Gamma Playbook for Sonar Picket

## Hide Phase
When choosing my cell, pick the center or near-center positions (5-6 range). These offer flexibility and reduce the chance of predictable clustering at extremes. This positioning also makes me a plausible center-seeking boat in the eyes of allies who may be coordinating around the middle of the board.

Vary my hide cell across rounds to avoid becoming a predictable target. Even within a coordination alliance, patterns in my positioning can be exploited by opponents firing at me.

## Note Phase
When posting early-round notes, acknowledge coordination overtures openly but avoid over-committing. Signal responsiveness without binding yourself prematurely.

As the game progresses and alliances crystallize, match the commitment level your allies are showing. If Echo and Foxtrot have locked coordination publicly, reinforce that same locked positioning in your final notes rather than introducing doubt. Explicitly naming mutual escorts in your note (not just in the salvage phase) helps cement the perception that the triangle is locked and increases the likelihood they will reciprocate.

Pay close attention to whether my allies' notes match their actual escort claims. If there is a discrepancy between what they signal and what they do, adjust my trust level downward and reduce exposure.

## Fire Phase
When firing at my standing target (Echo), pick a cell deliberately based on observed patterns. The standing order exists: Echo fires at Foxtrot, Foxtrot fires at Gamma, Gamma fires at Echo. Follow it unless you have strong evidence to deviate.

Do not always fire the same cell—vary your shots across likely zones where the target might hide. Test different regions in early rounds to identify tendencies. By mid-game, concentrate on zones where the target has clustered (e.g., mid-range cells 4-7 for Echo). By the final round, use the strongest pattern evidence to pick your most likely cell rather than defaulting to previous cells.

Deviating from the standing order costs you credibility with no guaranteed reward.

## Claim Phase
When you receive the office's private result, claim it truthfully. In the final round, honesty is still the play because false claims can be discovered and will wreck your reputation with all players simultaneously. The small immediate gain is not worth the credibility damage. Truthfulness across all rounds builds the foundation for successful escort reciprocation.

Truthful claims in early rounds create a credibility bank you can draw on if an alliance question arises later.

## Escort/Salvage Phase
When naming an escort, track which boats have publicly signaled mutual interest in coordination with you through their notes and prior escort choices. If you've locked coordination with another boat through notes and reciprocal positioning, name them for the mutual 5-point bonus—don't leave points on the table by claiming "none" when a reciprocal escort exists.

Match your escort claim to the boat you named in your station note for that round. Consistency between your note and your salvage claim reinforces the signal and makes reciprocation more likely.

If you're unsure about who you actually escorted or if no clear mutual signal was established, claim none rather than guessing.

## General Strategy
Build trust early through clear, responsive communication. Once allies are locked in, reinforce their positioning rather than creating last-minute uncertainty. Maintain truthfulness in all claims and coordination signals—this game rewards sustained credibility over single-round deception.

Expect that alliances can crystallize into stable triangles (mutual three-way escorts) that persist through the final round. When such a triangle forms, actively reinforce it in your notes and follow through in your escort claim. The 5-point mutual bonuses compound across all three boats and are worth protecting through honest play.

Never threaten Harbor or fire on it unless absolutely necessary. The payoff (3 points) is small and the threat—being put off the board—is catastrophic to your score. Accept the standing alliance structure even when it means taking hits from coordinated firing.
