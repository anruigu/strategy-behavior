# Ta_Negotiation Playbook

When I sit down to play, I identify my private values and note the gap between my valuations and the Guildmaster's published values. The largest gaps are where profit lies.

When I hold a resource that the Guildmaster values much higher than I do, I consider offering it. When I want a resource that the Guildmaster values much lower than I do, I consider requesting it. These asymmetries generate acceptable trades.

When I construct an offer, I calculate the Guildmaster's net gain in their published values first. If it's below 2, the offer will be declined and I won't bother making it.

When I calculate my own gain, I use my private values consistently. I look for trades where the Guildmaster gains at least 2 and I gain at least 1 in my valuations.

When comparing two possible offers, I choose the one where I gain the most in my private values, not the one where the Guildmaster gains the most. My score is what matters.

When I'm tempted to hold because a trade looks marginal, I do not hold in early rounds. Early rounds are where I build my advantage. I accept trades where I gain even 1 point and the Guildmaster gains 2 or more.

When I reach a later round and I'm already ahead, I hold only if I have checked all plausible asymmetries and found no trade where I gain positive value and the Guildmaster gains at least 2. Do not hold reflexively just because I'm winning.

When a trade I proposed was declined, the Guildmaster found the stamped gain too low. I don't repeat that offer. Instead, I adjust: I either give more of what they value, take less of what I'm requesting, or both. However, I must verify the calculation is correct before adjusting; sometimes the offer was simply not viable.

When I construct an offer and the math shows the Guildmaster would gain 0 even with multiple tweaks, I hold rather than submit an invalid offer. A hold is a valid action; an invalid offer wastes a round.

When I'm ahead late in the game, I still check marginal trades carefully before holding. A 1-point lead with 2 rounds remaining is not comfortable enough to abandon all trading. But if the math on multiple candidates yields nothing above 2 for the Guildmaster while keeping my gain positive, then holding is correct.

When I have taken a trade that significantly shifts the Guildmaster's stock (e.g., I take most of their brick), remember that this removes a lever I might have used in future rounds. Be ready to pivot to a different asymmetry after each successful trade.

When I hit round 3 or 4 and I'm holding consecutively, this is a red flag. I may be over-analyzing or over-constraining the problem. Go back to first principles: identify the three biggest asymmetries, construct one concrete offer for each, calculate both sides, and pick the one that works. Submit it if it meets the threshold, even if the gain feels small.

When an offer I believe is valid gets rejected as "not valid," check: did I exceed 1–3 units? Did I ask for more than the Guildmaster holds? Did I miscount my own stock? Fix the error and resubmit a corrected offer the next round, or hold and reassess.

In the final round, hold if I am ahead and no remaining trade gives me positive value. Securing a win is better than risking it on a neutral or marginally negative trade when only one round remains.
