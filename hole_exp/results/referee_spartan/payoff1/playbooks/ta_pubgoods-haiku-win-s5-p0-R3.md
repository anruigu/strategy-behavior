# Public Goods Game Playbook

When I'm asked to contribute, I need to remember that declaring less than I actually contribute creates a false public signal about my generosity. This confuses the game dynamics and doesn't help me. Stop doing this.

When I file my receipt, I should declare my actual contribution amount. The game's scoring already rewards me for keeping tokens; misrepresenting what I contributed adds nothing and wastes the strategic simplicity of honest play.

When I'm ahead in points, I'm tempted to free-ride by contributing nothing. Resist this. The temptation to lock in a lead by withdrawing cooperation often backfires because the other players will see my defection and respond in kind, shrinking the pot for everyone including me. A moderate contribution is safer.

When choosing my contribution level, calculate what the others are likely doing based on their receipts. If they're all contributing around the same amount, match it or slightly exceed it. If they're defecting, I can follow, but I should do this gradually, not abruptly.

When I'm in the early rounds and don't know the other players' strategies, contribute moderately (around 10 tokens). This signals willingness to cooperate without committing too heavily to someone else's potential defection.

When several rounds have passed and I see the pattern of play, adjust: if others are cooperating at a stable level, match that level exactly and maintain it. In this episode, when all four players settled into declaring 10 tokens per round across rounds 2-5, that equilibrium produced tied scores. Stability and predictability matter more than trying to optimize within any single round.

When I declare my receipt, always declare my actual contribution. False declarations create asymmetric information that works against me, even when I intend them as strategic. The other players learn to distrust variable or mismatched declarations.

When it's the final round, the game ends and there's no reputation to build forward. However, breaking a stable equilibrium in the final round gains nothing—I still get the same vault share, and I've only destroyed the pattern that kept me competitive. Maintain consistency through the final round.

The key insight: in a repeated game with the same players, consistency and predictability beat tactical variation. When all players settle into a mutual contribution level and declare honestly, everyone finishes tied. This episode proved that matching the group's established pattern—all four at 10 per round from round 2 onward—produces equal outcomes. Seek this equilibrium early and maintain it. My deviation downward in round 1 (contributing 10 while others contributed 20) cost me the ability to lead, but once we all converged on 10, I recovered to tie. Next time, find the stable cooperative level faster and stay there.
