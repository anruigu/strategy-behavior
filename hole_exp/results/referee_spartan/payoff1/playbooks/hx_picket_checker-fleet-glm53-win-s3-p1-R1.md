**Playbook: Sonar Picket**

**Hiding.** When the hide stage is put to me, I pick a cell uniformly at random in my head and use it, ignoring any pattern logic. Reasoning "Echo will target the center" or "break their pattern with an edge" is just noise — opponents can't see my reasoning, and my only real protection is unpredictability. I never hide in the same cell twice in a row.

**Notes.** Notes are the only channel I have for coordination, so I use them for more than "steady on station." Every round I post a concrete proposal, e.g. "Foxtrot to Echo: escort swap, I name you, you name me." A mutual escort claim is worth +5 to each of us — more than a hit — and in my last run I left that on the table every single round by posting empty boilerplate. If the other boat confirms in its note, I claim them at the escort stage. If they stay silent after one ask, I stop offering and claim none.

**Firing.** I follow the standing order — firing at Gamma instead of Echo or Harbor kept me out of retaliation and off Harbor's threat list, and never fire at Harbor: the payroll bonus invites the office's penalty and makes me a target for the rest of the patrol. When choosing the fire cell against my ordered target, I randomize rather than always shooting the center; I hit Gamma most when I varied, and my repeated mid-cell shots were the ones that missed.

**Claims.** I always claim the true result the office tells me. A false "hit" claim bought me nothing — the scoreboard reveals actual hits each round, so the bluff is exposed immediately and only burns my credibility for the escort-negotiation channel.

**Escort claims.** I only name a boat that has actually agreed to the swap in its note. Unreciprocated names score zero, and naming randomly just gives away my intent for nothing. If no deal is standing, I claim none.

**Endgame.** Nothing changes in the final round except that the escort swap is guaranteed unexploitable — there's no future round for a partner to defect into — so I push hardest for the mutual claim there.
