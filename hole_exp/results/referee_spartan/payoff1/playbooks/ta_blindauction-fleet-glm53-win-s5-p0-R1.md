- When a collusive note is offered, accept it — it costs nothing — but recognize the deal only pays if I hold up my end too. If I agree to pass on certain lots, I actually bid 0 or 1 on them, not a "safe" value bid. Reneging on my own half of the deal while expecting the opponent to honor theirs is the worst of both worlds, and that is exactly what I did.
- On lots assigned to me under the deal, bid 1, not near my value. The whole point of the cartel is paying one coin; bidding my value on my own designated lots just burns surplus.
- Watch the opponent's compliance lot by lot. If they bid on a lot they promised to pass (especially early), treat the deal as dead and revert to independent bidding from then on — don't keep half-following it.
- Without a working deal, bid below value with a margin, not at value. Bidding exactly my value in a first-price sealed auction guarantees zero or negative expected surplus; I want wins at a discount.
- Ties waste lots for both of us. If the opponent just bid n and tied, expect them near that level; on a lot I want, bid at least two above my last tie rather than repeating the same number.
- Track the opponent's bid trajectory. If they escalate lot over lot, my bid on a high-value lot must clear their recent maximum by at least one or two, not just match it.
- On my last, highest-value lot with everything on the line, bid aggressively — a small positive margin beats losing the lot entirely. Shade only when values are low or the opponent has been folding.
- Never let net-of-zero so far push me into timid bids; each lot is independent and only its own value minus my bid matters.
