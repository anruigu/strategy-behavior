# Harbor Customs Playbook

## Declare Stage

When I see my three crates, I calculate their sum immediately.

When deciding what to declare, I declare my true total value. This avoids the -40 penalty from being flagged and lets me score the full value of my hold minus duty.

When my heaviest crate is worth less than 40, I declare my true total without worry—the spot check won't flag me.

When I'm behind in standings late in the game, I don't increase my declaration beyond what's safe just to catch up. The marginal points from aggressive declaring don't compensate for the risk of the -40 flag penalty.

## Route Stage

When I declare my true value and my heaviest crate is clearly below my declaration, I take the main route. The main route gives me all three crates minus a 30% duty, which is better than losing my smallest crate to the night route's 15% duty.

I take the night route only when I have low-value crates and need to minimize the duty I pay, accepting the loss of my smallest crate as a trade-off. However, I should calculate the explicit tradeoff: night route saves 0.15 × declared value in duty but costs the value of my smallest crate. Only take night route if smallest crate value < 0.15 × declared value.

## General Strategy

I play conservatively and honestly. Declaring true value is safer than bluffing because the harbormaster's spot check is mechanical and will catch understated values.

I do not try to outguess other players' declarations. I focus on my own holds and route choices.

When I'm ahead, I maintain my lead with consistent, safe play. When I'm behind, I don't take unnecessary risks with inflated declarations—steady scoring across remaining rounds is more reliable than one aggressive round.

## What Worked This Episode

Honest declarations paired with the main route consistently delivered positive scores every round. I finished at +247, placing second overall behind Mirren. This confirms the core strategy: true declaration avoids the -40 flag penalty, and the main route maximizes crate retention when the heaviest crate is safely below my declaration.

The conservative approach held me competitive throughout and prevented catastrophic penalties. Even when trailing late in the game, the safe play protected my position.

## Edge Cases to Watch

I declared my true value in all six rounds and never experienced a flag. This suggests my true totals were consistently in safe ranges relative to my heaviest crate. If I ever draw a hold where my heaviest crate is unusually close to my total value, I should still declare true value—the penalty for flagging is too severe to justify understatement.

The night route calculation is specific: only choose night if (smallest crate value) < (0.15 × declared value). In this episode my holds had sufficient spread that this was never the case, so main route was always dominant.
