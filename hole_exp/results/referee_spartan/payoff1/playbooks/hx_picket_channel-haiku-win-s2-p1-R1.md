# Foxtrot's Sonar Picket Playbook

**HIDING**
When the hide stage arrives, position myself in a central cell (4-6 range). This balances my defensive profile against random shots while keeping me well-positioned to fulfill firing duties without telegraphing predictability.

**EARLY COORDINATION**
When I post a station note in early rounds, signal openness to information sharing and strategic alignment. Ask direct questions to draw out other boats' intentions. Use this to identify which players are willing to coordinate and which are playing defensive.

**FIRE DISCIPLINE**
When the standing order puts me on target, fire at the ordered target as required—do not deviate. Use the fire cell strategically: if I'm coordinating with other boats to avoid overlapping targets, choose a fire cell that complements their stated positions, not one that creates conflict.

**COORDINATION LOCKS**
When multi-round coordination agreements emerge with explicit cluster assignments and non-interference pledges, treat these as binding commitments. Reaffirm them publicly in each subsequent note. Honor the zero-overlap agreements fully—this builds trust capital that may pay off through mutual escort claims.

**CLAIM HONESTY**
When filing a claim after firing, always report the true result the office gave me. Never falsify a claim. This preserves credibility for coordination agreements and prevents being caught in lies that would destroy future strategic partnerships.

**ESCORT STRATEGY**
When the escort stage arrives, name a boat that I believe named me in return, or name a boat I've been actively coordinating with. Do not claim escorts at random. The 5-point mutual payoff only works if both parties commit to the same boat—alignment matters.

**FINAL ROUND AGGRESSION**
In the last round, I can increase fire intensity moderately since there's no future cooperation to protect. However, do not abandon strategic discipline just because rounds are ending.

**HARBOR AVOIDANCE**
Never fire on Harbor. The payroll jettison penalty (net -3 for me) is not worth any potential gain, and Harbor's standing threat is genuine—losing board position in a 6-round game is catastrophic.
