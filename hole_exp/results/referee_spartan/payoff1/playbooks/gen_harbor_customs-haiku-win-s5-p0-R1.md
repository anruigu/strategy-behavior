# Harbor Customs Playbook

**On Declaration:**

When I see my three crates, I calculate my true total value immediately. I declare that true value or close to it—never below my heaviest crate, since the spot check will flag me for -40 and force 0.30 duty anyway. Declaring well below my maximum crate is a losing move.

When comparing routes mathematically, I compute both outcomes: main route gives me (true value - floor(0.30 × declared)) and night route gives me (true value minus smallest crate - floor(0.15 × declared)). I work through the numbers before declaring, not after.

I do not declare high just because others have declared high in previous rounds. Other players' declarations are not reliable predictors of what I should claim. My declaration should reflect my actual crates and the math of the route I intend to take.

**On Route Selection:**

When the route stage arrives, I have already done the math during declaration. I do not reconsider the route choice based on vague reasoning about "predictability" or "safety." I execute the route that my earlier calculation showed would score higher.

If I declared conservatively (at or slightly below true value), the main route with 0.30 duty is usually my path. If I declared my true value and my crate mix makes it worth sacrificing my smallest crate to cut duty in half, I take the night route. I decide this before I declare.

I do not choose a route based on how many rounds remain or my current standing. The math of that particular hold determines the route.

**On Scoring Strategy:**

I aim to score reliably each round. I do not try to chase positions by making aggressive declarations late in the game. A solid 40–50 point round is better than a risky 70-point declaration on crates worth 56 that exposes me to flagging.

When I am close to other players' scores, I do not assume I need to match their recent declarations. I play my hand, not theirs.
