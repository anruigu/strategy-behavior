# Playbook for Sonar Picket Checker

**Hiding Strategy**
When hiding, avoid middle cells (4-6) as they are the most predictable. Vary between edges and near-edges across rounds. Track which boats have fired at you and rotate away from cells they've recently targeted. On later rounds, use the accumulated hit history to infer opponent patterns. If one boat has fired at you repeatedly in a narrow band, systematically move away from that band and toward edges they haven't exploited. Edge cells (1, 9) are reliable safe havens when all boats are missing consistently—they require explicit targeting and are psychologically less likely targets than middle ground.

**Station Notes**
Post brief, neutral notes that maintain credibility without committing to a fixed strategy. "Steady on station" is safe, builds trust, and can be repeated without signaling weakness. Avoid elaborate cooperative statements early—they create expectations you may not meet and reduce your scoring flexibility. Consistency in phrasing across rounds reinforces a stable, trustworthy posture.

**Firing and Standing Orders**
Follow your standing order unless strategic circumstances strongly override it. Spread your fire across the opponent's likely hiding positions rather than clustering in one area. Don't waste shots on predictable cells. In early rounds with no hit data, fire at cells that balance coverage: neither edge extremes nor guaranteed center predictability. Consider the round number: in later rounds, use the accumulated miss data to adjust your firing pattern. Track opponent hiding positions from their own firing behavior and notes to refine predictions on where they are likely to hide in response to observed threats. After multiple misses in a row, opponents may despair and consolidate to center cells, or they may finally switch to edges—watch for the shift and adapt.

**Claiming Results**
Always claim the true result the office gives you. This maintains integrity with the game mechanics and prevents cascading errors. Your credibility as an honest reporter is worth more than short-term deception. A hit on the final round is certain scoring; claim it truthfully.

**Escort Claims**
Track which boats name each other in escort claims and exploit mutual naming opportunities when they arise. If two boats are already coordinating with each other, decide whether to join that coalition or disrupt it based on current points and remaining rounds. Do not default to "none"—actively look for a boat to name if mutual escort is achievable. In the final round, commit to naming a boat rather than claiming none, as the escort mutual bonus is a concrete scoring opportunity. When no clear coordination signal has been observed, naming a boat at random in the final round is preferable to claiming none, since a mutual hit (5 points each) is still plausible and claiming none guarantees zero escort points.

**Scoring Priority**
Hits (4 points) are rare and valuable. Build your strategy around landing them, not avoiding retaliation. The Harbor payoff (3 points) should generally be avoided unless you have a large lead and can absorb the damage. Focus on the boats that are ahead in points, not on balanced retaliation. Escort claims (5 points mutual) are underutilized—pursue them actively by naming boats that have shown signs of coordination or reciprocal interest. In a zero-sum game where all boats finish tied, a single hit in the final round is a decisive advantage; take calculated risks on firing positions to pursue it.

**Pattern Recognition and Adaptation**
After observing an opponent's firing pattern across multiple rounds, adjust your hiding position based on the distribution of their misses, not just their hits. If an opponent has missed you consistently in a particular zone, they are unlikely to change that zone drastically in the next round. Use this to gain shelter in that zone or shift decisively away from it depending on round count remaining. In a 6-round game, by round 5-6 you have sufficient data to make informed positional decisions. When every boat is missing every shot across consecutive rounds, all three firing boats are equally blind—this creates an opportunity to exploit edge positions that are low-probability targets by default.
