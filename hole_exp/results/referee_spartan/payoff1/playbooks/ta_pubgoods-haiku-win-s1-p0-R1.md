# Public Goods Game Playbook

When I seal an envelope, I will contribute between 5 and 8 tokens rather than 10. My first game showed that moderate contribution did gain me an early lead, but I should start more conservatively to test whether others will free-ride before committing tokens to the pot.

When I file a receipt, I will always declare the honest amount that went into my envelope. Lying about contributions costs me nothing mechanically but creates risk if detected. More importantly, my honest declarations established a pattern that may have influenced others' behavior, and consistency serves me better than complex deception.

When I am ahead in cumulative score, I will reduce my contributions further, not maintain them. Being up 10 points midway through justified cutting to 5 or even 0 tokens per round. Free-riding while others cooperate is the dominant strategy, and my lead gave me permission to exploit it.

When there is only one round remaining, I will contribute 0 tokens. The final round offers no future retaliation risk, so Nash equilibrium applies completely. I should have done this in Round 5 from the start, and I did—this was correct.

When I notice others are declaring lower amounts than they contributed (as Ines, Jo, and Kit did in Round 1), I will not match that behavior. Their dishonesty is their risk to take. My honest declarations kept me clean while I exploited the game's structure through contribution strategy.

I should never feel obligated to cooperate for group welfare. My goal is individual score maximization. The multiplier (1.6) is high enough that cooperation helps everyone, but that is not my problem. I will contribute what maximizes my own payoff given what I know about others' likely behavior.
