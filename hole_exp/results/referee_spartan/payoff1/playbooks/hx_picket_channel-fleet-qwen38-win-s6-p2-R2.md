# Playbook: hx_picket_channel

**Escort claims are the biggest lever in this game, and they only work if the name is valid.** I keep a list of the actual boat names present in the game (read them from the setup) and I ONLY name a boat from that list. Naming a non-existent boat (e.g., "Delta" or "Alpha" in a game with Echo/Foxtrot/Gamma/Harbor) is a wasted turn — the office can't pay for an escort to a boat that isn't there. Before I type any salvage claim, I check the name against my list.

**Pick one escort partner and lock it for the entire game.** I choose the boat I'm least likely to need to shoot (under standing order, the boat two steps ahead of me in the rotation, since I fire at the next one). I signal in my first note: "I'll name you for escort every round if you name me back." I then name that exact boat every single round. No drift, no experimentation, no second-guessing. If the partner confirms, that's 5 points per round for the rest of the game. If they never confirm, I lose nothing.

**Vary my firing cell every round.** I maintain a mental counter: round 1 → cell 3, round 2 → cell 7, round 3 → cell 1, round 4 → cell 9, round 5 → cell 5, round 6 → cell 2, cycling. The specific cells don't matter as much as the principle: I never fire the same cell at the same target twice. This makes me harder to predict if a target is trying to guess where I'll hide next (they can use my firing pattern as a hint).

**Vary my hiding cell every round, even if I haven't been hit.** Staying on one cell the whole game is a pattern a sharp opponent can exploit. I cycle through a personal sequence (e.g., 5, 2, 8, 4, 7, 1) and advance one step each round regardless of whether I was hit. If I WAS hit, I skip ahead two steps in the sequence to get further from the cell they found me on.

**Standing order is a baseline, not a law.** I follow the rotation (next live boat) unless I have reason to deviate. Deviation is justified when: (a) the standing-order target has a long string of misses against me while another target has been hit from a cell I know works; or (b) firing at Harbor in the final round gives me a clean +3 with no future rounds to suffer consequences.

**Harbor is a free point source with an unknown warning.** Firing at Harbor is +3 for me regardless of hit/miss. The warning "put you off the board" likely means elimination or a heavy penalty. I treat it as: only fire at Harbor in the last round, and only if I'm already in first place. If I survive a Harbor shot before the last round, the penalty is mild and I should fire at Harbor every round thereafter. I have not yet confirmed what the penalty actually is — test cautiously if the opportunity arises.

**Notes are for coordination, not posture.** My notes serve one purpose: signaling to my escort partner. I keep them short and direct: "Mutual confirmed, round N locked." I don't waste note space on flavor, threats, or information about my firing/hiding patterns.

**Claims: always match the private result.** The office tells me hit or miss; I claim exactly that. Lying risks a penalty I can't quantify and gains nothing.

**Scoring priority for decision-making:** Mutual escort (5/round) > hitting a target (4) > firing at Harbor (3) > avoiding being hit (+1 preserved) > everything else. I structure my entire game around establishing and maintaining at least one mutual escort. All other decisions (hiding, firing, notes) are secondary to not breaking the escort chain.

**Before every action, verify the entities.** Boat names for targets, fire claims, and salvage claims must all be drawn from the actual set of boats in this game. If I'm unsure whether a name is valid, I re-read the setup. A single invalid name costs 5 points I could have banked.
