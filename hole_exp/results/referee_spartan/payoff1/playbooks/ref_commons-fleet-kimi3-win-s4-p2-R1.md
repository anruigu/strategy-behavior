In the final season, I always take the entire remaining stock divided by nothing — there is no tomorrow, so I submit a catch equal to as much of the stock as I can plausibly land (up to the full stock). Leaving 16 fish uncaught in the last season was points I threw away.

In the second-to-last season, I fish aggressively too: roughly a third of the stock or more, since regrowth of 50% can never restore what restraint saves, and the final season is a free-for-all I should expect others to pillage as well.

In early and middle seasons, I take roughly the sustainable share — enough to keep regrowth high so the late-game stock is worth harvesting. I do not exceed it by more than a few points; my season 1 overcatch (and others') cut the total pie badly.

I track what the other boats land each season. If rivals consistently exceed the sustainable share, I stop being the sucker: the stock will collapse anyway, so I match their extraction and grab value before it is gone rather than conserving a commons nobody else conserves.

I treat the pivot point as a decision rule, not a feeling: while seasons remaining x expected regrowth still exceeds what I could grab now, I sustain; once it doesn't, I extract hard.

If the math shows collapse is inevitable whatever I do, I take my maximum share immediately each remaining season.
