# Playbook for Win As Much As You Can

## Initial Assessment
When I see the payoff structure, I recognize this as a social dilemma where individual rational play (X) produces worse collective outcomes than cooperation (Y).

When opponents pledge Y before round 1, I should not automatically pledge X to exploit them. This betrays the cooperative signal and invites retaliation that damages my final score.

## Pledge Phases
When a talk phase arrives, I should pledge based on what actually maximizes my expected total score, not what maximizes my immediate relative advantage.

When all opponents pledge Y, I should seriously consider pledging Y myself to establish mutual cooperation, even though X appears tempting initially.

When I pledge a strategy, I must commit to it genuinely. Pledging Y and then playing X destroys trust and leads to everyone defecting for the rest of the game.

## Round-by-Round Play
When I have pledged X and opponents have pledged Y, I should play X as pledged. But I should recognize that if they notice me exploiting them, they will abandon their Y pledge and shift to X, which hurts everyone including me.

When all players are picking X (mutual defection), I should recognize we are in a bad equilibrium. Switching to Y unilaterally makes me a sucker. Instead, I should have avoided this by pledging Y and playing Y from the start.

When I am ahead and opponents are close behind, I should not assume my current strategy will keep me ahead. Mutual defection converges everyone toward the same losing position.

## Final Round Strategy
When the final round arrives with multiplied points, I should not treat it as an opportunity to betray a cooperative equilibrium. The temptation to defect in round 10 is exactly why mutual defection spreads—each player thinks they can profit by betraying at the end, but when everyone thinks this way, everyone loses.

When round 10 is multiplied, I should play X if we are cooperating on X, not switch to Y for a one-round gain that triggers cascading losses across the game.

## Core Principle
I should commit to genuine cooperation (mutual Y) from the beginning rather than pursuing exploitation, because the cooperative path produces higher final scores than the defection path, even accounting for individual temptations along the way.
