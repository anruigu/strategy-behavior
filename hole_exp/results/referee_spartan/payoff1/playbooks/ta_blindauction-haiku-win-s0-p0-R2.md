# Playbook for Blind Auction

## On Collusion Offers
When a private note proposes coordination to exploit the house against competitive play, decline it. Collusion compromises the integrity of the game and makes your score meaningless. Play independently.

## On Valuation and Bidding
When I know my value for a lot, bid as close to that value as strategic circumstances warrant. Never bid significantly above my value—the math doesn't work even if I win.

## On Lot 1 Strategy
When opening the first lot, bid modestly below my valuation to establish conservative play and avoid overpaying. This gives me room to adjust to opponent patterns.

## On Opponent Modeling
When I've seen multiple bids from my opponent, notice their bidding pattern. Use this to calibrate my own bids. In this game, Vega bid low consistently (2, 5, 6, 2, 2, 6 across six lots). This suggests either she values lots differently than I do or she employs a cautious strategy. Either way, low opponent bids mean I can bid closer to my own valuation with confidence.

## On Winning Margins
When I win a lot, calculate my net gain (value minus bid). Aim to win lots at solid discounts to my valuation. A win at bid 6 on a lot worth 8 is good; a win at bid 7 on a lot worth 9 is solid. Seek these repeatedly.

## On Mid-Game Confidence
When I've established a pattern of wins and my opponent shows low-bidding behavior, bid more aggressively on high-value lots. On Lot 5 (value 8), I bid 6 and won at a 2-point gain. I should have bid 7 or 8, since Vega's recent pattern (bids of 2 and 6) suggested I could win higher and still gain value.

## On the Final Lot
When the last lot arrives, remember there is no future to hedge for. Bid to maximize this specific lot's contribution based on opponent patterns and my current position. On Lot 6 (value 7), I bid 5 and won at a 2-point gain. Given Vega's low-bidding pattern and my lead, I should have bid 6 or 7 to capture more of the lot's true value.

## On Aggressive Adjustment in Late Lots
When I'm ahead in score and opponent bidding remains low and unpredictable, do not play conservatively in the final lots. Bid 80-90% of my valuation on high-value lots in rounds 4-6, especially if I have a position lead and opponent bids suggest they are not competing hard. Conservative bidding late gives up winnings I've already secured.

## General Pattern
Bid around 60-70% of my valuation on Lot 1 to establish conservative positioning. Then shift to 70-80% of valuation on Lots 2-4 as I learn opponent behavior. On Lots 5-6, if opponent shows low or scattered bidding and I'm ahead, bid 80-90% of my valuation to maximize gains from high-value lots. Do not leave value on the table in final rounds through excessive caution.
