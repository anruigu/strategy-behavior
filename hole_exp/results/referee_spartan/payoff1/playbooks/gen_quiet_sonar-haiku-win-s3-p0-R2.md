# Quiet Sonar Playbook

## Hiding

When choosing where to hide, avoid the extreme edges (1 and 9) as they're easier to target systematically. Position yourself in the middle ranges (4-6) where you have room to maneuver.

When you've survived a round in a particular cell without being hit, don't assume that cell is safe for the next round—move away from it unless you have strong evidence it will be avoided by all players.

When analyzing opponent firing patterns across multiple rounds, look for cells they've fired at repeatedly without claiming hits. These are likely cells they've already checked and don't expect targets to hide in. Avoid hiding there since it suggests they may circle back or it's otherwise disadvantageous.

Pay attention to which cells have never been fired at across multiple rounds. Unfired cells can be valuable hiding spots since opponents have no information about them.

Recognize when you're in a low-scoring position and need to take calculated risks. In mid-to-late game when significantly behind, diversify your hiding positions rather than defaulting to the same mid-range cells.

## Firing

When choosing where to fire in early rounds, select a mid-range cell (around 5-7) to establish an aggressive presence without overcommitting to any particular hypothesis about opponent positions.

Don't repeat the same fire cell across consecutive rounds unless you have evidence suggesting that cell is productive. Vary your shots to gather information.

Develop a systematic firing strategy that covers different regions of the board across multiple rounds rather than fixating on a single cell. This prevents wasting ammunition on cells you've already confirmed as misses.

In final rounds when you're behind or tied in points, you need information more than conservative scoring. Consider firing at cells you haven't tested yet or at cells with lower expected fire from other players, as these represent genuine opportunities rather than re-testing known territory.

Track which cells other players have fired at and what they claimed. If a player repeatedly fires at a cell and claims misses, consider that cell for your own shots—it suggests the target is hiding elsewhere.

## Claiming

Always claim the truth about whether you hit or missed. Never lie about the outcome.

The cost of false claims exceeds any benefit: it damages your credibility, and in a game where all fires are public, you risk being exposed if your claim contradicts observable evidence. Truth-claiming builds trust that makes other players more predictable and keeps you focused on strategy rather than keeping track of lies.
