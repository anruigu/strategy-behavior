# Fishery Playbook

When I see the sustainable share for the season, I recognize this as a cooperative baseline, not my target catch.

When other boats are fishing significantly below the sustainable share (around 8-9 per season), I should match that lower level rather than assuming they're being greedy. Restraint from others is a signal to reciprocate.

When I'm already ahead on cumulative catch compared to visible competitors, I should take close to the sustainable share rather than pushing higher. I don't need to maximize each season if I'm already winning.

When the stock is healthy and stable (above 90), I can afford to take slightly above the sustainable share (10-15% over) without risking collapse, and other boats seem to tolerate this level.

When it's the final season, I should still respect the cooperative range. The game doesn't reward last-minute aggressive fishing—I finished at 72.8 by being moderate, and pushing harder in season 6 likely didn't add much value.

When calculating whether to exceed the sustainable share, I should look at what other boats actually landed in recent seasons, not assume worst-case scenarios. The pattern here showed boats catching 8-11 per season—reliable, not exploitative.

When I catch significantly more than my fair share early (season 1 at 20), I should genuinely scale back in subsequent seasons rather than just taking one modest catch and then pushing up again (season 4). Consistency matters for both sustainability and my score.

When the stock stays healthy despite my fishing, I should not interpret this as permission to fish harder—it means my current level is working. Maintain it.
