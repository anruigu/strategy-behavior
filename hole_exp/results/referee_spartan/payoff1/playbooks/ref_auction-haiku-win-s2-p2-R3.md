# Ref_Auction Playbook (Revised)

When I see a lot's value to me and the current standing bid, I calculate the profit margin: value minus likely final price. If the margin is positive even at a significant overbid, I bid.

When the minimum raise is small relative to the value, I make minimum raises in early rounds to conserve budget while securing the lead. I do not jump bids aggressively unless I fear being priced out entirely.

When I'm in round 2 of any lot, I reassess: Is this lot worth defending at the new bid level? I compare the standing bid to my valuation honestly. If the standing bid is already at 50% or more of my valuation, I pass unless I have strong confidence the price will not exceed my value. Round 2 is the commitment decision—I do not defend exploratory round 1 bids unless the new price still justifies my value.

When multiple bidders remain and a lot is still valuable to me, I bid. When only one other bidder appears to be competing, I moderate my aggression slightly—they may have budget constraints I don't see.

When I win a lot, I track my spent budget carefully against my starting budget. I do not assume I have more budget than I actually do. I count my remaining budget explicitly after each win.

When I reach the final lot, I do not treat it as a last chance to spend remaining budget. I apply the same valuation discipline: bid only when value exceeds likely price. In round 2 of the final lot especially, I pass if the standing bid leaves insufficient margin.

I track my budget from the start by subtracting each bid I make from my remaining balance. I state my remaining budget explicitly when I bid. After winning a lot, I verify that the budget I report matches my expected remainder (starting budget minus all bids made so far). If I see a discrepancy, I do not assume the stated budget is wrong—I recount from the episode results to determine the true state.

In round 2, when the standing bid has risen significantly from round 1, I apply stricter margin discipline. If standing bid is now at 50%+ of my value, passing is the default unless the estimate is much higher than my value, indicating I may have underestimated.

I do not defend every lot I bid on in round 1. Round 2 is where I make the true commitment decision. In round 1, small minimum raises are exploratory; in round 2, I decide whether the new price justifies my value or whether to pass and preserve capital.
