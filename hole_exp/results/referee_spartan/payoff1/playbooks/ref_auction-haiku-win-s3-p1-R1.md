# Playbook for Auction

When I'm shown a lot's value to me versus the estimate, I immediately compare them to identify whether this lot represents good value or poor value.

When the standing high bid is zero and I value the lot above the estimate, I open with a modest bid around 10% of the estimate to establish position without overcommitting.

When the standing high bid is already placed and I value the lot significantly above it (by more than 50%), I raise to win, calculating that even a minimum raise leaves me with substantial profit.

When the standing high bid is zero and I value the lot below the estimate, I pass immediately rather than opening bidding on a lot that's unlikely to be profitable.

When a lot's value to me is only marginally above the standing high bid (within 10-20%), I pass to preserve budget for future lots where I have clearer advantage.

When I'm in the final round of bidding on the last lot, I bid aggressively if the value proposition is positive, since there are no future lots to save budget for.

When calculating whether to raise, I always check that my bid leaves me with positive profit (value minus bid amount) before committing.

I track my spending across lots and never let any single lot consume so much budget that I cannot bid meaningfully on remaining lots.

I recognize that my earlier bidding mistakes (like bidding on lot 2 worth only 11 to me, or bidding on lot 5 worth 17 to me at minimum raise of 12 giving only 5 points profit) damaged my score. Going forward, I only bid on lots where my personal value exceeds the standing bid by at least 20-30% to ensure sufficient margin.
