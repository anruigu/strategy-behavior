# Public Goods Game Playbook

When I seal an envelope, I will contribute between 5 and 8 tokens rather than 10 in early rounds. Moderate contribution allows me to test others' behavior while maintaining a sustainable position. Once I observe that others are cooperating at high levels (like the 20-token declarations in Round 1), I can safely reduce my contributions to 5 tokens and benefit from the multiplier effect without matching their cooperation.

When I file a receipt, I will always declare the honest amount that went into my envelope. Honest declarations cost me nothing mechanically and create no detection risk. They also provide valuable information about what the pot actually contained, which helps me predict future outcomes and adjust my strategy accordingly.

When I am ahead in cumulative score, I will reduce my contributions further, not maintain them. Being up points midway through justifies cutting my contribution to match the emerging baseline (5 tokens) rather than maintaining cooperation. Free-riding while others cooperate is the dominant strategy when I have already secured an advantage.

When there is only one round remaining, I will contribute 0 tokens. The final round offers no future retaliation risk, so Nash equilibrium applies completely. I should execute this without hesitation or delay—it maximizes my final payoff given that others' contributions are locked in.

When I notice others declaring amounts equal to or higher than what I contributed, I will take this as evidence they are playing cooperatively and use it as a signal to reduce my own contributions in subsequent rounds. Their higher contributions make the pool larger, and I can benefit from that pool while contributing less myself.

I should never feel obligated to cooperate for group welfare. My goal is individual score maximization. The multiplier (1.6) is high enough that group cooperation helps me significantly, but I should capture that benefit through strategic free-riding on others' contributions rather than by matching their cooperation. The winning strategy is to start observing what others will contribute, then systematically undercontribute relative to that baseline while collecting my share of the enlarged pot.

I will expect convergence toward lower contributions from other players over time as they observe my pattern and adjust. This convergence is favorable to me because it means the pot stays modest while I have already built a scoring lead from earlier rounds when others contributed more heavily.
