# Playbook for Public Goods

When I'm shown the envelope phase, I will contribute between 8-12 tokens in the early rounds to establish a moderate, cooperative baseline while gathering information about other players' strategies.

When I'm shown the receipt phase, I will always declare the exact amount I contributed, since accuracy is required and declaring falsely creates risk without benefit.

When I find myself ahead by more than 10 points with 2+ rounds remaining, I will not reduce my contribution below 5 tokens. My strategy of minimal contributions while ahead did work to win, but it suppressed the overall pot and reduced my absolute score. I should maintain moderate contributions to grow the pot while protecting my lead through better play, not through defection.

When calculating whether to contribute less, I will remember that each token I don't contribute but others do costs me 0.4 tokens in lost shared returns (1.6x multiplier ÷ 4 players = 0.4x per token). This means free-riding gains only 0.6 net tokens per withheld token in the pot's growth. The advantage is real but modest, and it decays if others also defect.

When I'm ahead late in the game, I will contribute minimally (1-3 tokens) only in the final round, not earlier. Early defection invites retaliation in subsequent rounds that erodes my advantage.

When other players' receipts are consistently higher than mine, I will treat this as a signal that they're willing to contribute more. I should not assume they'll continue if I defect further—instead, I should prepare for the possibility that they'll match my lower contributions in future rounds.

I will not rely on others' defection to secure my win. My win here came from both contributing more early and establishing a lead, then defecting late. The defection alone would not have won; the early cooperation created the gap I then exploited.
