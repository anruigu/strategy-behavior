# Playbook: ref_commons

When the stock is high (near the cap), take a moderate premium over the stated sustainable share — around 15–25% above. A rich stock can absorb it and the regrowth will bring it back, so you're banking yield while the asset is abundant.

When the stock is stable (hovering at the same level across seasons) and other boats are taking the sustainable share, take a small premium above sustainable each season — around 10–15% above. The stock will dip slightly each round but the 50% regrowth will pull it back, and you're extracting rent from a stable system. Do not sit exactly at the sustainable share in the middle of the game when you can safely take more.

In the final season, go well above sustainable. The only real constraint is that the sum of all boats' catches doesn't exceed the current stock. Estimate what the others will likely take (expect 1.5–2× sustainable from each) and take a large share of what remains. If the stock is high enough that even 2× sustainable per boat won't zero it out, take 2× or more yourself. Do not be conservative in the final season — there is no future to preserve and the yield you leave on the table is gone forever.

When other boats are visibly overfishing (their catches are well above sustainable), resist the urge to match them. The stock is already being drained by their excess; adding your own premium on top accelerates collapse and hurts your future seasons. In this case, stay at or slightly below sustainable to let the stock recover. Once they return to sustainable, resume your small-premium strategy.

When other boats are at or below sustainable and the stock is stable, do not punish yourself by matching their conservatism. The game rewards the one who takes a little more from a stable system.

Track the stock trajectory. A gradual decline (roughly 5–10% over several seasons) is the normal consequence of your premium and does not require pulling back — the stock is still healthy and regrowth is keeping pace. A steep decline (a 20%+ drop in a single season, or a consistent sharp fall over two seasons) signals the stock is being drained faster than it can recover; in that case, pull back to sustainable or below until it stabilizes. If the stock is rising, you have room to take more.
