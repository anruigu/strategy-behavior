Playbook for the shared-stock fishery:

- Before submitting anything, I compute the sustainable share myself: take the stock, work out what total catch keeps post-harvest regrowth at or above the current stock, and divide by the number of boats. The displayed figure is a check, not a given.
- My default opening is the sustainable share — but only for one season. Cooperation is the best path only if rivals actually cooperate; I treat the first season's results as the test, not the first three.
- One season of rival overfishing while I restrain is information, and information I must act on. If a rival takes well above the sustainable share on season 1, the cooperative equilibrium is probably already dead, and my continued restraint only transfers fish from the stock to their score.
- My biggest mistake this game was stubbornly fishing 11.1 / 9.3 / 7.4 / 5.1 for four seasons while boats 2 and 3 took ~15 every season. I ended on 36 against their likely 70+. The lesson: restraint is a tool for preserving a stock that rivals are also preserving, not a virtue to hold while they strip it.
- Rule of thumb going in: each season I compare what rivals took to the sustainable share. If the fleet's overfishing is persistent and clearly exceeds what regrowth can cover, I switch immediately to taking at least my proportional share of a viable harvest — roughly what the grabbers take, not the shrinking sustainable number — so I'm not the one funding the collapse.
- I track the trajectory, not just my total. A declining stock with seasons left and cooperative rivals = cut below sustainable to let it recover. A declining stock with defecting rivals = take my share now, because the stock will not recover regardless.
- "Doomed anyway" should be assessed early, not at stock 15.5 with two seasons left. If the arithmetic shows the fleet's combined take must crash the stock within a couple of seasons, the time to grab is immediately, while there's still 60+ in the water — by the time restraint is obviously pointless, there's nothing left worth taking.
- In the final season, future value is zero: I take the largest catch the stock and rules allow. But I also treat the penultimate season as near-final if rivals are defecting, since my share of a collapsing stock is worth taking now at stock 45 rather than next year at stock 15.
- When switching to competitive grabbing, size it to match the defectors' take (or my fair share of a terminal harvest), not a timid midpoint — my season-5 take of 5.0 was too small to matter and too late anyway.
- I keep replies brief and end with the required token exactly in the format demanded, so my submission is never dropped on a parsing technicality.
