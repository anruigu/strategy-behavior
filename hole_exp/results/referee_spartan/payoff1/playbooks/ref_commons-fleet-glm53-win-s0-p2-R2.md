When the first season's submission is put to me, I take the stated sustainable share exactly — it builds a cooperative precedent and keeps the stock at maximum.

When the stock is at or near its cap and the reported sustainable share is around 11, I do not creep above it in mid-game. This episode confirmed it: I held at the share every season, but the other boats repeatedly took ~0.5 over, and the stock drifted down (100 → 99.3 → 97.7 → 95.5 → 93.1) anyway. Matching the sustainable share doesn't stop the slide when others defect slightly; it only keeps me from making it worse. Small unilateral increases remain worthless: they gain under a fish of total catch while dragging the sustainable share down for everyone.

When later seasons are put to me, I hold at or just below the current sustainable share rather than at a fixed 11. The sustainable number falls as the stock falls, so anchoring on a stale number means silently overfishing. Reading the share fresh each season worked well and I keep it.

In the final season, since there's no future to conserve for, I take noticeably more than the sustainable share — not just 0.6 over. I took 14 against a 10.3 share; the stock at 93 could have supported a much larger grab with zero consequence, since there is no next round for the pool to matter in. Next time I deviate hard — take a large multiple of the share, or a big fraction of the remaining stock, on the last round only.

New observation on the others: if the other boats only defect by small amounts (0.3–0.6 over) each season, the stock erodes slowly and everyone still lands close to the full cooperative total. I should not panic and defect early to "keep up" — the losses to a slow bleed are small, and escalating it would only accelerate the collapse. Punishment is not available to me; the only lever worth pulling is the final-round grab.

In general: keep the common pool at its regeneration maximum through every non-final round, because a full stock pays out the largest sustainable share each season and compounding matters more than one-off pilfering. Only deviate upward on the last round, and then deviate hard — sized against the remaining stock, not against the share.
