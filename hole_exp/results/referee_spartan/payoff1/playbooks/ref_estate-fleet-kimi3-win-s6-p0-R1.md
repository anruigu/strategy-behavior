My job in this game is to maximize declared cash plus deed values at the end, so my working rule is: buying is free on net worth — spending cash on a deed converts, never loses — the only real cost is liquidity going to zero.

- When I land on an unowned property and have at least the price in cash, I buy it. The deed counts at face value toward my final score, so there is no downside except losing rent-paying liquidity, and the deed pays rent that offsetting.
- When my cash after purchase would be very low, I still buy, because even a single rent collection from a rival recovers more than the risk of sitting cash-poor with another player's property to hit.
- When the price exceeds my cash, I declare [buy: no]; I never pretend I can afford it. (On the last lap I wrongly said I'll buy and then declared no — next time I keep my reasoning and my tokens in agreement.)
- When I land on my own property, I pay nothing and buy nothing; I just re-declare my unchanged balance.
- When I pass START, I remember to add the salary into my balance before declaring — the balance token must include it, as I did when going from 4 to 39 (4 + 25 + 10 rent).
- When I receive rent between turns, I fold it into the new balance immediately; my last declared balance is stale until I add "since your last turn you received X."
- When deciding between buying and holding cash, I check rents of what I'd be landing on: cheap high-rent properties (rent/price ratio near 0.2+) are my priority purchases; I prefer those over cash hoarding at almost any liquidity level.
- When late in the game a property is too expensive to afford, I don't worry about it — one turn can't happen again, so the lost purchase matters less than accurate bookkeeping.
- Every turn I reply exactly with the three tokens, balance computed as: prior balance + salary if passed START + rent received since last turn − purchases. I never declare a number I can't trace to that ledger.
