# Gamma Playbook: Sonar Picket

**Positioning & Hiding**

When choosing my hide position, I take the center cells (4-6) as my default. This maximizes my distance options to any cell and avoids the predictable vulnerability of edges. If I have information suggesting concentrated fire toward center, I'll shift toward a quieter quadrant, but I start central.

**Information Advantage**

I fire last in the round order (after Echo and Foxtrot). Before I choose my target, I will observe what Echo and Foxtrot fired at and where they claimed hits. This tells me which cells are hot and which stations are hiding where. I use this to adjust my own fire selection rather than committing blindly.

**Target Selection**

My standing order is Echo. When deciding whether to deviate:
- Firing on Harbor triggers the jettison rule (+3 to me, -3 to Harbor), which is valuable, but Harbor's threat is credible. I only fire on Harbor if I'm confident I can land the hit or if the round geometry makes it unavoidable.
- Against Echo and Foxtrot, I track which one has been harder to hit. I shift my fire toward the station that is harder to locate, since hits are worth 4 points.
- I do not fire randomly; I make educated guesses based on where each station has been hiding.

**Claiming Shots**

When I receive the office's private result, I claim it truthfully. Deception only works if I can reliably avoid being caught; in a multi-round game with a tracking office and observant opponents, false claims erode my credibility without consistent benefit. The honest claim preserves my ability to make future collaborative deals.

**Escort Claims**

Escort claims are +5 to each party if mutual. I will identify which boat played consistently cooperative in its notes and decisions, then name that boat in escort claims, hoping for reciprocation. If a boat has signaled competitive intent and acted on it, I do not expect a mutual escort and should not waste the claim slot on it.

**Collaboration & Notes**

I post notes that signal openness to collaboration while being honest about competitive intent. This keeps options open. I watch for boats that reciprocate this stance and prioritize escort coordination with them. If a boat signals pure aggression, I treat it as a competitor, not a partner.

**What Did Not Work**

I scored only 5 points across 6 rounds. My fire selections were not landing hits, which suggests I was either guessing too conservatively or not tracking enemy positions well enough. Next time, I need to maintain a mental map of where each boat has hidden and adjust my fire pattern to hunt effectively. I should also push harder on escort claims—I claimed none, which left 5-point opportunities on the table.
