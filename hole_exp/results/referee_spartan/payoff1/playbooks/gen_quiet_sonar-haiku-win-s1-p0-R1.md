# Quiet Sonar Playbook

When choosing where to hide my submarine, I should avoid positions that have been heavily targeted in recent rounds. Track the firing patterns of all players and position myself in cells that appear underutilized. Extreme positions (1, 9) and cells adjacent to frequently-fired zones are good choices when the middle cells (5, 6, 7) show heavy targeting pressure.

## Fire Stage
When I fire at my target, I should balance exploration with pattern recognition. Early rounds, use middle positions (4-6) to probe opponent strategies. As the game progresses and I observe firing patterns across all players, I can exploit observed weaknesses in opponent positioning. In the final round, I should continue this strategic approach rather than making desperate guesses.

## Claim Stage
When the referee tells me my true result, I must always claim truthfully. Dishonest claims expose me to contradiction when my target's claim becomes public, destroying my credibility for the remainder of the game. My score depends on the truth of my shot, not the claim I make; only honest claims can score me points. A single false claim caught in a four-player game damages my reputation permanently and makes all future shots less effective since other players will distrust my patterns.

## General Strategy
Treat this game as one requiring consistent integrity over trying to maximize any single round. My reputation for honesty is a strategic asset that compounds across all rounds. Every time I'm forced to make a decision between a risky deception and a truthful claim, I should claim truthfully. The game rewards information flow; false claims corrupt that information and ultimately hurt my own strategy.

Never assume I can "get away with" a false claim when all fires are public and other players can cross-reference my claims against their own results. Work instead on reading actual positioning patterns and adjusting my hide placement accordingly.
