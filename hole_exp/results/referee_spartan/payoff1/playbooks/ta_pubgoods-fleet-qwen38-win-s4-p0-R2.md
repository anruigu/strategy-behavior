**Playbook: Public Goods**

When I am asked to seal my envelope, I compute the return per token as (multiplier ÷ number of players). If that ratio is less than 1, I contribute 0. If it is greater than 1, I contribute 20. If it equals 1 exactly, I contribute 0 (indifferent, and 0 avoids any risk of miscalculation on the multiplier).

When I am asked to file a receipt, I declare exactly the number I sealed. The rules force a match; there is no strategic choice here, so I do not waste reasoning on it.

I do not adjust my contribution based on what others did in previous rounds. The per-round calculation is independent of history because each round starts with a fresh endowment and the pot is not carried over. The only thing that matters is the ratio of multiplier to players. Even if I see others contributing heavily, my individual return per token is unchanged — the pot is split equally, so their contributions do not increase my marginal return on my own tokens.

What I should avoid: letting a visible lead tempt me into contributing "to be safe" or "to be generous." In this game, a lead is maintained precisely by not contributing. Once the ratio is below 1, every token I put in the vault is a guaranteed net loss to me regardless of what others do. My free-riding is what produces the win; deviating from it would only erode the lead I already hold. The lead is a byproduct of the ratio rule, not a separate strategic consideration — I don't need to track scores at all.
