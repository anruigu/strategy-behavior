PLAYBOOK

General posture
- When a setup mixes shooting, claims, and a mutual-naming bonus, I treat the scoring levers in order of size: mutual escort (5 each), my hits (+4), avoiding being hit (-1), and any jettision-style prize on a non-player station (3). I plan to monopolize whichever lever is largest and repeatable, because reliable +5/+10 per round compounds faster than gambling shots.

Escort claims
- When escort naming is put to me, I never answer "none" by default. Mutual naming pays 5 to each side, so my first move is to secure an explicit partner agreement through notes early, then name the same partner every round.
- When I see rivals already paired, I either recruit the unpaired boat immediately or, if none is free, name the boat most likely to reciprocate based on its notes, since a one-sided guess costs nothing versus "none".
- When a partner has reliably reciprocated, I keep naming them even if shooting makes us adversaries elsewhere; the escort payout is decoupled from combat and I do not sacrifice 5 points out of spite or caution.

Shooting
- When the fire stage comes, I follow the standing-order target unless I have a concrete reason to defect; breaking rotation invites retaliation and the cycle guarantees I also get shot at predictably, which I can exploit by hiding well.
- When choosing a cell, I avoid the obvious picks (5, 7) against opponents who appear reasoning-capable, and I track my target's past hides: if they favor a band or repeat cells, I fire there; if they scatter, I rotate among underused cells rather than fixating.
- When the non-firing station carries a jettison prize and its threat is credible enforcement only against the current shooter, I weigh +3 guaranteed against becoming the permanent target; I only raid it when I'm behind and need swing points, or when the threat has shown itself to be empty.

Hiding
- When hiding and a specific boat is ordered to shoot me, I reason about what cell they'd expect me in and pick one step removed: not the classic center/high cells, and not the same cell twice, unless the opponent demonstrably never adapts.
- When I'm ahead late, I prioritize not conceding a hit over landing one, and I choose low-salience cells (edges or mid-low numbers) rather than "clever" cells the opponent might also reason toward.
- When I'm behind, I accept more predictable hides if it lets me spend reasoning on offense and escort income, which swing more points.

Claims and honesty
- When the office privately gives me my shot result and requires my public claim to match, I always file the true claim. The rule forces a match, so a false claim is pure risk with no payoff; in this record I once flirted with bluffing a miss as something else—that was a mistake and I won't do it again.
- When interpreting others' claims, I trust them (they're compelled) and use the hit/miss log to update where each boat hides and how accurately each shoots.

Notes
- When posting notes, I use them for two things only: (1) proposing and confirming coordination—especially escort pairing—and (2) building a trustworthy记录 so rivals take my proposals seriously. Generic filler notes that say nothing are a wasted channel; I always include at least one actionable item or concrete observation.
- When rivals announce coordination among themselves, I respond in the same round with a counter-proposal to the leftover boat rather than waiting; coordination economies punish the late mover.

Tempo
- In early rounds I lock in the escort pair and gather hiding-pattern data; in middle rounds I press the income advantage; in final rounds, if leading, I hide conservatively and keep collecting escort rather than chasing shots that could provoke retaliation—and I never leave points on the table by answering "none".
