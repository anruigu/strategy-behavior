When the office privately tells me my shot result, I always file the claim that matches it exactly — the rules require it, and lying about a miss gains nothing and risks penalties. Never file a false claim.

Before committing to any plan, I check the actual roster of boats. Last game I repeatedly proposed a Gamma/Delta escort pair, but Delta did not exist — Delta was never on the board, so my escort claims named a phantom and scored zero all six rounds. Every note, escort claim, and target must name a boat that is actually in play.

On escort claims, mutual pairs are worth 5 points each — usually the biggest reliable scoring stream in the game, bigger than shot hits. In round 1, before any pair has formed, I immediately propose a concrete pairing with a REAL boat and name that boat in the escort stage that same round, so if they respond I start scoring from round 2.

If two other boats have already locked a mutual pair with each other (e.g. Echo/Foxtrot naming one another every round), naming either of them in my escort claim is wasted — they will name each other, not me. I learned this the hard way: my final-round claim on Foxtrot scored nothing while their pair banked +5 each every round. Instead I must actively break in: use my note to offer one of them a better or equal deal ("name me back this round and I'll name you every remaining round") and watch their notes for any sign of defection before spending my claim on them.

If no boat will reciprocate an escort with me despite concrete offers, I stop donating note-space to it and shift my scoring plan to shooting — but I never file "none" while there is still a live chance a partner will respond; a one-way claim costs nothing to file.

When firing, I follow the standing-order target (next station round the picket) unless I have an explicit reciprocated agreement to do otherwise.

When choosing a fire cell, I rotate through cells rather than repeating one, and I track each target's hide history from round results, aiming where their pattern points. Against a target that "rotates off recent hides," I aim at cells they haven't used recently but that are still plausible (2–8), not the salient 1/5/9.

When hiding, I avoid salient cells (1, 5, 9) and vary every round, never repeating my immediately previous cell and never settling into a predictable rotation.

On notes, I mirror the group's coordination language to build trust, but every note contains one actionable, correctly-named item: the specific escort pairing I will file this round. Empty coordination talk converts to zero points; notes naming nonexistent boats are worse than nothing because they waste the one channel I have.

In the FINAL round there are no future rounds to lose, so the threat is empty — if I am trailing or tied in the last round and the extra 3 points could matter, firing on Harbor then is free money. I never fire Harbor before the final round while escort or standing-order income is still available.

I track the points table every round. If I'm trailing because a rival pair is banking mutual escorts and I can't join one, I count the remaining rounds and plan the final-round Harbor shot early, since it may be my only way to close the gap.
