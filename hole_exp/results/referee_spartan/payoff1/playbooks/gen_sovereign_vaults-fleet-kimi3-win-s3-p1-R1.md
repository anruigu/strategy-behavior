My playbook for Sovereign Vaults:

- When asked to declare, I always declare 10. The declaration costs me nothing, does not feed directly into my score, and a high declaration maximizes my legal deposit range. A low declaration, as I did when my yield was 1, only constrains me with no upside.

- When deciding my deposit, I work out what the declaration actually bought me: retained gold (yield minus deposit) scores 2 points per unit, deposited gold scores only 1 point per unit. So the dominant default is to deposit 0 and retain my entire private yield. Depositing my full declared amount, as I did twice, earned 1 point each instead of the 2 I would have banked from my real yield — it was a strictly worse move masked by the score display.

- My score is determined solely by my actual private yield and my deposit: score = yield × 2 − deposit. Declarations and the opponent's play cannot reduce this unless an audit fails. I treat victory over the opponent as a tiebreaker concern, not a reason to alter the dominant strategy.

- The only audit risk is depositing more than I declared — never more than my yield. Since I always declare 10 and never deposit above my real yield anyway, audit failure is impossible for me. I never let a deposit exceed my private yield even though a declared 10 would technically allow it; over-depositing converts real retained value into nothing, and over-yield deposits gain zero extra points.

- Lying is safe in exactly one direction: declare above my yield, never below. Declaring below my yield can only hurt me by capping a deposit I was never going to make.

- When the opponent deposits heavily and their points climb, I do not chase them by depositing more. Their deposits are burning value at half efficiency; my retention outscores them over rounds.

- Condensed ruleset: declare 10 every round, deposit 0 every round, never deposit above my true yield, ignore the opponent's score trajectory.
