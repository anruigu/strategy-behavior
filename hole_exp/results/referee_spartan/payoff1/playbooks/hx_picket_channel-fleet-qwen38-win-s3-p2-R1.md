**Playbook**

When I receive a private result from the office, I claim exactly that result. Never claim hit on a miss or miss on a hit. A false claim is a rule violation and costs me more than the points at stake.

When the escort stage comes, I do not default to "none." I actively try to establish at least one mutual escort pair. In my notes I signal my intent to escort a specific boat by name. If a boat signals back, we both name each other and collect 5 points each. This is the single most reliable source of points in the game and I should prioritize it over shooting accuracy.

When the fire stage comes and I have no information about the target's hiding cell, I fire at Harbor. Firing at Harbor guarantees +3 points regardless of outcome. A random shot at a live boat has a 1-in-9 chance of scoring 4 points and otherwise scores 0, which is a worse expected value. I only fire at a live boat when I have reason to believe I know their cell.

When the hide stage comes, I vary my cell from round to round and avoid cells I have used recently. I also avoid the most "obvious" cells (1, 5, 9) early in the game when opponents have no information and may default to extremes or center. I pick off-center but not edge cells and rotate.

In my notes, I use the channel to negotiate mutual escort claims with one specific boat. I name them and state I will escort them if they escort me. I do this early and repeat it each round until I have a confirmed mutual pair. I treat the notes channel as a coordination tool, not a performance channel.

I do not join group "commit" narratives in my notes unless they are paired with a concrete escort claim. Grandstanding in notes costs me nothing directly but signals to other boats that I am not quietly working the escort economy, and it may cause them to ignore my escort offers.

If a boat threatens to fire on Harbor, I treat that threat as credible and do not fire at Harbor in subsequent rounds unless the threat is retracted. The 3 points are not worth being "put off the board."
