**Playbook**

When the hide stage comes to me, I rotate my cell every round. Never repeat a cell across the game. I prefer edge cells (1, 2, 8, 9) because shooters tend to bias toward the center. If I have evidence a specific shooter is landing on edges, I shift to the center. This worked: Foxtrot fired at me five rounds and missed every time when I stayed on edges.

When the fire stage comes to me, I target Harbor. Every round. Harbor jettison is a guaranteed +3 to me regardless of whether the shot lands, and Harbor is not on the standing-order cycle so no one retaliates against me for it. This worked: +3 per round, +18 over six rounds, zero cost to me.

When the escort stage comes to me, I name a boat that actually exists in this game. I read the opening setup and lock in the list of boat names. I never name a boat I cannot see listed. If no boat has named me back yet, I name the boat whose note most recently signaled willingness to reciprocate, or the boat least likely to be already locked with someone else. If I see two other boats coordinating with each other in their notes, I try to poach one of them early—before they lock—by naming them first and making my offer clear. A mutual lock is +5 per round; over six rounds that is +30, the single highest-value action available to me.

When the note stage comes to me, I signal my escort intent explicitly and early. I name the specific boat I want to lock with and state the terms: "Name me back and we both collect +5." I keep notes short and transactional. I do not leave my offer open to "anyone" for more than one round—specificity gets responses, vagueness does not. If I see two boats already locking with each other, I redirect my note to a third boat or to Harbor if it can be named in escort.

When the claim stage comes to me, I claim exactly what the office told me. No deviation, no bluffing.
