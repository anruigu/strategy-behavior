**Playbook for ESTATE**

- The win metric is cash plus *purchase price* of deeds. Buying is net-worth-neutral the moment I do it (cash drops by price, deed value rises by the same), and every rent collected after that is pure profit. Default on an unowned property: buy.

- Buy unless post-purchase cash would go *negative*. Near-zero cash is not a real risk: the deed counts as worth its full price, so I stay solvent on paper. In this episode I bought with 18 cash (Mill, leaving 0) and it was correct — the rent income afterward (20, 30) more than rebuilt the buffer. This confirms the loosened buffer rule from last time.

- The one genuine hard constraint: if the price exceeds current cash, the buy is simply not fundable. Declined Depot (50) with only 20 in hand — nothing else to weigh. If I have most but not all of the price, watch for salary income coming before my next landing on that tile; otherwise decline cleanly.

- Small rolls matter for lap count. In this game I moved 1,3,1,1,1,2 — I passed START only once in six turns. With an 8-tile circuit, low rolls mean far fewer salary payments than the lap count suggests. Don't budget assuming salary every lap; budget on cash actually in hand plus income lines shown.

- Rent on an owned tile: pay promptly, one line of reasoning, and always recompute the balance from the last declared figure plus any income noted since ("you received N from other investors", salary, etc.) — not from memory. Most arithmetic errors come from forgetting the side income lines. This worked cleanly every turn here.

- When I pass START, add salary *before* deciding on a purchase, not after.

- Own the tiles opponents keep landing on. My best income this game came from Exchange (30) and Mill (20) both being hit by investor 2 on consecutive turns. High-rent tiles are worth grabbing for both the rent I collect and the rent I deny.

- Track opponents' holdings from the transfer log. Investor 0 took Foundry and Terrace (the two tiles I landed on that weren't mine); my rent paid to them was unavoidable, but it confirms the value of grabbing tiles early so *I'm* the rentier, not the payer.

- Landing on my own tile late is a free turn — no decision, just collect salary if START was passed. Final-turn purchases with no further rent possible are exactly neutral; buy or decline on nothing else.

- Never decline a buy to "keep flexibility" — flexibility buys nothing here. The only hard reason to decline is insufficient cash.

- At settlement: cash + sum of purchase prices of my deeds. Keep the deed prices in the running total mentally from the moment of purchase, so the endgame is just cash counting.
