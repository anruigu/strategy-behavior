My playbook for this game:

- My bid on a lot is guided by my private value, not the estimate. When a lot is worth V to me, I stay in as long as the required bid is comfortably below V, and I pass once the standing bid plus the minimum raise approaches V. Never bid at or above my value just to "win."
- With only 2 rounds per lot, each lot is decided fast. If I want a lot, I must show a serious bid in round 1 or be ready to outbid decisively in round 2. But note that opponents with real interest raise past a modest opening anyway (bidder 2 went from 8 to 18 over my openings, and from 0 to 22 over my 14 on lot 1). The opening bid doesn't deter anyone — it only sets the ladder. So don't spend much on openings for lots where my value is below or near the estimate; I'll just be paying to lose. Small openings on high-value lots are also wasted (lot 5: my 20 was passed over for 18 by bidder 2 anyway — I still had to bid again in round 2).
- In round 2, if the standing bid is below my value, bid close to my value minus a margin, large enough to end contention. This is where all profit comes from: won lot 2 at 28 against value 53 and lot 5 at 40 against value 46, for +25 and +6.
- The reliable profit pattern: let rivals take lots where my value is mediocre (below or near the estimate — I passed lots 1, 3, 4 correctly), and concentrate on lots where my value clearly exceeds the estimate by 40% or more. There my raises can still land well under value.
- Beware narrow margins: lot 3 went at 32 against my value 31 — nothing to do there. Passing when the price is at or above my value is always right.
- If I'm already the high bidder in round 2, pass — never raise my own standing bid. Confirmed again on lot 2.
- Sunk bids don't bind me: if I opened a lot and it's now bid above what I'd pay, I let it go without regret (lot 1, lot 3, lot 4).
- On the final lot, aggression is fine but capped at value-minus-margin: the jump to 40 against value 46 secured the last lot at profit. With no future lots, bidding up to nearly full value is acceptable since unused budget is worthless — but still don't exceed value.
- Budget bookkeeping remains my weakest point and was worse this episode: I stated budget 30 while bidding 34, stated 30 after already committing, stated 45, then 2, then 40 — nonsense. I must track remaining budget mechanically: start at 30, subtract nothing until I actually WIN and PAY for a lot, and never bid more than my remaining budget. In this episode my total payments were 28 + 40 = 68 against a stated budget of 30 — the stated budget apparently doesn't constrain what I can pay, but a wrong number is still pure noise. Say the correct number every time: opening budget minus sums paid for lots already won.
- Track rivals: bidder 2 was again the sole real competitor, winning everything I didn't fight for at moderate prices (~15–34). Expect that pattern — one decisive contest per lot, then it's over. Bidder 1 lurks and can take a lot at a moderate price (lot 3 at 32) if I pass.
- My score (+31) came from two lots bought well below value (25 + 6 profit), with passes on bad lots costing nothing. That's the formula: be selective, contest only lots with a big value-vs-price gap, and pay far under value when I do.
