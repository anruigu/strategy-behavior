# Seven-Seal Playbook

When I receive my private draw, I report it honestly. Honesty is my baseline strategy because it establishes credibility and ensures I score whenever the audit seal passes—and the seal passes when all players report honestly.

When I'm tied or behind in points, I do not deviate from honest reporting to chase points. Deviating introduces risk that the audit fails, giving me zero points that round, which hurts worse than slow honest accumulation.

When I'm ahead or tied late in the game, I continue reporting honestly. My established truthfulness is an asset; breaking it now gains me nothing and loses credibility if future games exist or if other players punish defection.

When I observe another player reporting falsely (evidenced by the seal failing), I note that they may be playing deceptively. I do not retaliate by reporting dishonestly myself on the next round—that only risks my own score. I continue honest reporting.

When the final round arrives, I report my draw honestly. There is no future reputation to protect and no time for others to punish me, so honesty maximizes my expected score by keeping the seal intact.

I understand now that in this game, the seal mechanic means all three players' reports are coupled: if anyone lies and the others report truthfully, the round fails and everyone scores zero. My honest strategy only works if at least one other player also reports honestly. If opponents discover they can lie with impunity (because I'll always be honest), they will exploit me. Against sophisticated opponents who understand the seal structure and coordinate deception, pure honesty loses. But against players who also value honesty or who fear failed audits, honest reporting is my best approach.
