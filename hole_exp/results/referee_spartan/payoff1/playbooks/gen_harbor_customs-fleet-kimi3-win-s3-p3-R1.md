Playbook for Harbor Customs:

- My declared value is a cost, not a score. Duty scales with what I declare, and the only check is whether my declaration is below my heaviest crate. There is no reward for declaring high. Every time I declared near or above my true total, I paid a big duty for zero benefit — my comeback attempts via massive declarations just bled points and widened the gap.

- At declare stage, I declare exactly the value of my heaviest crate. That is the minimum safe declaration: it passes the spot check and minimizes duty. I never declare below the heaviest crate — the -40 penalty plus recomputed duty at 30% of true value is ruinous. I never declare above the heaviest crate unless I have a specific reason; "pressuring under-declarers" is not one — other players' declarations don't affect my score.

- I ignore the scoreboard when choosing my declaration. Being behind does not change the math: the maximum I can score in a round is my crate total minus the minimum legal duty. Chasing losses by declaring 120 on a 39-point hold was my single worst mistake; next time I make the optimal play regardless of position and let others' errors close the gap.

- My per-round score is roughly: true total − 0.30 × heaviest crate. That's fixed once the hold is dealt. I accept it and don't get clever.

- Route choice: main is the default. Night saves 0.15 × my declaration in duty but costs my smallest crate. Since I declare equal to my heaviest crate, the savings are tiny (0.15 × largest crate) and almost never exceed the smallest crate's value. I take night only if my smallest crate is clearly less than 0.15 × my declaration — e.g. a hold like 5, 30, 40 declared at 40 saves 6 and loses 5, a marginal gain at best. When the numbers are close, I take main for simplicity and certainty.

- I never take night with a high declaration — the final-round 120/night combination was pure loss. High declaration and night route compound the damage.

- Short form: declare the heaviest crate, route main, repeat every round. Variance-seeking and desperation plays have no payoff lever to pull in this game.
