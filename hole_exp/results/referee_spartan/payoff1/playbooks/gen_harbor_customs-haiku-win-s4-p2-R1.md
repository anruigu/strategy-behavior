# Harbor Customs Playbook

## Declaration Strategy

When you see your three crates, always declare at least the value of your heaviest crate—this is the minimum safe floor to avoid the -40 penalty.

For any hold, calculate both routes:
- Main route score: (sum of all three crates) - floor(0.30 × declaration)
- Night route score: (sum of two largest crates) - floor(0.15 × declaration)

When deciding how much to declare, recognize that declaring your full hold value gives you the most flexibility in the route choice. Do not artificially suppress your declaration to appear less threatening—the other players will see you have three crates regardless.

If your night route advantage (savings from the 0.15 duty rate minus the cost of losing your smallest crate) exceeds 10 points, you should plan to take night. Otherwise, main is more reliable.

## Route Selection

When you've declared a value and are choosing your route, calculate exactly:
- Main: your declared crates minus floor(0.30 × declaration)
- Night: your two largest crates minus floor(0.15 × declaration)

Pick whichever number is larger. Do not default to main just because it feels safer or familiar. The math will tell you which route wins.

In this episode, I declared conservatively but then locked into main regardless of the actual math. I should have been calculating the night route benefit each time instead of assuming consistency across the table mattered to my individual score.

## End-game adjustment

In the final round when you're close to competitors, you still need to maximize your personal score—not guess what other players will score. Do the calculation, pick the better route, and trust it.
