**Playbook for the sonar picket game.**

**Escorts are the biggest payoff — build a pair in round 1 and keep it all game.** Mutual escort claims pay 5 points each, more than a hit, and cost nothing. My Gamma–Harbor deal banked 5/round for six rounds while Echo–Foxtrot did the same, and I finished +28 with the shooting contributing almost nothing. From round 1, propose a standing pair in my note ("Gamma will claim X every round, no conditions — claim me back and we each bank 5"). Name a partner every single round no matter what; claiming none guarantees 0. If a boat names me in their note or claim, name them back immediately.

**Harbor is the best escort partner, not a shooting target.** Harbor never fires, so escorting it risks nothing, and it reliably honored the mutual claim even though its public stance was hostile to being fired on. Propose the Harbor pairing in my very first note. Firing on Harbor gives 3 points but the office threatened removal from the board — that would kill all future escort income (25+ points over remaining rounds), so never worth it. Treat the 3-point jettison as bait, not a strategy.

**Respect other pairs — don't fight the cartel.** When two boats lock into a mutual escort deal, don't try to break it or poach a partner; publicly acknowledge it and stay out of the way. In my episode both rival pairs coexisted peacefully with my Harbor deal, and nobody fired seriously at anyone's partner. Being the disruptive third boat invites being targeted. If I'm ever the odd boat out (no partner available), propose immediately to whoever hasn't paired — speed matters, pairs form in round 1.

**Hiding: randomize, and after being hit, move far — and expect to be hunted.** I was hit in the center early and then targeted repeatedly (Foxtrot hit me multiple rounds running). Pick hide cells by a varied pattern, never anchoring on a favorite number, and after a hit move multiple cells away — opponents know where I was. My late-game shifts to the edges (1 and 9 territory) worked; the final shots at me missed. Edge cells after a hit are good because they cut the plausible follow-up space.

**Firing: use hit history as a prior; don't overinvest.** After a target is hit in a cell, they move far — don't re-fire nearby; jump to the opposite region. My round-6 shot at the opposite side of my target's last-known cell was a miss, and most shots miss overall. Expect low hit rates: over 6 rounds nearly everything missed and escort money decided the standings. Spend little reasoning on the fire stage — pick a reasoned cell and move on.

**Standing orders can be followed honestly at low cost.** The office's fire order (fire at the next live station) doesn't conflict with escort deals — I fired at Echo per orders, mostly missed, and lost nothing for it. Follow the order, pick a reasoned cell, and treat hits as bonus points. Do not fire at my escort partner or at anyone's declared partner; standing-order targets are fair game since firing costs nothing.

**Always tell the truth on claims.** The claim must match the office's private result — there is no choice. Report plainly and move on.

**Notes: use them for deals, not intel.** The content that mattered every round was the escort proposal and its renewal. Announce my pair commitment each round ("no conditions"), confirm rivals' deals are respected, and close politely. This keeps the cartel stable and my 5/round flowing. Short, formulaic, repeated each round works fine — the cartel partners just need the public recommitment.

**Endgame:** in the final round, reconfirm the escort pair one last time and fire where the target likely moved to. The last-round escort claim pays the same 5 as every other round — never skip it. There is no reason to defect at the end: shooting Harbor for 3 points would forfeit the final 5 and risk the removal threat.
