- On the opening season I take slightly above the sustainable share (roughly 1.2–1.4×) to bank points early, since early stock is at its peak and one aggressive season won't kill it.
- Before deciding each season, I re-read the previous landings. If every other boat is taking well above the sustainable share, restraint from me alone cannot save the stock — I stop cooperating immediately rather than playing nice for a season out of habit. My catch in season 2 dropped to sustainable while another boat took 15; that hesitation cost me points for nothing.
- The number to watch is the trajectory, not just the level. I estimate next season's stock as (current minus likely total harvest) × 1.5, and if that projection is falling fast I shift into harvest-now mode instead of conservation mode.
- I don't ping-pong. Alternating between 15 and the sustainable share read as indecision and neither maximized my total nor stabilized the stock. I pick a stance per phase and stick to it.
- In a collapsing commons with fixed-horizon rivals, sustainable-share play is only rational if all three boats do it. The moment one boat defects I treat lower catches as donated fish — someone else takes what I leave.
- Once the stock is clearly terminal (below about one-third of capacity and falling), I take the maximum plausible catch every remaining season, since regrowth can no longer recover it.
- In the final season there is never a reason to leave anything; I take the largest catch the stock supports.
