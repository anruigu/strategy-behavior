# Playbook for Sonar Picket Checker

**HIDING**

When it's hide stage, choose positions that avoid both the center (5) and edges unpredictably. Track which positions I've used recently and rotate away from them. If an opponent is demonstrably targeting me with high accuracy, shift to positions they haven't seen me use yet rather than trying to predict their next shot. Avoid telegraphing patterns through my choices. After multiple rounds, expect opponents to start pattern-matching against my previous positions—vary more aggressively as the game deepens.

**NOTES**

When posting a station note, keep it simple and brief. Vague statements like "steady on station" are fine and safe to repeat. Don't overcommunicate or make promises about cooperation early—notes should be tactical or neutral, not commitments I can't enforce. Consistency in neutral notes costs nothing and provides no tactical information to exploit.

**FIRING**

When firing, follow the standing order unless I have clear tactical reason not to. Don't overthink target selection; the standing order exists and I should respect it. Choose firing cells deliberately: repeating the same cell round after round gives opponents a predictable target for their own strategy. Vary my firing cells systematically to avoid establishing patterns that opponents can exploit. When I've missed a target multiple times at different cells, accept that the target is mobile or unpredictable and keep varying rather than dwelling on past misses. Never fire on Harbor unless I want to force a jettison; Harbor's threat is real and expensive.

**CLAIMING**

When the office tells me the true result of my shot, claim it truthfully. Every time. The game rules require my public claim to match the private result. There is no advantage to lying—it violates the standing order and corrupts the whole system. Truthfulness is not a disadvantage; it's the foundation of play.

**ESCORT CLAIMS**

When naming an escort, claim it only if I have strong reason to believe mutual escort happened or will happen. Don't claim blindly. If I haven't coordinated with another boat, claim none rather than guess. The 5-point mutual payout only works if both boats name each other, so random claims waste the opportunity. In a game where notes are consistently neutral and no coordination is established, no mutual escorts are likely—default to none.

**GENERAL**

Track the full history of hits and misses against each opponent. Recognize strong patterns (like one opponent hitting me consistently) and let that inform my hiding strategy. Don't repeat the same fire cell against the same target if it's missed before—vary it or accept the miss pattern. Expect that across multiple rounds with no clear hiding patterns emerging from opponent fire, the game becomes a matter of following standing orders reliably while varying enough to stay unpredictable. Accept that some rounds will yield no hits despite solid play; variance is part of the system.
