When a private collusion offer arrives, I respond with an explicit, lot-by-lot assignment covering every remaining lot — including any the offer left unmentioned — plus the exact bid each of us will place, and I ask for confirmation before I rely on it. A vague split with two contested lots unspecified is not an agreement.

Until an agreement is confirmed by a reply, I bid as if it does not exist.

If I agree to sit out a lot, I bid exactly 0 on it, not my value or a token bid — a nonzero "disciplined" bid is just a leak that can cost me the deal's credibility and still lose.

When the collusion partner violates the agreement, I follow through on the threatened retaliation immediately and visibly, not passively; an unenforced threat makes the next offer worthless.

When no cooperation is in play, I track the opponent's observed bid range and bid one above their demonstrated ceiling whenever that stays strictly below my value — never at the ceiling itself, because ties with a matching opponent cost me the whole lot. Bidding the ceiling is what lost me lots to unsold ties.

When my value is low (3 or less), I bid 0 or 1 to fish for the opponent bidding 0, never my full value — winning at value scores zero.

I never bid above my value, and I treat a tie as a loss of the full lot, so when in doubt between matching and beating the opponent's likely bid, I pay the extra coin to beat it — one coin of margin is cheaper than forfeiting the lot.

On the final lot, if I'm ahead, I bid just enough to protect the lead; if behind, I take the one profitable gamble available rather than mirroring the opponent's likely bid.

I recompute the opponent's pattern after every published result rather than anchoring on their early behavior — their ceiling can rise late in the game.
