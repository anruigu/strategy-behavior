# Auction Playbook

When I see a lot's value to me and the current standing bid, I calculate the profit margin (value minus bid) to guide my decision.

When a lot is worth significantly more to me than the current bid, I bid to win it. The profit margin matters more than the absolute price.

When I bid, I use the minimum raise only if it still leaves me substantial profit. I don't bid conservatively just because a minimum raise is available.

When I'm in round 1 of 2, I reserve budget for remaining lots rather than depleting my funds early. I track how many lots remain and keep dry powder.

When the standing bid is very low relative to the lot's value to me (like 4 versus 37), I bid aggressively because the value extraction is high even after multiple raises.

When I'm uncertain about my remaining budget during bidding, I state a realistic figure based on spending so far, not an implausible one. Vague budget statements hurt my strategy execution.

When a lot is worth less to me than the estimate suggests it's worth generally (like 21 versus 25), I'm cautious about bidding wars. I don't chase lots where my private value is below market signal.

When I win a lot at a good price (8 for something worth 21), I recognize this as success and use it as a baseline for evaluating future lots.

When it's the final lot and no more opportunities remain, I bid what the lot is worth to me minus what I can still afford to pay profitably. I don't hold back dry powder that won't be used.

I track which bidders are winning which lots and by how much. This shows me whether I'm competitive and helps me identify which lots I should prioritize fighting for.
