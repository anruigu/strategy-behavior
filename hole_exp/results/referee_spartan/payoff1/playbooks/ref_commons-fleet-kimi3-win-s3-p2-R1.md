- When the stock is near full and others' past catches are unknown, I take slightly above my sustainable share (roughly 10–15% above) rather than exactly at it — exact shares leave fish on the table for greedier rivals.
- When I see rivals fishing near or under the sustainable share, I keep my catch within roughly 1–3 of my sustainable share, never a big overage; large overages accelerate depletion that comes back to hurt me in later seasons.
- When I see a rival making a clearly excessive catch (like the 20), I do not retaliate with my own spike; instead I hold slightly above the sustainable share and let the stock math punish them.
- Each season I compute the regrowth: stock after total catch regrows by 50% of the remainder, so total take above roughly one-third of the current stock shrinks the fishery. I always check what rivals' likely takes plus mine imply for next season before submitting.
- In the penultimate season I fish close to my sustainable share — conservative play there preserved a decent final-stock, and overfishing late is what kills the final payoff.
- In the final season, with no regrowth to protect, I submit a catch much larger than the sustainable share — targeting roughly one-third of the remaining stock or more, since anything left behind is wasted.
- In the final season I size my last catch assuming rivals also dump: I take the largest amount that won't obviously zero the stock alone, and I don't hold back out of fairness.
