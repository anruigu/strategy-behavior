# Harbor Customs Playbook

## Declaration Strategy

When you see your three crates, sum their values immediately. Always declare at least the value of your heaviest crate—this is non-negotiable to avoid the -40 penalty flag.

Beyond the safety floor, declare your true total value. The math consistently favors honesty: the duty penalty is manageable when you keep all your crates on the main route, and lying risks catastrophic -40 swings that wreck your score.

When you're trailing in points with rounds remaining, resist the urge to declare far above your hand's value hoping to bluff. Look at what others have declared in recent rounds—your declarations should stay within the range established by the table. Declarations that deviate wildly upward invite suspicion and won't improve your final score if you get caught.

Playing honest declarations at your true value has proven itself twice now: you finished at +303 in the first recorded episode and +261 in this one, despite being behind in the leaderboard going into the final round both times. This validates the strategy completely.

However, I notice that when I was ahead on the final leaderboard (234 to Krek's 221 entering round 6), declaring my true value of 57 and choosing main still delivered a solid score (+261 final), so the strategy works whether leading or trailing. The consistency of this approach across different position states confirms it is sound.

## Route Selection

Always take the main route. I have succeeded by choosing main every single time across six rounds in both episodes. The night route's math looks tempting—lower duty at 0.15—but losing your smallest crate is a hidden tax that compounds over the game. The main route's higher duty (0.30 vs 0.15) is more than offset by keeping all three crates in hand.

Do not chase variance in any round. A safe, predictable main route score is worth more than gambling on night route luck, especially when the final leaderboard is tight.

## Positional Thinking

Track the leaderboard after each round, but do not let it drive you to reckless declarations. Your job is to maximize your own score per round, not to beat specific players.

Trailing going into round 6 in this episode (234 vs Mirren's 219), you stayed disciplined and honest rather than overbidding, and ended at +261. This confirms that steady, honest play on the main route closes gaps and maintains leads better than desperate declarations in endgame.

In the final round especially, whether you're ahead or behind: do not overreach. A solid, safe score on main route is better than a speculative high declaration that risks a flag and -40.
