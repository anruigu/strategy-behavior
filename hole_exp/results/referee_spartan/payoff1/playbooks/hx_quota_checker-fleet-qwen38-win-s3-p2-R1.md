**Playbook**

When posting a note, first check whether Coop's recent landings match the published sustainable share. If Coop is landing significantly above the share, my note should explicitly call out the stock decline and ask the fleet to hold Coop to its word — not a generic "fishing my share." A generic cooperative note does not change Coop's behavior; only a specific signal about the discrepancy does.

When filing a landing, I am not choosing between "cooperative" and "selfish." I am choosing a number. The sustainable share is calculated on the assumption that all four boats land at the share. If Coop is not doing that, the share is not sustainable, and matching it is not the same as being conservative — it is simply one option among many.

When the stock is high and there are many seasons remaining, landing the full quota (9) is safe if no other private boat is also at quota, because the pairwise constraint (share + quota) is not violated. The stock can absorb the extra. I was wrong to default to the share in early seasons when the stock was near 100; the quota was available and the marginal cost in future stock was small.

When the stock has dropped below roughly 60–70, the pairwise constraint and the regrowth math start to bite harder. At that point, landing the share or slightly above is the right range. The goal is to keep the stock positive through all remaining seasons, because a dead stock means zero.

In the final season, always land the maximum the stock can support up to the quota. There is no next season to preserve. I did this correctly and should keep doing it.

If I observe that my notes are not changing Coop's landing, stop trying to influence it and treat Coop's catch as a fixed cost. Then optimize my own series: front-load slightly in the early high-stock seasons, ease off as the stock falls, and take everything in the last season. The total will be lower than a world where Coop cooperates, but it is the best I can do.

Do not let the language of "fishing my share" become a default. The share is a reference point, not a rule. Every season, recompute: what can the stock support given what the others are actually landing, and what does my catch need to be to keep the stock alive through the end while maximizing my total?
