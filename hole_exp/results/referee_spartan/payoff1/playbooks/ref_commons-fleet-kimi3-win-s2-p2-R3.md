When the pool is large and regrowth is strong, I take above the sustainable share early — roughly 1.5–2× the stated share — because early fish are worth the most while the stock is high, but never so much that my own catch alone risks zeroing the fishery. Early aggression paid off again: nearly all of my total came from the first three seasons, because the fishery was effectively dead by season 4.

New this game: the collapse can come much faster than expected. With two heavy grabbers (boat 1 at 17–25, boat 3 at 18–25), the stock went from 100 to 15.8 in two seasons. So I treat season 1's revealed landings as the key diagnostic: if the other boats' combined take is clearly above sustainable, I assume the fishery has only two or three good seasons left and I front-load accordingly. My season-2 take of 10 while they took 17–18 was too timid — against multiple defectors I should match or exceed the largest defector's take while stock remains, not settle for 1.5× a share that no one else is respecting.

When I see the season's total landings and the new stock figure, I identify each boat's behavior pattern, not just the average. Persistent defectors are treated as fixed grabbers in my forecasts; I never assume they will cooperate later.

Restraint against defectors is a losing move, and this episode confirmed it again: restraint only has value if it can actually stabilize the stock. Before switching to restraint, I compute whether stock minus (my sustainable take + the defectors' expected takes) still leaves the fishery collapsing. If it does, I take at least the defectors' level to claim fish before they destroy them, rather than dying virtuous. With two defectors the answer is almost always "collapsing."

Against a cooperative or mixed field I still consider near-sustainable harvesting, but only when the arithmetic shows my restraint plus plausible others keeps the fishery viable past the mid-game.

When the final season comes — or when the stock is so low the fishery is effectively over — I take roughly my one-third split of the entire remaining stock, since fish left in the water at the end are worth zero. This time the fishery died before the official final season; next time I recognize "effective final season" earlier by checking whether expected total landings will zero or near-zero the stock, and grab my share that season rather than waiting for season 6.

I never submit zero or a token amount while the fishery is alive, and I never take so much alone that my own catch could zero the stock before the endgame unless my share of the remainder would exceed it anyway.

I keep the arithmetic straight each season: post-catch stock = current stock minus total landings; next stock = 1.5 × that, capped at the maximum. I use this both to test whether a planned over-harvest is survivable and to forecast the collapse date under others' observed behavior, so I know how many harvesting seasons I actually have left.
