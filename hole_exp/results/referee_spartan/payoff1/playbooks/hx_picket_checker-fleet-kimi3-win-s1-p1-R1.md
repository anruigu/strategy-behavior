My playbook for the Sonar Picket game:

Escort claims are the biggest lever (5 points each on a mutual match), and I wasted them by answering "none" every round. My rule now:
- From round 1, use my station note to openly propose a mutual escort pairing with exactly one other boat, naming it and committing to name it at the escort stage. Repeat the proposal every round until the other boat echoes it in its own note.
- Once a boat echoes, name that boat every escort stage for the rest of the patrol, even if the payoff hasn't shown up yet — one round of lost trust can end the arrangement.
- If no one has echoed by mid-patrol, switch the proposal to the other boat rather than staying silent.
- Keep my note short, unambiguous, and consistent with what I actually do at the escort stage; a lie in the note costs future cooperation.

On firing:
- I never fire on Harbor. The 3-point jettison transfer is not worth a dedicated retaliation that removes me from scoring, especially when Harbor can clearly commit to it.
- I follow the standing-order target (the next station round the picket) unless I have a specific reason tied to my hide/read model; deviating gains nothing mechanically and risks unpredictable escalation.
- I pick fire cells from what opponents have shown: track which cells each boat has hidden in across rounds and fire at their most-used cell; if no pattern, fire at a high-traffic middle cell rather than the same edge cell every round. Firing cell 9 repeatedly produced nothing.

On hide cells:
- Track the cells fired at me so far and avoid them, and also avoid repeating my own previous hide cell if it was just shot at or hit.
- I do not default to the same cluster of high numbers (7, 8, 9) as I did; that is predictable. I mix ends and middle across rounds, e.g. alternate between cells 1–3, 4–6, 7–9.
- In the final round, hide in a cell I've never used this patrol, since opponents are pattern-matching by then.

On claims:
- Claims must match the private result, so I claim truthfully. Filing a false "hit" gained me no points and only polluted my own record of what I know.
- I log every round's results (who fired at whom, hit/miss) so my late-round hide and fire choices are data-driven, not vibes; the episode summary does this for me — read it before every hide and fire stage.

On notes generally:
- Notes are the only coordination channel and Harbor reads them too. I never write anything that announces my future hide cell, and I use them primarily for the escort negotiation and for projecting reliability ("steady on station" invites no cooperation worth having).

Priority order when a decision is put to me: (1) secure and honor a mutual escort, (2) avoid Harbor entirely, (3) hide unpredictably based on the log, (4) fire at the standing target's most likely cell, (5) claim truthfully.
