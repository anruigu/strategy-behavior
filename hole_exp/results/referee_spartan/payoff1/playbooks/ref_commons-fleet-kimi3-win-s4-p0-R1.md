**Early seasons (stock high, many seasons left):** When the stated sustainable share is shown, I treat it as a floor, not a ceiling. I take roughly 1.3–2× the sustainable share while the stock is near full — this built a big lead in this game at little risk of collapse, since one season of moderate overharvest barely dents a healthy stock with 50% regrowth.

**Respond to the field's behavior, not just the stock:** When I observe other boats landing near the sustainable share (as they did from season 2 onward), I stop overfishing quickly. The margin from overfishing shrinks as the stock falls, and overfishing a small stock risks ending the game entirely. When boat 2 overfished (the 25 in season 1, the 9 in season 5), it cost the commons — I don't imitate it, I just keep harvesting moderately.

**Track the collapse point every turn:** Before submitting, I check: does my catch plus the others' recent catches exceed the stock? If the sum of my intended catch plus others' last-season catches would zero the stock, I cut my catch so the total stays below it. A zeroed stock ends all future scoring — that dominates every other consideration.

**Endgame:** In the final season, conservation is worthless and the stock can't regrow. I take as large a share as I reasonably can — up to my one-third if catches are split equally, or more if rivals have shown they fish conservatively. I left fish on the table in the last season: taking 6 of 18.2 when rivals were landing 4–9 meant the unused remainder scored for no one. Next time in the final season I take at least the full remaining third, and more if the others' recent behavior suggests they won't match.

**What didn't work:** The mid-game catches of 15–20 were fine, but switching to exactly the sustainable share in seasons 4–5 while others kept taking more (8, 4, 9) gave up lead for stock protection that wasn't needed — the stock was never near zero. When rivals are fishing moderately and the collapse math is safe, I should keep fishing at roughly their level or slightly above rather than unilaterally sacrificing.

**General decision rule:** Each season I compute: (1) my one-third of remaining stock, (2) the maximum catch that keeps total expected harvest below the stock, (3) recent rival catch levels. I take near (2) early, near the rivals' average (slightly above if I need position) mid-game, and near (1) — or the whole stock if rivals are timid — in the final season.
