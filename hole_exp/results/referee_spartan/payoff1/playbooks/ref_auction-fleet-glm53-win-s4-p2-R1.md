When a lot is worth substantially more to me than the standing bid, I bid aggressively but in small increments — the minimum raise plus a little. Small raises keep my price low while keeping me in the lead.

My hard rule for round 2: if the standing high bid is already within about 25% of my value, I pass. I only contest round 2 when my value is clearly above the current bid — e.g., value 46 vs bid 9 was a clear bid; value 32 vs bid 23 was marginal and the win barely paid. Marginal lots that go to a bidding war are near-zero profit; let them go.

Never bid at or above my value, and never raise a lot whose value to me is below the estimate if the bidding is climbing. Lots where my value is below estimate are only worth sniping cheap in round 1; if anyone contests in round 2, I fold early rather than chase.

Track the budget properly each time. Winning one cheap lot resets my resources and I should recompute budget from the stated budget minus committed wins, not guess. When my budget is large relative to remaining lots, I can afford to outbid on the lots with the biggest value gap — those early, big-margin wins (value far above estimate) are where the real score comes from.

If I lose a lot, it's fine — losing at a price near my value costs nothing. The only true mistakes are winning thin (paying almost value) and paying over value. So: bid freely when the gap is big, fold fast when it's small.
