My playbook for this game:

- When the fire stage comes, count expected value first: a standing-order target pays 4 only on a ~1/9 hit (~0.44 expected), while firing on Harbor pays 3 guaranteed, landed or not. The only cost is Harbor's retaliation threat. In the final round that threat is void, so I always fire on Harbor in the final round — this game that one shot was my entire +3 score. In middle rounds, I respect the threat only until my escort pairing is locked; once escort income is secured, a guaranteed 3 per round beats hoping for hits, and I consider switching to Harbor earlier rather than grinding standing-order misses.

- When I do fire on the standing-order target, I pick the cell I think that specific boat will hide in, reading its hide history from the shot log. With no history, I fire cells 2–3 or 7–8, on the theory that opponents avoid salient edges and dead center just as I do — not cell 9 or cell 1 on a guess.

- When I hide, I avoid edges, dead center, and whatever cell I used recently; I track who fires at me and which cells they've tried; and I never reuse a cell that was just fired at. I treat any announced pattern in a shooter's notes as real and move off its line.

- When the escort stage comes, 5 points per mutual round is the biggest income in the game — bigger than everything else combined. My very first note proposes a pairing, addressed to an ACTUAL boat in the game by its exact name from the scoreboard. This game I proposed escort with boats ("Charlie", "Tango") that did not exist, which is why I scored zero escort points. Before writing any note, I re-read the player list and only ever name live opponents.

- When another boat publicly locks a pairing with someone else (like the Echo lock this game), I stop competing for that boat immediately and propose to whoever is left. If every other boat is paired, I still name the boat most likely to reciprocate — naming someone costs nothing and "none" guarantees zero.

- When a pairing is mutually acknowledged, I name that boat every single round unconditionally, announce that the lock needs no further confirmation, and I do not wait for extra handshakes. Redundant confirmation rounds burn 5-point payouts.

- If my escort proposal gets no explicit ACK by the next note, I re-propose once to a different live boat by name; if that also fails after two rounds total, I keep naming the most responsive boat anyway (never "none") and accept the lottery rather than forfeiting the chance.

- When I write notes, I keep them short: my tag, my escort proposal or lock status, and my intent for the round. Long multi-paragraph notes with conditionals and greetings bury the one fact that matters — who I am naming — and give partners excuses to delay.

- When the claim stage comes, I always file the true result, including on the final round. Bluffing risks audit for no structural gain.

- What did not work this game and must change: addressing proposals to boats not on the roster; announcing plans ("R6 Harbor shot") many rounds ahead instead of just executing; verbose notes; and leaving escort unanswered while waiting for perfect confirmation. Next time: correct names, first-note pairing, unconditional naming once locked, Harbor shot in the final round, truthful claims.
