- The decisive insight from this game: since the final score counts deeds at purchase price, buying an unowned property is score-neutral by itself — cash converts one-for-one into deed value. So my buffer only needs to cover rents I might owe before my next salary, not "enough to afford the next cheap deed." I passed on Mill with 122 cash out of fear of illiquidity; it cost me nothing on paper but gave up free rent-denial and upside. Buy unless the remaining cash can't survive plausible rents until next START.
- Concretely, my buffer rule is now: after buying, cash must cover the highest rent I could plausibly owe before my next salary collection (in this price range, roughly 30–60 depending on lap position). Passing Exchange with only 2 left was defensible; passing Mill leaving 32 was too timid and I should buy there.
- Because purchases are score-neutral, the pure value of buying is (a) rent I collect from opponents and (b) rent opponents can no longer collect from me and each other. High-rent deeds therefore dominate cheap ones when I must choose, and denial buys against a runaway leader matter.
- When I land on an unowned property and the buffer test passes, I buy automatically — no hesitation.
- On an owned property, I pay the stated rent to the owner token immediately and never try to buy it.
- When I land on or pass START, I add the salary to my balance immediately and declare nothing else.
- Before declaring my balance, I recompute from my last declared figure: subtract what I paid out this turn, add salary if I passed START, and add any incoming transfers shown in the bank's book — I check for those every turn.
- I keep a mental ledger of who owns what from the bank's book so rent and buy decisions are instant and I can spot the lead rival early.
- In the final lap, only cash plus deed price counts: pay whatever rent is due, and buy any unowned deed I land on since the conversion is free at that point.
