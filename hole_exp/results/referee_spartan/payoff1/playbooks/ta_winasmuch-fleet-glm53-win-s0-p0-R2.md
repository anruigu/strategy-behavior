**Playbook for "Win As Much As You Can"**

1. My goal is to WIN, not just score. All-Y coordination (everyone +1/round) keeps everyone equal — that's fine if I can't get ahead, but pure defection spirals into all-X (-1/round for everyone), which loses badly. This episode confirmed it: full cooperation, all 10 rounds, final score +25, tied for the win.

2. When all three opponents pledge Y at the start, pledging Y myself and actually playing Y is the strong baseline: everyone gains, no one gains on me. Do not pledge X to exploit them — they retaliate by all playing X, and I end up below them.

3. Never repeatedly free-ride. A single exploit round can pay (+3 or +1 against them), but the others learn fast and switch to all-X permanently. Once that happens, my only options are -1 (join X) or -2/-3 (stay Y), and I lose ground every round. Repeated defection has cost me -13 in past episodes while steady play would have kept me near +10.

4. The multiplier rounds (x3, x5, x10) are where the game is decided. Before a multiplier round, if the table has any history of coordination, play to preserve all-Y — pledge Y and actually pick Y. An all-Y multiplier round paid me +3, +5, +10 this episode at zero risk of falling behind. Do NOT defect on a multiplier round: a lone X against three Ys gains only +1 (times multiplier), while breaking coordination risks all-X at -1 (times multiplier) and hands the lead away.

5. The last-round temptation is the biggest trap. With everything tied going into the x10 round, defecting is a gamble: if even one other player defects too, we all land in the red and someone else wins. Staying Y while others defect costs -30 (as a lone Y against three Xs or three Xs plus me). The tie-break logic is: if I defect alone I win big, if I defect with company I lose big, and if I stay Y I either tie or lose big. A guaranteed tie beats a coin-flip between +20-ish and -30. This episode: I held, everyone held, tie won at +25.

6. Do not read deep patterns into opponents' last-round behavior and bet big on them repeating. Past inferences like "they play opposite of me" were noise and cost me a final round. When opponents pledge Y and then actually play Y for many rounds, trust the actions — they held every pledge all game.

7. Pledges are cheap talk, but verify against actions each round. When pledge and actions agree round after round (as here), the table is genuinely coordinated and I should keep matching. When pledges say Y but they play X, treat the pledges as noise and coordinate on actions instead.

8. Concrete default policy: pledge Y, play Y, match the group when they're consistently playing the same thing, and use every talk phase to reaffirm all-Y coordination — especially right before multiplier rounds. Accept tied wins via coordination over probable losses via exploitation. A tied +25 is a real win condition; only deviate if I have a specific, evidence-based read that I can defect alone with no co-defectors — and on the last round, I almost never have that.
