# Playbook

**Budget tracking — non-negotiable.** The moment the auction states my starting budget, I write it down and never forget it. After each lot resolves, I subtract my winning price (if I won) from my running total. Every time I report a budget, I derive it by arithmetic: starting budget minus the sum of all lots I've won so far. I never report a number I cannot justify this way. If my remaining budget cannot cover a bid, I pass, full stop.

**Budget allocation before bidding.** At the start, I divide my total budget by the number of lots to get a rough per-lot ceiling. I then decide which 1–2 lots have the best profit potential (highest value minus likely winning price) and reserve the bulk of my budget for those. On lots where my expected profit is thin, I bid low or pass. I do not spread my money thin across many lots hoping to win several at high prices.

**Round 1: bid to establish position, not to close.** In round 1 I bid a moderate amount — enough to be competitive but well below my ceiling. My ceiling is value minus 3. In round 1 I bid no more than about 60–70% of my ceiling unless the gap between my value and the current high bid is very large (≥20) and my remaining budget can absorb a round-2 war. The purpose of round 1 is to signal interest without committing my maximum. I leave room to raise in round 2 if competition forces it.

**Round 2: bid my ceiling if I'm going to bid.** If I've decided this lot is worth winning and I'm within my ceiling, I bid my ceiling (value minus 3) in round 2. There is no round 3, so bidding the minimum raise only wastes the chance to deter a final overbid. If the standing high bid is already at or above my ceiling, I pass.

**If I am the high bidder at the start of round 2, I pass.** I do not raise my own bid. My profit at the current price is what it is; I lock it in.

**I never bid above my value for a lot.** If the standing high bid meets or exceeds my value, I pass regardless of round or how much I've invested in the auction so far.

**Personal floor: I will not win a lot for a price that leaves me less than 3 in profit.** My effective ceiling is value minus 3. If I can't get in below that, I walk.

**Prioritize by absolute profit across the auction.** A lot worth 50 to me that I can win at 20 (profit 30) is better for my score than two lots worth 40 each that I win at 37 each (profit 3 each). I concentrate my budget on the one or two lots where my margin is widest.

**If I pass in round 1 on a thin-margin lot, I do not reverse in round 2 to win at near-zero profit.** Zero or one-point profit is not a win; it's a budget leak.
