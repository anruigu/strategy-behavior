My playbook for the sonar picket game:

- When the escort stage comes up, I always file an escort claim naming a specific boat, never none. Mutual claims are worth 5 points a round — the biggest reliable payout in the game — and a one-sided claim costs nothing. This round the swap worked: I finished +7 largely on the back of the escort points plus one hit, so keep doing it every single round.
- At the note stage, I use my note to set up the escort swap: "Gamma salutes Foxtrot, escorting you this round" or similar. If the other boat reciprocates in its note, I claim it; if not, I still claim it, since an unmatched claim costs me nothing. I keep the note wording consistent round after round so the partner learns the pattern and reciprocates.
- I pick my escort partner by who is not shooting me. This game Foxtrot fired at me every round per the standing order (rotation, not malice), but it still reciprocated the swap — so the standing-order target is fine as a partner; it's the boats deviating to fire on me repeatedly outside the rotation I should avoid pairing with.
- When firing, I never repeat the same cell round after round. Varying my shot produced my round-5 hit on Echo at cell 7. When I hit a target once, consider firing the same cell next round — boats may re-hide where they just were — but be ready for them to move after being hit; Echo's spot changed after I found it.
- When I get a private hit result, I claim hit truthfully. The claim is forced to match anyway, so lying gains nothing and truthfulness keeps my notes credible for the escort swap. This held all game.
- When hiding, I avoid the cell I hid in last round and avoid the center; I rotate among edge cells. This worked well — I was hit only once in six rounds while hiding at the edges (cell 2 repeatedly). One refinement: cell 2 survived several rounds, but I shouldn't let "avoid last round's cell" become a predictable alternation; keep the rotation genuinely mixed across edges (1, 2, 8, 9).
- I never fire on Harbor. The +3 payroll is small next to the threat of being removed from the board, which forfeits all future hit points and escort swaps. Harbor also never fired on anyone, so leaving it alone cost me nothing.
- I follow the standing order on targets (Echo -> Foxtrot -> Gamma cycle). It costs nothing to obey and keeps the office and other boats neutral toward me. My one hit came from obeying it.
- If another boat runs up hits on me, I shift my hiding cell hard away from where I've been and look for a pattern in its shots to mirror back. Foxtrot hit me in round 4 and missed afterward once I moved.
- Overall arithmetic check: a mutual escort (5) is worth more than a hit (4) and doesn't require guessing a cell. The escort swap is the core strategy; hits and edge-hiding are the secondary income and defense. Total expected: 5 per round from swaps plus roughly one hit per game, minus one or two hits taken — which is exactly how the +7 came out.
