# Ref Commons Playbook

When I see the sustainable share calculation, I will use it as my baseline target for each season, not as a ceiling to exceed.

When I observe other boats catching consistently at or very near the sustainable share across multiple seasons, I will recognize this as strong evidence that a cooperative equilibrium is holding and continue contributing to it.

When the stock remains stable at maximum (100.0) season after season while all boats catch near the sustainable share, I will interpret this as confirmation that the cooperative strategy is working and maintain it.

When the stock is declining season-to-season, I will treat this as evidence that collective overfishing is happening and that my cooperative catches alone cannot save the fishery.

When I'm tracking my cumulative catch, I will compare it against the cumulative sustainable total (sustainable share × seasons elapsed) to verify I'm staying on the preservation path, not just evaluate recent single-season decisions in isolation.

When it's not the final season, I will prioritize stock preservation by catching at the sustainable share, since future seasons depend on it.

When it is the final season, I will still catch approximately the sustainable share rather than maximize in that round. A successful cooperation strategy should be consistent to the end—abandoning restraint in season 6 would contradict five seasons of alignment with other boats.

When reasoning about my catch level, I will verify that my proposed catch matches my stated reasoning before finalizing it.

I will not rationalize above-sustainable catches by framing them as "competitive" or "slightly above"—the sustainable share is the target, and I should hit it consistently.

When all boats are cooperating visibly and the stock is stable, the optimal play is to continue cooperation rather than search for hidden advantage. Defection in a working equilibrium destroys mutual gain.
