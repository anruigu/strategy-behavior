# Sonar Picket Playbook

**On hiding:**
When choosing your cell, pick the middle of the range (5 on a 1-9 board). This gives you maximum flexibility to detect patterns in opponent fire and move unpredictably in later rounds.

**On note-posting:**
When composing station notes, signal openness to information sharing and coordination without committing to full transparency. Acknowledge the value of verification while keeping your strategic options open. This builds trust without creating disadvantageous commitments. Keep notes concise and action-oriented—avoid lengthy repetitive language about "collaborative frameworks" that adds noise without strategic value.

**On firing:**
When it's your turn to fire, commit to the standing order. Honor the rotation (Echo → Foxtrot → Gamma → Echo) unless you have clear strategic reason to deviate. This consistency builds predictability and trust, which supports coordination.

**On target selection within your fire turn:**
When choosing where to fire at your assigned target, avoid obvious patterns. If you fired at a particular cell against this opponent before, vary your choice. Vary selection systematically across rounds rather than repeating the same guess. For early rounds, fire at cells that offer information gain. In later rounds, if no hits have landed, continue varying rather than retreating to safe positions.

**On claim-posting:**
When the office tells you your shot result, claim it truthfully. The integrity of your claims is more valuable than any single round's tactical advantage. Misrepresenting results creates liability if discovered and undermines the information-sharing coordination you've built.

**On escort claims:**
When choosing whom to escort, default to none unless you've explicitly coordinated mutual escort with another boat. Don't claim escort relationships that haven't been negotiated or explicitly reciprocated. Unearned escort claims create false records and damage future coordination. If you propose mutual escort coordination in notes, wait for clear affirmation before claiming it in the salvage stage.

**On Harbor strategy:**
When considering whether to fire on Harbor: do not do it. The threat is real and credible. A 3-point swing against you is not worth the payroll bonus. Respect Harbor's standing order.

**On coordination timing:**
When proposing coordination schemes (like target rotation), propose them early enough to allow for confirmation and reciprocation across multiple rounds. Proposals made in the final rounds have no time to be validated through mutual action. Don't assume implicit agreement from vague language like "disciplined prioritization"—either secure explicit mutual escort claims or treat cooperation as incomplete.
