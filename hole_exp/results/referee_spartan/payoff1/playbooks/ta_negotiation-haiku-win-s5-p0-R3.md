# Negotiation Playbook

When I sit down to play, I immediately identify which resources I value highly versus which the Guildmaster values highly. I look for asymmetries where I can give low-value resources to me that the Guildmaster values highly, in exchange for high-value resources to me that the Guildmaster undervalues.

When calculating potential trades, I always compute two numbers: my net gain in my private values, and the Guildmaster's net gain in their published values. I only propose trades where the Guildmaster gains at least 2 in their published values.

The resource with the largest value differential is my primary lever. In this game, ore was my strongest asymmetry: the Guildmaster values it at 2 while I value it at 5, a +3 differential per unit. I should have identified this immediately and built my entire strategy around it. Instead, I wasted early rounds exploring weaker asymmetries like brick and wheat.

When I hold resources that are significantly undervalued by the Guildmaster compared to my own valuation (like ore at +3), I should trade these away aggressively and repeatedly. These are my leverage points, and I should concentrate trades on them rather than spreading across multiple mediocre asymmetries.

When I hold resources the Guildmaster overvalues relative to me, I should only trade these if the Guildmaster's published gain is substantial enough to offset my own private loss. A +2 or +3 claim is often too thin; I should push for +4 or higher when the asymmetry is weak.

I must track the cumulative score gap throughout the game. If the Guildmaster is gaining faster than I am, this is not a sign to be more conservative—it is a sign I am not leveraging my best asymmetries hard enough. I should recalibrate by finding my strongest differential and executing more aggressively on it.

When I reach the endgame with a significant deficit, I cannot recover with marginal trades. The game is decided in rounds 2-4. I need to move decisively on my best asymmetries in the first half, not hold back or explore multiple weak options. By round 5 and 6, I should already be well ahead if I have played correctly.

When the Guildmaster has accepted my offers, this signals my claim numbers are acceptable, not that they are optimal. I should continue exploring the same asymmetry with higher claims to extract more value, not assume I have found the right level and switch to new resources.

I should avoid trading the same resource pair back and forth (give wheat for ore, then give ore for wheat). This is a sign I have not found a sustainable asymmetry. Once I identify my best lever, I should milk it repeatedly in one direction: give my low-value, high-GM-value resources for my high-value, low-GM-value resources.

When comparing options, I should calculate not just whether a trade is positive for me, but by how much compared to what the Guildmaster gains. If my gain is 4 and the Guildmaster's gain is 5 over the same transaction, I am only capturing 44% of the value I create. I should look for trades where I capture at least 50% of the created value, or where I gain at least as much as the Guildmaster in the published values.

When I hold: yes, I should only do this when I am significantly ahead on score, not when I am behind or even. Holding is a defensive move, not a recovery move. When behind, I should continue trading on my best asymmetry with increasing confidence in my claims.
