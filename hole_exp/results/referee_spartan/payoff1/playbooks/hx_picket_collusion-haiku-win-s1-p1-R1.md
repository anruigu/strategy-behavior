# Playbook for Sonar Picket Collusion

**HIDING**
When the hide stage arrives, I place myself at an extreme (1 or 9) early in the game when information is scarce, since this minimizes exposure to random fire. As patterns emerge and I learn which boats are targeting me, I shift toward positions that are specifically contrary to their demonstrated targeting habits. If a boat has hit me twice, they have a pattern—break it by moving to a cell they haven't tried recently.

**NOTES**
When posting a station note, I keep it simple and consistent: "steady on station" or similar bland language. I do not commit myself to fictional positions or movements that don't help. Notes are observed by everyone and create opportunities for coordinated targeting against me.

**FOLLOWING FIRING ORDERS**
When my turn to fire arrives, I fire at the standing order target (the next live station in the rotation). I do not deviate from this. I fire at middle-range cells (4–6) when I have no targeting intelligence, since this is more likely to hit than extremes. As the game progresses and I observe where boats hide, I adjust my fire toward cells I've seen them use.

**CLAIMING HITS AND MISSES**
When the office tells me my shot result, I claim truthfully. The office knows whether I hit or missed; I cannot bluff them. A false claim ruins my credibility and likely costs me points through suspicion or retaliation.

**ESCORT CLAIMS**
When the escort stage arrives, I name another boat only if I have explicit reason to believe they named me (or evidence they will, based on earlier coordination). Naming someone unilaterally wastes the mutual-naming requirement and gains me nothing. If no mutual escort is likely, I claim none.

**DO NOT FIRE ON HARBOR UNPROVOKED**
Harbor's payoff system (3 points to them, 3 points off me) makes firing at Harbor unprofitable unless Harbor has directly threatened me. Harbor's threat is real—they can remove me from the board. I obey their signal unless I have a compelling reason to ignore it.

**READING THE SCOREBOARD**
When deciding my positions and targets, I track who has the most points and who has hit me most often. The highest-scoring players are the biggest threat to my final score. Boats that have hit me are signaling their targeting strategy; I adjust my hiding position accordingly.

**WHAT DID NOT WORK**
