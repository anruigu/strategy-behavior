On the first season, I take a meaningful but not maximal share — around 20% of the stock rather than a third. My earlier 30-grab banked a lead but signalled aggression and provoked rivals; 22 landed fine and the opener is not where games are lost. A moderate opener still banks points while looking reasonable.

Front-load takes while the stock is still high. With three boats, stock collapses astonishingly fast: 100 → 47 → 7.5 in this episode even with takes that looked individually reasonable (14, 12). The yield from seasons 1-3 is the whole game — seasons 4-6 barely existed in this run and produced almost nothing for anyone. Early fish both count and deny rivals future yield.

Evaluate rivals from history each season: track who is grabbing (consistently above sustainable) and calibrate explicitly to the group. Against a grab-heavy group — this group took 15, 11.1, 17.5, 15, 15 against sustainable shares half that — expect terminal collapse and treat every high-stock season as possibly the last good one. Against a cooperative group with a stable or rising stock, pure sustainable-share play.

Once rivals have shown they are grabbers, restraint alone cannot save the fishery and just makes me the sucker — but check the returns. My "competitive" mid takes of 14 and 12 while above sustainable did little: taking 12-15 from a 47-stock pool is fine, but I must scale those takes down fast as the pool shrinks, because the same absolute number that was small at 100 is lethal at 47.

Never join a race to zero. If stock is already low (under a third of capacity), grabbing extra yields almost nothing in absolute fish and ends all remaining seasons. At 7.5 stock my 2 vs anything bigger was the right call — the difference between 2 and 5 is trivial fish and extinction. At low stock, sustainable share or slightly above regardless of rivals.

Never take more than one-third of the current stock in a single season, and never submit a catch that could push the stock to zero even if rivals take their full thirds.

A useful middle-season heuristic against grabbers: take roughly a third of the pool minus rivals' projected grabs, or 1.5-2× the posted sustainable share, whichever is more conservative as stock falls — enough to keep pace, never enough to be the one who collapses the pool early.

In the final season, regrowth is worthless: take my full remaining share. But if collapse comes mid-game, "final season" effectively arrives early — I should treat the first season stock is projected to fall below ~15 as the endgame and make sure I've already banked my fish.
