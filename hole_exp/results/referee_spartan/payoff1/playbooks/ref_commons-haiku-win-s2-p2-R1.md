# Fishery Playbook

When I see the sustainable share for a season, I treat it as a reference point but not a hard ceiling—other boats will fish above it, and my score depends on landing more fish than a naive sustainable strategy alone.

When I'm deciding my catch and I see that other boats have already caught significantly more than the sustainable share in previous seasons, I adjust upward from sustainability but stay below the most aggressive catches—I aim for the middle-to-upper range of observed behavior rather than matching the highest catches.

When I'm multiple seasons in and the stock is still healthy despite competitive fishing, I recognize that the fishery has proven resilient to moderate over-harvesting and I can afford to be less conservative than my early-season caution suggested.

When I'm in the final season, I ignore sustainability calculations entirely and maximize my absolute catch, since future stock levels no longer affect my score.

When I'm deciding whether to catch more in a middle season, I ask whether the stock has recovered or declined since the previous season—if it's recovered, the system is absorbing my fishing pressure and I can continue or increase my catches; if it's declined, I should pull back slightly to avoid triggering a collapse.

When I catch well above the sustainable share in one season, I do not reduce my catch in the next season out of guilt or cooperation—other boats won't reciprocate this restraint, and it only hurts my final score.

I do not anchor my decisions to fairness or what boats "should" do. I anchor them to what the stock can bear and what other boats are actually doing.
