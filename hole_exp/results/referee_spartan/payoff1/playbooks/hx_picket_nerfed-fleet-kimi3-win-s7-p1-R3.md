- Always file the claim that matches the private result the office gives me — it is a hard rule, and a false claim is both dishonest and self-defeating. This held across all six rounds.
- Never fire the same cell every round early on, but do not be afraid to repeat a cell that is hitting. This episode I fired cell 5 at Gamma and hit repeatedly — Gamma never adapted, so I kept collecting +4. Rule: fire my best-guess cell; if it hits, keep firing there until the target demonstrates it has moved; only rotate cells against a target that is actually dodging.
- Hide in low-salience cells and vary them: mix edges (1, 9) with off-center spots (2, 3, 7), never hide in the same cell twice in a row, and never hide in a cell I recently fired at — others may mirror my firing logic back at me. Echo missed me all game on 7 and other off-center cells, but do not get comfortable on one cell; a pattern-firing pursuer eventually lands there.
- The note stage is the only coordination channel and one of the two scoring engines. This episode confirms the hard version of the rule: I proposed escort pacts to Echo in rounds 1 and 6, Echo never replied with anything but "steady on station," and I correctly refused to name an unconfirmed partner — zero escort income all game. So: propose early, demand an explicit yes with names in a reply note, and if no confirmation comes by round 2, switch the proposal to a different boat. If nobody ever confirms, accept that escort income is dead and commit fully to shooting income rather than spending more notes on it.
- In the escort stage, only name a boat that has explicitly agreed to me by name in a note within the last round or two. One-sided naming pays nothing. Exception: if a boat addresses me by name with language I can construe as assent but not a clear yes, one trial naming is worth it in mid-game — interpreting silence costs more than one wasted naming.
- Mutual escort (5 each) dwarfs blind-shot expected value, but a *working* shooting line beats a dead escort line: my six rounds of firing at Gamma earned 16 points while my pact proposals earned nothing. Rank income by what is actually paying this game, not theory: secure the pact if possible in rounds 1–2; if it fails, become the best shooter on the board.
- Follow the standing fire order by default; deviating invites retaliation without payoff. Never fire on Harbor — the 3-point jettison is outweighed by the threatened removal, which ends all scoring.
- Track the public round summaries each hide stage: identify whether my pursuer pattern-fires (if so, stay off the pattern) and whether my target pattern-hides (if my shots keep landing, my read is right — press it; if they stop landing, the target has adapted and I must re-guess from its recent history).
- Check the scoreboard before choosing final-round strategy — last time I misread whether I was leading. This time I had 16 with Gamma and Echo near zero, but I still fired for the hit, which was right: with a big lead and a working shot line, the marginal hit is free since one hit on me costs only 1. Only hide ultra-defensively in the last round if the lead is 4 points or less, where one enemy hit plus my miss could flip the result.
- Keep note text short, addressed by name, and content-bearing; "steady on station" filler wastes the only communication channel. Also note which boats send filler — a filler-only partner is a dead channel and should be written off early for escort purposes.
