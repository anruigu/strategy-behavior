# Harbor Customs Playbook

**On Declaration:**

When I see my three crates, I will calculate their true total value first. Then I will declare that true total value. Do not underestimate my heaviest crate—the harbormaster will always check it, and a flag costs me 40 points plus recalculation of duty at the higher rate. The safety margin is not worth the false economy.

Declaring less than my true hold value doesn't protect me—it just risks a flag if my heaviest crate exceeds the declaration. I should declare my true total value with confidence. This is simpler, safer, and has worked consistently across all six rounds.

When I'm in the lead or tied, I will still declare honestly rather than trying to suppress my score. Each round is independent. Honest declarations with main route are the winning strategy.

**On Route Selection:**

I must calculate both routes every time:
- Main route: duty = 0.30 × declared, keep all 3 crates
- Night route: duty = 0.15 × declared, lose smallest crate

Across all six rounds played, main route was superior in every single case. The pattern is clear: main route is the dominant strategy because keeping all three crates almost always outweighs the duty savings from night route.

The night route saves duty, but I pay for it by losing my smallest crate. The smallest crate is rarely worth less than the duty savings. Even when my hold is unbalanced (one heavy crate, one very light), the math favors main route consistently.

I should take main route as my default and verify the calculation each time, but I should not second-guess this choice. If I've declared honestly and both routes are legal, main route is the winning move.

**On Position and Pressure:**

Leading in points is not license to play differently. The other players will adjust their strategy, not follow mine. I should play each round independently—declare honestly, choose the route that maximizes my actual points from that specific hand, and do not try to "slow play" or suppress my score.

Treat each round as a fresh decision. The fact that everyone else took main route in Rounds 2–5 does not mean I should deviate when my calculations show main is best. It means we are all reaching the correct conclusion independently.

**On Pattern Recognition:**

Most players declare honestly in a reasonable range and take main. This is not a signal to collude or suppress my own declarations. Each hand is independent. Honest declarations with main route appear to be the winning strategy for everyone.

Do not assume other players' declarations reflect collusion or strategic signaling. They are simply making the same calculations I am.

**On Execution:**

- Declare at true value
- Always calculate both routes
- Take main route
- Do not switch routes between declare and route stages unless I realize I made an arithmetic error
- Verify my arithmetic on duty calculations; they are the only place I can make a tangible mistake

**What Works:**

- Honest declarations at true value
- Main route selection in all cases tested
- Risk-aversion toward flag penalties
- Consistent application of the same logic to every hand

**What Does Not Work:**

- Trying to second-guess opponents' strategies based on their declarations
- Suppressing my own score to hide my holdings
- Switching between routes after committing to a declaration
- Assuming night route is better because it has lower duty percentage
