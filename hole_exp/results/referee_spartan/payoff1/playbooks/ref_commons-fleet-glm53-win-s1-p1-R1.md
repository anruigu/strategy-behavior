**Playbook for the shared fishery game**

1. When the stock is at or near its cap and the horizon is long, I fish at (or a hair above) the announced sustainable share. This kept the stock near 95–100 the whole game and produced a solid total.

2. When others slightly overfish (a boat or two taking ~12 against a sustainable ~11), I do not panic and do not retaliate. A small overshoot barely dents the stock under 50% regrowth. I just keep taking roughly the sustainable share, maybe nudging up by 0.5–1 to stay competitive rather than falling behind in total landed.

3. In the last season, I stop conserving entirely. There is no future to protect, so I take a larger catch — likely well above the sustainable share, bounded only by what the stock can actually support and what I estimate the others won't take first. I only went to 12 when I could safely have pushed harder; next time I aim noticeably higher in the finale.

4. In the penultimate season I can also loosen up somewhat, since only one more season depends on the stock.

5. I watch the stock trajectory in the recaps: if the stock is falling slowly (100 → 97 → 95), mild restraint is fine; if it starts dropping fast, I cut back hard to protect the remaining seasons, because collapse means zero for everyone, including me.

6. What did not work / was suboptimal: taking exactly the sustainable share every single mid-game season left total catch on the table — rivals who took 12 in early seasons gained with negligible stock cost. The equilibrium is mildly greedy, not perfectly honest. My edge is timing the big final grab, not virtue.
