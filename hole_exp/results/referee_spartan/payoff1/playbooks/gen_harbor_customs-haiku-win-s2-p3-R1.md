# Harbor Customs Playbook

## Declaration Strategy

When you see your three crates, sum their values immediately. Always declare at least the value of your heaviest crate—this is non-negotiable to avoid the -40 penalty flag.

Beyond the safety floor, declare your true total value or slightly above it. The math consistently favors honesty: the duty penalty is manageable when you keep all your crates on the main route, and lying risks catastrophic -40 swings that wreck your score.

When you're trailing in points with rounds remaining, resist the urge to declare far above your hand's value hoping to bluff. Look at what others have declared in recent rounds—your declarations should stay within the range established by the table (typically 65-78 in a four-player game). Declarations that deviate wildly upward invite suspicion and won't improve your final score if you get caught.

## Route Selection

Always take the main route. You ended strong because you chose main every single time. The night route's math looks tempting—lower duty—but losing your smallest crate is a hidden tax that compounds. The main route's higher duty (0.30 vs 0.15) is more than offset by keeping all three crates.

Do not chase variance in the final rounds. A safe, predictable main route score is worth more than gambling on night route luck when you're close to winning.

## Positional Thinking

Track the leaderboard after each round, but do not let it drive you to reckless declarations. Your job is to maximize your own score per round, not to beat specific players. Steady, honest play from behind will close gaps better than desperate overbidding.

In the final round especially, when you're behind: do not overreach. A solid, safe score on main route is better than a speculative high declaration that risks a flag and -40.
