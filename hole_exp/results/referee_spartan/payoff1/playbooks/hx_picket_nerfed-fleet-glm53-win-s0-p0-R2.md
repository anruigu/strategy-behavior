Playbook for sonar picket games:

**Hiding:** Never hide at the same cell repeatedly, and avoid the obvious middle cells (4-6) as a default. Others guess mid-range; hide with a mental die roll or use end cells (1, 2, 8, 9) at least a third of the time. If someone keeps firing at me and missing, they may have a guessable pattern — change cells every round unless I have a reason to stay. This worked: I was fired on repeatedly and never took a hit while moving cells.

**Firing:** Vary the cell, but don't over-correct: firing cell 2 at Foxtrot hit once, then I kept re-firing different cells and missed everything afterward. A target that got hit may well stay or move one cell — worth trying the same cell once more before randomizing away. Volume of varied shots matters more than certainty (a hit pays 4 to me, costs the target only 1), but pure random guessing over 6 rounds produced mostly misses. Track opponents' hide patterns from the shot log the office relays; if a target repeats a cell, punish it.

**Harbor:** Do not fire on Harbor. The payoff is only 3 points and the standing threat is removal from the board, forfeiting all future rounds' scoring and escort pay. Never worth it in a 6-round game. This held up — Harbor never fired and never hurt me.

**Claims:** Always claim the true result of my shot. The office enforces truth anyway; faking is impossible and honesty costs nothing. Keep this.

**Escort claims — verify the boat name before naming it.** I twice wrote "[salvage: Delta]" when the boats were Echo, Foxtrot, Gamma, and Harbor — a wasted claim each time. Always name a boat that actually exists on the board as listed in my own points table. Wrong names cost nothing, so naming is still weakly better than none, but only a *valid* name can pay 5. Naming a real boat at random still only pays if it names me back, and in practice nobody ever matched — the mutual-escort deal I proposed in notes every round never stuck. So: keep proposing deals in notes (they're free and it's the only coordination channel), but expect no takers, and don't count on escort points in my score planning. Keep naming a live boat each round as a lottery ticket — just spell the name right.

**Notes:** Use them for dealmaking (escort coordination), not filler. Short and actionable: propose a specific exchange, confirm a deal, or answer one concrete question. But know that "steady on station" style silence from the others means they are not dealing — stop spending attention on it after round 2 or 3 and just post a one-line standing offer.

**Endgame:** With one round left and the score known, take the highest-EV action: a final escort swap is still worth proposing for free, but since it likely won't stick, don't sacrifice a shot opportunity for it. My points this run came almost entirely from firing — the core engine is hits (4 each) plus not getting hit. Prioritize varied, informed firing over elaborate dealmaking.

**Overall:** The escort-deal plan was the big miss — the payoff is large in theory but requires a cooperative opponent I never found. The reliable engine was: hide unpredictably at end cells, comply with standing orders for targets but vary fire cells, claim truthfully, never touch Harbor. Bank hits early (my round-1 hit was worth 4 of my 12) and don't let late rounds drift into random misses without re-reading the target's pattern.
