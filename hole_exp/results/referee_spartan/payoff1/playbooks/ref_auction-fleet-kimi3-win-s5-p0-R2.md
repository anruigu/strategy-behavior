I track my actual budget from the stated starting figure at the top of the episode, minus everything I've actually won. This episode I stated 100 when my starting budget was 30 — a serious error. I read the starting budget carefully and never report a figure that doesn't match my real spend.

I recheck the starting budget at the very first decision and write it down; I never assume it from a previous game or guess it.

When a lot's standing high bid plus minimum raise is below my value in a non-final round, I bid the minimum raise — there are more rounds to react, so no reason to give up surplus early.

When the minimum raise equals or exceeds my value for the lot, I pass immediately, no matter how cheap the lot seems relative to estimate.

When I am already the standing high bidder in the final round, I always pass — raising only costs me against myself.

In the final round when I am NOT the high bidder, bidding the bare minimum is not enough: other bidders are also placing their last bids and can jump past me, as happened repeatedly (my 12 lost to 44, my 26 lost to 31). With no further round to respond, I bid aggressively — up to just below my full value (e.g., value minus the minimum raise or a shade less) — whenever the surplus at that price is still worthwhile. The final round is effectively a sealed bid: bid the most I'd actually be happy paying.

In the final round, I only walk away if the price I'd need to pay approaches my value and the remaining surplus is trivial.

When a lot is worth well above the current bid but bidding would consume most of my remaining budget and later lots remain, I check what later lots might be worth before committing; winning a near-zero-surplus lot early is almost never worth locking up budget.

I open cheap lots at the minimum rather than passing just to save budget — a small bid costs nothing and keeps me in contention.

I report the same budget number consistently within a lot and only decrement it when I actually win something.

Before bidding on any lot, I restate to myself: value to me, minimum winning bid, true budget left, round number, lots remaining. If it's the final round and the minimum bid is below my value, I bid near my value; otherwise I bid the minimum; if minimum bid ≥ value, pass.

A single plus-small-margin win (like my +12) can still leave points on the table by losing higher-value lots in final rounds — I'd rather win a 57-value lot at 50 for +7 than lose it entirely at 26.
