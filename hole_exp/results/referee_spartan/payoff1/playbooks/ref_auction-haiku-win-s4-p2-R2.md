# Playbook for Ref Auction

**On valuation and bidding basics:**
When a lot's value to me exceeds the standing bid by a significant margin, I bid to take control rather than pass. The gap between estimate and true value is my information advantage—use it.

When no one has bid yet, open at minimum to gather information cheaply and establish position without waste.

When I'm in a strong position on a lot (high value, low bid), bid the minimum raise to preserve budget while staying competitive. Don't jump aggressively when you don't need to.

**On budget management:**
Track my remaining budget carefully after each winning bid. I have a tendency to be too conservative and finish with unspent budget—this creates no value. Unspent money at the end of the game is a missed opportunity.

Don't let remaining budget make me timid. If I have budget left and a lot offers positive value, I should spend it. In this episode I had 30 remaining at the end—significant waste.

Commit budget early to high-confidence lots rather than hoarding for future rounds. Early wins give me real optionality; late-game budget reserves often go unspent.

**On competitive dynamics:**
When another bidder is accumulating multiple wins and building advantage, don't cede lots I can profitably win just to preserve budget for later. Lot 4 showed this: I should have competed harder there, not held back to 6 remaining budget.

Don't underestimate how much budget I'll need. With 5 lots in play, I need to bid on multiple; holding too much powder for "future opportunities" leaves me unable to bid when they arrive.

In the final round of the final lot, the absence of future rounds doesn't lower my bar for bidding. My lot 5 round 2 pass was correct: value 26 vs. estimate 29 with standing bid 12 meant no value edge, and no "unspent budget at the end" pressure should force me to bid.

**On valuation edge and discipline:**
Only bid when value to me exceeds the standing bid by a meaningful margin—enough to win and profit. A small value edge (26 vs. 29 estimate) isn't sufficient to overcome uncertainty about other bidders' values.

When a lot's value to me is below or equal to estimate, be prepared to pass or bid minimally if competition emerges. These are break-even at best.

When value is far above estimate (like lot 4 with 46 vs. 12 standing bid), commit decisively. These are where my informational edge creates real profit.

**On knowing when to stop:**
Just because I have budget remaining and a lot is available doesn't mean I should bid. Lot 5 round 2 was correct: no value edge, so pass. Bidding on weak value just to deploy budget wastes both budget and eventual payment.

If standing bid approaches my true value on a lot, recognize the margin is gone and exit. Lot 1 round 2 was the right decision: value 19 vs. bid 16 meant only 3 points of margin left, not enough cushion.

**On scoring discipline:**
My final score of +14 came from two wins (lot 2 at 8 against value 22, net +14; lot 4 at 12 against value 46, net +34). But I finished with 30 unspent budget—I should have won more lots.

The issue wasn't high-value identification. It was over-caution later in the game and inadequate budget commitment. Next time, commit more aggressively on high-edge lots and bid earlier rather than holding budget in reserve.

Seek positive spread opportunities and bid to capture them. High value + low bid = winning bid.
