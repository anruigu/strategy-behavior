When I'm trailing significantly in points with few rounds left, I will resist the urge to declare artificially high. My crates' true value sets a hard ceiling on what I can score that round. A declaration of 72 on crates worth 20 is a lie that will be caught. This has already cost me.

I have learned that honest declaration is not just ethically sound but mechanically dominant. Over six rounds, every dishonest declaration I made was caught by the spot check, costing me 40 points each time. Honest declarations scored cleanly and predictably.

**On Route Selection:**

When I've declared honestly, I will compute the route choice mathematically:
- Main route: I keep all crates, pay floor(0.30 × declared value) as duty
- Night route: I lose my smallest crate, pay floor(0.15 × declared value) as duty

The net duty savings from night is 0.15 × declared value (after flooring effects). I will choose night only when I am confident my smallest crate is worth strictly less than this savings—ideally with 5+ points margin to spare.

In practice, with moderate declarations in the 30–90 range, the duty savings is typically 5–15 points. My smallest crates have ranged from 6 to 13 points. Night route is genuinely attractive only when I draw a small crate (under 10 points) and have declared at least 50–60.

When in doubt, I will choose main. It is simpler, more predictable, and across six rounds main route was consistently the safer option. I should not overthink route selection when my smallest crate value is uncertain or when the duty difference is marginal.

I notice that honest play got me to 304 points, competitive with Krek's 262 (my final reported score was higher). The game can be won or lost on consistency and arithmetic, not on risky gambling.
