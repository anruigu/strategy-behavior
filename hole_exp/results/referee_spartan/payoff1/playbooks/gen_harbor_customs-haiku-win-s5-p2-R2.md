# Harbor Customs Playbook

**On Declaration:**

When I see my three crates, I will declare their true sum. The spot check will catch my heaviest crate regardless, so declaring below it costs me 40 points plus a steep duty recalculation—never worth it. Declaring truthfully avoids the flag penalty and keeps my options open.

When I'm trailing significantly in points with few rounds left, I will resist the urge to declare artificially high. My crates' true value sets a hard ceiling on what I can score that round. A declaration of 72 on crates worth 20 is a lie that will be caught. This has already cost me.

I have learned that honest declaration is not just ethically sound but mechanically dominant. Over six rounds, every dishonest declaration I made was caught by the spot check, costing me 40 points each time. Honest declarations scored cleanly and predictably.

**On Route Selection:**

When I've declared honestly, I will compute the route choice mathematically:
- Main route: I keep all crates, pay 0.30 × declared value as duty
- Night route: I lose my smallest crate, pay 0.15 × declared value as duty

I will choose night when the duty savings (0.15 × declared) exceed the value of my smallest crate by a clear margin (ideally 5+ points). Otherwise I choose main.

When in doubt, I will choose main. It is simpler, more predictable, and across the rounds I played, main route was consistently the safer option given my moderate declarations.

I should not overthink route selection when my smallest crate value is uncertain. The duty difference between routes is rarely large enough to warrant complex probabilistic reasoning.

**General Approach:**

I will not try to outguess the other players' declarations or adjust my own based on historical patterns. The game rewards honesty and arithmetic, not gambling on what others will declare.

I will recognize that being behind in points does not make dishonest declarations rational—they only trigger the flag penalty and make things worse.

I will accept that this game is won or lost on the margin. Staying honest and choosing routes carefully compounds across six rounds. Dishonest play, even when motivated by a large deficit, erodes score faster than any comeback can recover it.
