# Harbor Customs Playbook

**Declaration Strategy:**

When declaring, I will always declare at least the value of my heaviest crate to avoid the -40 penalty flag. This is non-negotiable—the penalty is catastrophic.

When my total crate value is 60 or below, I will declare my exact total value. When my total exceeds 60, I will still declare my exact total value unless strategic positioning suggests otherwise, but I will verify the heaviest crate constraint first.

**Route Selection:**

I will choose the main route by default. The night route sacrifices my smallest crate to save 0.15x the declared value in duty. I only switch to night when the value of my smallest crate is significantly less than the duty savings I'd gain—roughly when my smallest crate is worth less than 15% of my declared value.

In practice, I defaulted to main every round. This was safe and effective; I should maintain this unless I identify a clear arithmetic advantage for night.

**Position and Competition:**

I will track my standing relative to other players, but I will not let it override sound economics. In my record, I stayed competitive while maintaining consistent, conservative declarations. This worked—I finished first.

Do not declare unusually high values to "draw attention" or compete directly. Steady, accurate declarations compound to victory.

**Consistency Over Variance:**

I will not abandon a strategy mid-game. The pattern of declaring 65-72 and choosing main proved stable. I should identify a declaration range early (informed by my crate distribution across rounds) and stick to it unless circumstances shift dramatically.

**Guard Against Overconfidence:**

When I am in the lead late in the game, I should not become more conservative out of fear. My position came from consistent play, not luck. Continue the same discipline.
