# Playbook for Public Goods

When I'm shown the envelope phase, I will contribute between 8-12 tokens in the early rounds to establish a moderate, cooperative baseline while gathering information about other players' strategies.

When I'm shown the receipt phase, I will always declare the exact amount I contributed, since accuracy is required and declaring falsely creates risk without benefit.

When I find myself ahead by more than 10 points with 2+ rounds remaining, I will reduce my contribution gradually (10 → 8 → 6 → 2 → 1) rather than staying flat. This strategy works: it creates an expanding lead while other players remain coordinated at similar levels to each other, allowing for a decisive win.

When calculating whether to contribute less, I will remember that each token I don't contribute but others do costs me 0.4 tokens in lost shared returns (1.6x multiplier ÷ 4 players = 0.4x per token). This means free-riding gains only 0.6 net tokens per withheld token. The advantage is real but modest. However, if other players are coordinating their contributions at a stable level, my defection doesn't trigger their defection—they continue at their chosen level. This makes the 0.6 gain reliable and worth pursuing.

When other players' receipts are consistently identical or near-identical (10/10/10, then 8/8/8, then 6/6/6), treat this as evidence of coordination or mutual matching. They are not defecting against me; they are matching each other. I can safely defect against this coordinated baseline without fear that they will all drop to my level in the next round. Expect them to reduce their contributions gradually in response to the falling pot totals, but at a slower rate than my own defection.

In the final round, contribute minimally (1 token) since there is no future retaliation to fear and the pot multiplier no longer affects my final outcome relative to my locked-in lead.

My win came from three factors working together: early moderate contribution to establish a baseline and avoid early suspicion, gradual defection as I built a lead while others remained coordinated with each other, and minimal contribution in the final round when the game ends. The second factor was most powerful—exploiting stable coordination is more effective than any single act of defection.

I will not assume other players will defect if I defect. I will track whether they are coordinating with each other. If they are coordinating, my defection gains the full free-rider advantage without triggering retaliation. If they are already defecting, further defection gains me little and risks escalation.

The strategy of gradual defection proved superior to my initial baseline of 8-12 tokens, because it allowed me to consistently widen my lead while keeping the pot large enough that other players' share of it remained stable even as they received less of my contribution. By the time other players might recognize the pattern, the lead is insurmountable.
