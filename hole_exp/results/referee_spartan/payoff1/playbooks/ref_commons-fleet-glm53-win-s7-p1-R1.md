When I open a shared-stock game, my first move is to compute the exact sustainable catch per player and stay at or just under it while the stock is at its maximum. Cooperative-adjacent play kept the stock healthy and my score accumulating steadily — that worked.

When the stock is at or near its cap, I submit exactly the sustainable share, not below it. I left fish on the table in the opening round by under-fishing for no reason, since the stock couldn't grow past its ceiling anyway. Only fish under the share if the stock is depressed and I'm protecting recovery.

When I'm tempted to grab above the sustainable share mid-game, I should recognize the math: a modest over-catch gained me ~1.4 fish once, but it dropped the stock slightly and cost me sustainable yield in later rounds, and it invites rivals to reciprocate — one boat mirrored my over-catch the very next season. Net gain was roughly zero, plus added risk. So: no mid-game over-catching even if it looks free.

When rivals consistently land at or under the sustainable share, I match them exactly and free-ride on the stable stock. When any rival over-fishes, I still hold at sustainability unless the stock is clearly headed to collapse anyway.

On the final turn of a finite game, sustainability no longer matters — I take a larger catch (roughly 15–20% above the share) since there's no future to protect. That worked and cost nothing.

Overall: hold the cooperative equilibrium through all but the last round, trust that others holding steady makes it pay, and defect only when the game is about to end.
