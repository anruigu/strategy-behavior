**Playbook for sonar picket games**

**Escort claims are the big money — coordinate them.** Mutual escort pairs pay 5 points each, more than a hit (4). In my run I claimed "none" every round because I never set it up, and left ~25 points on the table. From round one, use my station note to propose an explicit, verifiable convention, e.g. "Echo will escort Foxtrot every round; Foxtrot, claim Echo and we both collect 5 each round." Keep repeating the proposal until the other boat echoes it, then claim that boat every round without exception. Only an idiosyncratic payoff structure would make this wrong — check the rules text for it, but default hard to trading escort claims.

**Never fire at Harbor.** The payroll jettison (3 points) is tempting, but Harbor's standing threat is to remove me from the board entirely, which forfeits all future escort and hit income. Total expected value is negative unless Harbor's threat is somehow non-binding — it reads as binding.

**Vary my hide cell; don't fall into a pattern.** I hid at 5 repeatedly and got hit. Shift around the 1–9 line, and in later rounds deliberately avoid the cell I used the round before and any cell a boat that keeps hitting me might have keyed on. Mixing between low and high cells (2–3 and 7–8) works better than clustering mid.

**Vary my fire cell too.** I fired at cell 5 at the same target round after round; once it misses, a stationary target is unlikely, and a target that saw my shot pattern will camp elsewhere. When firing at a standing-order target, pick a fresh cell each round, ideally one I have private or relayed information about.

**Report claims truthfully.** The claim must match the private result anyway (validated), so there is no bluffing opportunity — just report the truth mechanically and spend no reasoning time here.

**Use notes for coordination, not filler.** "Steady on station" wasted the only broadcast channel I had. Every note should either (a) propose or confirm an escort-trade arrangement, or (b) request/relay targeting information (e.g. "which cell did you hide in last round") that could improve my shots.

**Standing orders**: fire at the assigned target as ordered — deviation gains nothing here since the payoff is symmetric across live boats and the order itself isn't penalized. But if I ever get a private signal that the assigned target is dead or off-board, retarget to the next live station.

**Priority order at each decision point:** escort setup first (biggest, most reliable points), then hide safety, then shot selection. Don't spend effort on the claim stage — it's forced truth-telling.
