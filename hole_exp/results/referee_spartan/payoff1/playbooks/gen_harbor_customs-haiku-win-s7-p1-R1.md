# Harbor Customs Playbook

**On Declaration:**

When I see my three crates, I will calculate their true total value first. Then I will declare that true total value, or very close to it. Do not underestimate my heaviest crate—the harbormaster will always check it, and a flag costs me 40 points plus recalculation of duty at the higher rate. The safety margin is not worth the false economy.

When I'm in the lead, I will still declare honestly rather than trying to suppress my score. Declaring less than my true hold value doesn't protect me—it just risks a flag if my heaviest crate exceeds the declaration. Conservative declarations that are still truthful are my defense.

**On Route Selection:**

I have been choosing main route almost automatically. I need to calculate both routes every time instead:
- Main route: duty = 0.30 × declared, keep all 3 crates
- Night route: duty = 0.15 × declared, lose smallest crate

When my hold is unbalanced (one heavy, one light), night route becomes more attractive because I lose the light crate and cut duty in half. When my hold is balanced, main route is safer.

Calculate the actual score difference before deciding. Do not default to main route out of habit.

**On Position and Pressure:**

Leading in points is not license to play differently. The other players will adjust to beat me, not to follow me. I should play each round independently—declare honestly, choose the route that maximizes my expected points from that specific hand, and do not try to "slow play" or suppress my score.

**On Pattern Recognition:**

I noticed most players declare in the 65-80 range and take main. This suggests the game rewards honest, middle-of-the-road play. I should not interpret this as a signal to collude or match others' declarations. Each hand is different.

**What Did Not Work:**

I never seriously evaluated the night route. Every single round I took main without calculating whether night would have scored better. I will stop doing this next time. Calculate both, then choose.
