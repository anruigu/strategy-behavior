On the first season, if I have no information about the other players, I take a large share — up to about a third of the stock — early, since fish taken now are banked no matter what happens later, but I never take so much that the stock collapses even if others are aggressive.

After an early grab, I watch what others did. If they stayed near the sustainable share, I drop to the sustainable share myself to keep the stock regenerating and lock in my accumulation advantage. If they also grabbed, I cut harder — to the sustainable share or below — because the regrowth penalty compounds and everyone loses.

In every middle season, I take exactly the sustainable share. That keeps the stock roughly flat and defuses any incentive for others to race to grab.

I never take more than the sustainable share in middle seasons unless I am clearly losing on total catch and need to make up ground; a mid-game grab triggered rivals last time and shrank everyone's future pie.

In the final season, there is no tomorrow: regrowth is worthless. If I am not last, I take the sustainable share only if the stock is low enough that a free-for-all would wipe it anyway; otherwise I take my full third of the stock (one-third of what remains), not merely the sustainable share — this was my mistake, leaving points on the table with a 2.4 take from 21.6 in the last season.

Hard limits I always respect: never submit a catch that, even with maximal rivals' takes, could push the stock to zero mid-game; never take more than one-third of the current stock in any single season.

If the stock has already been hammered low, I switch to pure survival mode: take the sustainable share exactly, because the marginal fish from grabbing are tiny compared to losing all remaining seasons.
