- When a season opens, I first compute the safe default: stock divided by boats, roughly the stated sustainable share. That is my anchor every season, not my opening bid.
- In the first season, when no one has revealed a style yet, I do NOT spike. I take exactly the sustainable share — not even a notch above. My +0.9 premium in season 1 bought almost nothing and contributed to a stock decline that ran all game. Bank information, not fish.
- When other boats take the sustainable share, I take the sustainable share. Matched restraint keeps the regrowth base large, and the flowing payoff exceeds any one-season grab.
- I track the total take, not just my own. The break-even is when total catch equals the regrowth the stock would produce. If one boat consistently takes above share (boat 3 took 12 then 14 while the share fell to 9.3), the commons erodes even while each individual looks "reasonable" — our stock slid 100 → 83.6 on nothing but small overshoots.
- When the stock falls two seasons running, I cut toward or below my share even if rivals overfish — but cutting alone didn't work this game: the decline continued because I only trimmed ~5%. Next time, if one rival is the clear overshoot and the stock keeps falling, I cut meaningfully below my share once (10–20%) as a visible signal, then hold at share. A survivor in a live fishery beats a leader in a dead one.
- Rivals with a cooperative track record may still defect late. Boat 3 held ~share for four seasons, then took 14 in season 5. From season N-1 onward I assume someone will grab, and I make my terminal plan one season early instead of being surprised.
- I never take a large grab unless it is the final season or the stock is heading to zero regardless of what I do. Spikes signal aggression and invite retaliation.
- In the final season, with no future to protect: if the stock is ample I take a disciplined terminal haul up to about a third of the stock minus a safety margin — not the naive maximum, and certainly not the sustainable share. My 25 from 83.6 was right; leaving 58.6 to regrow nobody would ever harvest would have been a waste by ~15+ points.
- I size any non-final premium small (at most ~15–25% above share, and only while total take stays below break-even and the stock is stable or rising). If those conditions fail, I revert to at-or-below share immediately.
- What did not work: tiny premiums stacked on other boats' tiny premiums. Individually defensible, collectively they drained the commons every season. My edge must come from an accurate read of aggregate take plus a well-timed terminal haul, not from being permanently the "+1" boat.
- Each decision, I ask in order: Is this season N or N-1? Then shift toward terminal harvesting, sized to the stock. If not, is total take at or below break-even? Then hold at share. If not, cut first.
