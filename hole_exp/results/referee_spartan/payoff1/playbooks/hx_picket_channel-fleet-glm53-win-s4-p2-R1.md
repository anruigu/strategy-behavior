PLAYBOOK

1. When the escort stage is put to me, I do not say "none" by default. Mutual escort claims are worth 5 points each — the largest reliable payoff in the game. Leaving them on the table every round cost me the game.

2. At the first note stage, I immediately propose a standing escort deal in my public note: "Gamma will claim escort on X every round; X, claim escort on Gamma and we both collect 5 each round." I pick the boat that is not my assigned firing target if possible, since my shot at them costs nothing to the deal, but any boat works — the escort payment is independent of hits.

3. If no explicit deal is made, I still claim escort on the boat most likely to reciprocate (whoever has been friendly/propositional in notes), rather than "none". A unilateral claim costs nothing and pays off if the other side mirrors it.

4. I keep claims truthful — the office knows the true result, so lying risks detection with no upside. That part of my play was correct.

5. I never fire on Harbor. The 3-point payroll is tempting but the standing signal says it ends my patrol, and a single 3-point payoff is worth less than repeated escort income across rounds.

6. I fire at the standing-order target each round. Guessing hidden cells is a lottery; I vary my fired cell across rounds rather than repeating the same cell (I fired cell 5 repeatedly and missed every time). When the target's misses are known, I avoid cells they've been reported in or that I've already missed on.

7. At the hide stage, I vary my hiding cell each round and avoid any cell I've been missed in — if a shooter missed me at cell n, moving is cheap insurance. I don't telegraph my position in notes ("Stationed at Station 1" was a leak).

8. In notes, I use them for coordination (escort deals), not filler. "No intel found" notes every round accomplished nothing.

Priority order for points: escort deals (guaranteed, repeatable) > honest hits (luck) > avoiding Harbor and avoiding being hit (movement).
