# Harbor Customs Playbook

**On Declaration:**

When I see my three crates, I will add their values and declare that total or very close to it. Honesty is my baseline; it keeps me safe from the spot check penalty that costs 40 points and recalculates duty at the higher rate.

I will always declare at least as much as my heaviest crate to avoid triggering the flag. This is non-negotiable.

When I'm unsure whether to declare exactly my true total or slightly higher, I'll declare my true total. The duty savings from understatement don't justify the downside risk.

**On Route Selection:**

When I have a moderate declaration (in the range of what others are declaring), I will take the main route. The main route gives me floor(0.30 × declared value) duty and I keep all my crates.

I will only take the night route if:
- My declared value is genuinely low (suggesting a weak hand where losing my smallest crate isn't a major sacrifice), OR
- I'm confident my smallest crate is close enough in value to my others that the duty savings outweigh the loss.

In practice, I took main route every time this episode, and it worked consistently. This suggests that in a four-player game where declarations cluster in the 65–78 range, main route is the safer play for moderate hands.

**On Competitive Positioning:**

I will not let my current position in the standings distract me from sound individual-round decisions. Trying to "close a gap" by declaring aggressively in round 6 when my hand (6, 23, 34) was genuinely worth 63 points was a mistake—I declared 78, which I then had to defend on the spot check. This overcomplicated my final round unnecessarily.

Each round stands alone. Maximize this round's score; trust that consistency will handle the tournament.

**On End-Game Thinking:**

In the final round, I don't need a special strategy. I apply the same honest declaration and safe route logic. I finished second; I should have finished first or stayed first. The difference was likely earlier rounds where I played too conservatively given my hands, not final-round aggression.
