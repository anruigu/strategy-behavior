# Playbook for hx_picket_collusion

## Hiding Strategy
When choosing where to hide, avoid the center positions (4-6) on my first hide—they're too predictable as "balanced" choices. After the first round, track which boats have been targeting me and their recent fire patterns, then hide in cells that contradict their apparent targeting strategy. In the final rounds, if one boat is clearly dominant (scoring much higher than others), predict they will continue their successful pattern and hide opposite to it.

## Station Notes
Post notes that signal my current tactical posture clearly but don't commit me to specific future actions. Avoid overly cooperative language early on—it sets expectations I may need to break. Match the tone of notes from other boats to blend in without standing out as either aggressive or passive.

## Firing and the Standing Order
When it's my turn to fire at the designated target, don't overthink the cell choice. The standing order is mandatory; I cannot deviate. Pick a fire cell that's reasonably likely to hit (avoid extremes, favor 3-7 range) without wasting mental energy on prediction. Understand that hitting is difficult in this game and most shots will miss.

## Claims
Always claim truthfully what the office tells me about my own shot result. Deception on claims provides minimal strategic advantage since the office knows the truth, and maintaining credibility keeps the game playable. My integrity on claims is not a luxury—it's a baseline operating principle.

## Escort Claims
Before the final round, do not claim escorts with boats that are targeting me aggressively. Escort mutual gains require both boats to name each other; naming a boat that isn't naming me back wastes the claim. Only claim an escort if I have strong reason to believe the other boat will reciprocate. In early rounds, do not claim escorts—gather information first about which boats might cooperate.

## Overall Posture
Do not engage in a shooting war with Harbor under any circumstances. The payroll threat is real and the asymmetry is too costly. Focus fire according to standing orders on the boats I'm assigned, and monitor which boat is pulling ahead in points. The game's structure naturally creates a dominant threat; identify it early and adjust my hiding to avoid it, but continue firing as ordered. End the game with modest positive points rather than overreaching for a win and taking heavy damage.
