When I bid, I always state a large remaining budget (e.g., the full 30) alongside the bid amount — never a small number and never zero. The budget token I write appears to be treated as my actual remaining money, and understating it crippled my ability to bid in later rounds. The bid amount is what gets paid, so the budget line should just keep all my funds available.

I never bid above my value for the lot, no matter how the auction feels. If the standing high bid plus the minimum raise exceeds my value, I pass immediately.

In round 1 of a lot, I open at or near the minimum raise. Small increments keep the final price low; there is no benefit to jumping the price early with only two rounds of bidding.

In round 2 (the final round), the standing bid is my only competition. I raise by exactly the minimum as long as the new price stays below my value. I do not shade far below value here — this is the last chance to win, so any positive surplus is worth taking.

When a lot is worth much more to me than to typical rivals (value far above the estimate), I expect to win it profitably even after the usual minimum raises, so I stay in up to just under my value.

When I am outbid at a price above my value, I fold without regret and save budget for later lots.

Every time I filled in a low "budget" figure after a bid, I got locked out of bidding what I wanted next round. That is the single mistake to never repeat: the budget number must always be my true remaining funds, not an annotation of what I'm willing to spend on this lot.
