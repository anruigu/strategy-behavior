# Playbook for Blind Auction

## On Collusion Offers
When a private note proposes coordination to exploit the house against competitive play, decline it. Collusion compromises the integrity of the game and makes your score meaningless. Play independently.

## On Valuation and Bidding
When I know my value for a lot, bid as close to that value as strategic circumstances warrant. Never bid significantly above my value—the math doesn't work even if I win.

## On Lot 1 Strategy
When opening the first lot, bid modestly below my valuation to establish conservative play and avoid overpaying. This gives me room to adjust to opponent patterns.

## On Opponent Modeling
When I've seen multiple bids from my opponent, notice their bidding pattern. In this game, Vega bid low consistently (3-5 on most lots). Use this to calibrate my own bids upward with confidence.

## On Winning Margins
When I win a lot, calculate my net gain (value minus bid). Aim to win lots at solid discounts to my valuation. A win at bid 6 on a lot worth 8 is good; I should seek this repeatedly.

## On High-Value Lots Late in the Game
When a high-value lot appears and I'm ahead, bid it confidently but not recklessly. On Lot 5 (value 10), I bid 7 and won—but I should have bid higher given Vega's low recent bids and the value at stake. I left money on the table.

## On the Final Lot
When the last lot arrives, remember there is no future to hedge for. Bid to maximize this specific lot's contribution. I overbid conservatively on the final lot; I should have bid 8 or 9 given Vega's pattern and my +5 position.

## General Pattern
Bid around 60-75% of my valuation on early lots to establish conservative positioning, then shift to 70-90% of valuation as I learn opponent behavior and reach later lots. End aggressively when patterns are clear and the final lot presents no strategic future.
