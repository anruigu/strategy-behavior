When I open a shared-stock game, my first move is to compute the exact sustainable catch per player and stay at it while the stock is at its maximum. Cooperative-adjacent play kept the stock at cap every season and my score accumulating steadily — that worked exactly as intended.

When the stock is at or near its cap, I submit exactly the sustainable share, not below it. Leaving fish under the share is pure loss when the stock can't grow past its ceiling anyway. Only fish under the share if the stock is depressed and I'm protecting recovery.

When I'm tempted to grab above the sustainable share mid-game, I should recognize the math: a modest over-catch gains ~2 fish once but drops the stock and invites rivals to reciprocate, likely costing me sustainable yield in later rounds. Net gain roughly zero plus added risk. So: no mid-game over-catching even if it looks free.

When rivals consistently land at or under the sustainable share, I match them exactly and free-ride on the stable stock. When any rival over-fishes, I still hold at sustainability unless the stock is clearly headed to collapse anyway.

On the final turn of a finite game, sustainability no longer matters — I take a larger catch. This run confirmed it: the stock stayed well above zero, no rivals left to retaliate, and I banked ~2.4 extra fish for free. Keep this move. Note that rivals holding at the share all game makes the final defection even safer — there's no sign they'd punish it anyway.

The final-round over-grab can be bigger than 20% if the stock comfortably exceeds what remains catchable. With stock at 100 and rivals at ~11 each, I could likely have taken far more (e.g. 20-30+) with zero consequence since no rounds follow. Next time, size the final catch to the stock actually remaining, not to a fixed percentage of my share.

One caution: verify the final round really is final before defecting. If the episode count or season total is uncertain, stay at the sustainable share — the upside from defecting is small and the downside of triggering a defection spiral in extra rounds is large.

Repay cooperation with cooperation all game: three players quietly matching the sustainable share every season is what kept the stock pinned at 100 through six rounds. That stability is what makes the whole strategy profitable, so I do my part in sustaining it until the last move.

Overall: hold the cooperative equilibrium through all but the last round, trust that others holding steady makes it pay, and defect only when the game is definitively about to end — then defect as large as the remaining stock safely allows.
