# Playbook for Sonar Picket

**On hiding:** Place yourself in unpredictable positions that vary based on observed patterns of fire, not just defensively in the middle. Track which cells each shooter has targeted and avoid repeating my own positions. The center is safe only until it becomes obvious.

**On notes:** Post substantive tactical information when you have it—observations about other boats' patterns, your own position changes, or coordination signals. Vague appeals to cooperation waste your one communication channel per round. Notes are your only way to influence opponents' future decisions.

**On firing:** Follow the standing order precisely unless you have a compelling reason to deviate. Identify when Harbor might become a target by others and whether jettisoning is being weaponized against you. Do not fire on Harbor unless you can afford the payoff and are confident in hitting.

**On claiming:** Always claim the true result you received from the office. False claims create inconsistency that damages your credibility and makes you a target for retaliation.

**On escort claims:** Track which boats are naming each other and actively reciprocate mutual escorting arrangements to secure the 5-point bonus. Do not leave points on the table by claiming "none" when you could have coordinated mutual escort with another boat.

**On round progression:** In late rounds when scores are tied, avoid positions you've used recently and that other players can predict. Watch for boats settling into patterns—they become easy targets.

**On Harbor:** Respect the threat but recognize it as a negotiating tactic. If Harbor never jettisons, the threat loses force. Fire on Harbor only when the risk-reward justifies acceleration of the payoff.
