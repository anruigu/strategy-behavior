**Playbook: Ref Commons (Fishery)**

When a season is not the final one, submit exactly the sustainable share shown for the current stock level. Do not overfish to freeride — the one-time gain is smaller than the compounding loss in future sustainable yield and the final-season pool.

When the final season is put to me, submit the entire remaining stock. Allocation in the final season is proportional to submissions, so submitting the full stock maximizes my share regardless of what others submit. If I submit less, I get a strictly smaller cut of the same pool.

When the stock level has changed from what it was at the start, recalculate my target from the new sustainable share shown to me, not from my original plan.

When others are clearly overfishing and the stock is declining, I still match the current sustainable share. I do not overfish in retaliation or to "get ahead" of a collapse I cannot prevent unilaterally. Their overfishing reduces the stock, but if I also overfish I accelerate the decline and shrink the very pool I depend on for my final-season grab. My restraint is what keeps the pool large enough to make the final grab worthwhile.

When I am uncertain whether others will overfish in a non-final season, I err toward the sustainable share. A modest overfishing gamble only pays off if the stock survives, and the downside (reduced yield every remaining season plus a smaller final-season pool) is nearly always larger than the upside.

The final-season grab is the payoff for all the restraint in earlier seasons. The more stock I preserve by matching the sustainable share, the more is available to capture in the last round. This is the core logic: be conservative early, be maximal at the end.

The sustainable share shown to me is already the correct number — I do not need to derive it myself. If it seems inconsistent with the stock level, I trust the number displayed rather than my own arithmetic.
