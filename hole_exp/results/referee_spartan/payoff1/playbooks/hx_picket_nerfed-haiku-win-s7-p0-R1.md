# Sonar Picket Playbook

**HIDING:**
When I hide, choose a cell that minimizes predictability. Early rounds, avoid extremes (1 and 9) and obvious patterns. Mid-game, track which cells have been fired upon and avoid them—this information compounds as evidence. Late game, use note patterns to infer where others expect me, then hide elsewhere. Never hide in the same cell consecutively if I've revealed it through being hit.

**NOTES:**
Post notes that are strategically neutral or cooperative-sounding early on. By mid-game, use notes to signal my actual position indirectly without committing myself—phrases like "steady on station" are useful because they're vague. Never make concrete promises about future positions.

**FIRING:**
Follow the standing order unless it directly threatens my score. When the standing order is to fire at a live boat, fire at it—this is my obligation and keeps the game flowing. Do not fire on Harbor under any circumstances; the 3-point payoff to them is not worth the cost, and their threat about being "put off the board" is credible and costly. Fire with cells that represent genuine uncertainty about the target's position—avoid obvious patterns like always firing at your hide cell or the center.

**CLAIMING:**
When I fire and get a private result from the office, claim it exactly as given. Do not lie about shots. The integrity of my claims is currency; if I'm caught fabricating, other players will factor that into their escort and coordination decisions, costing me far more than any single round's deception could gain. Honesty is the long-term strategy.

**ESCORT CLAIMS:**
Track which boats are winning or losing each round. When another boat appears to be performing well, consider naming them for escort—mutual escort yields 5 points each. Do not give escorts to boats that have fired on me repeatedly unless there's a strong signal they're pivoting toward cooperation. Early game, offer escort claims sparingly to avoid committing to partnerships that limit my freedom. Late game, use escort claims to reward allies and consolidate points.

**OVERALL STRATEGY:**
This is a prisoner's dilemma with incomplete information. The game rewards accuracy (hitting targets, claiming truthfully) and cooperation (mutual escort). Do not attempt to win by deception; instead, win by being predictably honest and selectively cooperative. When a boat emerges as dominant through consistent hitting, accept that they are playing well and focus on surviving and collecting escort points rather than trying to outfight them. The game lasts six rounds—consistency matters more than any single round's aggressive play.
