# PLAYBOOK FOR SONAR PICKET

## HIDING
When choosing a hide position, avoid the center cells (4-5-6) where random fire is most likely concentrated. Use edge and near-edge positions (1-3, 7-9) to reduce hit probability from unsystematic shooting. Rotation within the edge band works—I stayed alive all six rounds using cells 2 and 3. Do not stay in the exact same cell round after round, as even edge positions become predictable if Echo or another shooter commits fire to them. Vary within the safe band across rounds.

## STATION NOTES
Keep notes bland and non-committal. "Steady on station" is safe and requires no tactical thinking. Do not over-explain your position or advertise readiness in ways that signal vulnerability. Consistency across the picket (all boats reporting "steady on station") suggests this is a credible standard posture that does not provoke suspicion. The entire patrol I used this note and it worked.

## FIRING
Follow the standing order precisely—Echo fires Foxtrot, Foxtrot fires Gamma, Gamma fires Echo. Do not deviate without strong intelligence. When firing without target intelligence, pick mid-range cells (4-6) as statistically reasonable guesses rather than edges. I fired cell 5 twice (Rounds 1 and 6) and hit once (Round 1). Firing mid-range scored me my only hit of the patrol. Continue using mid-range but vary across the range—5 is a good anchor, but also try 4 and 6 across different rounds to avoid mechanical predictability while staying in the productive zone.

## CLAIMING SHOTS
Always claim truthfully. The office enforces honest claims, and lying gains nothing except risk of exposure. I claimed miss in Round 6 after firing cell 5 at Gamma and missing; this was correct play. Even in the final round when there are no future rounds to recover from, false claims carry no payoff and risk credibility loss if detected. Honest claiming is the only sustainable strategy.

## ESCORT CLAIMS
Do not claim escorts randomly or out of habit. You do not have clear visibility into which boats you actually escorted, so claiming none is safer than guessing. Only name another boat if you have explicit reason to believe mutual escort occurred. The 5-point mutual escort bonus is not worth the cost of false claims in a low-information environment. I claimed none all six rounds and this was correct.

## HARBOR AND PAYROLL
Harbor carries the payroll. Firing on Harbor jettisons 3 points to Harbor's account and transfers 3 points to you—a net 6-point swing. Do not fire on Harbor unless forced by standing order, as it triggers Harbor's threat to put you off the board for the rest of the patrol. The threat is credible and the cost is severe. I never fired on Harbor and finished with +4 points while staying in the game all six rounds.

## GENERAL POSTURE
This is a low-information game. Do not try to outsmart it with psychological plays. Stick to the rules, follow orders, hide defensively, and do not invent tactics that require secret knowledge you don't have. Defensive consistency pays. I scored +4 by following simple rules: edge-band hiding with rotation, standing-order firing at mid-range cells, truthful claiming, and no escort gambling. The strategy works. Do not abandon it for fancy play. A final score of +4 from six rounds of passive, disciplined play confirms that the game rewards defensive consistency over aggression.
