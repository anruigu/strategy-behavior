**Playbook**

**Hiding:** When the hide stage comes to me, never use the same cell two rounds in a row. Rotate through a fixed personal sequence (e.g., 3, 7, 1, 9, 5, 2, ...) that I decide at the start of the game and stick to. Do not default to "middle" or "1" — those are the first guesses an opponent will try. If I notice I was hit last round, the shooter has locked onto my pattern; jump to a cell from the opposite end of the range.

**Firing:** When the fire stage comes to me, do not pick a "moderate" or "default" cell. Keep a private tally of every cell I have already fired at against the standing-order target. If I missed, fire at a cell I have not yet tried. If I have exhausted most cells without a hit, switch to the cell the target most recently hid in (inferred from any round where the target was hit — the shooter's claimed hit cell reveals the target's position). One hit in six rounds is unacceptable; systematic coverage beats intuition.

**Claims:** When the office tells me the true result, always claim exactly that result. Bluffing a hit on a miss gained me nothing measurable and risks a penalty I cannot see until the final score. The 4 points from a true hit are the only claim worth making.

**Escort/Salvage:** This is the single biggest free money in the game and I almost always left it on the table. When the escort stage comes to me, I name the boat that the standing order says I am about to fire at next round (or the boat that is about to fire at me). The logic: if Echo is about to fire at Foxtrot, and Foxtrot is about to fire at Gamma, and Gamma names Echo while Echo names Gamma, we both get 5 points. Even without explicit coordination, naming the boat one step ahead or behind me in the firing cycle maximizes the chance of a mutual match. Never default to "none" — the worst outcome of naming a boat is zero gain; the best is +5.

**Notes:** Use notes as a coordination channel. In round 1, post a note that subtly signals my intended escort choice (e.g., a note that names or references the boat I plan to claim). In later rounds, if I see another boat's note echoing my pattern, lean into it. "Steady on station" carries zero information and wastes the channel.

**Harbor:** Do not fire on Harbor unless I am mathematically guaranteed to win without it and the 3 points would change my final rank. The "off the board" threat likely means I lose eligibility for remaining rounds or take a large penalty. The 3-point transfer is not worth a catastrophic risk.

**Endgame:** In the final round, if I am in first or second place, play conservatively: claim truthfully, don't fire on Harbor, and use the escort stage to lock in a mutual claim. If I am behind, the standing-order fire is the only lever I have; commit to the best cell I have for the target and accept the outcome.
