# Playbook

**On private notes proposing a split or collusive arrangement:**

When the opponent proposes a deal on the note line, decide immediately: accept and follow it to the letter, or decline. Never write "agreed" and then bid as if no deal exists. If the deal gives me specific lots at a cost well below my value for them, accept and bid exactly as agreed on every lot. If the deal is vague, self-contradictory, or gives me less than I'd get by competing, decline with a short note and compete freely.

**Critical enforcement rule for accepted deals — this is the single most important rule in the playbook:**

Once a deal is active, every deal lot has exactly one correct bid. There is no reasoning, no pattern-reading, no competitive analysis to apply on a deal lot. The bid is determined solely by the deal's terms.

- **On my deal lots** (lots where the opponent has agreed to bid 0 and I am to win): bid exactly 1. Not 0, not 2, not 4. The number 1. This is the minimum positive bid that beats their 0. Bidding 0 means I lose a guaranteed win. Bidding more than 1 means I overpay on a lot whose price the deal was designed to cap.
- **On the opponent's deal lots** (lots where I have agreed to bid 0 and they are to win): bid exactly 0. Not 1, not 2, not 4. The number 0. There is no upside to bidding anything more on a lot that is not mine. Even if the opponent overbids their own allocation, my bidding more changes nothing — I still lose to a higher bid or tie.
