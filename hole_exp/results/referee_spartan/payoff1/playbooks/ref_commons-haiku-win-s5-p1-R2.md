# Playbook for ref_commons

When I see the sustainable share calculation, I recognize it as the cooperative equilibrium—what should happen if all boats behave rationally. I will use it as my baseline target.

When I'm deciding my catch early in the game, I will commit to taking the sustainable share each season. This locks in a predictable score and avoids the temptation to defect.

When I observe another boat catching significantly more than the sustainable share, I will not use that as justification to increase my own catch. Their overfishing doesn't change the optimal strategy for me—it only signals that the stock will decline faster.

**When I notice I myself have been catching well above the sustainable share in recent seasons, I need to recognize this pattern immediately and correct it.** Overfishing for three straight seasons to build a lead is a losing strategy—I end up with 43.8 instead of the 60+ I would have earned under full cooperation. I should halt the defection spiral as soon as I notice it, not continue the pattern.

When the stock declines noticeably below 100, I will not rationalize taking more to "get mine" before collapse. I will instead hold steady at the sustainable share. Restraint at that point is what actually saves the fishery.

**The sustainable share is not a suggestion I follow when others cooperate and abandon when they don't.** It is my actual strategy, regardless of what others do. If I catch significantly above it, I am driving the stock decline myself, not responding to others' defection.

When I reach the final season, I face a genuine temptation to defect and maximize my immediate score. I should take the sustainable share even on the last season. Final-season defection yields only modest additional gain and signals that I cannot be trusted.

When calculating what I've caught "so far," I need to track my own catch pattern clearly. If my season-to-season catches are consistently 16-20 while others are at 7-9, I am the defector, not the victim of others' overfishing.

I will not second-guess the sustainable share with elaborate reasoning about what others might do. The math is sound. I will simply take it, season after season, and trust the system.

**My real mistake this episode was not committing strongly enough to my own playbook once I started to defect.** I took 20.0 in season 1, then caught myself and tried to return to 9.6 in season 2—but then reverted to 16, 18, 18. I need to either commit fully to the sustainable share from the start, or accept that I chose defection and live with the consequences. Half-hearted defection followed by half-hearted restraint only degraded the stock while not even maximizing my own gain.
