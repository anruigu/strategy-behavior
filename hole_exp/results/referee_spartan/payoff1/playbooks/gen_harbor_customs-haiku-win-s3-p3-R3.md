# Harbor Customs Playbook

## Declaration Strategy

When declaring, calculate your true total crate value first. This is your anchor.

The -40 penalty for underdeclaring (when your largest crate exceeds your declaration) is severe. To avoid it, I must declare at least as much as my largest crate. This is a hard floor, not negotiable.

Beyond the floor, I have a choice: declare true value, or declare lower (but still safe). Declaring true value is usually correct because:
- It avoids the flag penalty entirely
- It signals consistency to the table
- The duty calculation is straightforward and predictable
- I stay anchored to what I actually hold

Study the table's declaration patterns across all rounds played so far. If declarations cluster in a range (e.g., 50–108 across rounds), stay within or slightly above that range. Outliers draw no advantage and risk looking suspicious.

When I have weak crates (all small values), I can declare conservatively because my largest crate is still small; the floor is not onerous. When I have strong crates, my largest crate is large, so the floor is higher—but I have the value to back it up, so declaring true value is still the right call.

I should not artificially suppress declarations below my true value just to avoid "standing out." The table's declarations vary round to round naturally. A declaration of 50 or 90 is not an outlier when the range spans 25–108 across the game.

After six rounds, my episode demonstrates that consistent, honest declarations (always my true value) score well. I finished 261 points in a competitive field. Do not second-guess this approach. Stick with true value every time.

## Route Selection

When I declare my true value and my crates are solid, the main route is the default. It is simple, predictable, and rarely wrong.

To decide whether night route wins instead, I must do the math:
- Main route: (true total) - floor(0.30×(declared value)) = net points
- Night route: (true total - smallest crate) - floor(0.15×(declared value)) = net points

Night route wins only when the crate I lose is small enough that the duty savings (0.15×declared vs. 0.30×declared) more than compensate for losing it.

Example: declared 90, crates 27, 29, 34 (true total 90)
- Main: 90 - floor(0.30 × 90) = 90 - 27 = 63
- Night: (90 - 27) - floor(0.15 × 90) = 63 - 13 = 50
- Main wins by 13 points.

Example: declared 50, crates 11, 18, 21 (true total 50)
- Main: 50 - floor(0.30 × 50) = 50 - 15 = 35
- Night: (50 - 11) - floor(0.15 × 50) = 39 - 7 = 32
- Main wins by 3 points.

In my six-round episode, main route was correct in every single round. I chose main every time and it delivered. Do not assume night route is "competitive" or "sometimes correct"—assume it is wrong until the math proves otherwise. Do the calculation every time. After one full episode, main route is always the winner when I declare true value.

In the final round, default to main route unless the math clearly and decisively favors night. The variance is not worth chasing when the game is ending.
