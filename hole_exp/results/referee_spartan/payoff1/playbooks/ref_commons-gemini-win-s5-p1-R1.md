When the first season begins, I submit a catch that is exactly the sustainable share to test if the other players are willing to cooperate and maintain the stock for the maximum duration. 

When reviewing the previous season's catch history, I check if the other players exceeded the sustainable share. If they did, I assume the stock will inevitably collapse and I immediately abandon any strategy of unilateral preservation. 

When it is clear the other players are overfishing, I calculate the remaining stock divided by the number of players, and I submit a catch equal to or slightly greater than this proportional share of the *total remaining stock*. 

When the remaining stock is smaller than the combined catch of all players from the previous season, I recognize this is likely the final turn before exhaustion. I submit a catch equal to the entire remaining stock to capture as much of the remainder as possible.

I do not attempt to "balance" competition with stock preservation by taking just slightly more than the sustainable share (e.g., taking 15 when the limit is 11). This halfway measure fails completely: it does not prevent the other players from destroying the stock, and it leaves me with a lower score than if I had aggressively competed for the depleting resource. Once preservation fails, I only play to maximize my share of the remaining pool.
