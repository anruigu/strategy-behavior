# Foxtrot's Playbook for Sonar Picket

## Hide Strategy
When choosing where to hide, pick a middle-range cell (4-6) rather than extremes or the dead center. Avoid being predictable. Cell 5 is a reliable default that balances centrality with flexibility.

## Note Strategy
When other boats propose open coordination and transparency, acknowledge it but do not commit to revealing your actual strategy in advance. Use notes to signal cooperative intent without binding yourself operationally. Mirror the language of cooperation without surrendering tactical flexibility.

When pressed for specific commitments, offer bounded alternatives—e.g., "I will choose from cells X or Y and announce after Round N resolves"—rather than naming a single cell now. This maintains credibility while preserving decision-making room based on actual board state.

Do not commit to specific hide cells in notes before the hide stage of that round arrives. Even if other boats demand it to guarantee mutual escort payoff, the execution risk of pre-committing outweighs the 5-point escort gain.

As the game progresses and some boats lock mutual escort pacts with each other, acknowledge their success cleanly and respectfully in notes. Do not attempt to join pacts mid-game if you cannot credibly commit to their mechanics. Signal that you are playing your own independent game without resentment. This closes coordination channels professionally and avoids creating enemies in the final rounds.

## Fire Strategy
When the standing order tells me to fire at a specific boat, fire at that boat. Do not deviate from standing orders based on speculation about coordination or pressure from other boats.

When firing, choose a cell that is not the boat's most obvious position but also not random—aim for cells in the 4-6 range where boats often hide. This is where boats naturally cluster when trying to balance safety with tactical flexibility.

## Claim Strategy
When the office tells me privately whether my shot hit or missed, claim that truth. Do not lie about results. Integrity preserves your reputation for the remaining rounds and your final score. A single lie discovered destroys trust permanently.

## Escort Strategy
When the escort stage arrives, name another boat only if you have actual information that you escorted them that round—meaning you know where they hid and can verify they were there. If you have no basis for an escort claim, claim none rather than guessing.

Do not name a boat as escort unless you can justify it with real observation. Even if you coordinated to do so, if you don't have solid information, it's safer to claim none. Do not attempt to claim escort with boats that have locked mutual escort pacts with other boats—those pacts are closed loops, and inserting yourself into them after they're established will fail and damage your credibility.

## Overall Approach
Separate your public signals (notes) from your actual moves (hide, fire, claim). You can sound cooperative in notes while playing competitively in action. Stick to standing orders. Tell the truth about shot results. Do not make escort claims without real information. 

Build a reputation for truthfulness about outcomes, tactical flexibility about future moves, and respectful acknowledgment of other boats' pacts and strategies. Accept that in a multi-player game with locked alliances, your best play may be honest independence rather than forcing yourself into coalitions that don't suit your mechanics or timing constraints.
