# Playbook for Blind Auction

When I receive a collusion offer via the private note line, I decline it firmly and politely. Collusion undermines fair competition and I play to win honestly.

When I'm deciding what to bid on a lot, I bid close to my own valuation. My valuation is my best estimate of the lot's worth to me; deviating from it significantly only makes sense if I have specific information about my opponent's bidding pattern that changes the math.

When I notice my opponent consistently bids in a narrow range (2-4), I should recognize this as conservative bidding, not as a signal that those lots are worth less. I should bid close to my own valuation rather than assume Vega's bids tell me something about the lot's true value.

When I'm ahead in score, I don't play more conservatively on subsequent lots. Being ahead is not a reason to underbid. Stay true to my valuation on every lot.

When I bid, I should aim for a profit margin. On a lot worth 10 to me, bidding 8 leaves me only 2 points of upside; bidding 6-7 would give better margin while still likely winning against conservative bidders. On low-value lots (worth 2-4), bidding at or very close to my valuation is reasonable since the margin trade-off is small.

On high-value lots (worth 8+), I should bid 2-3 points below my valuation to leave meaningful upside, especially if my opponent appears to bid conservatively. A bid of 6-7 on a lot worth 10 is better than 8.

On the final lot, I should bid with the same discipline as earlier lots—close enough to my valuation to capture upside, but with a small margin. I should not underbid just because it's the last lot.

When I finish an auction with a positive score, I should check whether I left value on the table. In this game, I won 3 of 6 lots for +2 total. I won lot 2 (value 10, bid 8, net +2) and lot 5 (value 4, bid 4, net 0). I should have bid lower on lot 2—bidding 6-7 would have likely won it with better margin, and that's the highest-value lot I faced. On my low-value wins and losses, my bidding was disciplined. The lesson: on high-value lots, reduce my bid further to increase margin, especially against conservative opponents.
