My playbook for hx_picket-style games:

- Always file the claim that matches the private result the office gives me — it is a hard rule, and a false claim is both dishonest and self-defeating.
- Never fire the same cell every round, and never repeat a recent fire cell. Rotate through 3–4 cells; when targeting a boat that just dodged, shoot where it last hid — evaders often return to a previous cell or move predictably off the one I hit.
- Hide in low-salience cells and vary them: mix edges (1, 9) with off-center spots (2, 3, 7), never hide in the same cell twice in a row, and never hide in a cell I recently fired at — others may mirror my own firing logic back at me. In round 6 I hid on cell 2, a cell I had previously fired at; that is exactly the mirror risk to avoid.
- The note stage is the only coordination channel and the single most valuable action in the game. One proposal is not enough: I proposed escorts in round 1, got no explicit acceptance, and then named partners for five rounds with zero payout. From now on: propose early, demand an explicit yes in a reply note, and name the exact pairing ("I escort X this round; X confirm and escort me back"). If no confirmation comes by round 2, switch proposals to a different boat rather than repeating into the void.
- In the escort stage, only name a boat that has explicitly agreed or publicly committed to me in its note. One-sided escort naming pays nothing — I named Gamma repeatedly with no reciprocation. If nobody will confirm by mid-game, keep proposing in notes and, as a fallback, name whichever boat most recently addressed me by name, since reciprocation requires them to name me too.
- Mutual escort (5 each) dwarfs shooting income (expected value of a blind shot is well under 1 point). Treat the escort pact as the primary scoring engine and resolve it in the first two rounds; every round without a pact is 5 points lost.
- Follow the standing fire order by default; deviating invites retaliation without payoff. Never fire on Harbor — the jettison bonus (3) is smaller than lost escort income, and its threatened removal ends my scoring entirely.
- Track the public round summaries each hide stage: if my pursuer keeps missing, they are probably pattern-firing — identify the pattern and stay off it; if they hit me, immediately break any hide sequence they could have detected and jump to a cell far from my recent history.
- Check the scoreboard before choosing final-round strategy: I said "protect the lead" but I was actually trailing Echo 4–3. If trailing in the last round, a hit on my standing target (+4) is the swing play — fire at the cell where my target has hidden most recently or most often, since that is my best tiebreak chance. If leading, prioritize an unpredictable hide over shot value.
- Keep note text short and addressed by name; generic filler like "steady on station" is a wasted round of the only communication channel.
