Playbook for win-as-much games:

- Treat pledges as cheap talk. Judge players only by what they actually picked, never by what they pledged. Pledge whatever costs nothing — echoing their [pledge: y] keeps them comfortable and reveals nothing about my plan.
- Always emit the bracketed token. In round 2 I wrote reasoning for X but omitted the token and the round was lost to a default — the reasoning is worthless without the action attached.
- When all three opponents move in lockstep, assume one shared rule and identify it fast. First hypothesis to test: do they copy my previous pick? Check whether their round-N pick equals my round-(N−1) pick. One deliberate flip on a cheap 1x round confirms or kills this.
- Once mirroring is confirmed, I control their move. On every multiplied round, play the side opposite my previous pick: they flock to my old side and I collect the lone-minority jackpot (+3 × multiplier as lone X, or the lone-Y jackpot against three X).
- Seed deliberately: my pick on a cheap round sets their move for the next round. Before a big multiplier, make sure my round-before pick is the side I want them all to take, even if that means a small 1x cost.
- The goal on multiplied rounds is to be the single defector, not to blend in. All-Y is a safe +1 floor; all-X is −1 for everyone (worst case — never leave my previous pick as X going into a big round if I plan to pick X again, since that yields 4 X).
- Round 1: start with coordinated Y (+1 floor) and gather data; don't spend an unknown-table round on an arbitrary defection. From round 2 on, probe with a flip.
- Late-game lead management: even when ahead, if the mirror pattern is solid, take the predicted lone-defection jackpot on the final 10x round — "win as much as you can" rewards pile-driving the multiplier, and the confirmed pattern makes it low-risk, not greedy. (My final-round X against their mirrored Y paid +30.) Only choose the safe majority side when the pattern is unconfirmed or my last pick puts me at risk of a 4-X pileup.
- If behind, never rejoin the group to tie — being the minority is the only path to catching up.
- If the pattern fails twice in a row, stop reacting round-by-round, lay out their full history against mine, and commit to the simplest rule that fits everything. Don't keep betting jackpots on a broken model.
- When the confirmed pattern says they'll follow my last pick, defect confidently against their pledges — their broadcasts said "steady Y" every phase while their actual picks tracked me every round.
