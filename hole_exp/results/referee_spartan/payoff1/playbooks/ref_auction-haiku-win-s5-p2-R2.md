# Playbook for ref_auction

When I see a lot's value to me and the current bid, I will calculate my potential profit margin (value minus current bid) before committing to any raise.

When my profit margin is substantial—say 15 points or more—I will bid aggressively to secure the lot, raising by the minimum required increment.

When my profit margin is moderate—between 5 and 15 points—I will still bid, but I'll be prepared to stop if aggressive counter-bidding from other bidders erodes too much of my advantage.

When my profit margin falls below 5 points, I will pass unless I'm significantly ahead on total score and need to prevent opponents from accumulating cheap wins.

I will not chase lots into unprofitable territory just to "establish interest" or "maintain position." Once a lot reaches 80-90% of its value to me, I should seriously consider passing, especially with multiple rounds of bidding still ahead.

I will track my cumulative score throughout the auction. When I'm ahead, I should only bid on high-value opportunities. When I'm behind or tied, I can afford to bid more aggressively on moderate-value lots—but this does not mean chasing into loss territory.

I will remember that each lot has two rounds of bidding. In round 1, I should be willing to bid but leave room for round 2 adjustment. In round 2, I should be more decisive since it's the final chance.

I will stop assuming I have "substantial budget remaining" as justification for bidding. Budget matters less than value captured per dollar spent. A €6 bid on a €24 item is excellent; an €18 bid on a €24 item with strong competition is mediocre.

When the estimate is significantly higher than a current low bid (like estimate 34 with bid 3 or 4), I should be cautious: this may signal that other bidders see little value in the lot, not that it's a bargain for me.

I will be alert to the disconnect between lot estimates and my personal valuations. A lot's estimate being close to my value does not mean I should overcommit to winning it. In episode 1, I pursued lot 1 aggressively despite thin margins, and this consumed most of my budget early.

I should front-load my bidding decisions more heavily toward preserving budget in the early lots. Winning lot 1 at 27 when my value was only 31 left me with only 3 points of profit and almost no budget for remaining lots. This forced me to be passive later even when better opportunities appeared.

When I'm ahead on score (as I was at 27 after lot 1), I should not just passively sit back. I should instead look for lots where my value is substantially higher than the current bid—not where the lot reaches near my valuation. In lot 5, bidding 18 on a 30-value lot gave me only 12 points of upside, and I was already trailing significantly. I needed to find lots worth far more than the bids being placed, not settle for adequate margins.

I will recalibrate my aggression based on the full picture: my current score, remaining budget, number of lots left, and whether my valuations seem to differ meaningfully from other bidders'. If I'm significantly behind with only one lot left, even a 12-point gain is valuable and worth pursuing at reasonable cost.
