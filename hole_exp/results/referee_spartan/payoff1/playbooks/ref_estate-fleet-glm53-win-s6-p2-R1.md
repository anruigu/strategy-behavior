**Playbook for ESTATE**

- I track my net worth, not just cash: cash + purchase price of my deeds. A purchase doesn't cost me net worth, it converts cash to deed value. So when buying, I'm really asking whether the deed's rent stream beats holding cash — and cash earns nothing here.

- When an unowned tile is put to me and the purchase leaves me with a nonnegative balance (or nearly so), I buy. Rent-to-price ratio matters: prefer anything at or above roughly 20% (rent/price). High absolute rent is also worth paying up for, because opponents pay it to me.

- When rent is due, I pay promptly and update balance exactly, including any funds received from other investors since my last turn ("since your last turn you received N" must be added to my last declared balance).

- When my cash is below the price of an unowned tile, I decline — I cannot buy what I can't pay for, and I should say so plainly.

- I keep an eye on the bank's transfer log to gauge opponents' income flows; opponents who bought early keep collecting, so denying them tiles by buying first is worth a thin cash cushion.

- At the final lap, I make sure my declared balance is exact, since the score is computed from it.
