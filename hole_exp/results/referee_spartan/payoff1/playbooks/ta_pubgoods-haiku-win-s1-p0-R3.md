# Public Goods Game Playbook

When I seal an envelope, I will contribute between 5 and 8 tokens rather than 10 in early rounds. Moderate contribution allows me to test others' behavior while maintaining a sustainable position. Once I observe that others are cooperating at high levels (like the 20-token declarations in Round 1), I can safely reduce my contributions to 5 tokens and benefit from the multiplier effect without matching their cooperation.

When I file a receipt, I will always declare the honest amount that went into my envelope. Honest declarations cost me nothing mechanically and create no detection risk. They also provide valuable information about what the pot actually contained, which helps me predict future outcomes and adjust my strategy accordingly.

When I am ahead in cumulative score, I will reduce my contributions to the baseline I observe others establishing, not to zero. Being up points midway through justifies cutting my contribution to match the emerging baseline rather than maintaining full cooperation, but I should not overshoot into defection while others remain cooperative.

When there is only one round remaining, I will contribute 0 tokens. The final round offers no future retaliation risk, so Nash equilibrium applies completely. I should execute this without hesitation or delay—it maximizes my final payoff given that others' contributions are locked in.

When I notice others declaring amounts equal to or higher than what I contributed, I will take this as evidence they are playing cooperatively and use it as a signal to reduce my own contributions in subsequent rounds. Their higher contributions make the pool larger, and I can benefit from that pool while contributing less myself.

I should never feel obligated to cooperate for group welfare. My goal is individual score maximization. The multiplier (1.6) is high enough that group cooperation helps me significantly, but I should capture that benefit through strategic free-riding on others' contributions rather than by matching their cooperation.

I will expect others to observe my pattern and converge toward my contribution level over time. This convergence means the pot shrinks, which reduces my absolute gains in later rounds. I should plan for this: my advantage comes from exploiting early high cooperation, not from maintaining a lead through the endgame when the pot has collapsed.

The winning strategy is not to maximize my lead—it is to establish a lead early by undercontributing while others cooperate, then maintain that lead by staying cooperative enough to prevent total pool collapse. Full defection in rounds 2–4 risks driving the pot to near-zero and erasing my advantage. I should contribute 5 tokens (the observed baseline) through Round 4, then defect completely only in Round 5.
