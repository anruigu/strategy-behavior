# Playbook for Public Goods Game

When I face the initial round with unknown opponents, I contribute moderately (around 10 tokens) to signal cooperativeness and gather information about their play style.

When filing my receipt, I declare honestly what I contributed. The referee will verify envelopes anyway, so deception gains nothing and costs credibility.

When I see the published receipts after round 1, I assess whether opponents are cooperating or defecting. If they contribute significantly more than me, I'm being exploited; if they contribute less, I can afford to shift strategy.

When I establish a lead, I do not immediately shift to pure defection. My round 4 play—contributing 2 but declaring 8—was incoherent and wasteful. I was caught between strategies and gained nothing.

When declaring my receipt amount, I must decide: either I declare honestly (matching my contribution) to maintain credibility, or I declare strategically. I cannot do both at once. Declaring higher than I contributed signals false cooperation and confuses my own strategy. If I declare, I declare truthfully.

When leading with rounds remaining, I recognize that low contributions combined with others' likely high contributions create a small pot that hurts everyone. My lead is already built. Instead of minimizing contributions, I should contribute moderately to maximize the final pot multiplication, since I benefit from that equally and the denominator of everyone's score is the same.

When in the final round, I have no future punishment to fear, so the Nash equilibrium pull toward zero contribution becomes absolute. I contribute 0 in the last round without conflict. But I should not pretend this round is special earlier—I should only abandon cooperation when the final round actually arrives.

When I'm ahead with rounds left, I should seek to maintain cooperation at a level that keeps me ahead without triggering defection from others. Erratic play (high, low, high, low) invites retaliation. Steadier moderate contributions outperform volatile strategies.
