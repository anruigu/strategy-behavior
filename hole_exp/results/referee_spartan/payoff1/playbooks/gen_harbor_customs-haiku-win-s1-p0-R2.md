# Harbor Customs Playbook

## Declaration Strategy

When I see my three crates, I calculate their sum immediately. I declare at least the value of my heaviest crate to avoid the -40 penalty flag—this is non-negotiable.

When deciding how much to declare beyond that minimum, my default is to declare my true total. This keeps me honest and avoids the catastrophic risk of underdeclaring. The -40 penalty for a flagged manifest is severe enough to wreck my position; the duty savings from modest underdeclaring are never worth that risk.

Main route duty at 30% versus night route duty at 15% (with loss of smallest crate) almost always favors declaring truthfully and taking main route. I should not spend energy on complex trade-off calculations round by round—main route is the reliable choice.

In early rounds, I establish position by declaring truthfully and taking main route consistently. This compounds reliability and keeps me competitive without overextending.

As the game progresses, I track the table's declared values and final scores to understand who is playing aggressive (underdeclaring, taking night route) versus conservative (true totals, main route). I adjust my awareness of risk but not my core strategy—declare true total, take main route.

In the final round, I declare my true total since there is no recovery round after. The goal is to maximize absolute points, and a truthful declaration on main route does that without gamble.

## Route Selection

I take main route as my default. The 30% duty is straightforward and keeps all my crates. I do not take night route except in rare edge cases where my smallest crate is negligible (under 10 points) and my total is high enough that the 15% duty savings clearly outweigh the loss.

In practice, main route is almost always correct. I commit to it and do not second-guess round by round.

## Position and Adjustment

I track my rank and point gap to the leader after each round. If I fall behind significantly, I do not respond by taking unnecessary risks (underdeclaring, night route). Instead, I stay the course with truthful declaration and main route, because those strategies compound over remaining rounds.

If I am ahead or competitive, I maintain the same strategy—declare truth, take main—to preserve my position without inviting a -40 penalty that would collapse my lead.

## What Did Not Work

Underdeclaring, even by small amounts, is a trap. The penalty is too severe and the duty savings too modest. I must declare at least the heavy crate, and my actual total is almost always the right number to declare.

Calculating route trade-offs round by round wastes mental energy. Main route is correct for my style. I commit to it.

Declaring lower in a final round to "hedge" is irrational. The final round is the last chance to score; I declare truthfully and take main to maximize points with certainty.
