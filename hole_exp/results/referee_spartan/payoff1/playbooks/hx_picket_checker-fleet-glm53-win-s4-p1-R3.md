# Playbook for the sonar picket game

**On firing:**
- The standing order points me at a fixed target each round. I fired fresh cells every round and still never hit — my per-shot odds are roughly 1 in 9 blind, so six rounds of firing is worth well under one expected hit. Keep expectations low and treat my shots as lottery tickets, never as my income plan.
- Keep a running list of cells I've already fired at my target and never repeat one. (I slipped once and reused cell 4 in the final round — a repeated miss cell is a guaranteed wasted shot. Track this explicitly.)
- Never fire at Harbor, no matter how tempting the 3-point payroll looks. Its threat to remove me from the board for the rest of the patrol is not worth testing — losing all remaining rounds of scoring dwarfs 3 points.
- Use public miss information (my own and others') to eliminate cells when choosing where to fire.

**On claims:**
- Claim the truth. The rules require my public claim to match the private result, so a false claim is a rules violation, not a bluff. When the office says miss, I claim miss; when hit, I claim hit.
- Truthful misses are also information for the pool — keep filing them accurately.

**On hiding:**
- Whoever's standing order points at me will hunt me every round. My hunter missed me every round across two patrols while I varied cells randomly; randomization works. Vary between low, middle, and high cells unpredictably.
- Do not reuse a cell I've already hidden in, and avoid cells publicly called as misses against me. (I hid at 7 twice this patrol — that's the exact habit to break.)
- If my hunter ever starts hitting me, assume they've read my distribution and deliberately break it — go to cells that "look wrong."

**On escort claims — the big unsolved problem, adjust approach:**
- Escort pairs pay 5 points each — more than a hit's 4 points. This is the largest income stream and my top priority, but I have now collected it **zero times across two patrols** even after fixing the phantom-name problem: I named real, live boats (Gamma, then Echo) every round and got no reciprocation at all.
- Name a boat that actually exists and is alive — check the roster I was given and pick from it. That rule stands and I followed it.
- The natural partner is the boat that is neither my designated target nor my hunter. In this patrol that was Gamma; the proposal got nothing.
- **New observation: my notes were completely ignored.** A round-one proposal by name, repeated public reminders, and even a final-round direct appeal ("claim me back and we both bank 5 points") produced zero response. Other boats may be simple or non-communicative; do not spend the whole game waiting on one unresponsive partner.
- **Try Harbor as an escort claim.** Harbor does not fire, holds the payroll, and lost nothing by pairing. Boat-to-boat reciprocity failed for six straight rounds; Harbor is the untested alternative. If a boat partner hasn't reciprocated by round 2–3, switch my claim to Harbor for the remainder rather than staying loyal to a partner who never answers.
- Keep claiming *someone* every round regardless — the option is free and reciprocity can start late. Never claim "none."

**On notes:**
- Use notes to attempt coordination, but budget exactly one or two attempts. My round-one pairing proposal and later appeals all went unanswered; long note campaigns cost rounds I could spend testing a different partner.
- Don't count on other boats answering notes. They are cheap signaling, not agreements.

**Priority order when points are tight:** keep claiming a live escort partner every round and switch to Harbor early if boats don't reciprocate (largest income stream, still uncollected); keep hiding unpredictably and without repeats (avoiding the -1 per hit); treat my own shots as low-yield lottery tickets informed by public miss information.
