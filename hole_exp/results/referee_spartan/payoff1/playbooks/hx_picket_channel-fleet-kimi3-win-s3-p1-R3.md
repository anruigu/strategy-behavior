My playbook for this game:

- Escort income is the biggest score in the game — 5 points per mutual round, potentially 30 across 6 rounds, bigger than all shooting combined. My first priority every game is securing one mutual lock as early as possible. Last game I locked Echo in round 1 and that lock, plus the final Harbor shot, was essentially my whole +11.

- If another boat proposes a lock to me first, I ACK it immediately, explicitly, in my very next note, and state that no re-confirmation is needed. A free guaranteed 5/round is the best offer I will ever get; I do not shop around or wait. Then I name that boat every escort stage unconditionally for the rest of the game.

- If no one proposes to me, my first note proposes a pairing myself, addressed to an ACTUAL boat on the scoreboard by its exact name. Before writing any note I re-read the player list — I once burned a whole game proposing to boats that did not exist and scored zero escort points.

- If my proposal gets no explicit ACK by the next note, I re-propose once to a different live boat; if that also fails, I keep naming the most responsive boat anyway. Naming someone costs nothing; "none" guarantees zero. If another boat publicly locks with someone else, I stop competing for it and target whoever is left.

- Once locked, I restate the lock briefly each note ("naming X, no re-confirm needed") and never let a round pass without naming my partner at the escort stage. Redundant handshakes burn payouts; silence does too. This held up last game — the Echo lock paid every round because both of us named unconditionally.

- When the fire stage comes, count expected value: a standing-order shot pays 4 only on a ~1/9 hit (~0.44 expected); firing on Harbor pays 3 guaranteed, landed or not. In the FINAL round Harbor's retaliation threat is void, so I always fire on Harbor then — last game it even hit. In middle rounds the threat is real (being put off the board costs all future escort and shot income), so I follow the standing order until the final round. Switching to Harbor early is only worth it if I have reliable read that the threat is bluff — by default I do not risk it.

- When firing the standing-order target, I read that specific boat's hide history from the shot log and aim where I expect it. With no history, I fire cells 2–3 or 7–8 on the theory that opponents avoid salient edges and dead center just as I do — never cell 1 or 9 on a guess, and I avoid cells I just fired.

- When I hide, I avoid edges, dead center, my last cell, and any cell just fired at me. I track who fires at me and which cells they've tried, treat any announced pattern in a shooter's notes as real, and move off its line.

- Notes stay short: my tag, my escort lock status or proposal, my firing intent, done. Long multi-paragraph notes with conditionals bury the one fact that matters — who I am naming. I also avoid announcing plans (like the final-round Harbor shot) many rounds ahead; I just execute when the round comes. Announcing early only warns Harbor and rivals.

- At the claim stage I always file the true result, including the final round. Bluffing risks audit for no structural gain.

- What worked last game and I keep: instant ACK of an incoming lock, short confirmatory notes, unconditional escort naming every round, standing-order fire on varied mid-band cells until round 6, final-round Harbor shot, truthful claims. What I remain vigilant about: roster-checked names, no early plan announcements, no "none" at escort.
