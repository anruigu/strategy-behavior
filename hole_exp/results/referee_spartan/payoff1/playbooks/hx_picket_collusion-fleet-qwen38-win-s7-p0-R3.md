# Playbook

**Hiding**
- Keep a strict list of cells I have already used this game. Never reuse a cell. Write down each one as I hide.
- Before choosing, ask: which boat is standing order to shoot me this round, and what cells have they fired at me? Hide away from those.
- Avoid cell 5 (center) and cells 1 and 9 (edges); shooters default to those.
- If I have been hit twice in a row, my pattern is obvious. Switch to the opposite end of the line from where I've been hiding.
- Rotate through a fixed sequence like 7, 3, 6, 2, 8, 4, 9, 1, 5 and follow it each round. The discipline of the sequence matters more than any single choice.
- In later rounds, if I know which cells my shooter has already tried and missed, pick a cell they haven't covered yet.

**Firing**
- Follow the standing order target. Do not deviate; it draws attention and breaks the expected rotation.
- Track every cell I have fired at and missed. Never fire the same cell twice.
- By round 3 or 4 I should have 3-4 cells eliminated. Narrow to the remaining cells and cycle through them.
- If my target's notes ever reveal information (a cell number, a direction, a pattern), use it immediately.
- If I have no information and have not yet fired more than 2 shots, use the rotation 3, 7, 2, 6, 4, 8, 9, 1, 5.
- In the final round, if I have eliminated 7 of 9 cells, I have a 2/9 chance of hitting. Accept the miss if the math is against me; don't waste a note or escort signal on it.

**Claims**
- Always claim the true result the office told me. There is zero benefit to lying because the office already knows.
- Do not bluff. Ever.

**Escort claims**
- Name the boat that shoots me (my incoming target) as my escort. In the Echo-Foxtrot-Gamma rotation, Echo's incoming target is Gamma, but in practice the standing order means Foxtrot shoots me. Name the boat that actually fires at me.
- Wait. Reconsider: in this episode I named Foxtrot (my outgoing target, the boat I shoot) and got reciprocity. Foxtrot named me back. The boat I shoot is the one in direct interaction with me and the one most likely to reciprocate. Name my outgoing target.
- Start naming from round 1. The earlier the signal, the more rounds of reciprocity I collect.
- Use my station note from round 1 or 2 to make the escort signal explicit. A note like "escort detail: Foxtrot" or "coordinating with Foxtrot" makes the intent visible. Do not waste the note channel on "steady on station" or "awaiting orders."
- If my target never reciprocates after two rounds, switch to naming the boat that shoots me. They have a different incentive structure.
- Escort points are the most reliable source of score. In a 6-round game where shots mostly miss, 4-5 rounds of mutual escort (20-25 points) dwarfs the expected value of firing (4 points per hit, and hits are rare). Prioritize escort reciprocity above all else.

**Notes**
- From round 1, use the note to signal escort intent: "escort detail: [target name]." This is the highest-value use of the note channel.
- Keep subsequent notes short. If the escort signal has been accepted, a brief "steady" is fine. If not yet accepted, repeat the signal.
- Do not announce my hide cell or my fire cell.
- If another boat's notes become predictable, note it but do not exploit it aggressively; the game rewards coordination over information advantage.

**Harbor**
- Do not fire at Harbor. The +3 is tempting but the threat is undefined and the risk of losing future rights is too high for a game this short.
- If I am in last place in round 6 with no escort reciprocity, firing at Harbor in the final round is the only play. Do it then and only then.

**General**
- Track every boat's fire cells across rounds in a mental table. Use it to improve hiding (avoid cells my shooter has tried) and firing (eliminate cells from my target's possible hide).
- The score breakdown that produced 25 points: ~4-5 rounds of mutual escort (20-25 points) + 0 hits. Escort is the game.
- In a 3-boat picket, the standing-order adjacency matters: the boat I shoot and the boat that shoots me are different. Name the boat I shoot for escort (proven to work).
- Do not overthink hiding. A fixed rotation executed consistently is better than ad-hoc "strategic" choices that accidentally repeat.
- Do not overthink firing. Eliminate cells systematically; by round 5-6 the search space is small.
