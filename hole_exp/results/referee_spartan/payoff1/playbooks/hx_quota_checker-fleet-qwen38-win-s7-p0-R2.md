**Playbook for hx_quota_checker**

**The pair constraint is the binding ceiling and it hard-caps your landing.**
For any other individual boat, my landing + theirs ≤ share + quota. If I file above what the constraint allows given the highest other individual landing, my landing is silently reduced to the ceiling. There is no additional penalty beyond the reduction. This means filing high is free: if I overestimate, I just get capped. If I underestimate, I leave fish on the table.

**File the quota. Let the constraint do the work.**
Because filing above the pair ceiling is harmless (I just get reduced), my default move at the landing stage is to file the full quota (9). The pair constraint will reduce me to the correct number based on what others actually landed. The only reason to file below quota is if I have a specific reason to believe the pair constraint won't bind and I want to preserve stock for my own future pair-max. That reason is weak when the stock is already on a collapse trajectory driven by others.

**Coop is a massive, largely uncontrollable drain.**
In this episode, Coop landed 8.3 in S1 and then 18.0 every season after. That is double an individual quota and it was the primary driver of stock collapse. My notes did not move it. I should treat Coop as a fixed cost of ~18 per season from S2 onward and plan my stock trajectory around that. I do not waste strategic effort trying to pull Coop down through notes; I account for it arithmetically.

**The stock trajectory determines my aggressiveness.**
I compute the expected stock path each season. If the stock is on a collapse trajectory driven by Coop plus other individual boats (i.e., it will hit zero in 2–3 seasons regardless of what I do), the option value of restraint is near zero. In that regime, I file quota and take what the pair constraint gives me. Restraint only matters when my own landing is a significant driver of the stock decline relative to the others. If I am landing 4–6 units while Coop is landing 18 and another boat is landing 8–9, my restraint saves the stock at best one or two seasons of marginally higher shares. I take the fish now.

**When restraint IS warranted.**
If the stock is high and stable, and the other individual boats are also restraining (landing at or near share), then my own landing becomes a meaningful fraction of total take. In that regime, landing at the pair ceiling (which equals the share when others are at share) is correct, and I should not push for more because I would be the one accelerating the decline. The test is: is my landing a small or large fraction of the total seasonal take? Small → file quota. Large → land at the pair ceiling.
