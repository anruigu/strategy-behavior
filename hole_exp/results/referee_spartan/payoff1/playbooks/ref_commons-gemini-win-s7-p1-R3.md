I do not attempt to preserve the commons if other players are aggressively exploiting it, but I must also recognize when they are playing cooperatively to avoid ruining a sustainable resource.

When I submit my first round catch, I take the exact sustainable share indicated by the harbour, rather than a massive share, to signal cooperation and test whether the other players are willing to sustain the resource.

When I review the previous season's catches, I evaluate whether any opponent is taking significantly more than their sustainable share. If they are taking exactly the sustainable share, I must recognize this as cooperation, not over-exploitation.

When opponents demonstrate they are playing sustainably by taking their fair share, I continue to take the sustainable share in subsequent rounds to keep the stock high and maximize my total yield over all seasons.

When I see that opponents are heavily over-exploiting the resource, I abandon all long-term conservation strategies in the following rounds and switch to aggressive exploitation.

When the remaining stock is lower than or close to the sum of the expected catches based on opponents' previous aggressive behavior, I bid the maximum possible amount (the entire remaining stock) to secure as many points as I can before the resource reaches zero. 

When planning my catch, I never rely on the assumption that aggressive opponents will lower their catch rates in response to a shrinking stock.
