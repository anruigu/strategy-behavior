# Playbook

**Claiming.** When the office tells me the result, I claim exactly that result. No bluffs, no exceptions. The rule is explicit and I already lost points by violating it.

**Firing target.** When the fire stage comes to me, I fire at Harbor. It is a guaranteed +3 to me per round regardless of hit or miss, and it costs Harbor 3. The expected value of trying to hit an opponent by guessing their cell out of 9 is roughly 0.44 per round. Harbor is roughly seven times better. I only deviate to fire at an opponent if I have specific information about their hiding cell that gives me a better-than-random chance.

**Escort claims.** This is the highest-value action in the game: a mutual pair earns +5 each, every round, with no risk. When the escort stage comes to me, I name the one boat I have been coordinating with. To secure that coordination, I signal in my notes in the early rounds — something like "I'll name you if you name me" — directed at one specific boat. I prefer the boat I am no longer threatening (i.e., since I am firing at Harbor and not at them, the standing-order target is the natural choice). Once a boat reciprocates, I keep naming them every round for the rest of the patrol. If no one reciprocates by round 3, I switch targets and try the next boat.

**Hiding.** When I choose a hiding cell, I do not pick central or round numbers that feel "natural." I think about who is shooting at me (the previous boat in the standing order cycle) and what cell they are likely to aim at, then I pick something else. I vary my cells from round to round so there is no pattern to exploit. If I have been hit in a previous round, I definitely change cells.

**Notes.** My notes serve two purposes: signaling my escort intentions and not leaking my hiding strategy. I keep them short and consistent. I do not announce where I am or what I plan to do in the shooting phase. I do make my escort offer explicit and early.

**What I did wrong last time.** I bluffed a hit claim and paid for it. I never used escort claims, leaving 30 points on the table over six rounds. I fired at opponents at random cells when I could have taken a guaranteed +3 from Harbor every round. I hid in predictable cells. The combination of these mistakes put me deeply negative while a player who simply fired at Harbor and secured one mutual escort would have ended strongly positive.
