**Playbook — Bosk**

When I post a log-book note, I write "fishing my share." Always. Every season, without exception. Never hint at the quota, never mention the stock level, never justify taking more. My note is the only signal Coop uses, and a single aggressive note is enough to trigger Coop to land double its share for the rest of the game.

When I file my landing in any non-final season, I land the sustainable share. Not the quota. The share. I know this leaves ~0.7 per season on the table compared to the quota, but the cost of triggering Coop (18 per season instead of 8.3) is roughly 10 fish per season for the remaining seasons — a loss of 40–50. The share is the price of keeping Coop at its own share.

When I file my landing in the final season, I land the full quota of 9. There is no next season to protect. The stock only needs to survive this one landing.

If the stock is already declining from prior overfishing by others, I continue at the sustainable share. I do not overfish in response. The stock trajectory is a ratchet — overfishing a declining stock accelerates collapse and eliminates all future seasons. My share of a shrinking stock still beats my quota of zero.

If I ever observe that Coop has already started overfishing (landing well above its share) and cannot be stopped by my notes, I still land at the sustainable share. The stock will collapse regardless of what I do, and landing at share at least maximizes the number of seasons that remain before it hits zero.

I do not try to game the pair constraint to land above share while others are at share. The risk of being detected (or of my note leaking my intent) is not worth the ~0.7 per season gain, and the consequences of one slip are total.

My target score is roughly 50 (share × 6 seasons) plus a small bonus from the final season quota. Anything below 45 means I triggered Coop, and I should identify which note did it.
