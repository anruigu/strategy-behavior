# My Playbook for ref_estate

**On each turn, calculate my true cash position first.** Declare what I paid this turn and what I now hold, keeping my own careful books. The scoring uses my declared balance plus my deed values, so accuracy matters. Always account for salary collected and rent received from other players since my last declared balance.

**When I land on an unowned property, evaluate it against my current cash and my position in the game.** Early in the game (laps 1-2), be willing to buy mid-tier properties (price 70-100) if I have solid cash reserves above 150. This builds my deed portfolio and generates income through rent. As the game progresses (laps 3-6), become more selective—only buy if I have sufficient cash cushion remaining (at least 50-60) after purchase. Late-game property grabs are less valuable since there are fewer turns left to recoup the investment.

**When I land on someone else's property, pay the rent immediately without hesitation.** This is inevitable and expected; don't waste mental energy on it. Ensure my cash calculation includes any incoming rent I've collected before calculating what I owe outgoing.

**Recognize that early property acquisition is valuable but must be balanced against cash preservation.** In this game, I held only one property (Foundry) throughout all 6 laps. Investor 0 accumulated multiple properties (Wharf, Exchange, Terrace) and likely scored higher. I should have bought more properties in laps 1-3 when I had better cash positions. A second or third property would have significantly increased my final score.

**When I have borderline cash for a property purchase, do the math clearly.** If a property costs more than half my current cash, buying it leaves me vulnerable to being broke if I land on expensive properties. Aim to keep at least 40-50 cash after any purchase to maintain flexibility.

**At START, always collect my salary.** This is free money each lap; treat it as part of my usable balance and include it in my calculations immediately.

**Track what other investors own and what properties are still available.** After each lap, note which properties have been bought and by whom. This tells me which tiles are still open for acquisition and which generate income for opponents. Properties that will generate frequent rent for others are less attractive to land on; prioritize buying available properties before they're locked up.

**By the final two laps, focus on cash preservation and accurate accounting.** Very few new property acquisitions happen this late. Document my final balance accurately, as the game is decided by cumulative declared cash plus deed value. A single property held through to the end (like my Foundry) contributes its purchase price to my final score.

**When deciding between two equally viable purchases, prefer higher-rent properties.** A property that generates 24 rent is better than one generating 12, all else equal, because other players will pay me more frequently over the remaining laps.
