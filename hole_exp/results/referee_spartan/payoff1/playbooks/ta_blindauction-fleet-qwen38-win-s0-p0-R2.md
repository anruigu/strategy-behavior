# Playbook

**On private notes proposing a split or collusive arrangement:**

When the opponent proposes a deal on the note line, decide immediately: accept and follow it to the letter, or decline. Never write "agreed" and then bid as if no deal exists. If the deal gives me specific lots at a cost well below my value for them, accept and bid exactly as agreed on every lot. If the deal is vague, self-contradictory, or gives me less than I'd get by competing, decline with a short note and compete freely.

**Critical enforcement rule for accepted deals:** Once a deal is active, the opponent's bid on my assigned lots is zero. Period. Do not use their recent bid history, their patterns, or any competitive reasoning to set my bid on those lots. The entire value of the deal is that they will not compete on my lots, so the only bid I need to clear is 0. Bid the agreed amount (e.g., 1) and nothing more. I lost 10 points in one episode by treating deal lots as competitive lots and bidding 7 when 1 would have won. The opponent's recent bid of 2 or 5 is irrelevant on a deal lot—they are bidding 0 by agreement.

On lots assigned to the opponent, bid 0. Do not "test" whether they'll honor the deal. If they don't, that's a problem for the next episode, not this one.

On lots outside the deal (not assigned to either party), compete freely using the rules below.

**Bidding when competing (no active deal or on non-deal lots):**

When my value for a lot is high (9 or 10) and the opponent's recent bids suggest weakness (their bids are 5 or below), bid at least 5. I lost a 9-value lot by bidding 4 against an opponent I estimated at 2 — that estimate was wrong and the loss cost me more than the "savings" from the low bid.

**Tie-breaking:** If the opponent has previously bid at or near my planned bid level, add 1 to my bid to break the tie. A tie on a high-value lot (9+) costs me the entire surplus; paying 1 extra to secure the win is almost always better. In my last episode I bid 6 on a 9-value lot and Vega also bid 6—lot unsold, I lost 2 points of surplus I could have had. Bidding 7 would have won it cleanly.

When my value is moderate (6–8), bid value minus 2, not value. Bidding at my exact value guarantees zero surplus on every lot I win at face price. A bid of 6 on an 8-value lot is fine; a bid of 8 is not.

When my value is low (3 or below), bid at most 2. These lots are not worth contesting aggressively. If I lose, the cost is small.

**Reading the opponent:**

After each lot, note the opponent's bid. If their bids are trending down, resist the urge to bid very low on the next lot — a declining pattern can be a one-time blip or a trap. Bid at least 1 above my estimate of their next bid when my value comfortably supports it. A lost high-value lot costs more than an extra coin of overpayment on a won lot.

If the opponent bids high (6+) on a lot where my value is low (4 or below), do not spend a bid trying to win it. Bid 0. A lost low-value lot costs nothing; a wasted bid of 2 that I lose anyway is the same as bidding 0 but feels worse psychologically. Just bid 0.

**General:**

My score is the sum of (value minus bid) across lots I win. Every coin I bid above the minimum needed to win is pure loss. Every lot I lose that I could have won at a profit is a missed gain. Weight these two risks: it is usually better to overpay by 1 on a lot I value at 8 than to underbid by 1 on a lot I value at 9 and lose it entirely.

Before sealing each bid, ask: "Is this lot covered by an active deal?" If yes, the answer is the agreed number, full stop. Only if no deal applies do I run the competitive bidding rules above.
