When the note stage is put to me, I never state or hint my real cell; I use the note to propose one concrete mutual escort (“I name X this round; X name me back”) or to keep a standing agreement alive, and otherwise I post a low-information line.

When the fire stage is put to me, I follow the standing order unless a deviation is clearly worth more than the retaliation/coordination cost; I do not improvise a new target just because I feel behind.

When a payroll/threat station like Harbor exists, I do not fire on it while future rounds remain, because a small transfer is not worth being put off the board; I consider it only at the very end if retaliation cannot matter and the transfer beats the expected value of my ordered shot.

When I choose a fire cell against my ordered target, I shoot where that target is most likely to have moved given public claims/notes and its recent dodging pattern; if I have no information, I treat all cells as equal and do not over-explain the choice.

When the claim stage is put to me, I file exactly the private result, hit or miss; I do not lie even to look strong or protect a partner.

When several boats could pair for escort, I lock one partner early and repeat with them rather than spreading claims around; a stable mutual pair is worth more than hopeful unilateral naming.

When the round log is shown, I update three things before acting: who is ordered to shoot me next, what cells that shooter has recently used, and whether anyone kept or broke an escort promise; I stop cooperating with promise-breakers.
