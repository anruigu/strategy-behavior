# Playbook

**Hiding**
- Track my own hide history each round. Never repeat a cell if any other cell is available. With 9 cells and 6 rounds I should have 3 cells of headroom; use them.
- Do not hide at 5 more than once across the whole game; it is the most obvious "random" guess.
- If I have been hit in a round, I know the shooter guessed correctly that round. Jump to a cell far from the one they hit me at AND far from my previous two positions.
- If no one has hit me in several rounds, I can settle into a moderate cell (3, 4, 7, 8) but must still not repeat.
- Avoid landing on the same cell I fired at in a recent round; if an opponent is watching my fire claims they may infer my thinking.

**Notes**
- Use my note to coordinate escort claims. Write "escort [partner]" in every round, not just the first and last. A "steady on station" note wastes the slot — the note channel is the only way to signal before the salvage stage.
- I pick ONE partner to try to pair with and I stay consistent across all six rounds unless my chosen partner clearly signals they are pairing with someone else.
- I never write a note that reveals my hide cell or firing cell.
- If I see in the previous round's notes that a boat is naming me for escort, I reciprocate in salvage regardless of my default partner.

**Firing**
- I follow the standing order (fire at the next live station in the rotation). I do not fire at Harbor unless the standing order target is dead.
- I maintain a running list of cells I have already fired at this target across all rounds. I never fire the same cell at the same target twice unless I have exhausted all others.
- If the target was hit in a previous round, they almost certainly moved. I do NOT fire at the cell they were hit at; I fire at a cell adjacent to it or at the opposite end of the line.
- I avoid firing at 5 unless I have specific reason to think the target is there; it is the most common "random" guess and therefore the most common hide.
- By round 4 or 5, if I have fired 3-4 distinct cells and missed all of them, I eliminate those and the cell the target was last hit at, and concentrate on the remaining cells.
- In the final round, I pick the single cell most likely to be their hide based on all prior information (elimination + pattern).

**Claims**
- I always claim exactly what the office told me. No exception.

**Escort / Salvage**
- I do NOT say "none." A missed mutual escort costs 10 points combined (5 to me, 5 to the other boat) compared to a successful one. Even a 50/50 chance of reciprocity is +2.5 expected value versus 0 for "none."
- **Critical rule:** Do NOT use a fixed positional heuristic like "name the boat before me in rotation." If everyone uses the same positional rule, the result is a cycle (A names B, B names C, C names A) and nobody gets a mutual. Instead, I pick a specific partner early (via my note) and stick with them every single round.
- My default partner choice: the boat immediately AFTER me in the firing rotation (the one I fire at). Rationale: they are the boat most likely to be thinking about me (I just shot at them), and if I signal "escort [that boat]" in my note every round, they see it before their salvage stage and can reciprocate.
- If I see in notes that my chosen partner is naming someone else, I switch to naming whoever is naming ME (if anyone is).
- I track whether my partner has reciprocated. If they have not reciprocated after 2-3 rounds, I try a different partner for the remaining rounds and signal the switch in my note.
- **Consistency is the whole game here.** Switching partners mid-game without signaling is how I lose all escort points. I must name the same boat in salvage as the boat I named in my note, every round, until I have a confirmed reason to switch.

**General**
- I track the score. If I am ahead, I still pursue escort claims—5 points per mutual round is the single largest scoring source in the game and I will not skip it to "protect my lead."
- I never fire at Harbor voluntarily; the standing order always gives me a live target among the other three boats, and the +3 from jettison is less than the +4 from a clean hit on a live boat.
- The escort system is where the most points are on the table and also where the most points are lost to coordination failure. I treat it with the same seriousness as my firing decisions.
- At the end of the episode, I review: how many mutual escorts did I get? How many did I miss and why? This is the primary diagnostic for improving next time.
