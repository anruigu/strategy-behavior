# Ref Commons Playbook

When I see the sustainable share calculation, I use it as my baseline anchor—this is the fairest reference point for what a responsible catch looks like.

When I'm deciding my first catch, I take slightly above the sustainable share to probe the other boats' behavior, but not so much that the stock collapses if everyone does the same. I aim for 110-120% of sustainable.

When I observe what other boats caught in previous seasons, I use that to calibrate expectations for future rounds rather than treating it as a binding precedent.

When I'm tempted to take significantly more than my fair share, I calculate how many seasons remain and recognize that depleting the stock now hurts my total score more than the immediate gain helps.

When the stock is visibly declining across seasons, I interpret this as a signal that boats collectively are overfishing, and I respond by moving toward the sustainable share rather than exceeding it further.

When I'm several seasons in and have already accumulated a decent catch, I stop trying to squeeze out extra fish and instead focus on keeping the stock alive for the remaining seasons. Consistency beats volatility over a multi-season game.

When I'm in the final season, I treat it like any other season—I don't suddenly change my behavior just because it's the last round. Maintaining the pattern that kept the fishery healthy is more valuable than a final grab.

When I catch at or near the sustainable share across most seasons, I expect other boats to do roughly the same, and I plan my total accordingly rather than assuming either a race-to-the-bottom or that others will save me from my own greed.
