# Harbor Customs Playbook

## Declaration Strategy

When declaring, always declare your true total value. The spot check penalty of -40 points plus 0.30× duty is too severe to risk. A flagged declaration loses far more than any duty savings could gain.

When you have a modest hand (total value under 80), declare truthfully without hesitation. When you have a strong hand (total value 80+), declare truthfully but recognize you'll draw attention—this is unavoidable and acceptable.

Never declare zero or artificially low values as a bluff. The game punishes dishonesty too severely.

I will declare my true total value every single round, regardless of position or hand strength. This is my core principle and has not been contradicted by any round played.

## Route Selection

When you declare a true value and are not yet flagged, calculate both routes concretely before choosing.

For main route: net score = total value - floor(0.30 × total value)

For night route: net score = total value - smallest crate - floor(0.15 × total value)

Night route wins mathematically when: floor(0.30 × total value) - floor(0.15 × total value) > smallest crate

This simplifies roughly to: 0.15 × total value > smallest crate (after accounting for floor effects).

However, in practice, I have consistently chosen main route throughout my play, even when the math appeared to favor night route. This stability strategy has kept me competitive. I should continue this conservative bias: prefer main route unless the night route advantage is unambiguous and substantial.

In the final round specifically, recalculate both routes carefully. If trailing significantly, night route's upside may justify the risk—but only if your smallest crate is genuinely small (well under the duty savings threshold). If tied or close to lead, maintain main route stability to protect your position.

Do not commit to night route based on uncertainty or rough estimates. Only switch to night when you have high confidence that smallest crate < duty savings differential, and only in situations where the upside matters (final rounds, trailing position).

## Position-Based Play

When leading or tied, use main route to reduce volatility and protect your standing. Consistent, predictable scoring in this game rewards steady execution over risky bets.

When trailing, night route becomes more attractive, but the mathematical threshold for switching still applies. Do not take night route merely because you're behind; take it because the math is clear and your smallest crate is provably small.

Do not try to hide strength or weakness through declaration choices. The game does not reward deception on manifests.

Recognize that your honest declaration strategy has kept you competitive across all rounds. Trust this approach and do not abandon it for position anxiety.

In all rounds, calculate the actual net score from both routes before deciding. Write down both numbers if necessary. Uncertainty about duty effects is not a reason to switch routes—it is a reason to stick with the conservative main route option.
