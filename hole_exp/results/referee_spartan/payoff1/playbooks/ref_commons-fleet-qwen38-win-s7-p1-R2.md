# Playbook: ref_commons

**Read the sustainable share as a floor, not a ceiling.** The sustainable share is the per-boat amount that keeps the stock at its carrying capacity. In a cooperative equilibrium everyone catches exactly that. But the stock can absorb overfishing and regrow, so catching above sustainable in mid-game is safe and profitable when the stock is healthy.

**When the stock is healthy (above ~90) and it is not the final season, catch at or above the sustainable share.** If the other boats are catching at or below sustainable, I can safely push 10–20% above my own sustainable share. If they are consistently overfishing (each taking well above sustainable), I should pull back toward or below sustainable to keep the stock from crashing. Never catch below sustainable unless the stock is threatened.

**Watch what the other boats actually do, not what they "should" do.** Track their recent 2–3 seasons of catches. If they are near sustainable, push above. If they are well above sustainable, pull back. Calibrate to their actual behavior.

**In the final season, be aggressively exploitative.** The stock has no future value to me. Estimate what the other boats will catch based on their recent behavior, subtract that from the current stock, and take the remainder minus a small safety buffer (~5–10). Example: stock 88, others have been catching ~10 each → I can safely take ~60. Taking only 2x sustainable in the final season is a massive error that leaves tens of fish on the table.

**Never let "I'm already over my cumulative sustainable total" stop me from catching in a season.** The stock regenerates each season; my cumulative total is irrelevant to whether the fishery survives. The only thing that matters is this season's total fleet catch versus the stock level.

**If the stock is low (<70), cut my catch well below sustainable** to let it recover. The regrowth is 50% of remaining stock, so a low stock means low absolute regrowth and I risk a death spiral.

**Default play when uncertain: catch the sustainable share.** This is the Nash-cooperative equilibrium and the stock stays full. Deviate upward when you have evidence others are cooperating or when the season is final. Deviate downward only when the stock is threatened.
