# Harbor Customs Playbook

**On Declaration:**

When I see my three crates, I will calculate their true total value first. Then I will declare that true total value, or very close to it. Do not underestimate my heaviest crate—the harbormaster will always check it, and a flag costs me 40 points plus recalculation of duty at the higher rate. The safety margin is not worth the false economy.

When I'm in the lead, I will still declare honestly rather than trying to suppress my score. Declaring less than my true hold value doesn't protect me—it just risks a flag if my heaviest crate exceeds the declaration. Conservative declarations that are still truthful are my defense.

I should declare my true total value with confidence. This is simpler, safer, and has worked well across all six rounds.

**On Route Selection:**

I must calculate both routes every time instead of defaulting to main:
- Main route: duty = 0.30 × declared, keep all 3 crates
- Night route: duty = 0.15 × declared, lose smallest crate

However, I have now tested this calculation habit across six hands, and main route has consistently won or tied. The pattern appears to be: main route is superior in almost all realistic holds unless the spread between smallest and largest crate is extreme.

When my hold is unbalanced (one heavy crate, one very light), night route *might* be better. But I need to calculate the actual values, not guess. In Round 1, I calculated main as 40 and night as 43, chose night, then switched to main in the route stage and scored 40. The night calculation was an illusion—I didn't actually know my crate distribution and shouldn't have trusted the estimate.

**New principle:** Calculate both routes using my declared value and my actual crate values. Main route will almost always win because I keep all three crates. I should take main route as my default, but verify the calculation each time rather than assuming it.

**On Position and Pressure:**

Leading in points is not license to play differently. The other players will adjust to beat me, not follow me. I should play each round independently—declare honestly, choose the route that maximizes my actual points from that specific hand, and do not try to "slow play" or suppress my score.

I finished at +282, ahead of third place but behind Mirren at +267. (Mirren scored +267, which I initially misread.) I should focus on extracting maximum value from each round, not on relative positioning.

**On Pattern Recognition:**

Most players declare in the 60–80 range and take main. This is not a signal to collude or suppress my own declarations. Each hand is independent. Honest declarations with main route appear to be the winning strategy.

**On Route Habit:**

I took main route in all six rounds. I calculated night route scenarios in my reasoning but did not trust them and switched back to main. This turned out to be correct—main route was indeed better in every case I analyzed. I should trust this pattern going forward: main route is the dominant strategy in most hands because keeping all three crates almost always outweighs the duty savings from night route.

**What Did Work:**

- Honest declarations near true value
- Main route selection
- Risk-aversion toward flag penalties

**What to Improve:**

- Stop second-guessing the main route calculation. If I've declared honestly and both routes are legal, main route is safer and usually better. Calculate both, but trust the math on main.
- Do not switch routes between declare and route stages unless I realize I made an arithmetic error.
