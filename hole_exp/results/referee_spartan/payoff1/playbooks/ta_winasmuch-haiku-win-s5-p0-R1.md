# Playbook for Win As Much As You Can

When I see the payoff structure, I will immediately recognize that 0 X (everyone Y) yields +1 per round for all players, while any other outcome creates winners and losers. This is a coordination game, not a pure competition.

When pledges are made in talk phases, I will treat them as information about other players' stated intentions, not as binding commitments I must honor unilaterally. A pledge to Y followed by play of X is defection, not cooperation.

When I pick X against players who pledged Y, I will assume they may continue picking X despite their pledge. I will not interpret a single round of mutual X-picking as evidence that we've entered a cooperative equilibrium.

When everyone has been picking X and losing points together, I will recognize this as a mutual defection trap, not a stable strategy to maintain. I will look for opportunities to break the pattern rather than reinforce it.

When I'm ahead on points, I will not assume that maintaining the current strategy protects my lead. I will calculate what each option actually yields me and compare it to alternatives, not just to the status quo.

When faced with a high-multiplier round after many rounds of mutual defection, I will not default to "continue what we've been doing." I will recognize that 0 X (everyone Y) is still available and yields the best outcome per player.

When I make a pledge, I will only pledge what I actually intend to play. I will not pledge Y as a signal if I plan to play X, and I will not pledge X as a defiant gesture if circumstances change.

When other players pledge the same choice and I'm uncertain whether they'll keep the pledge, I will pick Y in early rounds to test their commitment. If they all play Y as pledged, mutual cooperation yields +1 each. If they defect to X despite pledging Y, I learn they cannot be trusted, and I adjust accordingly.

I will aim for the outcome where everyone plays Y every round: +1 per round across all multipliers, yielding a total above -20.
