**Playbook for the shared fishery game**

1. When the stock is at or near its cap and the horizon is long, I fish at (or a hair above) the announced sustainable share. This kept the stock near 92–100 the whole game and produced a solid total.

2. When others slightly overfish (a boat or two taking ~12 against a sustainable ~11), I do not panic and do not retaliate. A small overshoot barely dents the stock under 50% regrowth. Matching their ~12 keeps me level in total landed at negligible stock cost — this worked exactly as planned all game.

3. In the last season, I stop conserving entirely. There is no future to protect, so I take a large catch bounded by what the stock can support minus what the rivals will plausibly take (~12 each). Taking 40 against a stock of ~92 landed everything I asked for — the stock is nowhere near exhausted by the finale if I've fished sensibly. Next time I can push toward stock minus ~2×rivals' expected take, since leaving fish behind is pure waste.

4. In the penultimate season I loosen up only slightly (take ~1 above sustainable). A big penultimate grab is unnecessary — the final season still yields plenty, and the marginal restraint protects that last big haul. Going 11.5 vs 10.5 sustainable was about right; bigger was available but the finale is where the payoff is.

5. I watch the stock trajectory in the recaps: slow decline (100 → 99 → 97 → 96 → 94) under mild greed is fine and stable. If the stock starts dropping fast, I cut back hard to protect remaining seasons, because collapse means zero for everyone, including me.

6. The core equilibrium that bore out: match rivals' mild greed mid-game (~1 above sustainable), then convert conserved stock into one large final-season grab. My edge is timing that grab, not virtue. Final-season size scales with how healthy the stock is — the healthier I've kept it, the bigger the finale can be, so mid-game restraint is really an investment in the last season.

7. Read the rivals' pattern from the recaps each season: if they hold steady at a fixed number, plan the final grab around that number. If a rival goes rogue with big grabs earlier, I scale my finale down accordingly and consider trimming mid-game catches to compensate.
