# Sonar Picket Checker Playbook

## Hiding

When choosing where to hide, avoid predictable patterns. Don't stay in the same cell round after round—rotate your position to make targeting harder. After observing which boats are firing at you consistently, move away from the cells where they've been hitting you. Central positions are reasonable for opening rounds, but vary them rather than anchoring to any single cell.

Track my own hiding history across the game. If I've used a cell recently, move away from it. If an opponent has hit me in a cell, strongly avoid returning there. If an opponent has missed repeatedly in a cell, they may be adapting, so don't assume safety.

## Station Notes

Post "steady on station" consistently. This is cooperative language that costs nothing and helps establish predictability in my own positioning, which I control. Don't overcomplicate my notes. Maintain this through the final round as part of the overall strategy of controlling what I can control.

## Firing

Follow the standing order without deviation. I have no discretion here—fire at my assigned target because that's the rule. Choose my fire cell based on pattern observation of where my target has been hiding recently. If I've hit a target before in a cell, they're likely to move away from it; if I've missed repeatedly in a cell, they may not be there. Look for recent rotation patterns in their hiding, not just individual cell history.

Against a target with strong recent behavior (like hitting me in cell 5), assume they will rotate to a neighboring or nearby alternative cell rather than moving randomly. This narrows the search space more effectively than scattering shots across the full line.

## Claims

Always file claims that match the private result the office gives me. This is mandatory and non-negotiable for game integrity. False claims corrupt my own decision-making in future rounds because I lose track of what's actually happening, and I cannot extract strategic value from a lie that only I know about. The office knows the truth; other players learn my patterns through observation. Lying to myself serves no one.

When the office tells me the true result of my shot, I match my claim to that result exactly. No exceptions, no matter what strategic intuition suggests.

## Escort Claims

When I name an escort, I need mutual recognition for the 5-point payoff. Name none unless I have a clear pre-established signal with another boat or have already observed mutual escort-naming in previous rounds. Don't guess. The penalty for an unreciprocated escort claim is silence—no points either way—so the risk-reward is asymmetric. Only name an escort when I have evidence of prior mutual intent.

## Overall Strategy

Pay close attention to which boats are targeting me and where they're hitting. Adjust my hiding accordingly. Observe which boats' hit and miss patterns indicate skill versus luck or random firing. Prioritize defensive positioning over all other considerations since I take 1 point per hit.

Track the standing order strictly and fire mechanically at my assigned target while varying fire cells based on observed opponent movement. Cooperate through consistent notes but compete through evasion and accurate shooting. The game rewards both reliable positioning information and hard-target defense; balance attention between understanding what others are doing and making myself hard to find.

In the final rounds, maintain all prior strategies—don't break discipline in round 6 just because points are running out. Consistency of approach compounds over time.
