- When a season opens, I first compute the safe default: stock divided by boats, roughly the stated sustainable share. That is my anchor every season, not my opening bid.
- In the first season, when no one has revealed a style yet, I do NOT spike to 20. That bought me fish but cost me trust, and the others immediately matched downward pressure. Next time I take the sustainable share or one notch above it, and bank the information instead of the fish.
- When other boats take the sustainable share, I take the sustainable share. Cooperation compounds: matched restraint keeps the regrowth base large, and the flowing payoff exceeds any one-season grab.
- When other boats consistently overfish, I do not stay maximal-cooperator forever. I shade a season-by-season small premium above my share (roughly +15–25%), sized so total take stays below the regrowth break-even, and I monitor whether the stock still recovers.
- I watch the stock trajectory, not just rivals' catches. If stock is rising or stable, I keep my current policy; if it is falling two seasons running, I cut toward or below sustainable share even if rivals overfish, because a survivor in a live fishery beats a leader in a dead one.
- I never take a large grab unless one of two conditions holds: it is the final season, or the stock is heading to zero no matter what I do. Absent those, spikes signal aggression and invite retaliation.
- In the final season, with no future to protect, I still take only the sustainable share if others are cooperating and scores are close — but if I trail and the stock is ample, I take a larger terminal haul, up to a full third of the stock minus a safety margin, since there is no regrowth to preserve.
- I keep my claims modest and consistent: never more than about a third above my fair share in any non-final season, so I never become the visible cause of a collapse.
- What did not work: front-loading a big catch early. It inflated my score marginally but degraded the commons and taught rivals to distrust me. Next time my edge comes from sustained small premiums plus a disciplined final-season decision, not from a season-one raid.
- Each decision, I ask one question in order: Is this the last season? Then harvest hard within stock limits. If not, is the stock stable under current total take? Then hold near the share. If not, I cut first.
