# Playbook for Auction

When I'm shown a lot's value to me versus the estimate, I immediately compare them to identify whether this lot represents good value or poor value.

When the standing high bid is zero and I value the lot above the estimate, I open with a modest bid around 10% of the estimate to establish position without overcommitting.

When the standing high bid is already placed and I value the lot significantly above it (by more than 50%), I raise to win, calculating that even a minimum raise leaves me with substantial profit.

When the standing high bid is zero and I value the lot below the estimate, I pass immediately rather than opening bidding on a lot that's unlikely to be profitable.

When a lot's value to me is only marginally above the standing high bid (within 10-20%), I pass to preserve budget for future lots where I have clearer advantage.

When I'm in the final round of bidding on the last lot, I bid aggressively if the value proposition is positive, since there are no future lots to save budget for.

When calculating whether to raise, I always check that my bid leaves me with positive profit (value minus bid amount) before committing.

I track my spending across lots and never let any single lot consume so much budget that I cannot bid meaningfully on remaining lots.

I recognize that marginal value propositions are dangerous. I only bid on lots where my personal value exceeds the standing bid by at least 20-30% to ensure sufficient margin for profit.

I must verify my budget calculation after every bid and track which lots I actually won versus lost. My stated budget tokens must reflect reality.

I will not bid aggressively on the final lot simply because it's final if the value margin is thin. A marginal profit on the final lot is still only marginal profit; I should pass on lots where my value is close to or only slightly above the standing bid, regardless of round or lot number.

I notice that my actual budget after winning lot 3 at 8 and bidding 16 on lot 4 should have been 30 - 8 - 16 = 6, not 92. I made a critical error in budget tracking during this episode. I will recalculate my budget meticulously after each bid: subtract the amount I bid from my previous budget, not from an imaginary pool.

When I pass on early rounds, I do not spend budget. My budget only decreases when I place a bid that wins a lot.

I will end each episode with a clear accounting of: lots won, amounts paid, total value received, and final score. If my final score is low or zero, I will trace back through my decisions to identify where I bid on low-margin lots or failed to bid on high-margin ones.
