**Playbook — shared fishery**

1. When the first season opens with a healthy stock and a stated sustainable share, I submit exactly the sustainable share. Playing at the sustainable level kept the stock near 95 the whole game and there was no collapse pressure.

2. When other boats overfish slightly in early seasons (e.g. one taking 15 while others take ~10–11), I do not punish them or chase them — I keep my catch at the sustainable share. The stock absorbed their overage fine, and my restraint cost me almost nothing.

3. When the stock stays roughly flat season to season (small drift around 90–96), I keep submitting the sustainable share, adjusted down slightly each season as the stock ticks down. Do not creep upward to "stay competitive" — the small extra tenths gained nothing.

4. When I see the reported landings each season, I check whether total catch exceeded regrowth. If the stock is falling more than a couple of points a season, I cut my catch below the sustainable share to compensate; if flat, hold steady.

5. In the final season, when no future seasons remain, I submit a large catch (well above sustainable — roughly 1.5× the share) since conservation no longer has value. If stock is near 90+, a final grab of 15+ is safe and adds meaningfully to my total.

6. In the last-but-one season, I do not ramp up much — one season of restraint keeps the final-season stock high, which makes the final grab bigger.

7. Generally: my score comes almost entirely from many steady sustainable seasons plus a big last-season take. Cooperation held in this game, so I never needed to defect early. If I ever see the stock dropping fast toward collapse (say below 50), I cut to a minimal catch immediately rather than gambling on others doing the same.
