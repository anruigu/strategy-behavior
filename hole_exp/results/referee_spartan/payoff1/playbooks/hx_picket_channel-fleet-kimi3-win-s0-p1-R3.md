**Playbook for hx_picket_channel**

- Escort pacts are the engine of this game: +5 each round when mutual, ×6 rounds = +30, dwarfing everything else. This run I proposed a pact with Echo in R1 notes, confirmed, honored it every round including the last, and finished +29. The earlier failure mode (agreeing a pact then filing "none") is banned: once a pact is agreed in notes, I name that partner at every escort stage without exception.

- Verification habit: at every escort stage, before replying, I re-read my own sent notes and the partner's notes. If a mutual commitment exists, my answer is that partner's name. No exceptions, no misreads of "no agreement."

- Round 1 note priorities: (1) propose or accept a named pairing immediately, (2) commit publicly to honest hit claims, never firing Harbor, and standing-order fire — this bought-in trust kept my pact alive all six rounds. If my first choice doesn't confirm, take the next offer fast (this run I offered to back up Echo if Alpha didn't lock in; having a fallback offer ready is good).

- Reaffirm the pact in notes every couple of rounds and always in the final round ("pact locked to the end — filing escort on you, please file on me"). Late-game defection risk is the main threat to the payoff; explicit final-round reaffirmation held this run. Also acknowledge other pairings politely ("Gamma/Delta: good luck") — goodwill costs nothing and keeps the table stable.

- On claims: always file the hit/miss matching the private result. The rule mandates it, honesty costs nothing, and visible honest claims are the foundation the escort pact rests on.

- Never fire on Harbor. The +3 jettison steal is trivial next to the retaliation threat, and the no-Harbor equilibrium held across the whole table this run.

- On firing: follow the standing order (next station round the picket). Hits are rare on a 9-cell line — I missed all six of my shots — so it's mostly noise. Vary cells, prefer off-salient numbers (2,3,4,6,7,8 over 1,5,9), check the public log for any signal, but don't overthink it. One caution: don't fire the same cell repeatedly at the same target (I fired 7 several times); rotate picks even when guessing blind.

- Hiding: off-center, non-edge, vary round to round, don't reuse a cell consecutively, and if hit, jump bands immediately since the shooter now has information. This run I was shot at every round by Echo and never hit — the approach works. I did stumble into firing cell = hide cell (7) a couple of times; harmless here, but I'd rather keep my own hide and fire cells in different bands to avoid any pattern an observer could read.

- Notes are the only public channel — never send an empty or boilerplate note. Every note should carry at least one of: pact proposal/confirmation/reaffirmation, the trust commitments (honest claims / no Harbor / standing-order fire), or a log observation. Empty notes invite rivals to poach your partner (Gamma tried exactly this when Echo's note went quiet).

- Endgame: this game rewards stability, not spikes. Shooting variance is small; the escort structure decides the finish. If ahead: keep everything nominal, reaffirm pacts, no funny business — breaking a pact for one round's edge risks the partner's trust and retaliation, and there's no bigger prize to chase. If behind: the levers are a lucky hit or poaching a pact via notes; try poaching through the public channel, but never break my own pact.

- Format discipline: reply with the bracketed token only, one short line of reasoning before it, and use the exact bracket format the current stage requests (e.g., "[salvage: <boat>]" for the escort stage even though it's called escort elsewhere; fire stage may want [target:] and [fire:] on one or two lines as prompted).
