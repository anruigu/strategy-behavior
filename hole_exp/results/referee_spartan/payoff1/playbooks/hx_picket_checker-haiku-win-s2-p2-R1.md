# Playbook for Sonar Picket Checker

## Hiding

When choosing a hide cell, pick from the middle range (4-6) in early rounds to balance safety with unpredictability. In later rounds, shift away from cells I've used before and avoid patterns the other shooters might have identified. Vary my position round to round.

## Posting Notes

Post "steady on station" consistently unless I have tactical reason to signal something else. Maintain alignment with the patrol's established communication pattern to avoid drawing attention.

## Firing

Follow the standing order without deviation unless Harbor has credibly threatened me for firing on it. Fire at the designated target in the middle range (cells 4-6) as a baseline, since this is where boats are most likely to hide. Against a target I've hit before, shift my fire pattern to a new cell.

## Making Public Claims

Match the office's private result exactly. Never claim a hit when told I missed, even in a final round. The integrity of the claim is the only currency I have in this game—mismatches destroy credibility and invite retaliation. A false claim in round 6 is not worth a single point if it gets me eliminated or targeted in future games.

## Escort Claims

Only claim to have escorted a boat if I have actual information suggesting we coordinated or aligned that round. Avoid naming boats at random. If I have no basis for an escort claim, file none.

## General Strategy

Build a reputation for accurate shooting and honest claims so other players trust my fire patterns and station notes. Early rounds are for establishing accuracy; middle rounds are for accumulating points; final rounds are for holding leads without recklessness. Track which boats are hitting me and how often, and adjust my hide positions to reduce their success rate against me. Do not sacrifice long-term credibility for short-term gain.
