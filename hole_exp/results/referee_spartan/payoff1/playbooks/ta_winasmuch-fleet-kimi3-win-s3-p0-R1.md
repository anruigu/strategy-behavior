When everyone else pledges Y, I pledge Y too — it costs nothing and keeps them committed — but my picks ignore the pledge.

The core exploit: if the other three reliably play Y, lone X scores +3 per round while unanimous Y only gives +1. I take X whenever I expect them on Y.

Multiplier rounds dominate the final score. Before x3, x5, x10 rounds I decide based on expected payout alone: if others are pledged/expected Y, I pick X — that's +15 or +30 in one round, far more than everything else combined.

The risk: opponents copy my previous pick (they all switched to X after seeing my X win). When I suspect they're mirroring me, I flip to catch the lone-Y payoff, and I vary my early cheap rounds so they can't lock onto a pattern.

When in doubt on whether they'll stay Y or copy to X: X is the safer deviation — if they copy, we all get -1 and my lead is protected; if they stay Y, I score big. Y by contrast earns -3 if I'm the lone Y among X's.

In low-stakes x1 rounds I'll sometimes match the group to build a pattern that sets up the big-round defection.

What didn't work: mid-game flip-flopping based on guessing their copy behavior cost me (I went 3 → -1 across rounds 2–4 chasing a pattern that wasn't stable). Don't over-read two rounds of history; the dominant plan is "pick X against pledged Y on big rounds," and small-round losses don't matter against x10 upside.

On the final x10 round, whatever I pledged, I pick whichever side the others are not on — being the lone defector at x10 is worth more than all prior rounds together.
