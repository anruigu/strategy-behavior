BLIND AUCTION PLAYBOOK

- Never bid at or above my value. Winning at a price ≥ value scores zero or worse while the house profits. Cap every bid at value − 1 so any win is worth at least +1.

- value − 1 is a ceiling, not a default. My bids of 5, 7, 9 at value − 1 each won only +1, and twice at value − 1 I lost anyway (lot 1: 5 vs 6). Vega's actual bids were erratic and often low (6, 2, 2, 4, 6). Before defaulting to value − 1, check the published history: if Vega's recent/modal bids are low, bid modal + 1 instead and bank the larger surplus. A 3 on lot 3 (Vega bid 2) would have scored +5 instead of +1.

- When a collusion offer arrives on the private line, do not trust it — this episode confirmed it again: Vega proposed skipping lots 4 and 6, then bid 4 on lot 4. Reply ambiguously ("sounds fair, let's see") to keep them guessing, but bid as if no deal exists. Treat the note only as information about which lots Vega wants (the ones they ask me to skip) and contest those normally.

- Use the deal terms against the reneger. Vega asked me to skip lots 3 and 5 — those are likely the lots they value. Lot 3 they won anyway at 2, so their "want" signal was weak; don't over-read one note, but weight the lots they asked me to concede as worth bidding on competitively, never the lots they offered to concede to me.

- Exploit reneging via the published history, not the note. Each lot, check what Vega actually did on deal-covered lots. If they bid on a lot they promised to skip, the deal is dead; play pure surplus-max from then on. I did this correctly and lost nothing to the fake deal.

- Bid for expected surplus, not for winning. On a low-value lot (value 2), bid 1 and accept losing cheaply; let Vega overpay (they paid 4 for a lot worth 2 to me — pure loss for them). Every lot Vega wins at a bad price is as good as points for me.

- Track Vega's published bids every lot and update. Over 6 lots they showed no consistent value+1 pattern — bids ranged 2 to 6 with no clear tie to my values. With an unpredictable opponent, prefer bids that win against their low-to-mid range (3–5) on mid-value lots, reserving value − 1 only for my highest-value lots where losing is the costliest outcome.

- Avoid ties. A tie destroys the lot for both of us. Where history suggests Vega lands on a specific number, bid one off it — one higher if that price still nets positive, otherwise one lower or concede.

- Don't bid break-even hoping the opponent folds; net zero at best. Either commit to a positive-surplus bid or concede cheaply with 0–1.

- Keep notes short and non-committal. Anything I write on the private line binds me to nothing and them to nothing; use it to extract their intentions, never to coordinate real bids.

- Final-lot discipline: on the last lot there is no future history to exploit, so bid the full value − 1 on a high-value lot (my 9 on value 10 won +1). On earlier lots prefer cheaper bids guided by Vega's observed range; only the last lot justifies paying near-ceiling by default.
