# Quiet Sonar Playbook

## Hide Phase
When choosing where to hide my submarine, I will avoid positions that have been frequently fired at in previous rounds. I will analyze the fire history and concentrate my hiding in cells that appear less targeted. Middle positions (4-6) offer reasonable balance early on, but I should shift toward lower numbers (1-3) as the game progresses and I see where shots concentrate. When I detect a clear firing pattern from my shooter (especially dominance of one or two cells), I will prioritize avoiding those cells in my hide choice, even if it means moving to less intuitive positions.

## Fire Phase
When firing at my target, I will choose positions based on where I think my target is most likely to hide. Early in the game with no information, I can choose middle positions. As rounds progress, I will adjust based on observed patterns in my target's behavior and the overall distribution of fires across the board. I should not rely on guessing the same position repeatedly if it hasn't worked. However, I should be cautious about over-rotating away from a position just because I missed once—a single miss does not invalidate a reasonable hypothesis about an opponent's hiding pattern.

## Claim Phase
When I receive a true result from the referee, I will always claim truthfully—claiming hit when I hit, and claiming miss when I miss. The temptation to lie about a miss for extra points is not worth the long-term cost. False claims create a reputation for dishonesty that hurts my strategic position and can be exposed. Maintaining integrity gives me credibility and better positioning for future decisions with other players. This remains true even in the final round, as my reputation carries forward beyond any single game.

## Position Management
When I am behind in points, I should not panic into high-risk strategies. Instead, I should focus on survival—hiding in less-fired positions and taking conservative fire choices. Desperation claims (false hits when behind) will not recover my score effectively. The gap between my score and leaders should influence my hide choice toward safety, not my claim honesty.

## Observation and Adaptation
I will track which cells are fired at each round and note any player patterns. When I see a player consistently firing certain positions (especially overwhelming concentration like 11 of 12 shots at one cell), I will treat this as a strong signal to avoid hiding there. This applies especially to my shooter (whoever fires at me) and my own target—understanding their patterns helps both defense and offense. I should update my threat model each round based on fresh data rather than relying on early-game impressions.

## Restraint in Pattern Recognition
I will avoid over-interpreting noise in limited data. Early rounds have high randomness; clear patterns only emerge after multiple rounds of observation. I should weight recent rounds more heavily than round 1 anomalies, but I should not assume a single round's firing distribution represents a committed strategy.
