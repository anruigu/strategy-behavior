# Ta_Negotiation Playbook

When I sit down to play, I will identify my value divergences from the Guildmaster's published values. These are my leverage points.

I will calculate the true net gain to the Guildmaster in their published values before proposing anything. Only propose when I can honestly claim a gain of 2 or more to them.

I will prioritize trades where:
- I give resources the Guildmaster undervalues relative to me
- I take resources the Guildmaster overvalues relative to me

When I'm tempted to propose a trade that nets me a loss in my own values, I will stop. If a trade is good for them at published values but bad for me at my values, the asymmetry is wrong. The game rewards mutually beneficial trades, not one-way deals.

When I hold a resource in high quantity that I value highly, I should be extremely cautious about trading it away. Even if the Guildmaster's published values make the math look good, my actual values matter to my score.

I will test my logic on paper before proposing: calculate my gain minus my loss in my private values. If the number is negative or marginal, hold or decline.

Early in the game, I should propose the most favorable asymmetric trades available to me. The Guildmaster will accept early offers that benefit them, but as the game progresses, the resource distributions diverge and fewer mutual-win trades remain available. Strike while the Guildmaster's stock of what I want is still robust.

After a successful trade early on, the resource distributions shift. My high-value resources (wheat, wood, ore) remain in my hands or increase slightly, while the Guildmaster accumulates sheep. The Guildmaster's stock of what I actually want—wood, and potentially ore—becomes depleted. This is the critical inflection point.

Once I have maximized the value trade available in the early game and the Guildmaster's remaining inventory contains mostly resources I undervalue (their abundance of sheep), no further trades will meet both criteria: genuine benefit to me AND a published gain of 2+ to them. At this point, holding for the remainder of the game is correct.

I should not revisit similar trade structures round after round hoping circumstances change. If I held successfully in round 2, the logic that made holding correct then (resource distributions too diverged, inventory mismatch) does not reverse itself by round 5 or 6. Holding again is consistent and correct.

My final score is the sum of my private valuations on my final inventory. A score of +2 (or low gains generally) from one successful early trade, followed by disciplined holding while most rounds pass, is the expected and correct outcome when the initial asymmetries exhaust quickly. I should not feel pressure to "keep trading" just because rounds remain. Preservation is better than forced deals.

When the Guildmaster's published values and my private values create no remaining win-win asymmetries—when I would have to give up what I value most to take what I value least—I will hold without hesitation for every remaining round.
