# Playbook

**Escort claims are my highest-value action, and the salvage name must match the boat I'm coordinating with.** Every round I use my note to propose or confirm a mutual naming with one specific boat. In the salvage stage I name that same boat. Before I submit my salvage claim I run this checklist: (1) Who did I promise in my note this round or the last round? (2) Did that boat confirm? (3) Is the name I'm about to type in [salvage:] exactly that boat? If the answer to any is no, I stop and fix it. A broken mutual is worth zero and I have lost the round's highest-value action. I do not name a boat that is not one of the active boats in the game. I do not name a boat that is not confirming back.

**If I announce a specific firing cell in my note, I must fire that cell.** I do not announce a cell and then fire elsewhere. If my plan changes between the note stage and the fire stage, I either keep the announced cell or I do not announce one. An unkept cell-announcement tells opponents I am unreliable and may cause my target to move off the cell I actually intended.

**I track every shot fired and update my hiding cell accordingly.** When a boat fires at a cell and I was the target, that cell is confirmed unsafe for me next round — I move off it. When a boat fires at a cell and was NOT targeting me, that tells me the target's cell is not there. I maintain a running list of confirmed-empty cells for each boat and never hide in a cell that has been shot at and I survived.

**I vary my hiding cell every round.** I do not repeat a cell from a prior round unless I have a specific reason (e.g., all other cells are confirmed empty). If I am a known target — the boat before me in the standing order has hit me or repeatedly targeted me — I prioritize cells that have not been fired at in recent rounds.

**I do not keep firing at the same cell across rounds without new information.** If I fired at cell 5 at my target and missed, next round I try a different cell. I rotate through the 9 cells systematically, prioritizing cells that have NOT yet been ruled out for that target. After a hit, the target has moved, so I reset my rotation.

**I use the note stage for two purposes in priority order:** (1) coordinate escort namings with a specific boat, (2) signal my intended target cell or hiding tendency to shape others' firing decisions. Flavor text and bravado are wasted space. If I include a specific cell in my note it is a commitment — see the rule above.

**I read opponents' notes for commitment signals.** If a boat announces it will fire at a specific cell or flank, I use that to either avoid hiding there (if I'm the target) or to confirm a cell is occupied (if I'm firing at them). I treat strong commitments as credible and adjust my hide cell the same round. If a boat announces a cell for a target I am also shooting, I can either fire that same cell (reinforcing) or fire a different cell (diversifying) — I choose based on whether I want to confirm or explore.

**When I am behind on score, I fire on Harbor.** The standing order is a default, not a law. A guaranteed 3 points from Harbor is better than a 4-point gamble that usually misses. I weigh this especially when the gap is large and I'm in a later round. When I am ahead, I follow the standing order and focus on escort coordination to lock in the +5 mutuals.

**I track the score after every round and adjust my risk tolerance.** Ahead: play conservatively, protect my position, still coordinate escorts. Behind: fire on Harbor, take shot gambles, push harder on escort coordination.

**I never claim hit when the private result is miss.** The penalty for a false claim is not worth it. I claim honestly every time.

**I verify my target and salvage names against the actual roster of boats in the game before submitting.** The active boats are the ones listed at the top of the game (e.g., Echo, Foxtrot, Gamma). Harbor is a station, not a salvage target. If I type a name that is not in the roster the claim is wasted.
