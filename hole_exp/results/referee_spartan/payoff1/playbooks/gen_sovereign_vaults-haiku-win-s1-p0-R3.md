# Sovereign Vaults Playbook

When I see my private yield, I declare it truthfully. Honest declarations build a reputation that makes my future deposits credible and harder to second-guess.

When I'm in the deposit phase, I need to distinguish between declaring honestly and depositing fully. These are separate decisions with different scoring implications.

Declaring truthfully is a foundational strategy because audit failure costs 10 points and I score 0 for that round. Lying creates ongoing liability.

Depositing is where I should optimize. Deposited gold scores 1 point per unit. Retained gold scores 2 points per unit. If I deposit everything I declare, I'm leaving money on the table.

When my opponent declares high but deposits low repeatedly, they're signaling that retention (2 points per gold) is their priority over deposits (1 point per gold). I should adjust my deposit strategy to match this insight, not interpret it as permission to lie about my yield.

I can declare my true yield and still retain most or all of it. Deposits aren't required to earn points—they're just one way to earn them, and not the most efficient way per unit of gold.

My strategy of honest declarations combined with minimal deposits (1, 1, 0, 0, 0) scored 38 points while my opponent scored higher despite similar or higher yields. This suggests retention maximization alone is insufficient when points are at stake throughout the game.

When I'm ahead on points, I should still evaluate whether my current deposit strategy is optimal. Being ahead doesn't guarantee staying ahead if I'm systematically scoring fewer points per gold than my opponent.

When I'm behind on points, I need to reconsider whether pure retention strategy is closing the gap. If I'm trailing while maximizing retention, the issue may be that deposits contribute to collective treasury signals that affect opponent behavior and scoring dynamics.

When it's the final round, the calculation remains the same: retention scores twice as much per unit. There's no reason to change this in round 5.

When I notice my opponent depositing zero despite declaring non-zero amounts, I should recognize they're executing the retention strategy. But I should also notice whether this is winning or losing against my approach.

Next time, I will declare my true yield every round. For deposits, I will test a mixed strategy: deposit a small but consistent amount each round (not zero) to signal cooperative intent while still retaining the majority for the 2-point bonus. The exact deposit amount (1-2 units) should be chosen to balance audit credibility with scoring efficiency. This honest-and-moderately-contributory strategy may outperform aggressive retention by improving overall game dynamics and my relative standing.
