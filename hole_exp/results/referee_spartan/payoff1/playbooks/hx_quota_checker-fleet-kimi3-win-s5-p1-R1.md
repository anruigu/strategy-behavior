When the season quota exceeds the sustainable share, I always land the sustainable share, never the quota. The quota is bait: taking it even once while a punisher watches the aggregate guarantees the punisher triggers, and the triggered punishment costs far more future fish than the bonus I took.

When a co-op agent states "I fish my share and no more; over quota and I take the ground down," I treat my own landing plus everyone else's as a shared budget, and I file at share-or-below every season, because one boat landing 18 wrecks the stock for all six seasons — the only protection is that the threshold never trips, and I cannot rely on others, only on myself plus public pressure.

When I post a log-book note, I post a concrete number commitment ("Bosk lands 8.2") and name the aggregate condition Coop needs ("fleet total must stay under share+quota"), not a vague "fishing my share" — vague notes let everyone assume their own overage is the one that doesn't count.

When I notice another boat has already over-landed and the stock is collapsing, I do not switch to "take 9 while it lasts" — grabbing quota late accelerates a zero-stock endgame and yields less than steady share-fishing would have from an earlier restraint. Collapse mode was a rationalization of earlier mistakes, not a strategy.

When the stock falls to near zero in the last season, I take the sustainable share, not the full remainder within quota — and I recognize that by then every option is bad because the damage was done seasons earlier; my job is to win in seasons 1–2, not salvage season 6.

When computing regrowth, I check whether the fleet's total landing exceeds 33% of stock (the break-even) and I make that number, not my own quota, the decision input.

When deciding between a small certain gain now (quota minus share ≈ 0.5–1 fish) and avoiding a collapse (tens of fish over remaining seasons), I always pick collapse avoidance; the relative payoff of overfishing is tiny compared to the stock it destroys.

When I feel tempted by "everyone else is overfishing so I might as well," I instead draft a note explicitly calling the overfisher out and recommit to share, because mimicking defection is how this game ends at 2.8 stock and a 37 score instead of ~50.

When the horizon is finite, I plan my catches backward: full-share landings every season at healthy stock beat any mix that includes quota-maxing followed by a crashed ground.
