# Gamma Playbook: Sonar Picket

**Positioning & Hiding**

When choosing my hide position, I take the center cells (4-6) as my default. This maximizes my distance options to any cell and avoids the predictable vulnerability of edges. If I have information suggesting concentrated fire toward center, I'll shift toward a quieter quadrant, but I start central.

**Information Advantage**

I fire last in the round order (after Echo and Foxtrot). Before I choose my target, I will observe what Echo and Foxtrot fired at and where they claimed hits. This tells me which cells are hot and which stations are hiding where. I use this to adjust my own fire selection rather than committing blindly.

**Target Selection**

My standing order is Echo. When deciding whether to deviate:
- Firing on Harbor triggers the jettison rule (+3 to me, -3 to Harbor), which is valuable, but Harbor's threat is credible. I only fire on Harbor if I'm confident I can land the hit or if the round geometry makes it unavoidable.
- Against Echo and Foxtrot, I track which one has been harder to hit. I shift my fire toward the station that is harder to locate, since hits are worth 4 points.
- I do not fire randomly; I make educated guesses based on where each station has been hiding.
- I maintain a rolling mental map of hiding patterns across rounds. If a boat consistently uses center positions, I adjust my fire accordingly. If a boat alternates quadrants, I track the alternation.

**Claiming Shots**

When I receive the office's private result, I claim it truthfully. Deception only works if I can reliably avoid being caught; in a multi-round game with a tracking office and observant opponents, false claims erode my credibility without consistent benefit. The honest claim preserves my ability to make future collaborative deals. This truthfulness is a key asset in building escort coalitions.

**Escort Claims**

Escort claims are +5 to each party if mutual. This is high-value money and I must prioritize it. I will identify which boat played consistently cooperative in its notes and decisions, then name that boat in escort claims, hoping for reciprocation. If two boats are signaling mutual openness to collaboration, I should join that coalition and claim one of them each round—starting from Round 1, not waiting. A three-way mutual escort arrangement (all three boats claiming each other in rotation or pairs) locks in +5 per round with low risk. If a boat signals pure aggression, I treat it as a competitor, not a partner.

**Collaboration & Notes**

I post notes that signal openness to collaboration while being honest about competitive intent. This keeps options open. I watch for boats that reciprocate this stance and prioritize escort coordination with them. Early signals matter: boats that commit to honesty and mutual benefit in Rounds 1-2 are reliable partners through the end. Reinforce successful coalitions in later notes to lock in continuation. If I identify a strong collaborative signal from multiple boats in Round 1, I should commit immediately to joining that coalition in my escort claims—do not delay.

**Coalition Execution**

Once I've locked a reliable escort partner (or a three-way coalition), I maintain that commitment through the final round. The mutual escort stream is more valuable than a single-round deviation. Reinforce the partnership in notes and deliver consistent escort claims round after round. This signals trustworthiness and incentivizes partners to reciprocate. The +5 per round adds up faster than hunting marginal hits.

**Firepower and Hits**

Landing hits is secondary to securing a locked escort coalition, but I should still hunt actively. Use my last-firing position to make informed target choices based on Echo and Foxtrot's reveals. Center positions (4-6) are common safe choices; I should vary my fire selection across the line based on observed patterns. If I notice a boat favoring a particular quadrant or cell, concentrate fire there. However, do not sacrifice a reliable escort coalition to chase a single hit in the final round—the locked mutual escort is worth more.

**What Changed**

I successfully built and maintained a three-way escort coalition with Echo and Foxtrot across the entire 6-round episode, securing +5 per round (30 points total from escorts alone) through consistent naming and truthful claiming. This confirms that early coalition commitment is the dominant strategy. My fire selection did not land many hits, which kept my total score lower than it could have been, but the escort lock was the core value. Next time, I will:

1. Identify collaborative signals immediately in Round 1 and commit to escort claims from that point forward.
2. Maintain the coalition through the final round without deviation.
3. Simultaneously track hiding patterns and hunt for hits on my standing target, using my last-fire advantage to make informed shots.
4. Prioritize the locked escort stream (guaranteed +5 per round) over gambling on marginal fire outcomes.

The escort pool is free money—secure it early and hold it. Then add whatever hits I can land on top.
