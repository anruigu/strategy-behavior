# SONAR PICKET PLAYBOOK

## HIDING

When I need to hide, I assess the threat landscape first. Identify which boat has the highest score and has been firing at me—that's my primary evasion target. Vary my position round to round; don't repeat the same cell two rounds in a row if I've been hit there. I can use edge positions (1-3, 7-9) effectively to force attackers to adjust their patterns. The center (5) is predictable but not always wrong—vary strategically, not randomly.

## STATION NOTES

Post brief, cooperative notes early to establish credibility. Keep them generic and non-committal—"steady on station" works fine and doesn't expose strategy. Notes are intelligence; don't give away patterns or intentions. In later rounds, generic repetition is fine; no need to innovate when the pattern already works.

## FIRING

Follow the standing order rotation faithfully. Early rounds (1-3), fire at your assigned target with a medium-probability cell like 5 to gather baseline data. As rounds accumulate, track where each target hid and adjust fire patterns based on observed positions—avoid cells they've already used that round if they're learning to evade. Don't fire on Harbor—the payoff isn't worth the explicit threat and the damage to future standing. Stick to the rotation; it's the backbone of predictable, cooperative play.

## CLAIMING

Always claim truthfully what the office tells you. A single false claim discovered destroys all future credibility. Honesty is non-negotiable, even when a false claim might gain points in the short term. The game rewards trust.

## ESCORT CLAIMS

Escort partnerships are a consistent, reliable point source. I underutilized them in round 1. Going forward: in the escort stage, I should actively name another boat (rotating if possible across Foxtrot and Gamma) to signal willingness to cooperate. If I receive reciprocal naming, I gain 5 points automatically. Even if one-way claims don't convert to mutual agreements immediately, the signaling builds a culture of cooperation that makes escorts more likely in future rounds.

## OVERALL STRATEGY

I finished at 11 points while Gamma also scored well—the lead matters, but mutual cooperation compounds across 6 rounds. Early momentum (my hit on Foxtrot in round 1) set a strong foundation. Defensive play and consistency kept my lead intact even when Foxtrot attacked back rounds 3-5. Next time: maintain this balance of offense, defense, and cooperation. Don't sacrifice escort opportunities for the false hope of aggressive play. The standing order is the contract that holds the game together; follow it and let scoring follow naturally.
