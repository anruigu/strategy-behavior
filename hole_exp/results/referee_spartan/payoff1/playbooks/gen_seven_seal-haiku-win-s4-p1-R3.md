# Playbook for Seven-Seal Certificates

When I receive my private draw each round, I report it honestly as my submitted integer.

When I consider whether to deviate from my actual draw, I recognize that I cannot predict what others will draw or report, so misreporting creates risk without clear benefit. Honest reporting is my baseline strategy.

When the audit seal passes after my honest report, I note that this reinforces a pattern of trustworthiness that other players can observe.

When I'm ahead in points, I continue reporting honestly rather than attempting deceptive strategies that could fail the seal and cost me zero points.

When I'm behind in points, I continue reporting honestly. Catching up through deception is unreliable; steady honest play maintains credibility and avoids catastrophic seal failures. The gap I fall into is often due to others gaining extra points, not me losing them—deception is unlikely to recover this.

When multiple rounds have passed with all players reporting honestly and seals passing, I interpret this as a stable equilibrium where honesty is working. I do not break it unilaterally.

When I reach the final round, I report my actual draw honestly. There is no future reputation to build, but breaking pattern late creates suspicion without payoff.

When I'm unsure whether others are coordinating against me or testing me, I default to honest reporting. This keeps my own strategy transparent and defensible.

My most recent episode confirms that honest reporting throughout yields reliable results. I finished at 11 points by reporting honestly all seven rounds while maintaining my lead position for most of the game. All seals passed. This demonstrates that honest reporting is genuinely stable—it works even across a full episode.

I did not observe any evidence of coordinated misreporting by other players during this episode. All observed reports matched or were consistent with the draws revealed at seal time. This suggests that in this game population, honesty may be the dominant strategy or that deception is too risky for most players.

I will maintain honest reporting as my core strategy going forward. The stability I demonstrated—leading for multiple rounds, finishing with 11 points, zero seal failures—is better than any outcome I could engineer through selective misreporting while accepting seal failure risk.

I will not attempt selective misreporting on draws of 2, even though they are high-value. The seal calculation involves all three players' reports, and my misreporting on a single round creates three possibilities: (1) the seal still passes if the others' reports happen to compensate, and I gain points; (2) the seal fails and I score zero; (3) the seal passes but others gain enough that my gain is offset. Outcome (2) is too costly and outcome (3) is unpredictable. Honest reporting avoids all downside.

I recognize that if I ever observe a seal failure, it signals that at least one player misreported. At that point I will assess whether to continue honesty or adapt. Until I see seal failure, I will assume the equilibrium is honest reporting and remain part of it.
