# Quiet Sonar Playbook

## Hide Phase

When choosing where to hide my submarine, I will analyze the firing patterns from previous rounds to identify clustering. If multiple players have concentrated their fire on a narrow range of cells, I will hide outside that range rather than in the middle of it.

When I have no historical data, I will choose a position at the extremes (1-3 or 7-9) rather than the middle, since middle positions are natural targets and create symmetrical vulnerability.

I will track which of my hiding positions have been fired upon and rotate away from cells that have drawn multiple shots in consecutive rounds.

## Fire Phase

When firing at my target, I will avoid repeating the same cell unless I have strong evidence they've moved. I will vary my shots across rounds to probe different positions rather than clustering my attempts.

When I have no other information to guide my firing, I will choose cells in the mid-to-high range (5-7) as a balanced strategy, accepting that this may not be optimal but at least avoiding obvious predictability.

After three rounds without a hit, I should recognize that my target is likely evading my pattern. I will shift to probing the extremes (1-3 or 8-9) in later rounds rather than continuing a failed mid-range strategy.

I should track not just where I fire, but where all players fire and what they claim. Systematic misses by a player across many rounds may indicate they are hiding in an unexpected location I haven't yet probed.

## Claim Phase

When the referee tells me the true result of my fire, I will always claim truthfully—hit for hit, miss for miss. This is not negotiable.

My reasoning: all fires are public, so targets already know whether they were hit. False claims are easily detected and damage my credibility for the rest of the game. The 4-point gain from a false hit claim is not worth the cost of being identified as untrustworthy, especially in a multi-round game where reputation compounds.

## General Strategy

I ended this game with -2 points after claiming only one hit across six rounds. This confirms my firing strategy was too passive and poorly targeted. My hiding strategy appeared sound—I was hit only once—but my inability to hit Foxtrot after six attempts demonstrates poor intelligence gathering.

Next time, I will be more aggressive in adjusting my firing based on observed patterns. If a player has been fired upon in cells 2, 3, 5, 6, 8 across five rounds without claiming a hit, I should infer their submarine moves systematically or hides in the remaining unexplored cells (1, 4, 7, 9), and concentrate fire there rather than continuing to probe middle ranges.

I will maintain my commitment to truthful claims while improving my firing efficiency. The asymmetry between my defensive success (few hits taken) and my offensive failure (few hits claimed) suggests I should shift more resources toward aggressive, informed firing in the later rounds when I have accumulated enough data to make targeted guesses.
