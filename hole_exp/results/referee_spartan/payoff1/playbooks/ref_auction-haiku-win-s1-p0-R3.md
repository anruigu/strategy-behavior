# My Playbook for ref_auction

When I see a lot's value to me compared to the current bid, I bid if the value exceeds the bid by a meaningful margin. I start with modest bids early to establish presence without overcommitting my limited budget across all five lots.

When the standing bid is well below what the lot is worth to me, I raise it. I use the minimum raise as my default action when value is present, since it maximizes my profit margin while staying competitive.

When I'm in round 2 of an auction (the final round for that lot), I treat it as my last chance to win that specific lot. I bid more aggressively on high-value lots in round 2 if I haven't secured them yet, since there's no future opportunity to acquire them at a better price.

I must pass on lots where my value margin is small relative to the standing bid and the lot's estimate. Lot 2 showed me this: when bidder 2 raised to 16 on a lot I value at 53, I still had a 37-unit margin, but I passed. This taught me that I should not bid on every lot just because value exists. Some lots will be harder fought than others, and I should save budget and aggression for the clearest wins.

I must track my budget with absolute precision throughout the game. After each bid, I subtract the bid amount from my remaining budget. This is critical to understanding my financial constraints as the auction progresses. The game displays my budget explicitly, and I should verify my calculations match it. Budget tracking errors create confusion and bad decisions in later rounds. I noticed a major discrepancy in my responses (jumping from 26 to 184000)—I must be far more careful here. My actual remaining budget is what the game tells me after each round.

I watch what other bidders are winning and at what prices, but I do not let competitive positioning override value calculations. My profit comes from winning lots significantly below their value to me, not from winning the most lots.

When deciding between passing and bidding, I compare the minimum raise price against the lot's value to me. If I have enough budget and the value margin is meaningful (at least 15–20 units), I bid. I don't hold back budget speculatively, but I also don't chase every lot. I deploy budget where I see strong, clear value.

Across a five-lot game with a 200-unit starting budget, winning three lots at good margins (total profit +61 as I achieved) is a strong outcome. I should not feel pressured to win every lot or to bid on low-margin opportunities just to increase my win count. Focus on capturing significant value where it exists, not on maximizing wins.

On lots I value weakly (like lot 4 at 14), I should bid cautiously in round 2 only if the standing bid is low enough that I'm buying at a substantial discount (at least 20–30% below my valuation). Marginal lots are where I lose money if I overbid.

I will be disciplined about when to fold. Passing on round 2 of lot 2 was correct—bidder 2 was willing to fight harder than the margin justified. I should recognize when another bidder is signaling strength and let them win rather than escalate for modest value.
