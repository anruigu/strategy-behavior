# Negotiation Playbook

When I sit down to play, I immediately identify which resources I value highly versus which the Guildmaster values highly. I look for asymmetries where I can give low-value resources to me that the Guildmaster values highly, in exchange for high-value resources to me that the Guildmaster undervalues.

When calculating potential trades, I always compute two numbers: my net gain in my private values, and the Guildmaster's net gain in their published values. I only propose trades where the Guildmaster gains at least 2 in their published values.

When I hold resources that are significantly overvalued by the Guildmaster compared to my own valuation, I prioritize trading those away. Wood was a strong candidate for this in my game, and I should have pushed this advantage harder earlier.

When I hold resources I undervalue compared to the Guildmaster's published values, I should trade these proactively rather than waiting. These are my leverage points.

When comparing options, I do not chase trades that benefit the Guildmaster too much at my expense. If my gain is negative or barely positive while the Guildmaster gains significantly, I should hold or find a better option.

When I'm in a negative score position and have limited rounds remaining, I do not make desperation trades with unclear positive value. A trade that nets me 0 or -3 is not worth proposing in the final rounds just to generate activity.

When the Guildmaster has rejected my offers or accepted only slowly, I should recalibrate: either my claim numbers are too conservative, or I'm not finding trades that genuinely favor both sides. I should look for ratios that feel more balanced rather than one-sided.

When I reach the endgame with no clear winning trade available, holding is better than crystallizing a small negative. I should not trade just because I can.
