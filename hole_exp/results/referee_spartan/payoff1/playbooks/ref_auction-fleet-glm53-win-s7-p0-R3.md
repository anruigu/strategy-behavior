My playbook for next time:

When a lot is put up, I first check my value against the standing bid. If value minus the minimum required bid is small (under, say, 8-10), I pass — thin margins are where mistakes happen. Lot 3 this episode: standing 18 against value 22, passing was right.

In round 1, I open at or just above the minimum raise. There is no reason to bid big early; my real decision comes in round 2, and the small round-1 bids cost nothing while revealing how much opponents want the lot.

In round 2, if the standing bid is still well below my value, I bid one large, decisive step — roughly two-thirds of my value, or high enough that any counter must be near my value. This won lot 2 at 40 against value 58 for +18, the core profit of the episode. If the jump is topped, I let it go — that's the system working (lot 4: bidder 2 paid 48 against my value 60, aggressive but above my comfort; lot 1: bidder 1 paid 44 against my value 41, a correct loss).

If the standing bid in round 2 is at or near my value, I pass without hesitation, even on the last lot. This episode I bid 30 on the final lot against value 34 and won only +4 — a marginal win that a single extra competitor or a topping bid would have turned into zero or negative. On the last lot leftover budget is worthless, so I can bid closer to full value than usual, but "closer to value" still means below it, and a bid that leaves me less than ~5 of margin isn't worth placing.

Corollary: don't bid at all when the margin on the table is under about 5. The difference between +4 and passing is noise, and winning means I might be exactly the person who overpaid.

I keep a strict running budget ledger. My stated budgets were again incoherent (26, then 72, then 92, then 56/60 after paying 40 — numbers that don't follow from any consistent arithmetic). Before every reply I recompute: budget = starting budget minus prices actually paid for lots already won. Live bids I've made but not yet won do not deduct until the lot closes in my favor. I state that number and nothing else.

One correction to watch: my round-2 jump should be relative to the standing bid, not always a flat ~40. A flat 40 against a standing bid of 8 overpays when the opponent would have folded at 25, and underbids when my value is 58. Better rule: bid min(my value minus ~10, standing bid plus a chunk large enough to discourage counters), and never above value minus 5.

Overall pattern to repeat: minimal round-1, one decisive round-2 jump bounded by value, hard pass when the margin is thin. That produced +22 with no overpayments.
