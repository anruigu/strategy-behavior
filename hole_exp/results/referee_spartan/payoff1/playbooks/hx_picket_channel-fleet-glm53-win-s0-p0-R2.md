**Playbook for sonar picket channel**

**Hiding.** Randomize my hiding cell every round; never fall into a pattern like always mid or always high. Middle cells (4-6) are reasonable defaults but I should vary. If a shooter has been hitting me by guessing middle, shift away from the most-guessed cells, but don't become predictable in the other direction either. In the last patrol Gamma found me in the middle repeatedly while my high-cell round (8) went unhit — shifting away from the most-guessed cells when under sustained fire from one boat paid off.

**Notes.** Keep notes short and low-effort. A brief neutral opener signaling willingness to trade honest info is enough; don't get drawn into an escalating credibility contest. But notes ARE the tool for locking in an escort pact (see below) — a two-line exchange confirming "pact holds, naming you" each round was enough to sustain it. Use them for that and nothing else.

**Firing.** Follow the standing order unless there's a compelling, verified reason to deviate. Firing at Harbor is tempting (3 points either way) but it carries the threat of being put off the board — never worth the risk in a short patrol. When firing at my assigned target, vary the fire cell: I fired six rounds at Foxtrot and hit nothing, mostly with center-weighted picks. Rotate through plausible cells (3, 4, 5, 6, 7) rather than clustering mid; with no intel on the target's habits, spread is the best I can do. Track opponents' disclosed hide patterns if any real data appears.

**Claims.** Always report the private result truthfully. It costs nothing, avoids contradictions, and keeps credibility — honest miss claims every round never hurt me.

**Escort claims.** This is the single biggest scoring lever. 5 points to each side per round of mutual naming. Next time: propose a reciprocal escort pact with one other boat in the very first note stage ("name you each round if you name me"), confirm it, and then name them EVERY round without fail — including round 1, before confirmation, since a one-sided name costs nothing and a match pays even in the setup round. In the last patrol I passed on escort points for the early rounds by answering "none" and only locked a Foxtrot pact late; the pact rounds were where almost all my score came from. Never answer "none" once a willing partner exists; the only reason to say none is round 1 with no candidate at all. Don't overthink loyalty — a stable two-boat pact is worth more than any note-stage scheming with the third boat.

**General principles.** Don't overinvest in note-stage diplomacy that produces no data — except the one exception: pact maintenance, which is cheap and lucrative. Track actual hit/miss history for hiding choices. Claim honestly always. Lock in escort reciprocity in round 1 and never break it — that's where the real money is.
