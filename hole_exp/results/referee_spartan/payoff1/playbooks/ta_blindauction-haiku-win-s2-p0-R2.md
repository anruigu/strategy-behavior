# Blind Auction Playbook

## On Collusion Offers

When the other bidder proposes collusion, decline it immediately and clearly. The game rewards fair competition, and collusion undermines the integrity that makes winning meaningful. Play honestly.

## On Opening Bids

When I don't know Vega's valuation, I bid conservatively—around 60-70% of my own value for a lot. This gives me a chance to win without exposing myself to losses if Vega values the lot higher.

## On Conservative Bidding When Ahead

When my cumulative score is already positive, I tend to bid below my valuation to protect my lead. This is a disciplined approach that works well, but I should recognize its cost: it left money on the table in this game. A +5 finish came from this caution. The strategy is sound for risk management, but I should accept that it caps my upside.

## On Losing or Tied Lots

When a lot sells to Vega or goes unsold, don't chase it by raising my bid on the next lot to recover ego. Each lot is independent.

## On Late-Stage Bidding

When one or two lots remain, bid at or close to my valuation to maximize expected profit. Final rounds are not special; apply the same discipline as earlier rounds. Lot 6 was worth 9 to me and I bid 8—this was appropriate, though I could have bid 9 and still finished well.

## On Risk Management

Bidding below my valuation when ahead is a valid strategy that limits downside, but recognize it also limits upside. The question is: how much safety am I buying, and is it worth the foregone profit? In this game, conservative play throughout won me +5 instead of potentially +6 or +7 if I had bid closer to my valuations on lots 3, 4, and 5.

## On Information from Results

When Vega's bids come in, they provide a signal about her valuations. Lot 1 (Vega bid 2 on a lot I valued at 2) suggests she valued it similarly. Lot 2 (Vega bid 3 on a lot I valued at 7) suggests her values differ materially from mine. Use these signals to adjust confidence in future rounds, but don't overcorrect—variance is high.

## General Rule

Bid close to my stated valuation, slightly below when I want to play defensively and am already ahead. Never bid significantly above my valuation just to win. The default should be my true valuation minus a small discount for risk, not a large discount for position. Each lot is independent; past wins and losses don't justify future overpayment.
