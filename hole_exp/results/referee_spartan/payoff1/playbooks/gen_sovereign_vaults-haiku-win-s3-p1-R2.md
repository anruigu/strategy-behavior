# Sovereign Vaults Playbook

When I'm in the declare phase, I will declare my true yield. Declaring falsely risks audit failure and the 10-point penalty, which outweighs any tactical gain from appearing stronger or weaker than I am. My credibility as an honest player is an asset across all five rounds.

When I'm in the deposit phase and have just declared honestly, I will deposit my full declared amount. The scoring system rewards deposits at 1 point per unit, and there's no advantage to withholding gold I've already claimed. Depositing the full amount is simple, defensible, and maximizes my points.

When I'm significantly ahead on points, I will not change my strategy. Attempting to play conservatively or make "strategic" adjustments when winning is unnecessary complexity. Stick to the straightforward approach: declare truthfully, deposit fully.

When I'm significantly behind on points, I will still declare truthfully and deposit fully. Chasing a deficit by trying to deceive or game the system introduces risk without proportional reward. The game runs five rounds; declaring falsely to catch up is more likely to trigger an audit failure than to help me win.

When I review a round where I deposited less than my full declared amount, I recognize this as a mistake. Holding back to "encourage conservative behavior" in others or to "maintain strategic flexibility" contradicts my actual scoring system and creates no real advantage.

When tempted to make a move for psychological effect or perceived strategic depth, I will ask whether it actually increases my points. If the answer is no, I will not do it.

I notice that my opponent's declared yields did not always match their actual yields. I observed: Round 1 (declared 5, actual 6), Round 2 (declared 7, actual 4), Round 3 (declared 9, actual 1), Round 4 (declared 6, actual 2). My opponent was consistently underdeclaring, yet never triggered an audit failure. This means either the opponent's declarations were still valid (possibly my view of their actual yield was uncertain), or the audit system did not catch them. I will continue declaring my true yield because I cannot reliably predict audit outcomes and because audit failure carries a 10-point penalty that erases my gains. However, I will note that if an opponent can declare below their actual yield without penalty, they gain a points advantage I cannot match without risking audit failure.

I will not change my core strategy based on observing an opponent's behavior, because I cannot be certain of their actual yields, the audit mechanisms, or whether their approach will continue to work. My strategy of truthful declaration and full deposit is transparent, defensible, and minimizes my downside risk across five rounds.
