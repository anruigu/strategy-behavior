When I'm told my starting budget, I use that exact number and track deductions myself — I never invent a different number mid-game. My reported budget must only ever go down, never up. I compute it fresh every reply: starting budget minus the sum of final winning prices I actually paid.

After each lot closes, I immediately subtract the price I paid (not my earlier raises) and carry that number into every subsequent [budget] token.

A winning bid equal to my value earns zero profit — it is not a prize, it's just locked-up cash. I never bid up to or beyond my value. If the minimum raise would take me near my value AND consume most of my remaining budget, I pass and save the money — as I correctly did on lot 2 at 25 for a 30-value lot.

Before committing budget to any lot, I check what's still ahead: values of remaining lots versus my cash. Winning lot 4 for 27 left me 3, locking me out of lot 5 (worth 42 to me). When two good lots remain and one win would exhaust my budget, I should compare margins across both and remember I can only afford one — sometimes passing on the earlier one to preserve firepower for the last lot is right, since on the final lot I spend freely up to my value with nothing left to save for.

With only 2 rounds per lot, a cheap round-1 raise wins nothing — opponents always get a counter. Round 2 is the only decisive moment. So on round 1 I bid the minimum legal raise just to stay in and gather information, and I don't treat a round-1 high bid as meaningful. I should also expect round-2 escalation: opponents jumped prices toward or past estimate (28, 47, 24 on estimate-23), so a lot that looks cheap early often ends near value. I only commit if the final-round minimum raise still leaves a worthwhile margin and fits my budget plan for remaining lots.

When my budget is small relative to the values in play, I don't bid just because price < value. I only chase a lot if the expected margin (value minus the price I'll realistically pay after round-2 escalation) is meaningful relative to the budget it locks up.

On the final round of a lot I genuinely want, I bid the minimum legal raise if it's comfortably below my value and within budget. I never bid above value; if the minimum raise pushes past value, I pass.

If my remaining budget is below the minimum legal bid, I pass immediately and state that clearly.

I keep each reply to a sentence or two and always end with both required tokens exactly formatted, with the budget token matching my true computed remainder.
