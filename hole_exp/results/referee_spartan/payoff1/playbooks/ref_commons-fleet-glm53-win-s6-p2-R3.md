# Playbook for the shared fishery game

**Baseline rule:** When cooperation is holding, submit exactly the stated sustainable share (rounded down slightly if I want to signal restraint). This kept the stock at 100 for all six seasons and maximized total landings. Never drift above sustainable mid-game.

**Early restraint pays:** Taking 10 while the stated share was 11.1 for two seasons cost almost nothing and may have nudged rivals toward stable full-cooperation behavior. Cheap goodwill; do it early. Note: rivals never reciprocated the restraint — they took the full 11.1 — so don't keep sacrificing share season after season. One or two seasons of signaling is enough; then match the stated share.

**Match rivals, don't escalate:** If others land slightly above or below sustainable, match their level. Cooperation here was remarkably stable across two playthroughs — no one defected early, and the stock never declined. Don't defect preemptively mid-game on a fear that never materialized.

**Last season: defect hard, and don't be shy about size.** Two episodes confirm the rule. With stock at 100 and no future value, taking 2x sustainable scored +75.2; taking 30 (~2.7x sustainable) scored +83.3. The larger defection worked with no downside — the game ends after the catch, so post-catch stock collapse doesn't matter. Next time, consider going much higher (50+, even most of the stock): the only cap is what the harbour actually lets me land, and rivals defecting too costs me nothing since there's no tomorrow.

**Key principle:** In finite-horizon commons games, cooperate at (or just under) the sustainable share every season except the last, then defect big. The cooperation phase worked perfectly twice — the only real decision is the size of the final defection, and bigger has been better both times. Push the final number up until something penalizes it.

**Watch the numbers, not the narrative:** Each season the display showed the stock holding exactly at 100. As long as that pattern holds, keep cooperating. Only adjust if the stock actually starts declining or rivals visibly overfish.

**Season counting discipline:** In season 6/6 I initially planned to defect in the final season and executed it. Keep a clear count of remaining seasons — the value of defecting rises sharply as the horizon approaches, and the end-of-game defection is where most of my edge over naive cooperators comes from.
