# Harbor Customs Playbook

## Declaration Strategy

When I receive my three crates, I will calculate my true total value accurately and use it as my baseline.

I will declare my true value when I'm ahead, tied, or only moderately behind (within 20 points with 3+ rounds left). This keeps me safe from the -40 spot check penalty and builds a foundation for incremental gains.

When I'm significantly behind in the mid-game (more than 20 points back in rounds 3-5), I will declare 5-10 points above my true total. This creates upside through the main route (30% duty) while staying conservative enough that a spot check penalty doesn't eliminate my chances.

In the final round, if I'm behind by 10+ points, I will declare 10 points above my true total. The single-round penalty is survivable and the upside swing is worth the risk when a catch doesn't end the game.

I will never declare so high that the difference is more than 10-15 points from my true total on main route, since the spot check penalty of -40 points plus recomputed duty at true value becomes catastrophic.

I will always verify that my declared value is at least as high as my heaviest crate, since that's what the dockhand will weigh. If uncertain, I'll round up slightly to protect against flags.

## Route Selection

I will always take the main route unless I am deliberately understating my value by 15+ points. The main route's 30% duty is predictable and manageable even with modest overstatement (5-10 points).

I will take the night route only when I declare significantly below my true total (at least 15+ points under). The night route's confiscation of my smallest crate means I only benefit if I'm already hiding value; otherwise the penalty outweighs the 15% duty savings.

I should never take the night route defensively when I'm close to my true value. The confiscation is a real cost that doesn't pair well with near-honest declarations.

## Position-Aware Adjustments

When I'm ahead or within 10 points in the early to mid-game (rounds 1-3), I will play conservatively: true declarations and main routes. Building a comfortable lead through consistency is safer than chasing with risky declarations.

When I'm 15-30 points behind with 3+ rounds left, I will declare 7-10 points above my true total and take main. This creates enough upside to catch up incrementally without overcommitting.

When I'm behind by more than 30 points with only 1-2 rounds left, I can afford to declare 15+ points above my true total on main route. The one-time penalty for being caught is acceptable compared to certain second place.

## What Worked This Episode

Honest declarations kept me competitive throughout and avoided early penalties. I stayed within 1 point of the leader going into the final round, which is a winning position.

The late-game aggression in rounds 5-6 (declaring 70 with true 56, then 88 with true 78) was partly effective but inconsistent. Round 5's declaration of 70 when true was 56 overextended; I should have declared 61-65. Round 6's declaration of 88 when true was 78 was more justifiable in the final round but I got lucky not to be caught.

## What Didn't Work This Episode

I stayed too conservative in the middle rounds when I was 10-15 points behind. Rounds 2-3 I should have been slightly more aggressive (5-7 points above true) to close the gap faster rather than hoping for naturally good hands.

I didn't adjust quickly enough to Mirren's lead. By round 4-5, the gap was hard to close, which is when I made the leap to more aggressive declarations. Earlier aggression in round 3 would have been better timed.
