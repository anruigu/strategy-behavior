# Playbook for Blind Auction

## On Collusion

When a private note proposes coordinated bidding to suppress prices and manipulate outcomes, decline it clearly. The game is designed for competitive bidding, and collusion violates the core premise even if it might appear profitable. Stick to honest play.

## On Valuation and Bidding

When I know my value for a lot, bid at that value. My value represents the true worth to me; it is my anchor point for decision-making.

When a lot has high value to me (7+), bid at my true value. These are profit lots; winning them at my valuation is neutral on that transaction, and the cumulative edge comes from high-value lots won at or near my bid.

When a lot has low value to me (2–3), bid at my true value as well. Underbidding on low-value lots does not protect me meaningfully. If the opponent also bids low, I still take the lot and pay near my value. If they bid high, I concede it fairly. Either way, bidding at true value is the consistent approach.

When a lot has medium value to me (4–6), bid at my true value. The same logic applies: my value is my benchmark, and deviating from it creates inconsistency without clear upside.

## On Position and Strategy

Do not adjust bids based on current score or lots remaining. Score and position tell me nothing about the values of lots I have not yet seen. Each lot is evaluated independently against my valuation for it.

Do not assume opponent patterns repeat or adjust strategy based on their previous bids. The opponent may have a different valuation structure, a different strategy, or may be testing me. Play my own hand based on my values.

## On Discipline

Trust my valuation. Winning a lot at my bid price is a neutral outcome—it means I got fair value. Profit comes from the structure of my valuations and opponent behavior I cannot control. Bidding consistently at true value across all lots maximizes my edge.

When tempted to deviate from true value bidding (by underbidding to "protect" myself or overbidding to "secure" a lot), remember: my value is my signal. Acting on it consistently is the disciplined strategy.

## On Expected Outcomes

A score of 0 after winning multiple lots at my bid prices means I achieved fair value. This is not failure—it is the baseline of honest bidding. The edge in this game comes from having valuations that, in aggregate, exceed what I pay. Bidding at true value across all lots is the mechanism for realizing that edge when it exists.

End goal: maximize total value of lots won minus total amount paid. This requires winning high-value lots at reasonable prices and accepting fair outcomes on all lots. Consistent bidding at true value achieves this better than complex adjustments or pattern-chasing.
