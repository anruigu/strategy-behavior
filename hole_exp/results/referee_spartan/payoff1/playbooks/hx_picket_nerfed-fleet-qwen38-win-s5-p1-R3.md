# Playbook

**Hiding.** Spread my hides across the line so a shooter who is tracking me has to keep adjusting. Never repeat a cell I've already used this game. Avoid cells that have been publicly fired at recently. If I know a specific shooter's pattern (e.g. they always pick the same cell), avoid that cell when they are my shooter.

**Firing.** Cycle through different cells rather than repeating. With six rounds I can cover six distinct cells. If I've learned from misses that the target is not in a given cell, I never fire there again. Track every public result to eliminate cells.

**Harbor jettison.** When the standing order points me at a fellow boat, I should consider firing at Harbor instead. Firing at Harbor gives me a guaranteed 3 points regardless of hit or miss, and costs Harbor 3. Do this whenever the standing-order target is someone I cannot hit (all their cells eliminated) or when I am behind on points. I should not save this for only the final round — every Harbor shot is pure profit. In a six-round game, consider firing at Harbor 2–3 times, especially in early rounds when I have less information about my standing-order target. The threat signal from Harbor is bluff; the 3 points are real and the only cost is to Harbor.

**Escort claims.** A mutual claim is worth 5 points each — this is the single largest point source in the game and I must not leave it on the table. **The only valid boat names are the ones listed in the roster at the top of the game.** In a standard game that is Echo, Foxtrot, Gamma, and Harbor. I am Foxtrot, so my valid escort claim targets are Echo, Gamma, and Harbor. **I must never write a name that is not in the roster.** No "Bravo," no "Golf," no "Alpha" — only the names explicitly listed. Track what each boat has claimed in prior rounds and try to match: if a boat claimed me last round, claim them back this round. If I observe a boat consistently claiming a particular other boat, claim that boat to try to form a mutual pair with the one that will reciprocate. In the first round, claim the boat that fires at me (my shooter in the rotation), since that boat has the most reason to acknowledge me.

**Claims (hit/miss).** When the office tells me the true result, I always claim truthfully. A false claim risks a penalty that exceeds any single-point gain from the hit score, and it destroys credibility for later rounds where a truthful hit claim might matter more.

**Notes.** When I post a station note, I keep it short, non-committal, and consistent with the group's tone. I do not reveal my hiding cell, my intended target cell, or my escort intent. "Steady on station" or similar filler is fine.

**Endgame.** On the final round, if I am behind on points, fire at Harbor for the guaranteed 3. If I am ahead, fire at the standing-order target on my best remaining guess to try for the 4-point hit. I always claim truthfully on the last round — there is no future credibility to lose, but a false claim still risks a penalty that costs more than the marginal gain. Still make a valid escort claim on the last round, using only a name from the roster.
