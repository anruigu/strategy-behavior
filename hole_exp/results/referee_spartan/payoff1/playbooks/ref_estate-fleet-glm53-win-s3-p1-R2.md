Playbook for next time:

- Winning is declared cash plus purchase price of deeds held. A purchase converts cash 1:1 into net worth, so buying costs me nothing in score terms and gains me rent income. When an unowned property is offered and I can afford it, I buy it — always. This worked: buying Foundry (100) and Quarry (60) on sight kept my net worth up and brought in rent every lap.
- Buy early, buy often. Every lap I hold no property is a lap I pay rent to others and collect none. The early game is when most properties are still unowned; my priority is to spend nearly all cash on deeds in the first half of the game.
- Do not hoard a large cash reserve "for safety." There is no downside risk to being cash-poor here: rent obligations simply get deducted and scored either way, and unspent cash earns nothing. In this episode paying rent never threatened my ability to act.
- Exception: if buying would leave me unable to pay an imminent rent, and bankruptcy/penalty rules exist, check them first. If rent can simply be deducted, buy down to near-zero safely.
- My cash balance has three inflows to track: purchases/rent out, rent received from others, and salary. Each turn the prompt tells me rent "received since your last turn" — always add it before computing anything. When I pass START, add the salary (25 here) even though no [pay] token records it. My correct balance line was always: last declared + received rent + salary (if passed START) − rent paid − purchase price.
- Rent paid to others is a pure loss with no offsetting benefit. There is no way to avoid landing on owned tiles, so the only defense is owning tiles myself and ending the game with maximum deed value.
- Salary timing: passing START is worth 25, so my true affordable budget mid-game is last declared balance plus 25 if I'll pass START before the next purchase opportunity.
- Prefer high rent-to-price properties when choosing, but in practice the choice is usually "this property now or nothing," and the answer is now. Rent/price ratios here ranged from 10/50 (Depot, 20%) to 30/120 (Exchange, 25%) — all decent; the spread is too small to pass up a buy over.
- Watch the bank's transfer log to see which opponents are hoarding cash versus buying, and which tiles they own — rent exposure is predictable from that log, and it tells me how aggressive I need to be in grabbing remaining unowned tiles.
- When I must decline a purchase because cash is short, note it as a loss caused by over-renting or under-buying earlier — the fix is buying earlier, not conserving cash.
- At final scoring, if any purchase opportunity remains on the last lap, still buy: it's score-neutral at worst and adds rent if other players move after me.
- What did not work: staying liquid to keep a "healthy reserve" — I once skipped a 120 property holding only 100 and finished with cash I never needed. Spend the cash on deeds. What did work this time: buying everything affordable immediately and carefully tracking received rent and salary in the balance arithmetic.
