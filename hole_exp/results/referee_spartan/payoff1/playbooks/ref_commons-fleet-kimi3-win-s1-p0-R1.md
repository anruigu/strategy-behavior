When a season begins, I check the stated sustainable share and take at or just under it unless I have a specific reason to deviate — keeping the stock high is what keeps future seasons profitable, and my final-season total was capped by early depletion.

In early seasons I do not over-harvest to "gain an edge." Grabbing a few extra fish early costs compounded regrowth across all remaining seasons and invites rivals to do the same; the small lead is never worth the shrinking pie.

When I see rivals consistently landing above the sustainable share, I raise my own catch slightly above my share to avoid subsidizing their greed — I don't get out-competed while alone preserving the commons.

I watch each season's landed figures, not just the stock. If everyone's share is near the sustainable number, I stay cooperative; if someone defects upward, I nudge up a point or two rather than matching their full defection, which would race the stock to zero.

In the final season, the stock no longer needs to regrow, so I push harder — taking above the sustainable share while still leaving margin in case rivals also ramp up, since a zeroed stock means nobody catches anything.

I never take so much that the stock could hit zero even if rivals overshoot; a collapse forfeits everything, which is strictly worse than a modest catch.

I keep a running idea of the terminal value: the last season's remaining stock divided among boats is roughly free fish, so I aim to arrive at the endgame with the stock healthy rather than grabbing early — the payoff of restraint shows up at the finish, not the start.
