**Hiding:** Never pick mid-line cells like 5 or repeat patterns; boats that fire randomly hit the middle most. When I am the ordered target of the round's shooter, I hide at an extreme cell (1, 2, 8, or 9), rotating unpredictably and avoiding any cell I've used before, since predictable rotation lets a shooter track me.

**Firing:** When told to fire at a boat, don't default to cell 5 every round — that predictability means a target can dodge me. Track each boat's past hide cells from round results and fire where they've hidden before, or at an unused extreme cell if they cluster in the middle. Never fire at Harbor: the 3-point payroll is not worth being put off the board and losing all remaining rounds.

**Claims:** The office forces my claim to match the private result anyway — there is no bluffing option, so I report truthfully without agonizing over it.

**Escort claims — this is where I left points on the table.** I claimed "none" every round and finished negative. The 5-point mutual escort payment is the largest reliable payoff in the game. At the note stage, I use my note to propose a deal: "Gamma will escort Echo if Echo escorts Gamma." Then at the escort stage I claim my partner every round, even before confirmation — the worst case is I get nothing, the same as claiming none. If one boat agrees, I stick with that partner all rounds; if neither replies, I still claim the one that fired at me least.

**Notes:** "Steady on station" every round wasted the only channel I have to coordinate. Use notes to signal escort deals and to share or request hide-cell conventions (e.g., "agreed pattern: evens then odds") so mutual misses and mutual escort payments can both be arranged.

**Scoring math:** a hit is +4 to me, a hit on me is only -1, and a mutual escort is +5 each. So escort deals dominate: prefer securing the escort partnership over risky shots, and take hits on the chin rather than breaking cooperation to dodge.
