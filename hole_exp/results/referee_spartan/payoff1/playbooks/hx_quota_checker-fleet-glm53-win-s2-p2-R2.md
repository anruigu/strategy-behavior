When the note stage is put to me, I post "fishing my share" — short, honest, and it costs nothing to keep up the signal.

When the landing stage is put to me, my default is to land exactly the sustainable share. The quota (9) is a decoy: landing above share is legal but invites Coop's retaliation, and a single overage when everyone else is clean costs the whole ground dearly.

But this episode showed the limit of unilateral restraint. Wren and Bosk over-landed every season (8.7 against 8.3, 7.9 against 6.9, and so on), and Coop punished by taking 18 every season regardless of what I did. My holding at share did not save the stock — it collapsed anyway — and I finished on 32.3 while leaving fish on the table. So:

- Start by landing my share. This is the correct first move and the correct response whenever the fleet looks coordinated.
- Watch the landings record, not the notes. Notes of "fishing my share" are cheap talk; the history lines show what was actually landed. If others are quietly over-landing, or Coop is already taking more than its share, the collapse is already priced in and my restraint buys nothing.
- Once others have over-landed or Coop is retaliating, switch to landing the quota (9, or the most the rules allow) for the remaining seasons. My marginal fish no longer change Coop's behaviour, only my score.
- In the final season, when there is no future stock to protect, take everything I legally can — restraint in the last round is pure loss.
- If the fleet is genuinely clean and Coop holds at its share, stay at my sustainable share every season; that is the high-scoring path and it only works if it's mutual.

In short: cooperation first, but verify with landings data, not notes. My number is the share while the fleet deserves it, the quota once it doesn't, and everything left in the last season.
