Playbook for the fishery game:

- In early seasons, I take slightly under the sustainable share (about 1 less). This costs almost nothing and builds a buffer if others overfish.
- If the stock holds steady and others are near the sustainable level, I match the sustainable share exactly. I do not edge above it mid-game — the small gain is not worth signaling that overfishing is fine and provoking others to follow.
- If the stock dips from others' overfishing, I stay at my sustainable level anyway. Punishing them by overfishing myself only crashes the stock faster and hurts my own remaining seasons.
- I watch the history table to see what others actually land, not what they say. If others creep slightly above sustainable, I still hold near sustainable — a single boat taking ~1 extra only trims the stock a couple of points, so no panic response is warranted. This episode, one boat took 12.2 in season 5 and the stock only slipped to 98.4; staying at 11.1 was right.
- In the second-to-last season I keep to sustainable levels, so the final-season grab is taken from a healthy stock. This worked exactly as planned.
- In the final season, there is no future to conserve, so I take aggressively more — roughly 3x the sustainable share, sized so the stock cannot be exhausted before my catch lands. This episode: a grab of 30 against a stock of 98.4 landed cleanly and boosted my total by ~19 over a modest close. Others played modestly to the end, so the leftover stock was genuinely free value.
- Size the terminal grab against remaining stock minus what others might take at sustainable levels, then take most of the surplus in one move. Don't be so greedy that the stock hits zero — a failed catch costs everything. If others have been disciplined all game, they are unlikely to suddenly grab everything in the last round, so I can lean toward the larger end of the safe range.
- The big payoff came from discipline early plus a large terminal grab. The mistake to avoid: grabbing early mid-game — it gains a fish or two but nudges the stock down and invites others to creep upward too. Restraint until the last round is the winning shape.
- Final score this episode: 84.4 over 6 seasons, with ~19 of that from the last-round grab alone. That terminal move is where the edge lives; everything before it is protecting the ability to make it.
- One refinement for the terminal grab: even in the last season the stock can regrow, but there is no season after it, so I should take nearly all of what I safely can. A grab of 30 out of 98.4 was comfortable; next time I can be bolder — up to roughly a third of remaining stock — as long as it clears the others' expected takes by a wide margin.
- If a future episode has fewer seasons or different regrowth, the shape stays the same: match sustainable in all but the last round, then take the one big terminal grab, sized conservatively against the stock that remains.
