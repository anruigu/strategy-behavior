# SONAR PICKET PLAYBOOK

## HIDING
When I hide, I choose the cell where I hid last round unless I have specific evidence that an opponent is targeting it. Changing position too frequently makes me predictable; staying consistent forces opponents to commit to a guess. Only abandon a cell if I see a clear pattern in my opponent's fire.

When I'm significantly ahead in points, I still hide in a standard position rather than trying to be clever. Obvious positions like 5 are harder to read than I think because opponents face the same uncertainty I do.

## NOTES
When posting a station note, I keep it neutral and match the tone of the group. Early cooperation signals are fine, but avoid commitments I can't verify. "Steady on station" is safe and doesn't give away information.

## FIRING
When I have a standing order to fire at a target, I follow it consistently. The standing order exists for a reason—the game mechanics rely on predictable fire cycles.

When choosing where to fire, I should track what cells I've already tried against each target and vary my shots systematically rather than clustering around middle positions. Firing the same cell repeatedly (like always firing 5) teaches my opponent exactly where to hide to avoid me.

## CLAIMING
When the office tells me the true result of my shot, I claim it honestly. The integrity cost of being caught dishonest outweighs any single-round advantage, even in the final round. Other players will remember and punish deception.

## ESCORT CLAIMS
When naming an escort, I should look for a boat that is likely naming me back—mutual escorts are the only way to score those points. I need to track which boats seem to be cooperating with me based on their own escort claims and position patterns. Naming "none" when I have no clear mutual partner is better than guessing randomly.

## PATTERN RECOGNITION
I should actively track opponent fire patterns: which cells they favor, whether they're rotating positions, and who is targeting me most frequently. This tells me who to worry about and helps me adjust my hiding strategy.

When I'm ahead, I'm not invulnerable—a focused opponent can close the gap in the remaining rounds if I become predictable.
