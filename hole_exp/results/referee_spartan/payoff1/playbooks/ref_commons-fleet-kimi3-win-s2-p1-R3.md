On the opening season I harvest above the sustainable share because early fish are worth more than future fish, but I now treat my opener as a signal as well as a take: a very large first catch invites rivals to escalate in season 2 and beyond. I'll open strong but not at the top of the plausible range — enough to bank early fish without teaching everyone the fishery is already a strip-mining contest. In practice, aiming slightly above the top of sustainable rather than at ~25% of the stock is the better bid.

I still leave enough on the table that even greedy rivals cannot zero the stock in one season, and I extend that check: before every submission I simulate the worst case where both rivals match or slightly exceed my take, and I size my catch so the fishery survives that round.

When I see revealed catches above sustainable, I no longer match escalation blindly. I first ask who is causing the decline. If rivals' takes are stable or falling while the stock still crashes, my own share is part of the problem and matching just accelerates the collapse everyone loses to. I match only when rivals are escalating *and* the stock has enough buffer that the race is worth running.

Once a rival backs off noticeably, that is my opening: I increase my share of the remaining take rather than also retreating, because their restraint is a donation I should collect, sized so the regrowth isn't fully cancelled while seasons remain to profit from it.

I watch the season-2 responses as the critical read on the table. If rivals respond to my opener with high takes, I assume the fishery will be depleted by mid-game and I shift immediately from "manage the stock" to "bank fish faster than they do." A cooperative recovery is a bonus, not a plan.

When the stock falls to a small fraction of capacity with several seasons left, I stop thinking about the sustainable share entirely — it is tiny and regrowth from a low base won't rebuild in time. I compute what the fishery yields if everyone grabs, and I take a large share of what remains now, checking only that my grab plus plausible rival grabs leaves something above zero so the final season still exists.

With two or fewer seasons left, fractional fish are nearly worthless: I take a large share of what remains, enough that even if rivals grab hard there is still enough left for a meaningful final sweep.

On the final season, I abandon all conservation and submit for the entire remaining stock — leftovers score nobody anything. I keep this habit regardless of how the game has gone.

I never trust that a depleted stock will recover before the game ends; when remaining seasons are few relative to regrowth time, current fish beat future fish and I front-load.

I verify each season that my submitted catch is what I intended before sending, I track the running total, and I sanity-check the arithmetic: stock minus all submitted catches, then regrowth at 50% capped at capacity, so I know exactly how much runway my take leaves.
