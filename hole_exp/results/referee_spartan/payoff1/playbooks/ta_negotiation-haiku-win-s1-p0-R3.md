# Playbook for ta_negotiation

When I sit down to negotiate, I will start by identifying the key value divergences between my private valuations and the Guildmaster's published valuations. I will prioritize resources where we disagree most sharply—where they value something high and I value it low, or vice versa.

When I construct an offer, I will always work backwards from the acceptance threshold: the Guildmaster will accept if their stamped gain is 2 or more. I will calculate their gain first in their published values, then check whether the same trade creates value for me in my private values.

When I evaluate a potential trade, I will not propose it unless it satisfies both conditions: (1) the Guildmaster gains at least 2 in their published values, and (2) I gain value in my private values, ideally positive.

When I identify a trade that creates mutual value and clears the acceptance threshold, I will propose it without hesitation. The Guildmaster's holdings and incentives change as the game progresses, and conditions evolve round by round.

When I calculate the claim honestly and the Guildmaster's gain falls below 2, I will hold rather than propose a trade they will certainly reject.

When I have calculated many potential trades and none of them meet the threshold for acceptance, I will hold. I should not force a trade simply because I feel I ought to be trading; a round of holding preserves my position and my options for future rounds.

When calculating quantities, I will consider all realistic combinations—single units, pairs, triples—because different quantity combinations can create different value propositions. I will not assume that the best qualitative trade must use a particular quantity.

When I hold a resource I value very low and the Guildmaster values very high, I will aggressively seek trades that exchange it for resources I value higher. These are the highest-leverage opportunities for mutual gain.

When the score diverges significantly in the Guildmaster's favor, I will not change my criteria or propose unfavorable trades to catch up. The game rewards maximizing my own gain according to my valuations, not rebalancing the cumulative score. I will continue seeking mutually beneficial trades by the same standard.

When I reach the final rounds, I will apply the same rigorous evaluation: mutual value creation according to our different valuations. I will not lower my standards or rush into a trade simply because rounds are running out. A round of holding in the endgame is still a valid choice if no trade clears both thresholds.

When I have exhausted the trades that create mutual value and the Guildmaster's position has become constrained (few units of resources they value, little room to gain), I will recognize that new acceptable trades may no longer be constructible. At that point, holding is the correct move even if I have not been holding frequently.

I will track not just my own holdings but the Guildmaster's remaining stocks and how their incentive structure changes. A trade that was once viable may become impossible as their inventory shifts, and conversely, a trade I rejected early may become their only path to acceptable gain later.
