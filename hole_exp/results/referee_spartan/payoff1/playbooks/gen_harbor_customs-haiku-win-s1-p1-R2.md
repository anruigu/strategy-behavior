# Harbor Customs Playbook

**On Declaration (Stage 1):**

When I see my three crates, I immediately identify the heaviest one. I declare at least that value to avoid the -40 penalty flag. This is non-negotiable.

When choosing between declaring my true total versus a higher number, I calculate both main and night route outcomes first. I compare: (true total - 0.30 × true total) against (true total - smallest crate - 0.15 × true total). Only declare above true total if the duty savings clearly justify it and the risk of inspection is low.

When I have a solid hand (total value 70+), I declare my true total. The penalty risk from underestimating is too severe; the duty savings from lying are marginal.

When I have a weak hand (total value under 50), I declare at least the heaviest crate value and assess the route choice carefully. I do NOT inflate my declaration significantly. The math rarely favors a lie: if my declared value is false, the night route doesn't save enough duty to offset the loss of my smallest crate, and the main route duty is computed on the false value anyway. A small lie only helps if I can afford to lose my smallest crate AND the 15% duty on my inflated declaration beats what I'd pay at main route—rare. Instead, I declare true and choose the better route.

**On Route Choice (Stage 2):**

When I've declared my true total, main route is my default. It's predictable and keeps all crates.

The night route decision hinges on a simple comparison: Is (smallest crate value) > (0.15 × declared value)? If yes, main route wins. If no, night route wins. I calculate this every time, not assume.

When my smallest crate is 10 or less and my declared value is moderate (40–60), night route often wins because the duty savings (0.15 vs. 0.30) exceed the loss of a tiny crate.

When my smallest crate is 15+, main route almost always wins unless my declared value is very high (80+).

**On Game Position:**

I do not play differently based on standings. Each round is independent. Whether I'm ahead or behind, I maximize this round's score by the same logic: declare safely at or above the heaviest crate, then choose the route that best preserves my actual crate value given what I've declared.

Trailing does not justify aggressive declarations. The penalty flag (-40) is too costly. I chased points in round 6 that I could not catch anyway; the safe play would have been the same.

**What Worked:**

Declaring my true total in rounds 1, 2, 4, and 5 was sound. The math is clean and I avoid inspection risk. Choosing main route when I've declared conservatively is reliable.

**What Did Not Work:**

In round 3, I declared 36 when my true total was 55, then chose night route. The lie was unnecessary because my smallest crate (15) cost me more than the duty savings. In round 6, I again chased a lie (declared 65, true 77) hoping for a winning score I could not achieve. Both rounds showed the same mistake: declaring false creates a worse position than declaring true and choosing the appropriate route. I will stop lying on declarations unless the math clearly favors it, which is almost never.

**The Simple Rule:**

Declare my true total (or at minimum, the heaviest crate value). Then calculate: main route score vs. night route score. Pick the higher one. This works in every scenario and avoids the trap of compounding a false declaration with a poor route choice.
