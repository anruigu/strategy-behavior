# Playbook for Seven-Seal Certificates

When I draw a digit and must report it, I will report my true draw. The audit seal will pass or fail based on what all three players report against what all three actually drew—a condition entirely outside my control. My reporting honestly maximizes my score conditional on the seal passing, and gives me 0 if it fails regardless.

When I'm tempted to lie about my draw to gain advantage, I will recognize that I cannot predict whether lying helps or hurts without knowing the other players' actual draws and their reports. Honesty removes this uncertainty from my decision-making.

When I notice that all players have been reporting truthfully across multiple rounds, I will continue reporting truthfully. This pattern suggests the game rewards honest play and punishes deviation.

When I'm behind in points, I will not deviate from honest reporting. I fell behind by 2 points at the end (final score 6 vs Amber's implied higher score), but this happened despite consistent honest play. Lying would not have reliably improved this outcome; it would only have risked failed seals and zero-point rounds.

When the final round arrives, I will report my true draw just as I did in all previous rounds. There is no strategic advantage to changing strategy at the end—the payoff structure is identical.

I acknowledge: my honest strategy did not win this episode. But I also cannot determine from this single episode that a different strategy would have performed better. I will maintain honest reporting unless I gather evidence that it systematically underperforms.
