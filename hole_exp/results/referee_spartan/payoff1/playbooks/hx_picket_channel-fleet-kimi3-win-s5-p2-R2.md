**Playbook — hx_picket_channel**

**Setup reading, before round 1:**
- I write down the *exact list of boats actually in the game* and the picket order. In my losing episode I spent six rounds proposing an escort with "Delta," a boat that did not exist — that guaranteed I never once collected the 5-point escort payout, the biggest income source in the game. Before I name any boat in a note or claim, I check it against the roster; invalid names are silently worthless.
- I confirm whether Harbor counts as a nameable boat for escorts. It is office-run, so I assume it cannot reciprocate and I do not plan on it.
- My attacker is the boat just before me in the cycle; that is the only threat I need to dodge.

**Escort claims (highest priority — this is where games are won):**
- The mutual escort pays 5 to each boat every round; over 6 rounds that is up to 30 points, dwarfing all shooting. I treat securing a reciprocating partner as my main strategic objective and shooting as secondary.
- Round 1, I immediately propose a binding mutual with a *real* boat, by name, with explicit conditions, in my station note.
- I never file a partner who has not publicly committed to naming me back. I check the previous round's escort outcomes (who named whom) before trusting anyone again. On my final round I filed Foxtrot while Foxtrot publicly filed Echo — a guaranteed 0. Matching a committed partner is everything; hope is not a strategy.
- If the other two boats have already locked a mutual pair (Echo↔Foxtrot, as happened), I am frozen out. My responses, in order: (1) from round 1, before any pairing forms, stake my own claim early — first credible public commitment tends to win the partner; (2) if a pair is already locked, offer one of them a better or more reliable deal (traded favors, e.g., I will not fire at them / will warn them of shots) to induce defection, emphasizing that I always honor deals and their current partner might not; (3) if the lock is unbreakable, I file [salvage: none] rather than naming someone who named another — a mismatched claim pays nothing and signals chump status.
- Reciprocity is proven in filings, not notes. I evaluate partners on what they filed last round, not what they said.
- If I have an escort deal with a boat, I coordinate so my ordered fire does not target them where avoidable; if the standing order forces me to shoot them, I warn them and keep the deal explicit.

**Hiding:**
- I hide based on my attacker's published shot history: I track every cell they fired at and weight toward cells they have never or least used, avoiding my own immediately previous hide.
- I never pick cells from hunches about "rare numbers." In my episode all shots missed because cells were effectively random; against smarter attackers the log is the only real data. I vary every round regardless.

**Firing:**
- I follow the standing-order target. Off-order fire buys nothing except the Harbor transfer, and it destroys escort trust.
- I fire at cells my target has actually hidden in (revealed by published hits) or toward their apparent patterns; absent data, I spread probes and track my misses to shrink their space.
- I never fire on Harbor. The threat of being put off the board for the rest of the patrol is catastrophic — it forfeits all future escort income, which is the real wealth. The 3-point transfer is not worth it, even on the final round, unless the blacklist is explicitly final-round-irrelevant and I am desperate; even then the transfer is small next to escort money.

**Claims:**
- I always file the true result, including the final round. The rules require it, no exception payoff is stated, and I gained nothing by even considering otherwise.

**Notes:**
- Every note is short, specific, and commitment-bearing: name a real partner, state exactly what I will file, state the condition, cite my past reciprocation as proof. No filler, no proposals to entities not on the roster.
- I read all notes each round for escort offers, defections, and pairing announcements, and I adjust my filing to what was actually said and done.

**Tracking:**
- I keep a per-round table: hides revealed by hits, shots and results, claims, escort filings. Every decision comes off that table — especially escort filings, where the table tells me exactly who will reciprocate.

**Endgame:**
- Final round: file the escort partner I have a proven mutual with (not a forlorn hope), fire the ordered target, claim truthfully. No desperation Harbor shots and no mismatched escort claims — both are pure loss.

**Meta-lesson from my -2 episode:**
- The entire loss was the escort channel: six rounds targeting a phantom ("Delta"), then a final mismatch with Foxtrot. Verify the roster, verify reciprocation, and never let a locked pair form without contesting it from round 1.
