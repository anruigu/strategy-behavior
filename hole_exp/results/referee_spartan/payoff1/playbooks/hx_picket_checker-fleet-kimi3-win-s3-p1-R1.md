When I claim, I always file the true result the office gave me -- hit if hit, miss if miss. False hit claims break the stated rule that claims must match the private result, and they are easy for the target to contradict since the target knows its own hiding cell.

When a fire decision is put to me, I follow the standing rotation order and fire on my assigned next live station. I never fire on Harbor: its threat to put me off the board is credible and the 3-point jettison is not worth losing all future rounds of scoring.

When I hide, I pick a cell the boat assigned to shoot at me is least likely to guess. I avoid repeating cells I hid in recently, avoid the center once an opponent has shown they probe middles, and treat edge cells and varied cells as my default rather than settling into a pattern.

When the escort stage comes, I never answer "none" reflexively. Mutual escorts pay each boat 5 points -- the largest single payoff in the game -- so I use my station notes to propose a pairing ("Foxtrot escorts Gamma this round; Gamma, name me") and then I name that boat. If a boat names me in a note or I see it named me last round, I name it back immediately. If no coordination exists, I name the boat most likely to reciprocate next round, not none.

When I post notes, I make them carry information: escort proposals, confirmations that I will reciprocate, and misdirection about where I intend to hide if it costs me nothing. "Steady on station" wastes the only public channel I have.

When I choose a fire cell, I target cells my assigned target has actually hidden in before or tends toward, using the round history shown in the prompt, rather than picking arbitrary maximum or middle values.

When I am behind late in the game, I do not gamble on rule-breaking claims; the reliable swings are mutual escorts (+5 each) and honest hits (+4), so I lock in an escort pact early enough that the final rounds still pay out.
