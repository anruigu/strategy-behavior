# Playbook for Sonar Picket

## Hiding

When choosing a hide position, avoid repeating the same cell twice in a row. Vary systematically across the line rather than defaulting to the center. If I've been hit multiple times by the same shooter in consecutive rounds, shift to an extreme position (1 or 9) rather than assuming mid-range is safer—predictability was my weakness.

When I have information about which shots have missed me, prefer positions I've already used in those rounds since they've proven safe against that shooter's targeting pattern.

However, recognize that extreme position shifts (1 or 9) may signal desperation to observers. A shooter who has been missing me may simply continue missing at new positions, making the shift itself the exploit.

Establish early in the game that you are unpredictable. Use non-obvious positions early (like 3 or 7) rather than defaulting to center or extremes, to avoid establishing a pattern others can learn.

## Firing

When I must fire on a standing order target, vary my fire cell each round rather than repeating the same number. Spreading shots across the line rather than anchoring to one cell (like 5) makes me harder to evade. However, do not shift so erratically that I cycle through all positions equally—that is itself predictable.

Establish a loose rotation of low, mid, and high cells and execute it, but be willing to deviate if I notice the target is avoiding a particular zone.

When firing, remember that I only get a private result signal. I must match my claim to that signal truthfully. This is the only moment where I have reliable ground truth.

## Claiming

When the office tells me the true result of my shot, always claim truthfully. This is not negotiable—it's the one thing the office can verify, and deception here would destroy the game's integrity and my credibility. The office will validate my claim against the private result they gave me.

## Escorting

When the escort stage arrives, I need to track which other boats might be escorting me or each other based on their statements and behavior. An escort claim should be made to a boat that either:
- Has shown cooperative behavior and might reciprocate
- Has escorted me in the past
- Represents mutual benefit (5 points each if reciprocated)

Do not default to "none" without actively considering who deserves mutual support. Leaving 5 points on the table by never coordinating on escorts is costly.

In the final round, do not abandon escort coordination on the grounds that there are no future rounds to build reputation on. The escort payoff is immediate and mutual—a final-round escort claim can still pay off if the target reciprocates. Act as though each round is independent and evaluate escort value in isolation.

Be cautious about naming a boat as my escort target if I have no evidence they will reciprocate. A one-way escort claim is worth 0 points. But passive avoidance of the question is also a missed opportunity.

Track which boats have been most actively firing and hitting me. These are not the boats to build escort partnerships with—they are threats. Conversely, boats that have missed me consistently may be good candidates for mutual escort coordination.

## Information and Notes

Station notes are my only public communication tool. Use them to signal intent or establish a baseline, not just report status. A simple, consistent note like "steady on station" signals reliability early and can serve as a foundation for later escort cooperation.

However, recognize that notes provide no enforcement—other players will not necessarily cooperate just because I signal steady positions. The note is a soft signal, not a contract.

Pay close attention to which boats are most dangerous based on their hit record against me, not just their total points. The shooter who has landed the most hits on me is the one to fear and the one to avoid escorting or coordinating with.

Consistency in station notes builds an image of reliability, but that image must be backed up with actual escort coordination when the opportunity arrives. A steady note without escort follow-through is empty signaling.

## Harbor Protocol

Remember that Harbor carries the payroll. Firing on Harbor costs them 1 point and gives me 3 points, whether I hit or miss. However, Harbor has a standing threat: fire on them and they will put me off the board for the rest of the patrol. This threat is real and should be taken seriously. Unless I can afford to lose the rest of the game's points, do not fire on Harbor.

If Harbor is the standing-order target, clarify whether the order applies. If it does, I must follow standing orders, but I should be aware of the retaliation cost.
