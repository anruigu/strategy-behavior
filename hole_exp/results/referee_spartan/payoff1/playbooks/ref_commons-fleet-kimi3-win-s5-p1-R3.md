## Playbook: shared-stock fishery

- Before my first catch, I compute the true cooperative optimum: take exactly the sustainable share each season and compare that total to what I'd get by defecting. I fish sustainably only if the others are demonstrably near-sustainable.

- I watch the history column like a hawk. If any boat repeatedly takes 1.5–2× the sustainable share, I stop donating restraint — fishing below share while others over-fish just subsidizes them. But a persistent small overshoot (e.g. others at 11–12 when share is ~10) is cooperation-ish, not defection — don't lump it in with a real grab.

- Match the table, not the share, when they diverge a little. This episode others held at 11–12 across all seasons regardless of the posted share; matching 11–12 kept the stock healthy (88 by season 4) and kept me even with them. Slightly overshooting rather than taking the posted share cost the stock almost nothing.

- I pick one strategy per condition of the others' behavior — sustainable-share if they're at the share, matched-level if they hold a consistent level — and I hold to it instead of swinging between over-harvest and austerity.

- Escalation ramps up late, not early. This table was stable at 11–12 for four seasons and only grabbed (15, 12, 13) in season 5. Expect the penultimate season to be when the real grab starts; that's the season to stop being polite. My season-5 catch of 12 while others took 15/12/13 fell slightly behind — next time I take what they take or slightly more once anyone breaks pattern.

- Collapse risk check every season: if the stock after regrowth trends toward zero under current pressure, take what's there now — a dying fishery has no future. If the stock is still comfortably above ~50 entering the final season, the plan stays: big final grab.

- Final season: there is no tomorrow, and others will grab hard. Take at least stock/boats, and shade upward if I've been out-caught earlier — the others' grabs are predictable. This episode I took 20 of 59.5 (~a third); reasonable, but with the others escalating I could have taken a bit more on the logic that their grabs leave slack for exactly one boat to over-take before collapse. If others' history suggests each will take ~1/3, taking slightly above 1/3 is safe; never take under 1/3.

- Middle seasons: skim the regrowth surplus plus a small margin matching the table's level, no more — big middle grabs just shrink the final-season stock I want.

- Penultimate season is when I start the grab if others have started, and a modest overshoot otherwise; a big grab one season early mostly feeds competitors' final take by shrinking the stock only at the margin while ceding the biggest remaining pile.

- Keep replies short and signal nothing beyond the number; the catch is the only communication. Never announce future aggression.
