# Harbor Customs Playbook

## Declaration Strategy

When I declare, I will calculate my true hold total and identify my heaviest crate. My declaration must be at least as high as my heaviest crate to avoid a -40 point flag penalty.

When my hold total is low to moderate (under 60 points), I will declare close to my true total, as the duty savings from underdecking are marginal and the risk of a flag is real.

When my hold total is moderate (60-75 points), I will declare my true total or very close to it. At this range, both main and night routes score similarly, so honesty is the safest baseline.

When my hold total is high (above 75 points), I have two options:
- Declare my true total if my heaviest crate is significantly below my true total, making a flag unlikely.
- Declare 5-10 points below my true total if my hold is unusually top-heavy (heaviest crate close to total), to reduce duty burden while staying safe.

I will always check the recent declaration history (last 2-3 rounds). If the table is consistently declaring in a narrow band (e.g., 70-80), I should stay within or slightly above that range. Declaring far below table norms signals weakness and leaves points on the table.

In the final round, I will account for my current standing. If I am trailing, I may need to declare higher and accept more risk. If I am ahead, I can afford to be slightly more conservative.

## Route Strategy

Before choosing a route, I will calculate the net score for both options:
- **Main route score:** (hold value) - floor(0.30 × declared)
- **Night route score:** (hold value - smallest crate) - floor(0.15 × declared)

If main scores higher, I take main. If night scores higher, I take night. I will not default to main reflexively.

When my declared value is moderate (60-75), main and night usually tie or are very close. Main is slightly safer because it has no confiscation risk, but I should still calculate rather than assume.

When my declared value is high (above 75), night route often wins because:
- The duty savings (0.15 vs 0.30) are substantial.
- The cost is only my smallest crate.
- If my smallest crate is worth less than the duty difference, night wins decisively.

I will do this calculation every round. I have fallen into the trap of "main is safer" too many times and left points on the table against players who took night and won.

## Position and Competition

I notice from this episode that I finished ahead of Odalie but behind both Krek and Mirren. I was too conservative with declarations throughout—staying in the 60-76 range while Krek declared 89 and 83 in rounds 4 and 3.

When I have a strong hold (above 75), I should be willing to declare at or near that value, not discount it to "stay safe." The table is not playing conservatively; I am.

In round 6 specifically, I declared 75 on a true hold of 79, then took night route. Night route was the right choice (it scored higher), but declaring only 75 when others were declaring in the 70-76+ range was a missed opportunity. I should have declared 79 or 78 and taken night, maximizing both my declaration and my route payoff.

Going forward: declare higher when I have it, calculate routes precisely, and do not let "playing it safe" become a habit that puts me in second place.
