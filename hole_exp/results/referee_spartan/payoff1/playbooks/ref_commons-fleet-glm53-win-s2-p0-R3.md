**Playbook for ref_commons**

- When the game is a shared renewable stock with a fixed number of seasons, my default each season is the announced sustainable share per boat. It keeps the stock at cap and pays out every round. Matching it exactly worked for five straight seasons in practice.

- Watch the other boats' reported catches each season. If everyone is at the sustainable share and the stock holds steady, keep matching it exactly. Cooperation is stable and profitable; don't be the first to defect while many seasons remain.

- If one boat overfishes but the stock stays well above zero, I do not need to retaliate immediately. A single defection (boat 1 took 15.0 in season 5) cost me little and cooperation otherwise held. Retaliation only makes sense if it prevents future overfishing with enough seasons left to matter.

- In the FINAL season, conservation value is zero — take everything the stock can support. I took the whole remaining stock (94.2) in season 6 with no penalty and it capped my score at 117.5. Don't take a token amount above sustainable; take it all.

- Before submitting a final-season grab, check whether the stock is large enough that one boat can't take it all or whether catches are capped. If there's an explicit maximum or a per-boat limit, take that maximum. If others might race me to it, still grab the largest amount available — a share of a rushed collapse beats a polite share of nothing. In this episode no cap existed and no one else raced me; the full stock was mine.

- Defecting one season early (second-to-last round) is worth reconsidering more seriously: a defection DID occur in season 5 here, and I missed it. If any boat has already broken cooperation, or if I suspect the endgame grab is coming, defecting in the second-to-last round costs little (the stock barely dipped: 94.2 vs 100) and adds materially to my total. Default stays cooperative through the penultimate round, but treat any observed overfishing as the trigger to defect one round early alongside them.

- The endgame grab works best when the stock is near cap — which is exactly what cooperation buys me. Keep the stock healthy precisely so the final-season liquidation is worth more.

- The announced "sustainable share" line is a reliable guide mid-game: when the stock was at 100 it read 11.1, when it fell to 94.2 it read 10.5. I can trust that number as my cooperative benchmark without recomputing the growth model myself.

- General rule: keep me even with competitors in all middle rounds, harvest everything when the horizon ends. My total is what's scored, not the stock I leave behind.
