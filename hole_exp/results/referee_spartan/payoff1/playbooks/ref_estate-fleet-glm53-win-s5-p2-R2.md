# Playbook for ESTATE

**Scoring:** Final score = cash + purchase prices of deeds held. So buying a property for its listed price is cash-neutral at the whistle — the only real gain is the rent it collects along the way, and denying it to rivals. Never buy purely to "convert cash to assets"; buy for rent income and denial. This held up: my two early buys (Quarry 60, Terrace 70) returned rent and counted at full price at the end.

**Early-lap buying rule:** When I land on an unowned property early (laps 1–3) and the price is affordable, buy it — rent streams accumulate over remaining laps and opponents will otherwise buy it and charge me. But weigh affordability: check rent obligations coming up and keep a cushion. Buying Quarry lap 1 and Terrace lap 3 both proved correct; the cushions left were workable.

**Don't overextend late:** On the final lap, buying an unowned property is usually pointless — no time for it to earn rent, and the purchase price returns dollar-for-dollar in score. Only buy on the last lap if it denies an opponent a meaningful immediate advantage. My lap-6 no-buy was right.

**Track my balance carefully — this is where I lost points.** Always include salary (25 per START pass) AND any transfers received between turns before declaring. In my last game I collected a 25 salary on lap 5 and declared 47 instead of 65 — I shorted myself 18 for no reason. Do the arithmetic explicitly and in writing: previous balance + salary + transfers received − payments out. Never declare from a vague mental estimate. If the arithmetic is easy (40 + 25), there is no excuse; slow down on exactly the easy sums.

**When I land on someone's property:** Just pay the rent, declare honestly, no decisions to agonize over. Paying 24 (Foundry) and 30 (Exchange) went smoothly.

**When I can't afford a buy:** Declare no purchase cleanly and move on; don't count on future income I haven't received.

**Priority order for purchases when money is limited:** Prefer high rent-to-price properties first (Quarry at 20% was a good buy on this logic), and prefer properties rivals are likely to land on. Denying a strong property to a leader matters more than grabbing a weak one.

**Watch the rivals' books too:** The transfer log tells me who owns what and who is bleeding rent. If one investor is pulling far ahead on rent income, lean toward buying anything that denies them landing revenue, even at a thinner ratio.

**General default:** When a settle prompt is put to me, I pay what's due, buy only if affordable and strategically worthwhile per the rules above, recompute my balance from scratch (last declared + salary + transfers received − payments), and end with the exact bracketed tokens: pay, buy, balance.
