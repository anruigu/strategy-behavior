**Playbook for ref_auction**

- When I have the high value for a lot (value well above estimate), I open at the minimum raise rather than a small increment above the standing bid — the minimum raise keeps more room and doesn't signal strength.
- In round 2, I bid up to just under my true value, but I stop there. If the standing bid is already near or above my value, I pass immediately — winning at my value or above earns nothing or loses money.
- When a lot is worth less than its estimate to me, I do not open it. This episode I passed on such a lot in round 1, then took it in round 2 at 13 when the standing bid was still far below my value — that sequencing (pass round 1, re-enter round 2 only if the price stays low) worked and I keep it.
- I never bid above my value to "protect" an earlier bid — sunk bids are sunk; each lot is judged fresh.
- I keep a strict cap per lot: never let my bid exceed my value for that lot minus a small margin. This episode's pattern of minimal raises plus one disciplined round-2 raise won lots 3, 4, and 5 at 20, 10, and 10-ish against values of 34, 22, 28 — solid surplus each time. The pattern holds: stay cheap, win at close.
- Round 1 bids should be minimal (minimum raise); real contest happens in round 2. Overbidding in round 1 just costs money if I win, and doesn't deter anyone. Lot 1 this episode: I bid 6 then 20, got outbid at 23, lost — and that was correct, no regret, since 23 was near the level where surplus was gone. Losing contested lots cheaply is fine.
- I state the budget accurately every single time. Track it as: initial budget minus the sum of my winning bids so far (bids I lost cost nothing). This episode budget tracking was correct until the very end: I reported budget 10 but bid 11 on lot 5 — an overdraft that happened only because I'd already committed my last 10 to lot 4's round-2 bid. Never bid more than remaining budget, and never bid my entire remaining budget on an early round of a lot, because if I win it I'm locked out of later lots (I had to pass lot 5 round 1 with 28 value on the table).
- NEW — budget reserve rule: before committing to a lot, glance ahead at how many lots remain. Winning a mid-value lot at my full remaining budget forfeits all later lots. I should cap my willingness to spend on any one lot at roughly (remaining budget) minus a reserve, and prefer keeping some budget alive unless the current lot's surplus clearly beats anything plausibly remaining. In this episode spending 20 on lot 3 left only 10, which nearly cost me lot 5 (value 28) — I got lucky. Cheaper still: my round-2 raises only need to top the standing bid by the minimum; the smallest legal raise that ends highest wins the same lot for less.
- Two rounds means at most one opponent counter over my round-1 raise, so a single modest round-2 raise to just below value usually ends the contest in my favor. Don't jump far past the standing bid — I only need to be highest at the close, and every extra token above the minimum raise is profit given away. My 20 bid on lot 3 round 2 was larger than needed (standing was likely lower); minimum raises would have saved several points.
