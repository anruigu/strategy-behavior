# Playbook for Win As Much As You Can

When I'm shown the payoff matrix at the start, I study it carefully to understand the exact consequences of each choice, especially at multiplier rounds.

When a talk phase asks for my pledge, I commit to Y only if I genuinely intend to pick Y through that phase—not as a strategic signal. If I'm uncertain, I pledge none.

When others pledge Y and I've verified the payoff matrix shows I gain +3 per round from being the sole X while they're all Y, I pledge X and execute that plan systematically through non-multiplier rounds.

When I pick X against three Y's and gain points while they lose, I recognize this is working and continue with X until the next talk phase or until the payoff structure changes.

When a talk phase arrives and all others have pledged Y, I do not assume they will honor it. I look at their actual pick history in the rounds just completed.

When others have just demonstrated they picked X (defected) while I picked Y (cooperated), I do not pledge Y in response. I either pledge X to match their actual behavior or pledge none to stay flexible.

When I'm behind on score and a multiplier round approaches, I do not panic-pick. I calculate: what does each choice pay me versus each choice they're likely to make, given their recent actual picks, not their pledges.

When the final round carries a massive multiplier and I'm behind, I recognize that unilateral defection at that point gives me the biggest absolute gain. I pick X.

What did not work: Pledging Y and then hoping others would honor their Y pledges when they had broken them before. Switching to Y mid-sequence when the math showed X was beating them. Overweighting others' pledges instead of their actual picks.
