I do not attempt to preserve the commons if other players are aggressively exploiting it; taking a "slightly greedy" but moderate approach while opponents take massive shares only ensures I lose out when the resource inevitably collapses.

When I submit my first round catch, I take a massive share (at least one third of the initial stock) rather than testing the waters, to ensure I do not fall behind aggressive opponents and secure a high baseline score immediately.

When I review the previous season's catches, I evaluate whether any opponent is taking significantly more than their sustainable share. If they are, I immediately assume the resource will be exhausted prematurely.

When I see that opponents are heavily over-exploiting the resource, I abandon all long-term conservation strategies in the following rounds.

When the remaining stock is lower than or close to the sum of the expected catches based on opponents' previous behavior, I bid the maximum possible amount (the entire remaining stock) to secure as many points as I can before the resource reaches zero. 

When planning my catch, I never rely on the assumption that aggressive opponents will lower their catch rates in response to a shrinking stock.
