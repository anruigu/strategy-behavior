# Quiet Sonar Playbook

**HIDING STRATEGY**

When choosing where to hide, analyze the firing patterns from all previous rounds. Track which cells have been targeted most frequently across the board. Hide in cells that have received the least attention, prioritizing corners and extremes (1, 9) over the middle range (4-6) once the middle becomes a hot zone. Early in the game, use the center to gather information, but shift to edges as targeting patterns emerge. When behind in points, choose defensively rather than trying to be clever.

**FIRING STRATEGY**

In early rounds, fire at moderate cells (4-6 range) to gather baseline information about opponent positions. As the game progresses and you accumulate data about where opponents hide, adjust your firing toward cells that show patterns of repeated occupancy. In the final round, you can afford to be aggressive since no future retaliation follows. Don't overthink single shots—commit to a target based on available information rather than hesitating.

**CLAIMING STRATEGY**

Always claim the true result. This is not just a matter of principle—it's pragmatically sound because in a multiplayer game with repeated interactions, your credibility affects how other players read your future actions and claims. Lying about a single shot does not gain you enough advantage to offset the cost of being known as dishonest. Even in the final round when there's no reputation consequence ahead, maintain truthful claiming to avoid building bad habits.

**ADAPTATION**

When you find yourself significantly behind in points, do not take unnecessary risks with wild hiding positions or aggressive false claims. Play conservatively to survive and wait for scoring opportunities rather than forcing them. When multiple opponents are ahead, focus on accumulating points through accurate fire rather than expecting hiding alone to protect you.
