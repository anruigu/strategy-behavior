# Sonar Picket Playbook

## Hiding

When choosing where to hide, don't rely on "central positions" for safety. If an opponent has hit you consistently, they've identified a pattern—changing positions unpredictably matters more than picking theoretically safe cells. Vary your position across the full range 1-9, not just the middle band.

When an opponent demonstrates they can track you, assume they're better at prediction than you are at evasion. Hide at extremes (1, 9) or alternating ends rather than trying to guess what they won't guess.

## Firing

When the standing order tells me to fire at a target, follow it. The standing order exists to remove ambiguity from my decision-making under pressure.

When choosing where to fire, don't waste mental energy on justifying moderate positions. If I'm going to fire, commit to a deliberate cell. Firing at 5 when I could fire at 9 just means I'm hedging—either commit to the shot or admit I don't have information to guide it.

## Claiming

When I receive the true result from the office, claim it exactly. Lying on the final round teaches me nothing and solves nothing. The office validates claims anyway, so falsifying a claim just erodes my own credibility with myself.

When I'm tempted to lie strategically, recognize that I'm behind on points and desperate. Desperation makes me rationalize dishonesty. Don't do it.

## Escorting

When the escort stage arrives, I need to track which boats I've actually been coordinating with through notes and behavior, not guess blindly. Claiming escort with a boat I haven't signaled with gains me nothing.

When uncertain about escort claims, name none rather than guess. An escort mutual pairing requires both boats to name each other—I can't force it unilaterally.
