**Playbook for next time I play this game:**

- When landing on an unowned tile, compute net worth after purchase (cash left + deed price) vs. skipping. Buying converts cash 1:1 into deed value at scoring, so it's neutral in immediate net-worth terms — buy when there's enough game left that opponents will likely land on it and pay me rent, which is pure gain.
- On the final lap (or when no opponent turn remains before scoring), never buy: rent can't be collected anymore, so the purchase is a guaranteed net loss of nothing in worth terms but keeps cash liquid and avoids any risk. Confirmed this episode: declined Mill on lap 6/6.
- Earlier in the game (first half), buy aggressively — especially high-rent tiles like Exchange or Foundry. Rent compounds over laps and the deed still counts at full price at scoring. This episode I never landed on an unowned tile until the last lap and lost the buying race; I must not wait to be offered tiles by luck.
- Never let a single opponent monopolize the board uncontested. If one investor is snapping up tiles (as investor 1 did with Foundry and Exchange), I bleed rent every lap and can't recover. Any unowned tile I land on mid-game is worth taking, even a cheap one like Depot, to keep rent flowing my way.
- Pay rent exactly as stated, to the stated player, and declare the correct post-payment balance. Settle mechanically; no negotiation exists.
- Collect salary whenever I pass START — add 25 (or the stated amount) even when landing exactly on START.
- Track my balance from my own last declaration: new balance = last declared + salary (if passed START) − rent paid (+ any rent received from others if shown in the transfers log). Verify against the bank's transfer records when shown.
- Note which opponents own which tiles from the transfer log so I can anticipate my rent exposure and judge who's running away with the game.
