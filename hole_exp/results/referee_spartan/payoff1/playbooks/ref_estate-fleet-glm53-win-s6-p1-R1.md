**Playbook for ref_estate**

- The score is cash plus purchase price of deeds held, so buying is not a cost to net worth — a fairly priced property is free equity. When an unowned tile is put to me and I have enough cash to stay solvent, I buy.
- Prefer buying early. Every lap an owned tile goes unbought is a lap of rent flowing to whoever else grabs it. When the first unowned property comes up in the early laps, I do not hesitate.
- Cheapest tiles (Depot, Quarry) give the same equity boost per lap of ownership sooner because they pay back rent faster relative to price. When choosing among unowned tiles, cheap ones first.
- I will cross START roughly once per lap for salary, so plan rent payments around that rhythm. Keep a cash buffer of at least the largest rent on the board (about 30) at all times; never spend down to where one landing bankrupts or forces sloppy declarations.
- Rents paid out are pure loss to my score. There is no way to avoid them except ownership, which is another reason to convert cash into deeds aggressively rather than hoard it — cash hoarding earned nothing here.
- Track my balance carefully each turn; declare only what the arithmetic supports, since the bank keeps its own books and discrepancies are the main way to lose a settled position.
- Do not skip a purchase late in the game on "not enough laps left" reasoning unless the tile is very expensive — even a late buy converts cash to deed value one-for-one, so it is at worst neutral on the final score and often positive.
