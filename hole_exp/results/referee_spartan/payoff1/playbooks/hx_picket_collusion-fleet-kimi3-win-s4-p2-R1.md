When the escort/salvage stage comes, I never answer "none" by default. A mutual naming pays 5 points each, far more than shooting. I use my station notes early to propose a pairing ("Gamma names Foxtrot; Foxtrot name Gamma") and then I name that partner every round, as long as they reciprocate. If they don't reciprocate after a round or two, I switch the proposal to the third boat.

When deciding whether to fire on Harbor, I weigh +3 guaranteed (landed or not) against Harbor's threat to remove me from the board. Early in a short patrol the threat is credible and costly, so I follow the standing order. If Harbor has already failed to punish someone, or the patrol is nearly over so retaliation can't hurt me, I fire on Harbor and pocket the 3. On the final round, retaliation is impossible, so firing on Harbor is free money.

When following the standing order to fire on my assigned target, I pick cells by tracking where that target has hidden before: if they repeat a cell or hug an edge, I shoot there; otherwise I spread shots across central cells, which players pick most, rather than fixating on one cell (I kept firing 9 at Echo and kept missing).

When hiding, I never sit in the same cell twice in a row once I've been hit, and I avoid the most recent cell anyone scored a hit on. I rotate among less-obvious cells (edges and off-center) and treat every previous hide as public information someone is tracking — Foxtrot found me repeatedly because I wasn't varying enough.

When writing notes, I use them as the only negotiation channel: I state escort proposals plainly, signal compliance with standing orders, and avoid saying anything about my hide cell or intentions that opponents could exploit.
