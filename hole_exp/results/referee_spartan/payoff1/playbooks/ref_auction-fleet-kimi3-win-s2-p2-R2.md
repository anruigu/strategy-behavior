- When I sit down, the first thing I lock in is my actual starting budget and I recompute my remaining budget from the results line after every lot: spent so far = sum of my winning bids; remaining = start minus spent. I never guess or assume a round number. In this episode I reported [budget: 100] twice when my budget was 30 — confused two numbers because I let sloppiness in. Before I submit, I cross-check the budget token against "starting budget minus my wins listed in the results so far." If I have no wins, the token is the starting budget, full stop.

- When a lot's value to me exceeds the standing high bid plus the minimum raise, I bid exactly the minimum legal amount. I never jump to estimate or above early; cheap tags keep me in the lead while conserving cash, and if a rival blows past me I lose nothing.

- When the standing high bid already meets or exceeds my value, or the standing bid plus minimum raise would, I pass. Paying above value guarantees negative score. I did this correctly on every round 2 in this episode, and the score of 0 is the cost of being disciplined while a rival overpaid everything.

- When I am already the standing high bidder in the final round, I pass unconditionally. Never raise myself.

- With two rounds per lot, I bid the minimum in round 1 to take the lead cheaply, then in round 2 I re-evaluate only against the new high bid: minimum raise if it's still comfortably below my value, pass otherwise. I do not anchor on what I last bid.

- Observation from this episode: bidder 1 jumped from tiny openers straight to bids at or above estimate (28 on value-19 lot 1, 41 on lot 2, 54 on value-31 lot 5), pricing me out of every round 2. Against an aggressive rival like this, my minimum tags won't win anything, but raising into their range means paying over value and scoring negative. I accept a possible zero score rather than chase — a rival who overpays every lot is subsidizing my relative position. I should not "deter" with big jumps of my own unless the jump still leaves a healthy value margin, because anything near estimate appears to be what they bid anyway.

- On early lots I bid the minimum rather than "pricing in" future lots, but before committing on lots 4–5 I explicitly check remaining budget and prioritize the lot with the biggest value-minus-minimum-winning-price gap; if two lots remain and I can't afford both, I spend on the one with the larger surplus.

- On the final lot, if the minimum winning bid fits my remaining budget and is below my value, I bid it even for tiny surplus — unspent budget scores nothing.

- I ignore estimates except as a rough hint about rivals' likely values; only my value and my budget drive the decision. This episode confirmed estimates are a weak hint at best — rivals bid over estimate repeatedly.
