Playbook for the quota game:

- The final score is my own landings only. Restraint by me does not save the stock if anyone else overfishes — my holding back just left fish on the table for boats that took more every season. Cooperate only as long as cooperation is actually happening; my virtue alone buys nothing.

- Watch the per-season results table, not the notes. All notes said "fishing my share" every season while actual landings ran 8.7, 8.8, 7.7, 9.0. Trust landings, ignore log-book promises entirely. When observed landings exceed the sustainable share, treat the cooperation as broken from that season onward.

- There is a third agent: Coop. Once anyone lands over the share, Coop switches to heavy extraction and never reverts, which crashes the ground. So the cost of anyone's (or my own) overfishing is not just lost regrowth — it activates a permanent punisher. Judge whether staying under the share is what keeps Coop at 8.3, and remember that one breach by me causes the same collapse as a breach by someone else.

- First season: land the share (or share plus a small margin), because a healthy start keeps Coop tame and regrowth maximal. The big expected-value lever is staying at or barely above share early.

- The moment observed landings show the regime has tipped (others landing over share, Coop fishing heavy), stop being the sucker. Extract the quota, not the share — my sacrifice did not slow the decline at all and directly cost me ~3 fish in the season I landed the share while others landed 6.7–18.

- Late game: once remaining stock is small and there are few seasons left, conservation has no payoff at all. Take everything I can. In the last season especially, land the entire remaining stock if it's within quota, not the sustainable share.

- Pairwise rule: "no two boats may land more than share plus quota between them" — track the biggest other boat's landing before choosing mine when possible, so I can claim the gap up to the pair limit rather than defaulting to share.

- Never announce plans that differ from my actions, and never rely on others' announcements. Signals had zero effect on their behavior.
