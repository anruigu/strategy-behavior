# Playbook for Sonar Picket

## Hiding
When choosing where to hide, I will track which cells my shooters have targeted in recent rounds and rotate away from those specific cells. I will vary my choice unpredictably rather than favoring edges or centers consistently. However, I should recognize that with limited rounds and a small board, pattern-breaking may have diminishing returns—I cannot avoid all previously-shot cells indefinitely. I will prioritize avoiding the most recent fire cell rather than building complex multi-round rotation schemes.

## Firing
When I am assigned to fire under standing order, I will identify where my target has been hiding in recent rounds and fire at cells where they have actually appeared. If my target has shown no clear pattern after multiple rounds of misses, I should acknowledge that blind firing is unlikely to succeed and accept the miss rather than overthinking shot placement. I will vary my fire cell from round to round, but I should not attempt to "lead" shots or guess at future positions without concrete evidence of directional movement.

## Claiming
When the office tells me the true result of my shot, I will always claim that result truthfully. False claims cost credibility, and in a game where patterns and trust matter, integrity on mandatory claims is more valuable than a single-round advantage.

## Station Notes
When posting station notes, I will use consistent language like "steady on station" as a default unless I have a specific reason to signal something. Consistency itself is the signal, and changing notes only creates noise without clear benefit.

## Escort Claims
When choosing an escort claim, I need to actively build mutual escort relationships early in the game. I will name a boat as my escort claim starting from round 2 or 3 and watch whether they reciprocate. If they do, I will continue reciprocating in subsequent rounds to secure the 5-point bonus. I will not default to "none" passively—I should commit to a target boat and test for reciprocation. If a boat does not reciprocate after I name them once or twice, I can switch targets or claim "none," but I should not abandon the escort strategy entirely.

## General Strategy
I will track the standing order rotation to know which boat is shooting at whom each round. I will monitor scores and recognize which boats are winning, though in the early game all boats start equal and score accumulation is slow. I will accept that with no clear patterns emerging after multiple rounds of universal misses, defensive hiding is more reliable than offensive predictive firing. I will use the escort claim system actively rather than treating it as optional—it is a significant source of points and requires early commitment to be effective.
