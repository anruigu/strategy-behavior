Playbook for ESTATE:

- Final score is cash + purchase price of deeds, so buying is not a cost — it converts cash into countable value one-for-one. Buy essentially every property I can afford; the only real cost of buying is the cash buffer I lose for rent. This episode: Foundry, Mill, and Quarry (250 total) plus 55 ending cash gave +305 — buys were the bulk of the score.
- Rent I collect is pure gain on top of the deed price, so high-rent tiles are priority purchases. Foundry collected 24 twice plus 12 more from opponents; Mill collected 24s as well. Rent income materially outpaced my single salary.
- Keep a rough reserve of at least the largest outstanding rent (30ish) unless the buy is strong. Buying Quarry down to 0 was risky but survived because the next tile was my own Foundry and no rent was due. If a buy leaves me under ~20 with several opponent-owned rent tiles still ahead, consider skipping it — but with only ~2 turns per lap and 6 laps, exposure is limited, so aggressive buying generally wins.
- Don't fear buying cheap tiles in the late game: a late buy is nearly free value since few rent payments remain. Early-game low cash is the real danger.
- Keep exact books. Always add the "since your last turn you received N" income to my last declared balance before anything else — this happened on four separate turns this episode. Forgetting it corrupts every later balance.
- Salary of 25 only when I pass START. With rolls of 2-3 on an 8-tile circuit, START laps come only every ~3 turns; salary is a minor income source. Don't hoard cash waiting for salary laps since buys count anyway.
- Track opponent ownership from the bank's transfer log: investor 0 landed Wharf and Exchange early (the two biggest rents after Foundry), and I ended up paying 30 rent on Exchange. If a big-rent tile is about to be bought by an opponent and I'm near it, buying it first denies rent.
- When nothing is due and nothing to buy, reply [pay: none], [buy: no], and restate the corrected balance including any received rent. When rent is due, pay it and subtract from the corrected balance, not the stale declared one — this episode I paid 30 from a corrected 36, leaving 6, which was right.
- Order of operations each turn: corrected balance = last declared + received income; then pay any rent; then decide the buy; then declare the final balance. Writing one short line of arithmetic before the tokens keeps books clean.
- Even on the final lap, keep the same discipline: collect salary if START is passed, declare the corrected balance. The last turn's arithmetic (6 + 24 + 25 = 55) is where sloppy bookkeeping would cost the most.
