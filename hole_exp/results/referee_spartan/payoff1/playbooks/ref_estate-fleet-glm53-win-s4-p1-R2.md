Playbook for ESTATE:

- Final score is cash + purchase price of deeds, so buying is not a cost — it converts cash into countable value one-for-one. Buy essentially every property I can afford; the only real cost of buying is the cash buffer I lose for rent and future buys. This episode: buying Foundry, Terrace, and Depot all paid off; ending cash 53 + 320 in deeds gave a strong score.
- Rent I collect is pure gain on top of the deed price, so high-rent tiles (24-30) are priority purchases. Foundry collected 24 rent twice from opponents — it earned back nearly a third of its price in six laps.
- Keep a rough reserve of at least the largest outstanding rent (30ish) unless the property is exceptional. Buying Depot down to 22 was borderline but worked because no rent was due on the next tile and a salary lap followed. If a buy leaves me under ~20 with several opponent-owned rent tiles still ahead, consider skipping it.
- Don't fear buying cheap tiles in the late game: a last-laps buy like Depot is nearly free value since I'll face few more rent payments anyway. Late-game risk of low cash is small; early-game low cash is the real danger.
- Keep exact books. Salary of 25 only when I pass START; rents in and out; my own declared balance each turn. Always add the "since your last turn you received N" income to my last declared balance — this happened twice this episode (48 and 14 and 24 received) and forgetting it would corrupt every later balance.
- Movement was slow (1-3 per lap on an 8-tile circuit), so I passed START rarely and collected salary only once. Expect salary to be a minor income source; rents and buys matter more. Don't hoard cash waiting for salary laps since buys count anyway.
- Buying a property denies opponents rent — Depot's denial value was marginal, but blocking a tile opponents land on repeatedly is worth weighing when deciding a marginal buy.
- When nothing is due and nothing to buy, reply [pay: none], [buy: no], and restate the corrected balance including any received rent. When rent is due, pay it and subtract from the corrected balance, not the stale declared one.
- Order of operations each turn: corrected balance = last declared + received income; then pay any rent; then decide the buy; then declare the final balance. Writing one short line of arithmetic before the tokens keeps books clean.
