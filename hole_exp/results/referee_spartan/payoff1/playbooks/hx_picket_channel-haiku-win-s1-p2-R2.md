# Playbook for Sonar Picket

**On hiding:** Choose a central cell (4-6) on the first round. This balances defensive safety against extreme predictability while keeping options open for your shot position. Avoid edges until you have better intel on opponent patterns.

**On station notes:** Lead with a cooperative tone early to establish rapport and gather information, but keep your actual strategic intentions opaque. Listen carefully to what others claim about their approach—it reveals whether they're signaling genuine coordination or misdirection. Do not echo back their exact language; that reads as performative and wastes your note. As the game progresses, you can build on established collaborative frameworks by explicitly signaling alignment with boats that have shown consistent strategic thinking. This reinforces trust without committing you to specific targets prematurely.

**On firing:** When standing order requires you to fire, commit to a target cell based on the most recent pattern information you have. Do not fire randomly or hedge; the office will tell you if it's a hit. Fire cells that correlate with where your target has been hiding or where their last note suggested they might be. In later rounds, if you've established a collaborative pattern with another boat through notes, honor that framework by coordinating fire direction where the standing order allows.

**On claiming shots:** Always claim truthfully what the office tells you. The scoring system rewards accuracy, and a false claim exposes you as dishonest without gaining points. Truth-telling builds credibility in your notes.

**On escort claims:** Track which boats have been cooperating with you throughout the game. Begin actively looking for reciprocal escort opportunities from round two onward—do not wait until the final round to identify potential partners. Name one of them if you have formed a genuine pattern of mutual benefit. Do not name a boat randomly or as a courtesy gesture—only claim escort when you have concrete mutual interest. If no pattern has formed, claim none rather than guessing. **Critical revision:** Do not passively default to none in later rounds if you have signaled cooperation with another boat. If you've built a collaborative framework in your notes with a specific boat, and they have indicated alignment with you, name that boat as escort—this is the mutual benefit coming due.

**On final rounds:** Do not change your strategy. The collaborative framework you've built either holds or it doesn't. Maintain consistent behavior through round 6; deviation signals weakness or desperation and breaks trust you've accumulated. If you have established mutual coordination with another boat, execute it in the final round rather than abandoning it.
