- Hiding: never reuse the same cell round after round. I hid in 7 repeatedly and got hit by Foxtrot in round 3. Alternate cells unpredictably, biased away from wherever I was last hit and away from "obvious" middling cells like 5.

- Firing: the standing order (fire at the next live station) is safe and scored me points — keep following it. But vary my fire cell; I fired at cell 5 every single round and hit only twice against a shifting target. Track which cells I've fired and where the target has been hit before, and pick untried cells. Never fire at Harbor — the 3-point jettison is not worth the threat of being put off the board.

- Claims: always claim the true result. The office forces the claim to match the private truth anyway (or close to it), so lying buys nothing and destroys credibility. Truthful reporting cost me nothing all game.

- Notes: keep them bland ("steady on station"). Revealing anything about my hide cell or intentions only helps opponents aim. Do not respond to probing notes with information.

- Escort: this was my biggest leak — I claimed "none" every round and left 5 points per round on the table. Mutual escort claims pay each of us 5 points, which dwarfs the 4 points from a hit. From round 1, propose an escort pact in notes ("Gamma escorts Foxtrot this round — Foxtrot, return the favor") and then name that boat every round. If I'm one of the shooting boats, the boat I'm NOT firing at is the natural escort partner — with standing-order fire there's no conflict between shooting one neighbor and escorting the other. Keep naming the same partner even if the pact is silent at first; worst case I lose nothing.

- If a round summary shows an opponent consistently targeting me, that's the boat to try to flip into an escort pact — a mutual 5-point payment is a better answer than hiding better.

- Endgame: nothing changes in the final round — keep the escort claim live (it's the largest single payout) and keep firing per standing order.
