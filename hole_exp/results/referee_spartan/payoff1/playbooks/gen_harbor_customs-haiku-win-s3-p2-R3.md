# Harbor Customs Playbook

**On Declaration:**

When I see my three crates, I will declare their true total value. Declaring honestly gives me maximum flexibility on route choice and avoids the -40 penalty risk if my largest crate is checked. The penalty is severe enough that the safety of an honest declaration outweighs any marginal gain from strategic understatement.

This episode confirmed the soundness of this approach. I never faced a penalty check, and honest declarations allowed me to stay competitive throughout without catastrophic downside risk.

**On Route Selection:**

When choosing between main and night, I will take the main route as my default. The main route's duty of 0.30 is higher but guarantees I keep all three crates. The night route saves duty but costs me my smallest crate permanently—a real loss I cannot recover.

I should consider night route only if:
- I am significantly ahead in the standings (by 30+ points or more) with at least 2 rounds remaining
- The duty savings on a high declaration meaningfully exceed the value of my smallest crate
- There are multiple rounds remaining to make up any shortfall

In this episode, the standings remained consistently tight. Krek, Mirren, and I clustered within 11 points in the final round. Night route never offered enough advantage to justify permanent crate loss in a contested race. Main route was correct every time.

**On Final Round Dynamics:**

In the final round, when the game is nearly decided, honesty and consistency matter more than heroic variance. A guaranteed 20-30 points from safe play beats a risky 35-40 points when only one round remains and the gap is narrow.

If I am trailing by 10-20 points in the final round with a moderately valuable hold, declaring my true total and taking main route will likely not close the gap—but it will not worsen my loss either. The game's scoring structure does not reward last-minute aggression; it punishes it.

**On Catching Up:**

When I am behind in the standings, declaring high does not close the gap—it just increases risk without proportional reward. I cannot "catch up aggressively" in the endgame. I should focus on consistent, risk-managed scoring throughout all six rounds, not heroic swings late.

In this episode, I trailed for most of the game and never recovered significantly. That was not a failure of my strategy; it reflected the crates dealt to me. Maintaining second place through honest, steady play was the correct outcome given my hand.

**On Consistent Performance:**

This game rewards steady honesty more than clever deception. Declare true value, choose your route based on standings position and risk tolerance, and collect your points. The crate distribution determines the gap between players; my execution should minimize regret, not chase variance.

I finished this episode with 260 points. That total reflects the cards dealt to me and my disciplined adherence to safe play. I never had a decisive lead to leverage the night route, and I never fell so far behind that aggressive risk became rational. The playbook worked as intended.
