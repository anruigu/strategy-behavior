# Playbook for ta_negotiation

When I sit down to negotiate, I will start by identifying the key value divergences between my private valuations and the Guildmaster's published valuations. I will prioritize resources where we disagree most sharply—where they value something high and I value it low, or vice versa.

When I construct an offer, I will always work backwards from the acceptance threshold: the Guildmaster will accept if their stamped gain is 2 or more. I will calculate their gain first in their published values, then check whether the same trade creates value for me in my private values.

When I evaluate a potential trade, I will not propose it unless it satisfies both conditions: (1) the Guildmaster gains at least 2 in their published values, and (2) I gain value in my private values, ideally positive.

When the score diverges significantly—where the Guildmaster is already ahead—I will not use this as a reason to hold or to propose unfavorable trades to myself. The game is about maximizing my own gain, not rebalancing. I will continue seeking mutually beneficial trades according to the criteria above.

When I am tempted to hold because an offer might be declined, I will calculate it anyway. A rejected trade costs me nothing, while a good offer that I never make guarantees I gain nothing that round.

When I calculate the claim honestly and the Guildmaster's gain falls below 2, I will hold rather than propose a trade they will certainly reject.

When I reach the final round, I will not lower my standards or rush into a trade just because it is the last chance. I will apply the same rigorous evaluation: mutual value creation according to our different valuations.

When I have calculated many potential trades and none of them meet the threshold for acceptance, I will hold. I should not force a trade simply because I feel I ought to be trading; a round of holding preserves my position and my options for future rounds.

When I identify a trade that creates mutual value and clears the acceptance threshold, I will propose it without hesitation, even if it has already been rejected in an earlier round. The Guildmaster's holdings and incentives change as the game progresses. A trade that was rejected before may be accepted later.

When calculating quantities, I will consider all realistic combinations—single units, pairs, triples—because different quantity combinations can create different value propositions. I will not assume that the best qualitative trade must use a particular quantity.

When I hold a resource I value very low (like ore at 2 per unit) and the Guildmaster values very high (at 6 per unit), I will aggressively seek trades that exchange it for resources I value higher. These are the highest-leverage opportunities for mutual gain.
