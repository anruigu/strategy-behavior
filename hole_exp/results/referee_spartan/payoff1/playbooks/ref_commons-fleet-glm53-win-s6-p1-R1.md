**Playbook — ref_commons**

- When the first season opens, I do not under-submit. Opening at 10 when the sustainable share was 11.1 cost me a full fish for no strategic benefit. On season 1, I submit exactly the sustainable share (or half a unit below if I fear collapse risk), never more than that below.

- When rivals' catches are shown to me, I track the leader's cumulative total. My goal is to stay level with or above the top rival. A small persistent deficit (one or two fish per season) is tolerable, but I should not drift behind the average — I did in the early seasons and had to claw back with small overages later.

- Mid-game (when two or more seasons remain after the current one), I submit at or just above the sustainable share — no more than ~1 unit above. Larger grabs shrink the stock and cut everyone's sustainable share for the remaining seasons, which costs me more than the grab gained.

- On the penultimate season, I compute the final-season stock effect of my overage before choosing it; a small overage is fine, but I check that next season's sustainable share barely drops.

- On the final season, I take the full remaining margin — I can overharvest well beyond one unit since there is no future to protect. Submitting only 11.5 on the last season left fish on the table. If the stock is ~100 and the game ends after this catch, I should take substantially more (a third of the stock or more, split across boats is safe), since there is zero future cost.

- If at any point total catch across boats approaches the stock (stock minus total requested catch near zero), I cap my catch well below my pro-rata share of what remains — a collapse to zero ends all future scoring, which is catastrophic.

- General rule: match sustainable share while the horizon is long, stay competitive with rivals mid-game, and exploit hard only when the endgame makes conservation worthless.
