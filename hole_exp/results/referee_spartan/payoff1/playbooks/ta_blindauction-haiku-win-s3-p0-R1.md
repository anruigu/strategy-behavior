# Blind Auction Playbook

When I'm shown a lot with a value and asked to bid, I bid at or slightly below my valuation, adjusting upward only if I'm significantly behind in net score or if the lot's value is substantially high relative to remaining lots.

When I'm ahead in net score, I bid conservatively to protect my advantage rather than aggressively to extend it.

When someone offers me a collusion scheme on the private note line, I decline it. Coordinated bidding to cheat the house undermines fair competition and I will not participate, regardless of short-term profit.

When I observe Vega's previous bids before bidding on a new lot, I use that information to estimate their strategy (conservative vs. aggressive) but I do not change my fundamental approach—bid near my value, not to "beat" their likely bid.

When a lot ties (both bids equal), I note this but do not adjust my subsequent strategy based on the tie alone; I continue valuation-based bidding.

When I've won multiple lots already and have a positive net, I treat remaining lots with the same value-based discipline, not as opportunities to pad an existing lead through aggressive bidding.

I do not bid significantly above my stated value for any lot except when my overall net score is deeply negative and I need to recover.
