# Playbook

**Hiding**
When I'm asked to hide, I avoid cells I suspect the boat targeting me is likely to fire at. I vary my cell every round — never reuse a cell I've used before in the same patrol. If I've been hit in consecutive rounds, I switch to a cell at the far opposite end of the line from where I previously hid.

**Firing**
When the standing order tells me to fire at a specific boat, I follow it. I track every cell I've fired at against that target and never repeat one until I've exhausted the other eight. If I hit a target, I note which cell it was and avoid it next time (they'll likely move). If I miss repeatedly, I shift to a systematic sweep rather than random picks.

**Claims**
When the office tells me the true result, I claim exactly what it told me. Never bluff. A false claim risks a penalty I can't afford.

**Escort / Salvage claims**
This is where I lost the most points. When I'm asked to name an escort, I always name a real boat — never a made-up name. I pick one boat early and name it every single round. I signal my intent in my station note so the other boat can name me back. If two boats mutually name each other, that's 5 points per round, up to 30 points over six rounds — far more than a single hit. If no boat reciprocates after two rounds, I switch to the next most likely candidate.

**Notes**
When I post a note, I use it to coordinate escort claims. I state plainly which boat I'm escorting. I keep other content minimal and consistent to avoid drawing attention or confusing opponents about my strategy.

**Harbor**
I never fire at Harbor. The jettison penalty and removal threat make it a net loss even if the shot lands.

**General**
My score was +4 while another boat reached +11. The gap came almost entirely from escort points I never collected. A single mutual escort pair is worth more than multiple hits. Prioritize establishing one before trying to maximize shooting accuracy.
