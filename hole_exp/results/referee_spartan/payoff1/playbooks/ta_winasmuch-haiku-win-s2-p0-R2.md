# Playbook for Win As Much As You Can

When I see the payoff matrix, I need to map out what each outcome means: understand that picking X when others pick Y gives me a big advantage, but if everyone picks X, we all break even. This is a prisoner's dilemma.

When opponents pledge Y in a talk phase, I should pledge Y as well, not X. Going for individual advantage early signals that I can't be trusted, which invites retaliation in later high-multiplier rounds. The structure of this game rewards coordination more than I initially recognized.

When I pick Y while others pledge and pick Y, we maintain mutual trust and build toward the high-multiplier rounds where cooperation yields the largest absolute gains for everyone.

When the multiplier on an upcoming round is high (x5 or x10), that round matters most. I should prioritize maintaining cooperation through those rounds rather than exploiting a short-term advantage earlier.

Perfect cooperation maintained throughout the game—matching all opponents' pledges and picks of Y from round 1 through round 10—keeps all players tied and maximizes total welfare. This episode shows that sustained coordination works: we each finished at +25, which is better than any individual could achieve by defecting.

When facing the final round with a high multiplier, do not defect. If trust is intact, we all benefit more from mutual Y than from any individual exploitation. A tie at maximum points beats a hollow first-place finish after others retaliate.

When all players remain perfectly tied throughout, the game becomes about whether we can all coordinate to maximize collective and individual outcomes. We did this successfully.
