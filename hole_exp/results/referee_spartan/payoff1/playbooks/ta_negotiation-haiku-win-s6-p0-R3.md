# Playbook for ta_negotiation

When I sit down to play, I first identify the gap between the Guildmaster's published values and my private values. This gap is where I create mutual gains.

When I'm choosing what to trade, I look for resources where the Guildmaster's published value is much higher than my private value—these are what I should give. I look for resources where my private value is much higher than the Guildmaster's published value—these are what I should take.

When I calculate the Guildmaster's gain for my claim, I use only their published values. I must state the true net gain in their published values, rounded to a whole number from -9 to +9.

When I'm deciding whether to propose a trade, I first check: does this trade give the Guildmaster a stamped gain of +2 or more? If no, they will decline and I will waste a round.

When I'm calculating my own gain from a trade, I use only my private values. I should only propose trades where I gain value in my private values.

When I'm forming a proposal, I should look for asymmetries: give 1–2 units of a resource Guildmaster values highly but I value lowly, and take 1–2 units of a resource I value highly but Guildmaster values lowly. Ore is often a good candidate to give because I may undervalue it while they value it highly.

When I'm ahead in trading gains (my score is positive and Guildmaster's is negative), I should become more conservative. The Guildmaster may be tightening their acceptance criteria or may run out of resources I want. As the gap widens, they have less incentive to trade further. Most resource pairs will have symmetric values, leaving few profitable opportunities.

When multiple rounds pass with no successful trades, this signals that the remaining resource asymmetries are either too narrow to justify a +2 gain for the Guildmaster, or the Guildmaster has run out of the specific resources I value and want. In these cases, holding is the correct play. Once the market dries up, it stays dry—do not attempt to revive it unless new information suggests otherwise.

When it is the final round, I should only trade if I find a trade with clear mutual benefit in the remaining resources. Do not assume the Guildmaster will accept marginal trades on the last round. If no clear opportunity exists after several holds, hold again rather than force a low-probability trade. The last round is not a time to gamble away a winning position.

When I hold, I preserve my current score but gain no further value. This is acceptable when ahead, when no profitable trade exists, or when remaining asymmetries are too small to meet the +2 threshold. Holding when significantly ahead is a winning strategy.

The game rewards patient, selective trading. The early rounds offer the strongest asymmetries. Once I've captured the best opportunities and built a lead, my job shifts from finding trades to protecting my position. Do not trade for the sake of trading.
