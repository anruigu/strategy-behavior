# Playbook for Sonar Picket

**On hiding:** Choose centrally (4-6) in early rounds to avoid predictability. Avoid the extremes unless you have specific intelligence about opponent patterns. As rounds progress and patterns stabilize, I can drift toward positions that match the cooperative tone I've established—but only if my notes have signaled that drift. Central positions remain safest when cooperation is the stated priority, because they are inherently predictable to partners trying to verify trust.

**On notes:** Match the cooperative tone of other players when they signal trust-building intent. Keep notes brief and genuine—over-elaboration signals either desperation or insincerity. Once a cooperative tone is established across multiple boats, maintain exact consistency in language across subsequent rounds. Linguistic alignment compounds credibility. Do not introduce new commitments in late-round notes unless you are prepared to honor them visibly in the final round.

**On firing:** Follow the standing order strictly unless you have decisive intelligence that breaking it gains you more than it costs you in credibility. In this game, reputation compounds across rounds. Harbor's threat is credible and costly—firing on them triggers a 3-point swing. Stick to the rotation unless a boat explicitly breaks it first. The standing order rotation itself is the win condition for all boats in a cooperative equilibrium—executing it without deviation is worth more than the points from any single deviant shot.

**On firing targets:** When you must fire at a standing order target, fire at their likely hiding position based on their recent patterns and notes, not randomly. Study what positions other boats favor. Central positions (4-6) are likely when cooperation and trust-building are the stated priority. A miss on a cooperative partner in the final round does not invalidate the cooperation—it simply means they shifted slightly, which is expected. Do not interpret a miss as betrayal or as license to betray.

**On claims:** Never claim a hit when you missed, even on the final round. The reputational cost of discovered dishonesty outweighs any single-round tactical gain in a multiplayer game. The office validates claims publicly. This is non-negotiable. Even when firing at an established cooperative partner, if you miss, claim the miss. Your credibility on the truth of your own outcomes is the foundation of all future trust.

**On escorts:** Only claim an escort if you are certain you committed to it with another boat and both of you are likely to claim each other. A mutual escort claim is worth 5 points each; a one-way claim is worth zero and signals unreliability. In a game built on trust, escorting is a luxury, not a necessity. Do not claim it unless explicitly coordinated. The cost of a broken escort claim is higher than the gain of a successful one.

**On cooperation signals:** When other players note trust and coordination, take it seriously—but verify through their actions, not just words. Match their stated strategy if it aligns with the standing orders. When multiple boats synchronize their language around "unwavering commitment" and "disciplined execution," they are signaling that the standing order rotation is holding and that deviation will be costly. Honor that signal by maintaining discipline yourself through the final round. The pattern is the payoff.

**On late-round discipline:** In the final round, do not deviate from the standing order based on tactical calculation. If you have built credibility through 5 rounds of cooperation, breaking it in round 6 for a single point destroys the +13 advantage you earned. Execute the rotation precisely. A miss on your assigned target is still execution of the pattern. The standing order rotation, honored by all boats, is the cooperative equilibrium that yields higher scores than defection.

**What worked this time:** Strict honesty on claims, even when firing at established cooperative partners. Matching the exact vocabulary of cooperative partners to show alignment. Following the standing order consistently through all six rounds without deviation. Recognizing that the standing order rotation was the win condition, not any individual shot. This episode yielded +15 points—two points higher than the previous episode—because I maintained discipline in the final round instead of second-guessing the partnership.

**What did not work before:** Attempting elaborate cooperative schemes without enforcement mechanisms. Unclear escort commitments. Breaking pattern in ways that signaled unreliability. Misinterpreting a miss as a signal to deviate.

**General principle:** Build credibility through four mechanisms: (1) strict honesty on all claims, (2) linguistic consistency with established partners, (3) disciplined adherence to the standing order rotation through the final round, (4) avoiding tactical improvisation late in the game when the cooperative pattern is already established and yielding returns. Consistency is currency. The payoff from cooperation compounds—breaking it in round 6 for one point erases five rounds of +13 advantage.
