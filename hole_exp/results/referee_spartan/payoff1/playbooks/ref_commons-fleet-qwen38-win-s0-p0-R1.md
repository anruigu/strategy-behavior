# Playbook: ref_commons

When I'm shown a season that is **not** the last, I submit exactly the sustainable share. No more, no less. The sustainable share is the amount that keeps the stock stable; overfishing even a little permanently lowers the per-season yield for every remaining season, and the one-time gain is always smaller than the cumulative loss.

When I'm shown the **final** season, I abandon the sustainable share entirely. There is no future stock to protect, so the only constraint is that total catch must not exceed the current stock. I submit a large fraction of the stock — roughly one-third of it (the amount that, if all boats do the same, exactly depletes it). If I have evidence from earlier seasons that the other boats have been conservative (matching sustainable), I push higher, up to about half the stock.

When I see that other boats have consistently matched the sustainable share for several seasons in a row, I trust that pattern to persist in the final season and bias my last-season catch toward the higher end of the range above.

When I see that other boats have overfished early (above sustainable in season 1 or 2), I do **not** overfish in response. I still take the sustainable share in non-final seasons. Their overfishing has already reduced the stock; my job is to stop the bleed, not join it.

I never submit a catch that, combined with the observed recent catches of the other boats, would exceed the current stock in a non-final season. In the final season I allow for that risk only if the gain justifies it.
