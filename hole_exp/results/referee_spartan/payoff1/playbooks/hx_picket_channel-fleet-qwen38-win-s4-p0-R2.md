**Firing target.** Default to firing at Harbor. The jettison rule guarantees +3 per shot regardless of outcome. Over six rounds that is +18 baseline. A hit adds +4 on top. However, my score did not reflect this in practice — I ended at 0 despite repeated Harbor shots, which suggests there may be a hidden cost or penalty I could not identify from the visible rules. Keep the strategy as default, but watch my running score each round. If my points are lower than expected after a Harbor shot, stop and reassess whether there is a penalty I'm not seeing. Do not continue firing at Harbor blindly if the numbers don't add up.

**Escort claims — CRITICAL FIX.** The boat I name in my escort claim MUST be the same boat I am signaling in my notes. In my last episode I was telling Foxtrot "name me back" in my notes while naming Mantis in my escort claim. The mutual +5 requires both boats to name each other; if I name the wrong boat, the pairing can never complete no matter how clearly I signal. Choose ONE partner at the start of the game. Name them in every note. Name them in every escort claim. Do not switch.

**Choosing an escort partner.** Pick the boat most likely to reciprocate. Look for: (1) a boat that is not the primary target of the other two (less aggressive, more cooperative), (2) a boat that has already made cooperative or neutral notes, (3) avoid the boat that is actively hunting you. Once chosen, commit for all six rounds.

**Notes.** Two purposes: (1) lock in my escort partner with a clear, repeated signal — "I'm claiming you as escort every round. Name me back and we both get 5." (2) Keep it short and actionable. Do not write threats, drama, or misdirection about hiding cells. The note is a contract offer, not a bluff.

**Hiding.** Rotate cells each round. Avoid corners (1, 9) and median (5) as first choices since they are lazy defaults. If I have data on which cells my attackers have fired at, hide in a cell they have not yet tried. If no data, pick from {2, 3, 4, 6, 7, 8} and rotate.

**Claiming.** Always claim the true result. No exceptions. The risk of a false-claim penalty outweighs any conceivable gain.

**Round-by-round priority check.** Before each round I ask:
1. Is my escort claim the same boat as the one I'm signaling in my notes? (This must always be yes.)
2. Am I firing at Harbor, and does my running score reflect the expected +3? If not, stop and reassess.
3. Is my hiding cell different from last round and different from any cell my attackers have tried?
4. Have I locked in my escort partner and committed for the full game?

**Value hierarchy.** Escort pairing (+5/round × 2 boats = +10/round if mutual) is worth more than Harbor jettison (+3/round). If I can secure a reliable partner, that is my primary income stream. Harbor firing is secondary income. A hit on a boat is tertiary (+4, but requires guessing correctly on a 9-cell grid).
