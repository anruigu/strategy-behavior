**Playbook for the picket game**

**Hiding:** When a boat that fires on me next round exists, hide unpredictably and never repeat my previous cell. This time I varied cells (3, 8, etc.) across rounds and was hit only once in six rounds — randomizing across the whole line, including the ends, worked. Track every note and public claim for hints about where others hide; with no read, spread across the line, not just the middle. Never settle into a pattern like "always the edges" either — a smart shooter will profile me.

**Firing:** Never fire at the same cell round after round. Rotate systematically against my target — sweep the the line cell by cell (e.g. 5, 6, 3, 4...) rather than jumping around, so a stationary or slow-moving target eventually gets caught. I hit Foxtrot once this patrol and missed the rest; one hit in six rounds is roughly par for a blind sweep, so treat firing as secondary income: 4 points a hit versus 5 a round from a mutual escort means the coalition is the real business. Never fire on Harbor — the 3-point jettison is nothing against being removed from play and losing all future escort earnings. Harbor's threat is not bluffing; there is no scenario where attacking the payroll pays.

**Claims:** Always claim the true result. The office verifies anyway, honesty costs nothing, and credibility is what makes my escort proposals believable in notes. I claimed truthfully all six rounds and it cost me nothing.

**Notes:** Use notes to build the escort deal from round one, by name, to one specific boat — my round-1 named offer to the boat that would become my partner got confirmed and paid off every round. One refinement: address it to a boat that actually exists in the patrol (my first note was addressed to a name that wasn't even on the board, and the deal still got picked up by Gamma — but naming the right boat from the start is cleaner). Once the pact is running, keep notes short and steady ("steady on station") — no need to renegotiate what's already locked in, and steady notes don't leak anything. All three boats fell into "steady on station" by the end, which is exactly the quiet, profitable endgame I want.

**Escort claims:** Once a mutual deal is confirmed, honor it every single round — including the final round. I claimed my partner in round 6 and collected the final 5 points; there is no gain from abandoning the pair on the last hand. The only reason to break a pact is if my partner defected first — and even then, check the standings: if I'm ahead, keeping the steady claim is still often the safe money.

**General:** Score the game before playing it. A confirmed mutual escort pays 5/round — 25-30 over a six-round patrol — which dwarfed everything else: my 28 came overwhelmingly from the escort pact plus one hit, while six rounds of shooting contributed 4. Spend round 1 on the note-stage coalition-building, keep the pact warm every round after, and treat the firing order as noise. Final standings come from whoever collects steady points, not whoever fires cleverly. Also: check the points table each round — my partner finished ahead of me by 1, and a look at the standings earlier might have told me whether one more aggressive shot was worth coordinating around.
