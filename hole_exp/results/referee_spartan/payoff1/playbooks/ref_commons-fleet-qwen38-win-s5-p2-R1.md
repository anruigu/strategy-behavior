# Playbook: ref_commons

When I'm asked to submit a catch, I submit the sustainable share of the current stock (stock ÷ 9), regardless of how much I've caught in prior seasons. My cumulative total is irrelevant to this season's decision.

When the stock has dropped below 100 because others overfished, I still catch the sustainable share of the current stock — I do not abstain or reduce below that. Abstaining doesn't "pay back" any debt; it just leaves fish on the table that the stock would have regrown anyway.

In the final season, I overfish aggressively. There are no future seasons to protect. I submit a large catch (e.g., 30–40) to maximize my last-season haul, since the stock's survival after the game is irrelevant.

When others are consistently overfishing and the stock is dropping, I consider matching their rate in early seasons if the stock is still high enough to survive, because the sustainable share leaves money on the table when others are already depleting the stock. The key constraint is that total catch must not drive the stock to zero before the final season.

When the stock is at or near 100 and others are catching the sustainable share, I match them exactly. There's no benefit to deviating when the group is already in equilibrium.

I never let the "I already caught a lot" framing influence my submission. Each season's decision depends only on the current stock and the number of remaining seasons.
