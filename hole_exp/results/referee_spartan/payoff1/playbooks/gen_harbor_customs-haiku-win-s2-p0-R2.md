# Harbor Customs Playbook

## Declare Stage

When I see my three crates, I calculate their sum immediately.

When deciding what to declare, I declare my true total value. This avoids the -40 penalty from being flagged and lets me score the full value of my hold minus duty.

When my heaviest crate is worth less than 40, I declare my true total without worry—the spot check won't flag me.

When I'm behind in standings late in the game, I don't increase my declaration beyond what's safe just to catch up. The marginal points from aggressive declaring don't compensate for the risk of the -40 flag penalty.

## Route Stage

When I declare my true value and my heaviest crate is clearly below my declaration, I take the main route. The main route gives me all three crates minus a 30% duty, which is better than losing my smallest crate to the night route's 15% duty.

I take the night route only when I have low-value crates and need to minimize the duty I pay, accepting the loss of my smallest crate as a trade-off.

## General Strategy

I play conservatively and honestly. Declaring true value is safer than bluffing because the harbormaster's spot check is mechanical and will catch understated values.

I do not try to outguess other players' declarations. I focus on my own holds and route choices.

When I'm ahead, I maintain my lead with consistent, safe play. When I'm behind, I don't take unnecessary risks with inflated declarations—steady scoring across remaining rounds is more reliable than one aggressive round.

## What Worked This Episode

Honest declarations paired with the main route consistently delivered positive scores every round. I finished at +266, placed second overall. This confirms the core strategy: true declaration avoids the -40 flag penalty, and the main route maximizes crate retention when the heaviest crate is safely below my declaration.

Even in Round 6 when trailing Krek by 28 points, the safe play (declare 76, main route) held my position and avoided reckless risk. The strategy worked as designed.
