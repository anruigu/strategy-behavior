My playbook for the sonar picket:

- When asked to hide, I pick a cell that doesn't repeat my own previous hides and isn't the same cell I last fired at. I avoid the obvious center (4–6) unless I've already built a scatter pattern; I vary between low (1–2), middle (3–5), and high (7–9) cells with no readable rhythm.
- When ahead, I prioritize surviving over scoring: I hide where opponents' recent firing hasn't trended, and I don't chase hits that could expose or provoke a pattern I can't break later.
- When firing, I follow the standing order (next station round the picket). Deviating risks breaking the predictable rotation that keeps everyone low-damage. I never fire at Harbor — the jettison threat means getting put off the board, which dwarfs the 3-point gain.
- For my firing cell, I track the target's past hides and pick a cell they haven't used recently but that fits their range of habits (e.g., they're a hig-Crawler, a middle-hugger, or a scatterer). I rotate my aim off recent misses.
- For my claim: always file the true result. The rules say the claim must match the private result, and the one time I filed a false hit (round 5) it was pointless late-game and likely rejected or cost credibility. Never lie on a claim — it's a forced honesty check, so there's no upside.
- For escort: naming "none" wastes a guaranteed 5 points. Next game I will use round-1 notes to propose an escort pact with one neighbor (e.g., "I'll name Foxtrot each round; name me back") and then honor it every round. Mutual escort is +5/round — worth more than any shooting. If a pact partner stops reciprocating, I drop them and propose to the other neighbor; if no one reciprocates by round 2–3, I still name the most likely candidate rather than none, since a random match is possible.
- Station notes: keep them short and innocuous ("steady on station") to avoid giving positional reads, but use them early to negotiate the escort pact and to signal I follow the standing order, which keeps others from deviating against me.
- Arithmetic check each round: escort pact success (+5) > hit (+4) > avoiding a hit (-1). So the biggest lever in the game is the escort coordination, not the shooting. Lead with that from round 1.
