# Playbook

**Non-final seasons:** Submit the sustainable share (or within ±1 of it). Do not exceed it, even if others are overfishing. The stock is the asset that generates all future seasons' catches, and a small per-season gain from overfishing is dwarfed by the loss of future seasons if the stock collapses. This worked well in practice: staying at or near the sustainable share kept the stock healthy through the middle seasons and built up a solid base score.

**Final season:** Submit a number much larger than the stock. The allocation mechanism is proportional when total submissions exceed the stock: your effective catch = (your submission / total submissions) × stock. This means submitting exactly one-third of the stock is a mistake—if others also grab, the total exceeds the stock and your share shrinks. By submitting the full stock amount (or more), you maximize your proportional share. In my last play I submitted 21 against a stock of 64 and only got 17 back because the others' combined submissions pushed the total well above the stock. Had I submitted 64, my share would have been roughly double. The optimal move is to submit the stock amount or a multiple of it; there is no stated penalty for over-submitting.

**If the stock is already low in the final season** (say below 30): same principle applies—submit the stock amount or more. The proportional mechanism means your submission should always be large relative to what you expect others to submit. You want your fraction of total submissions to be as high as possible.

**If others were clearly overfishing in earlier seasons** and the stock dropped noticeably: still cooperate in non-final seasons (the stock is what it is; further depletion only hurts me more).
