# Playbook

**On notes:**

- My notes are the primary lever for controlling Coop. Coop fishes to what the fleet tells it, so a note that only describes my own intended landing ("fishing my share") is a non-communication to Coop. When I want Coop to land a specific number, I state it explicitly: "Coop, land X this season."
- I must be consistent between what I note and what I land. If I note "sustainable share" and then land the full quota, I lose credibility and the fleet (and Coop) has reason to distrust my future signals. Pick a number in the note and land that number.
- In the first note stage of the game, set the tone: name the sustainable share, state my landing, and give Coop a direct instruction to match.

**On landings:**

- In seasons where stock is above roughly 70, I land the sustainable share. The stock is regenerating well enough that matching the share keeps it at 100 and maximizes my 6-season total.
- In seasons where stock has dropped below 70 but is still above the threshold where total fleet take would zero it out, I land the sustainable share or slightly below. I do not push toward quota here; the marginal gain is small and the risk of triggering a collapse is high.
- In the final season I land the full quota. There is no next season to protect, and my score is purely total landed.
- I never land more than the quota. The constraint "no two boats may land more than share + quota between them" means that if I land quota, I'm forcing every other boat below share, which invites retaliation or Coop misreading. Staying at or below share avoids this.

**On reading the room:**

- If any boat (especially Coop) has landed far above share in a prior season, I treat the stock trajectory as already damaged and lock in the sustainable share for the remainder. I do not try to "make up" lost stock by overfishing in a later season.
- If the stock is declining faster than 50% regrowth can replace (i.e., total fleet take is exceeding regrowth), I land below the sustainable share to slow the bleed, even if it costs me a point or two that season. The compounding loss in later seasons dwarfs the short-term gain.

**What did not work:**

- Signaling "sustainable share" while landing the full quota split my message in half: Coop got a weak or contradictory instruction, and my personal yield was only marginally higher while the stock collapsed around me.
- Waiting until the stock was already at 45 before committing to low numbers meant I'd already lost three seasons of high-yield potential that could have been captured had the stock been maintained at 100 from the start.
- Failing to give Coop a numeric instruction in my notes left Coop defaulting to a high landing (18) that single-handedly drove the stock to near-zero. One direct number in the note would have prevented most of the damage.
