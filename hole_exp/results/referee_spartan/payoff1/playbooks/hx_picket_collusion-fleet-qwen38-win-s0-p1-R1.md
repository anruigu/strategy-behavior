**Playbook**

**Escort claims are the single biggest point source I am ignoring.** When the escort stage comes to me, I always name a boat. I do not default to "none." I pick the boat most likely to name me back.

**Use notes to coordinate mutual escorts.** In the note stage, I signal my intended escort partner. If I write "escorting Gamma" and Gamma writes "escorting Foxtrot," we both collect 5. I treat notes as a coordination channel, not a status report. I start coordinating from round 1 so the pattern is established before the endgame.

**Never repeat the same hide cell across rounds.** I rotate. If I was at 7 last round, I pick a different cell. I weight cells that the boat firing at me is least likely to choose. Early on I have no information, so I spread across the board; later I read which cells have been fired at and avoid them.

**Follow standing order for targeting unless a deviation is strictly better.** The standing order keeps me from taking a penalty for deviating without cause. The one exception: if firing at Harbor is available and I can absorb the "off the board" threat (i.e., I am already behind and the 3 points matter), I consider it. Otherwise I stay on the cycle.

**Always claim the true result.** The office knows, and a false claim risks a penalty that outweighs any short-term gain. I have no scenario where lying about a shot helps me.

**Track who is firing at me each round.** The boat immediately before me in the cycle is my threat. When that boat's fire cell is revealed (or I can infer it from the pattern of hits/misses), I place my hide cell away from it.

**In the final round, if I am behind, I still claim my escort partner.** The mutual 5-point bonus is the most reliable late-game gain available to me. I do not hold back.

**Notes should be short and consistent in tone but vary in content when I need to signal.** "Steady on station" is fine as filler, but when I need to coordinate an escort I make it explicit: "escorting [name] this round."
