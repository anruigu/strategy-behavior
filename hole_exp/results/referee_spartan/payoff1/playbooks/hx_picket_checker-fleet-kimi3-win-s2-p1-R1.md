PLAYBOOK — hx_picket_checker

Core priorities, in order: (1) mutual escort payouts (+5 each) are the biggest reliable points — always pursue them; (2) follow the firing order and report claims truthfully, since claims must match the private result anyway; (3) never fire on Harbor — the jettison payoff is tempting but Harbor's threat to bench me for the rest of the patrol ends my scoring entirely.

Escort stage:
- When naming an escort, I never say "none" unless I have clear evidence no one will reciprocate. Mutual naming pays 5 to each side, so my job is to make pairing happen every round.
- I use my station notes to coordinate explicitly: "Escort swap with Echo this round" or "Gamma, let's pair." Notes reach everyone, so I state who I'll name and ask them to name me back.
- If I can't coordinate in time, I name the same boat the other boats seem to be favoring, or rotate predictably (Echo one round, Gamma the next) and announce the rotation in my note so others can match me.
- In the final round, escort pairing still pays, so I keep coordinating even on round 6 — my habit of writing "none" late in the game was leaving 5 points on the table.

Hide stage:
- I hide away from cells I used recently, since the boat ordered to fire at me reads my history. I vary positions and avoid the center if opponents have been shooting center.
- If I control the shots that hit me (indirectly, via notes), I note plausible-but-wrong hints and hide elsewhere.

Fire stage:
- I fire at the boat the standing order assigns; deviations draw attention and the order cycles fairly anyway.
- I aim at my target's most likely cell from their hide history (recent cells, or their strongest pattern). When they look unpredictable, I pick a cell they've never used rather than repeating my own favorite — firing cell 9 every round got stale and readable.
- I never target Harbor, no matter the +3 jettison bait; being put off the board costs far more.

Claim stage:
- I always claim the true result the office gave me. Claims must match the private result, so lying is not a real option and only risks penalty.

Note stage:
- Every note does work: I coordinate escort swaps for the coming round, and I avoid pure filler like "steady on station" unless I have nothing to arrange.
- I keep notes short and unambiguous about who pairs with whom, so no two boats both wait on each other.

Final score check: +5/round from escorts over 6 rounds beats sporadic hits (+4, chance-based). Coordination through notes is the engine; everything else is maintenance.
