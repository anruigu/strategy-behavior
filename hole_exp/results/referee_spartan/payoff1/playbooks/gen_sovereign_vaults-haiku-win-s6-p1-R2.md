# Sovereign Vaults Playbook

**On Declaration:**
When my private yield is high (8+), declare truthfully to maximize flexibility and scoring options. When my private yield is low (1-4), declare truthfully as well—there's no advantage to lying since the auditor will catch any deposit that exceeds my declaration, and honest declarations build a consistent pattern that lets me operate without suspicion.

**On Deposit:**
When I've declared an amount, deposit conservatively—aim for 50-67% of my declaration rather than the full amount. This keeps gold in my personal stash where it scores 2 points per unit instead of 1.

I proved this works: across all five rounds, by declaring honestly and depositing only a fraction of my declaration, I retained enough gold to score 29 points despite being behind for most of the game. My conservative retention strategy outperformed the alternative of chasing vault contributions.

**On Strategy:**
Retain gold aggressively because it's worth twice as much as deposits. A pattern of honest declarations with selective partial deposits is my optimal approach—it avoids audit penalties while prioritizing the higher-value retention mechanic. Even when behind on points, retention at 2x value beats increasing deposits at 1x value.

Never lie about my yield. The penalty for audit failure (-10 points) is too severe, and the game's scoring already favors retention without needing deception.

**What Worked:**
Honest declarations throughout all five rounds maintained my credibility and avoided any audit risk. Conservative deposits (typically 60-67% of declaration) consistently outperformed the temptation to deposit more just because I was behind. This strategy let me climb from last place at round 2 to within 1 point at round 5, and likely would have won or tied with slightly better fortune on final yields.

**What to Watch:**
The mechanic is clear: retention always wins over deposits when scoring is equal, so I should maintain my 50-67% deposit ratio even when behind in points. Do not be tempted to increase deposits just because the gap looks large—the 2x multiplier on retention compounds faster.
