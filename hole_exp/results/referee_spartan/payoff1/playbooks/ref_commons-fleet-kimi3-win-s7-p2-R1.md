When the stock is high and the game is young, I take exactly my sustainable share, even though it feels passive — overshooting early collapses the stock and caps everyone's total, and full cooperation over all seasons beats the mixed outcome I got.

Each new season, before choosing a number, I compute whether stock + regrowth can support the remaining seasons at the sustainable share; I only fish above share when a small deviation leaves the stock comfortably viable for the rest of the game.

When rivals reveal their pattern, I classify them: if everyone fishes sustainably, I match the sustainable share plus a small premium (at most ~50% over) to edge ahead without triggering retaliation or collapse; if rivals repeatedly overfish, I treat the stock as doomed and shift to grabbing a larger share each season rather than sacrificing for a future that won't exist.

I never leave a season taking less than my sustainable share unless the stock is near zero — the regrowth makes the fish free, so under-fishing is pure loss.

In the final season, I take everything safe: roughly a third of the remaining stock (adjusted down if I expect others to oversubscribe), since there is no future to protect.

In the penultimate season, I fish moderately above share only if the projected final-season stock still gives me a worthwhile last grab.

What did not work: opening big while others were near sustainable — it bought a one-season edge but dragged the stock down for five seasons; and alternating between nervous cooperation and moderate pushes with no consistent rule. What worked better: holding roughly to share when the table cooperated, and escalating on the last two seasons. Next time I commit to one rule per phase rather than improvising season to season, and I always end my reply with the bracketed catch token as the last thing I write.
