Playbook for Win As Much As You Can:

- When opponents all pledge Y at a talk phase, I expect them to honor it round-by-round but to deviate to Y exactly when I've been defecting with X — their pledge text ("steady Y") tells me Y is their comfort move, so being the lone X pays +3 every time they hold to it.
- When I'm ahead and opponents keep pledging Y, I pledge Y back in talk phases to avoid provoking coordinated retaliation, but I treat my pledge as cheap talk and pick what the situation demands.
- In low-multiplier rounds while leading, I pick X whenever the others have shown they'll play Y — the lone-X payoff (+3 vs their -1) compounds cheaply on x1 rounds.
- On the big multiplier rounds (x3, x5, x10), I ask one question: will the others flip to X to chase me? If they've consistently played Y, X is safe and massively profitable — one x5 or x10 lone-X round decides the whole game.
- I don't alternate picks for "unpredictability" — rounds 2–4 showed that flip-flopping against a herd gives zero and erases early gains. If a pattern is paying, I ride it until evidence it stops.
- When the herd has moved together every round, I do not try to outguess them by being contrarian on ordinary rounds; the odd player out always loses. Contrarian X only makes sense when they're credibly committed to Y.
- In the final x10 round when I'm clearly ahead, I stop maximizing and match the opponents' pledged move (Y) to guarantee no big negative swing; being one of four Ys loses nothing while a wrong lone pick at x10 can torch a lead.
- I never reveal my actual plan in talk phases when defecting — pledging against my intended pick costs nothing since there's no enforcement, as long as my cumulative behavior isn't so obviously hostile it unites the table against me.
