# Playbook: ref_commons (Fishery)

**When it is not the final season:** Submit the sustainable share (or within ±0.5 of it). This keeps the stock at steady state and maximizes my cumulative yield over all seasons. Do not try to "bank" extra by overfishing early — the stock is already near cap, so there's no room to do that without risk.

**When it is the final season:** Submit the full current stock. Do not subtract others' recent catches, do not apply a buffer, do not try to "leave room." In the final season, every boat has the same incentive to defect, so the stock will be contested. Under any reasonable allocation rule (proportional sharing, first-come-first-served, or capping at remaining stock), submitting more weakly dominates submitting less. My previous attempt to be "precise" by subtracting an estimate of others' takes actually cost me roughly a third of what I could have gotten. Just submit the stock number you're shown.

**Why the old final-season formula failed:** I assumed the other boats would continue taking their sustainable share in the last round. They don't. In the episode I just played, I submitted stock-minus-others'-recent-catches-minus-buffer (60.9 out of 84.7) and only landed about 26 — roughly a third of the stock, consistent with all three boats defecting and the stock being split. If I had submitted the full 84.7, my proportional share would have been meaningfully larger.

**Calibrating the final-season defect:** No calibration needed. The move is always the same: submit the full stock. The only thing to watch is whether the game displays the stock after or before regrowth for that season — use whatever number is shown to you at the start of the final season.

**If the stock has dropped significantly below initial (e.g., below 70):** Reassess the cooperative phase (still take the sustainable share of the *current* stock, which the game tells you). In the final season, the same rule applies — submit whatever the stock is shown to be. Don't be discouraged by a lower stock; the principle is unchanged.

**Expect the stock to drift downward even under cooperative play.** Other boats will often take slightly more than their sustainable share, which erodes the stock season by season. The final stock will typically be noticeably lower than it would be under perfect cooperation. Don't anchor on the initial stock level when planning — use whatever the stock actually is.

**General principle:** The game rewards patience in the middle and maximum aggression at the end. The cooperative norm in early/middle seasons is what keeps the stock alive for the final round. Break it only at the very last moment, and when you break it, break it completely — submit the full stock, not a fraction of it.
