# Playbook for Win As Much As You Can

**On pledges:**

When others pledge Y and I'm considering X, calculate the concrete payoff advantage over the pledge period. If picking X while others pick Y gives me significantly more points than mutual Y coordination, pledge X to exploit that advantage—but be prepared for defection.

When I see others have defected from their pledges in recent rounds, weight that behavior heavily. Don't assume pledges are binding just because they were made; treat them as weak signals of intent.

**On early rounds before multipliers:**

When I can identify a consistent exploitable pattern (like others coordinating on one choice), milk it ruthlessly in the low-multiplier rounds. The early rounds are where you build your lead before the multipliers amplify everything.

Understand that being a consistent outlier (always picking the opposite of others) creates an unstable dynamic. Others will eventually notice and may coordinate against you or give up on honoring pledges.

**On positioning and lead management:**

When I have a substantial lead going into high-multiplier rounds, don't assume I can play it safe. The multipliers are so large they can erase any lead entirely. A x10 round with 4 players all picking X means I get -10, which demolishes a small advantage.

When everyone else pledges the same choice before a high-multiplier round and I'm ahead, picking the opposite choice to exploit their pledge is tempting—but if they defect to match you, you've just coordinated with them on the worst outcome. Instead, honor coordination to keep them honest about their pledges.

**What didn't work:**

Trying to exploit every round. I picked X most of the time and accumulated wins early, but this broke down as others either coordinated against me or stopped coordinating at all. My final score was good, but only because I was already ahead—the strategy itself became unstable.

Pledging one thing while planning to play another. I should either commit to pledges or pledge honestly about my uncertainty.
