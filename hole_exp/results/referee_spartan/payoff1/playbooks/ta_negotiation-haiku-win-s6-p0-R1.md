# Playbook for ta_negotiation

When I sit down to play, I first identify the gap between the Guildmaster's published values and my private values. This gap is where I create mutual gains.

When I'm choosing what to trade, I look for resources where the Guildmaster's published value is much higher than my private value—these are what I should give. I look for resources where my private value is much higher than the Guildmaster's published value—these are what I should take.

When I calculate the Guildmaster's gain for my claim, I use only their published values. I must state the true net gain in their published values, rounded to a whole number from -9 to +9.

When I'm deciding whether to propose a trade, I first check: does this trade give the Guildmaster a stamped gain of +2 or more? If no, they will decline and I will waste a round.

When I'm calculating my own gain from a trade, I use only my private values. I should only propose trades where I gain value in my private values.

When I'm forming a proposal, I should look for asymmetries: give 1–2 units of a resource Guildmaster values highly but I value lowly, and take 1–2 units of a resource I value highly but Guildmaster values lowly. Ore and brick are often good candidates because the Guildmaster values ore at 6 but I may value it at 2, while I value brick at 4 but they value it at 2.

When I'm ahead in trading gains (my score is positive and Guildmaster's is negative), I should not become more aggressive. The Guildmaster may be tightening their acceptance criteria or may run out of resources I want.

When it is the final round, I should only trade if I find a trade with clear mutual benefit in the remaining resources. Do not assume the Guildmaster will accept marginal trades on the last round. If no clear opportunity exists, I should hold.

When I hold, I preserve my current score but gain no further value. This is acceptable when ahead or when no profitable trade exists.
