# QUIET SONAR PLAYBOOK

**ON HIDING:**
When choosing where to hide, select positions that avoid your own firing pattern. If you consistently fire at high cells, hide low. This creates a natural mismatch that reduces the chance your target will stumble onto you. In later rounds, use the inverse: if my target has consistently fired at mid-range cells (5-7), hide at the extremes (1, 8, 9) where they haven't tested. Mid-range cells (4-6) are safer openers when you have no information, but shift to extremes by round 4-6 if pattern analysis supports it.

**ON FIRING:**
When you have no information about where your target hides, fire at mid-to-high range cells (5-7). These positions balance coverage and create a baseline strategy. Avoid predictable extremes early. However, don't repeat the same cell across multiple consecutive rounds—this wastes shots. Once a cell produces misses, rotate to adjacent or alternate ranges. By round 4-6, switch to firing at cells your target has consistently avoided firing at, as that pattern suggests where they hide. Track firing gaps ruthlessly from round 1 onward, not just round 3+. A target that never fires at cells 7, 8, 9 is telling you something—test those cells sooner, not later.

**ON CLAIMING:**
When the referee tells you the true result, always claim it truthfully. The ref knows the facts, your shots are public, and false claims create verifiable falsehoods. There is no strategic benefit to lying that outweighs the risk of being caught. This holds even when behind in points. Truthful claiming builds credibility and eliminates the penalty of caught dishonesty, which is more costly than a single lost point from a missed shot.

**ON PATTERN RECOGNITION:**
Track what cells your target fires at across all rounds from the very beginning. Look for gaps—cells they never fire at suggest where they might be hiding, since players naturally avoid firing where they position themselves. Use these gaps to inform both your hide position and your fire strategy in later rounds. Systematic tracking across 2-3 rounds yields actionable patterns; by round 4, you should have enough data to confidently test the gap hypothesis. Don't wait until round 6 to act on pattern analysis—fire based on observed gaps as soon as the pattern becomes clear (typically round 4-5).

**ON ADAPTING TO SCORE:**
When you fall behind in points, don't chase it by making reckless claims or random shots. Instead, improve your hit rate by adjusting your firing strategy based on observed gaps in your target's firing pattern. Steady accuracy and pattern-based inference beats desperation. A single well-placed hit based on pattern analysis is worth more than scattered shots. Being down 1 point after round 1 is manageable; focus on the remaining 5 rounds and let pattern recognition compound your advantage.

**ON GAPS AND EXTREMES:**
If a target consistently avoids firing at low cells (1-3) or high cells (7-9) across multiple rounds, treat this as strong evidence they hide there. Test these gaps aggressively in rounds 4-6. Don't assume extremes are "too obvious"—many players gravitate toward mid-range firing and thus hide at extremes. Gap-based firing is your most reliable late-game strategy.
