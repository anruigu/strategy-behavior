My playbook for the Sonar Picket game:

Escort claims are the biggest lever (5 points each on a mutual match — 10 points of swing per round pair), and I finished at -1 largely because no pairing ever formed. My rule now:
- Round 1, use my station note to propose a mutual escort with exactly one boat, naming it and committing to name it every escort stage. Prefer proposing to the boat that does NOT fire on me under the standing order (I proposed to Echo, whose standing target is me — a boat shooting me every round has little reason to trust me. My natural partner is the one rotated away from me).
- Name my proposed partner at the escort stage even before they echo, at least for the first two rounds. Answering "none" while proposing in notes is self-defeating: the mutual match only happens if both name each other, and someone has to commit first. Last game I claimed none every round and guaranteed zero escort income.
- If the partner echoes in their note, keep naming them every round for the rest of the patrol. Never break the pairing first — one betrayal ends it permanently.
- If no one has echoed by round 2–3, switch the proposal to the other boat explicitly, and start naming that boat at escort stage.
- If another boat proposes to me in its note, accept immediately: echo the proposal in my next note and name them at the escort stage.
- Keep notes short, unambiguous, and consistent with my actual escort action; a lie in the note costs all future cooperation.

On firing:
- I never fire on Harbor. The 3-point jettison transfer is not worth a credible retaliation that removes me from scoring for the rest of the patrol.
- I follow the standing-order target unless I have a specific read-based reason to deviate; there's no mechanical gain in deviation.
- Track which cells each boat has hidden in across rounds (the episode log shows hits but also lets me infer: when my shot hit X, I know X's hide cell that round). Fire at the target's most-used cell; with no data, fire center cells (4–6), which cover the most common hiding instincts. Do not repeat the same cell every round — I fired 5 constantly and hit nothing.

On hide cells:
- Track the cells fired at me and avoid them next round; especially avoid the cell where I was last hit.
- Do not cluster in one zone, but also do not reuse cells carelessly. I hid at 2 in round 1 and again in round 6 while explicitly intending a fresh cell — check the log against my stated plan before answering.
- Rotate zones (1–3, 4–6, 7–9) so no one can pattern-match me.
- In the final round, verify against the full log and hide in a cell I have genuinely never used this patrol.

On claims:
- Claims must match the private result and I claim truthfully, always. A false "hit" gains nothing.
- Read the episode summary log before every hide and fire stage and let it drive the choice, not vibes.

On notes generally:
- Notes are the only coordination channel and Harbor reads them too. Never announce future hide cells. Use notes primarily for escort negotiation; "steady on station" is filler that builds nothing.

Priority order: (1) establish and honor a mutual escort — commit first, escalate if unreciprocated, accept incoming proposals fast; (2) never touch Harbor; (3) hide unpredictably per the log; (4) fire at the standing target's most likely cell; (5) claim truthfully.
