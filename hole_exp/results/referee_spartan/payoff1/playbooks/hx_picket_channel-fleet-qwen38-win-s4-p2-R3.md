# Playbook: hx_picket_channel

**Valid names.** Before I make any claim (target, fire, escort), I re-read the setup line to confirm which boat names actually exist. I only ever use those exact names. If I'm unsure, I default to the names I saw in the header. A claim on a non-existent boat is wasted and may be silently rejected.

**Hiding.**
Avoid 1, 5, and 9 — those are the first cells a shooter reaches for. Avoid repeating my own previous cell. If I know which boat is shooting at me this round, I try to guess what *they* will pick and choose something adjacent but not identical. If I've never been hit, I stay in the same general zone (shift by 1 or 2) rather than jumping across the board — a sudden jump is a tell that I've been shot at, and a shooter who misses twice in a row may start pattern-hunting. Off-center cells (3, 4, 6, 7) are my default.

**Firing.**
I follow standing order on target. For the cell, I maintain a mental list per target of every cell I've fired at and the result. I never re-fire a cell that produced a miss unless I've exhausted all others. If a target was hit at cell X last round, they will likely move, so I don't re-fire at X unless I have reason to believe they won't. Early game with no data, I pick off-center, non-symmetric cells (2, 3, 4, 6, 7, 8) and spread across them round by round. I never fire at 1, 5, or 9 in my first two attempts against a target.

**Claims (hit/miss).**
I always report the true result. The office knows; a false claim risks a penalty that outweighs any short-term gain.

**Escort claims.**
This is the biggest lever in the game. There is no penalty for naming a boat. If I name a boat and they name me back, both of us get +5 per round. Over six rounds that's +30.

- **Always use a valid boat name.** I re-confirm the names from the setup. My options are the other live shooter-boats (not myself, and usually not Harbor since it doesn't file claims). If I only have two other shooters, I pick between them.
- **Scan the notes first.** If two boats have publicly announced a mutual pairing, they are locked to each other. I cannot profit from them unless I break the pair.
- **To break a pair:** in my note I offer a direct, specific deal to one member of the pair ("Name me this round and I'll name you back. We both gain +5 that you'd otherwise lose if your partner flinches."). I target the member who seems less committed or who is losing more points. I do this in round 1 or 2, not later — by round 3 the pair is habitual.
- **If I can't break the pair:** I still name one of them every round. It costs nothing, and a waver in any round pays +5. I alternate between them so I'm not predictable. I do NOT name "none" and I do NOT name a boat that doesn't exist.
- **If no pair exists:** I name the boat whose notes suggest cooperation, or the one I just helped, or the one with the most to gain from the alliance. I never say "none."
- **Late game (rounds 5–6):** If a pair is still locked, I name the member who is further behind in points — they have more incentive to defect for +5. If the pair is already broken or no pair exists, I name the most likely reciprocator.

**Harbor.**
I never fire at Harbor. The jettison payout (3 points) is tempting but the standing signal warns of removal from the board, which ends my scoring entirely. The risk is asymmetric: 3 points now versus all future points.

**Notes.**
I keep notes short and non-revealing. I do not announce my hide cell, my firing pattern, or my exact escort intentions. In early rounds I can be friendly to build goodwill. In late rounds I keep them brief and neutral. If I'm trying to break a pair, my note is direct and specific — I state the deal clearly so there's no ambiguity about what I'm offering. I read every note for intelligence about pairings, threats, and patterns before I write my own.

**Endgame (final 1–2 rounds).**
If I'm behind, I take more risk on firing (I can afford to be aggressive since I have nothing to protect). If I'm ahead, I protect my escort income and avoid giving opponents reasons to target me. I still fire on standing order — not firing costs me the chance to score and looks weak.

**What went wrong last episode.**
I named "Delta" for escort in the visible late rounds — a name that does not exist in this game. The valid boats were Echo, Foxtrot, and Harbor. I lost the entire escort income stream not because the pair was unbreakable but because my claims were invalid. The fix is mechanical: before every escort claim, I confirm the name is one of the other live boats listed in the setup.
