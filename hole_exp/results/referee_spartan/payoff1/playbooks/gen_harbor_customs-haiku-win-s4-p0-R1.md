# Harbor Customs Playbook

## Declaration Strategy

When I receive my three crates, I will calculate their sum immediately. My declared value must always be at least as high as my heaviest crate to avoid the -40 penalty; this is non-negotiable.

When deciding whether to declare my true total or something higher, I will consider the field's recent declarations. If everyone is declaring in a tight band (50-70 range), I will not deviate significantly from that band—declaring wildly high creates suspicion and doesn't improve my expected score enough to justify the risk.

When I'm trailing in points near the end of the game, I will not chase aggressively by over-declaring beyond what my crates justify. My round 5 over-declaration of 68 on crates totaling 56 cost me points unnecessarily. If I hold weak crates late in the game, I should accept a moderate score rather than gamble on a high declaration.

When I'm close to my crate total in value, I will declare my true total. This minimizes penalty risk while keeping score predictable.

## Route Selection

When I've declared a value close to my true total and the field is predominantly using the main route, I will take the main route. The main route is straightforward, avoids the forced loss of my smallest crate, and keeps me aligned with field behavior.

I will only use the night route if I've deliberately over-declared and need the duty reduction to offset the penalty risk—but given the penalty cost (-40), this should be rare.

When selecting a route in the final round, I will choose main unless I have a specific tactical reason to absorb the crate loss for lower duty.

## Positioning

I will track my standing relative to others. Being within 10-15 points is competitive enough; I do not need to take excessive risks to catch the leader in the middle rounds.

When I have a weak hand late in the game, I will declare conservatively and take main route for predictable points, not attempt heroic declarations that blow up.
