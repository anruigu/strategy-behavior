**Playbook for sonar picket channel**

**Hiding.** Randomize my hiding cell every round; never fall into a pattern like always mid or always high. Middle cells (4-6) are reasonable defaults but I should vary. If a shooter has been hitting me by guessing middle, shift away from the most-guessed cells, but don't become predictable in the other direction either. In this patrol Gamma hit me in the middle repeatedly while my low-cell round (2) went unhit — shifting away from the most-guessed cells when under sustained fire from one boat paid off.

**Notes.** Keep notes short and low-effort except for pact maintenance. Notes ARE the tool for locking in an escort pact — a two-line exchange confirming "pact holds, naming you" each round was enough to sustain it through the whole patrol. Use them for that and little else. One correction from this patrol: a public note saying "I name both of you" does nothing — the escort claim only allows ONE name, so a triangle can't be paid in full. Don't promise two boats the same thing; it just breeds mistrust and wastes the final note.

**Firing.** Follow the standing order unless there's a compelling, verified reason to deviate. Firing at Harbor is tempting (3 points either way) but it carries the threat of being put off the board — never worth the risk in a short patrol. When firing at my assigned target, vary the fire cell: I fired six rounds at Foxtrot, hit nothing, and lost nothing by it — hits are a bonus, not the plan. Rotate through plausible cells (2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8) rather than clustering mid; with no intel on the target's habits, spread is the best I can do. Don't agonize over fire picks; the escort pact is the real engine.

**Claims.** Always report the private result truthfully. It costs nothing, avoids contradictions, and keeps credibility — honest miss claims every round never hurt me and no one ever challenged them.

**Escort claims.** The single biggest scoring lever. 5 points to each side per round of mutual naming — nearly all my +7 came from pact rounds. Next time: propose a reciprocal escort pact with one other boat in the very first note stage ("name you each round if you name me"), confirm it, and then name them EVERY round without fail — including round 1, before confirmation, since a one-sided name costs nothing and a match pays even in the setup round. This time the round-1 proposal worked exactly as intended: Foxtrot confirmed and the pact held to the end. Two refinements:
- Only ONE escort name is allowed per round, so commit to a single stable partner and politely decline third-party offers ("my name is spoken for") rather than agreeing to a deal I can't honor. This patrol I let Gamma believe I'd name them in round 6 while I named Foxtrot — the note-stage promise was worthless to Gamma and could have poisoned a future pact.
- If two boats are both offering, take the first to confirm and hold it; don't try to run both. A stable two-boat pact beats any note-stage scheming with the third boat.
- The only reason to answer "none" is a round where no candidate exists at all.

**General principles.** Don't overinvest in note-stage diplomacy that produces no data — except pact maintenance, which is cheap and lucrative. Track actual hit/miss history for hiding choices. Claim honestly always. Propose escort reciprocity in round 1, honor it with exactly one partner every round, and never promise a name I can't deliver — that's where the real money is.
