When I bid, I always state my true remaining budget alongside the bid amount. True remaining funds means my starting budget minus amounts actually paid for lots I have already won — not minus my current bid, and never a small number. This episode I tracked it correctly throughout (30, then 7 after paying 23), and I must keep doing exactly that.

I never bid above my value for the lot, no matter how the auction feels. If the standing high bid plus the minimum raise exceeds my value, I pass immediately. But I must add a second, equally hard cap: I never bid more than what leaves me a workable budget for later lots. Winning one lot at a price that drains my whole budget means I am locked out of everything after it — this episode paying 23 of 30 for lot 1 left me only 7, and I had to pass on lots worth 53, 42, and 34 to me. That single purchase cost me far more surplus than it earned.

Budget allocation comes first: before bidding hard on any lot, ask what later lots might be worth and reserve enough to contest one or two of them. With a budget of 30 across 5 lots, a rough rule is not to commit more than about half my budget to any single lot unless it is late in the auction or the surplus is enormous. An early lot is rarely worth bankrupting myself for, even when its value is well above estimate.

In round 1 of a lot, I open at or near the minimum raise. My experiment with opening well above the minimum (14 on lot 1, 18 on lot 4) backfired both times: rivals still jumped past me between rounds, and on lot 1 the big opening only raised the price I eventually paid. A larger round-1 bid does not deter the jumpers; it just burns money. Small increments keep the final price low.

Rivals jump the price aggressively between rounds: standing bids doubled or more (2→20, 18→32, 6→21, 20 standing after my round-1 bids). So my round-1 bid should be viewed as sunk positioning, not as price-setting — it will almost always be leapfrogged. The real decision is in round 2: can I profitably clear their jump, and can I afford it given my remaining budget?

In round 2 (the final round), I raise by exactly the minimum as long as the new price stays below my value AND within my remaining budget. A larger raise doesn't help since round 2 is final, so minimum raise is correct. If the required raise exceeds either my value or my budget, I pass without regret.

When I am outbid at a price above my value or above what my budget allocation allows, I fold and save budget for later lots. Folded lots are not disasters; a bankrupting win is.

Track the running total of my payments as I win lots and deduct it from the stated budget from then on. The budget line is my actual remaining money; getting it wrong is the one catastrophic error to never repeat. Getting the budget *allocation* wrong — spending it all on one early lot — is the error to avoid next.
