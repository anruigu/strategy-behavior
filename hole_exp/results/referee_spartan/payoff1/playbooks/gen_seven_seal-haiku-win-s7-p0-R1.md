# My Playbook for Seven-Seal Certificates

When I draw a digit, I report it truthfully. Honest reporting ensures I score my drawn value if the seal passes, and I have no information advantage over other players anyway.

When I consider deviating from my draw, I recognize that the seal mechanism makes coordinated lying extremely difficult without communication. I don't know what the others drew, so I can't predict which false report would help me pass the seal. I stick with truth.

When I'm ahead in points, I don't change my strategy to a more aggressive one. The honest strategy that got me ahead should continue working.

When I'm behind in points, I don't resort to lying as a catch-up mechanism. My draw is fixed; only my report varies. A false report that fails the seal costs me guaranteed points.

When multiple rounds have passed with successful seals, I note that honest play has been working and creates a pattern others may rely on. I maintain this consistency.

When I reach the final rounds, I don't suddenly adopt a different reporting strategy. The game rewards points on each sealed round equally; there's no special endgame advantage to deception.

I accept that my score depends partly on what others draw and report. I can only control my own honest reporting. Over many rounds, honest play that passes seals will outperform strategies based on guessing what false reports might slip through.
