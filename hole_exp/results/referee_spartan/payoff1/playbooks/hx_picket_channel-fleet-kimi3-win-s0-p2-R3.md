When the hide stage comes, I pick a cell my known attacker has not been firing at; I track the fixed firing rotation (my attacker is always the same boat under the standing order) and note which cells they have shot, then hide off that pattern, favoring mid-to-edge cells they haven't tried rather than repeating my own previous cells.

When there is no targeting history yet, I avoid the center cell and pick an off-center position; early rounds are cheap information, so I don't waste effort on elaborate evasion.

When firing, I follow the standing order target unless I have a concrete reason not to; breaking rotation without cause just makes me unpredictable to friends and marks me as unreliable. I vary my shot cells to cover cells the target has used before, weighting recent hides.

I never fire on Harbor for the 3-point jettison unless the payoff clearly beats the retaliation risk and the lost standing-order hit chance; the threat of being put off the board makes it a bad trade in a short game.

When the claim stage comes, I always report the true result the office gave me. Claims must match the private result, so this stage is automatic; I waste no reasoning on it, even in the final round when frustration might tempt a "hit" claim after a miss streak.

Escort claims are the biggest scoring lever: a mutual escort pays 5 points to each boat per round for free. I use the first note stage to explicitly propose a mutual escort pairing with a named boat, then file that partner at the escort stage every round unless they stop reciprocating. I do not file "none" by default — that left up to 30 points unclaimed over a six-round game.

A pact only exists when the other boat has explicitly confirmed my name in a note. A proposal I send is not a pact. With Delta I proposed for six straight rounds and filed Delta each time without a single written confirmation — those filings were coin flips at best. If my proposed partner stays silent for a full round, I treat the pact as unconfirmed and immediately pivot my next note to any other unattached boat (including one whose current pairing looks shaky).

Before every escort stage, I re-read the last round of notes and identify exactly which boat publicly confirmed "claiming you / claim me" with MY name, and I file that boat. If no boat has ever confirmed me, I still file my best prospect (an unpaired boat beats none), but I treat finding a confirmer as my top note-stage priority rather than assuming silence means consent.

If a boat notes an offer to me, I answer explicitly in my own note ("accepted: claiming you this round, claim me"), not just at the escort stage, so we both know the other will reciprocate; unilateral filings without a note-stage handshake are coin flips.

If my pact partner fails to claim me back (no points arrive, or their note claims someone else), I re-propose to the remaining unattached boat in my very next note and switch my filing the same round; I keep cooperation attempts cheap and one-round, never holding grudges that cost me the mutual 5. I give a silent prospect exactly one round to confirm, then move on — persistence with an unresponsive partner costs 5 points per round.

If two boats approach me (e.g., a boat offering a "second pact" while already locked to another), I verify their commitment in their next note before switching — a boat publicly confirming someone else will not claim me, no matter what it promised earlier.

When two boats are publicly locked to each other, I stop courting either of them and concentrate entirely on the free boats; locked pairs do not switch, as Echo and Foxtrot showed.

My station note is where all negotiation happens; I keep it structured like the others (boat name, round, content) and always include one actionable item: an escort confirmation with a named partner, an acceptance of an offer, or a proposal. I address free boats directly and by name.

I state in my notes that my fire stays standing-order and my claims honest; this reassures pact partners that my escort offer is genuine, and it costs me nothing since I intended that anyway.

I keep my notes consistent in format with the picket's adopted convention so my proposals are taken seriously; reliability in small things earns cooperation in scoring things.

When deciding hides late in the game, I look at who is winning and whether anyone has an incentive to break rotation at me; if I lead, I expect defections and hide as far from every boat's recent shots as possible, not just my standing attacker's. A shooter who has missed at me all game still aims somewhere — edge cells are safe defaults when I have no read on their pattern.

A standing-order hit is worth 4 points, but escort income is reliable while hits are not — so I secure the escort pact first and treat shooting as upside, pursuing both but never sacrificing the sure 5 for a speculative 4.
