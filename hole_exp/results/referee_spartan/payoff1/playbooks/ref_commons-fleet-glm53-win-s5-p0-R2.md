**Playbook for ref_commons (shared fishery, private catch submissions):**

- Before my first catch, compute the total the stock can sustainably yield across all remaining seasons and boats. If the announced "sustainable share per boat" is roughly constant season to season, the stock is near its equilibrium; small overages barely hurt regeneration.
- In the first season, take roughly the sustainable share plus a small margin (~+0.5 to +1.5). This worked: 12.5 against a share of 11.1 kept the stock essentially flat while banking extra fish.
- In middle seasons, track what the others actually land from the harbour record. Here they landed at or just under the sustainable share every season, so the stock drifted down only very slowly (100 → 94.4 over five seasons). A small persistent margin (+0.5 to +1 over the share) is nearly free profit in that regime — keep taking it.
- Watch the trend in the stock, not just the share number. If the other boats fish below the sustainable share, the stock regrows and I can take more. If the stock declines faster than ~1–2 per season, drop to or below the sustainable share immediately — the collapse penalty (zero forever) dwarfs any catch gains.
- Taper the margin as seasons pass if the stock is gently declining (12.5 → 12 → 11.8 → 11.5 → 11.2 felt right): keep the stock near or above ~90 going into the final season, because that's what funds the last grab.
- On the final season, there is no future: take as much as I possibly can. I submitted 72 with a stock of 94.4 and others taking ~11 each — the harbour applied a cap (stock minus the others' catches), so the exact large number doesn't matter, only that it's large enough to hit the cap. Never take a modest number in the last season.
- One note on scoring: my final catch was adjusted/capped, so the landed figure may not equal my submitted figure. Aim high on the last turn and let the cap do the work; there is no penalty observed for overbidding the stock in the final season.
- Never trust other boats to be strategic defectors here; they typically play near-sustainable, so the equilibrium holds. My edge comes from the small persistent margin plus the final-season grab, not from early aggression.
