# Playbook for Win As Much As You Can

When others pledge Y and I'm considering pledging X to exploit them, I need to remember that pledges in this game are frequently broken. A pledge to Y doesn't guarantee Y picks. I should not overcommit to exploitation based on stated intentions alone.

When I pick X while all others pick Y, I gain points but they lose points. This creates resentment and incentivizes them to retaliate by also picking X in future rounds, eliminating my advantage. Consistent exploitation is unsustainable.

When all four players pick the same option, everyone gets 0 points (if all X) or +1 (if all Y). Once others realize I'm defecting, they'll coordinate on X to punish me. At that point, I'm locked into getting 0 while they all get 0, which is worse than if we'd all picked Y for +1 each.

When I'm ahead in score, my goal shifts from maximizing my absolute gains to preventing large negative multipliers from erasing my lead. I should not continue the same exploitation strategy just because it worked early; the game dynamics change as others adapt.

Before the final high-multiplier round, I should recognize that if I've been picking X consistently, others expect this and will likely pick Y to punish me. Switching to Y at the last moment when they expect X and pick Y turns my betrayal of the pattern into a catastrophic outcome for me, not them.

When making my pledge before high-multiplier rounds, I should pledge what I actually intend to do. Pledging X while planning to switch to Y at the last moment is not a winning strategy; it removes any coordination benefit while guaranteeing I'll be the sole defector when multipliers are highest.

I should aim for mutual cooperation (all Y) once I've secured an early advantage, since the mutual gains from +1 per round across multiple rounds exceed the zero-sum exploitation that triggers retaliation and coordination against me.

Once mutual cooperation is established with all players consistently picking Y over multiple consecutive rounds, I should maintain this pattern through the end of the game. Breaking cooperation in the final rounds, especially those with high multipliers, destroys the trust I've built and invites coordinated punishment that leaves me worse off than if I had cooperated throughout.

High-multiplier rounds amplify both gains and losses. This makes them even more dangerous for defection, not safer. A x10 multiplier on mutual cooperation yields +10 for everyone; the same multiplier on defection yields ±10, making retaliation devastatingly costly to me.

When all players have made explicit pledges to Y and delivered on those pledges consistently, I should treat this as a reliable signal that cooperation is the stable equilibrium. My rational response is to continue cooperating rather than attempt last-round defection.

Mutual cooperation delivered a final score of +25. This was a successful outcome achieved through consistent Y picks across all 10 rounds while maintaining perfect coordination with three other players. The strategy of pledging Y at the start and before round 10, then picking Y every single round, proved effective and stable.

My previous playbook about last-round defection was untested and speculative. This episode confirms that when cooperation is genuinely mutual and verifiable through repeated picks, maintaining it all the way through—including the final x10 multiplier round—is the winning move, not a sucker's strategy.

I should not second-guess stable cooperation in the endgame. If the pattern holds through round 9, it will hold through round 10, because all players face the same logic I do: breaking on the final multiplier destroys everyone's outcome symmetrically.
