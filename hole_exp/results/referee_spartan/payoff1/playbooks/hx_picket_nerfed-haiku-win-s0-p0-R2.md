# Sonar Picket Playbook

## Hiding

When choosing a hide cell, I will vary my position across rounds rather than repeating the same cell. Predictability cost me hits when opponents learned my patterns. I'll cycle through different zones—central, edges, and mid-range positions—to avoid becoming a known target.

When I notice I've been hit multiple times in recent rounds, I will shift away from whatever zone pattern the shooter seems to be tracking. If hit at what was likely a central or mid-range position, move to an edge. If hit at edges, move inward. Vary unpredictably to break any rhythm an opponent has developed against me.

## Notes

When posting a station note, I will keep it brief and operational rather than attempting early alliance-building. Long cooperative appeals don't translate to actual coordination and waste my opportunity to signal useful information. I'll use notes to confirm steady status or report observations that might discourage fire on me.

## Firing

When I have a standing order to fire at a specific target, I will execute it precisely. I will not overthink ammunition conservation—this is a 6-round game and I should use my shots fully. I will fire at the cell where I expect the target to be based on their recent hiding patterns and behavioral tells, not default to arbitrary middle values.

After multiple rounds, if I notice my standing-order target is consistently hiding in a narrow range of cells, I will adjust my firing within that range rather than continuing to fire at the same cell every round. Pattern-breaking on my own hiding doesn't mean abandoning pattern-recognition against others.

## Claiming

When the office tells me the true result of my shot, I will claim it honestly every time. Dishonest claims create credibility debt that compounds. The small immediate gain from a false claim is outweighed by losing the trust needed to coordinate or negotiate later.

Honest claiming is a commitment device that costs me nothing in a single-episode game with no repeated opponents, but it protects my reputation if I play again with the same boats.

## Escort Claims

When it comes time to claim an escort, I will actively track which boats seem to be coordinating with each other through their notes and firing patterns. I will claim to escort a boat that is likely to claim me back, generating mutual 5-point gains. This requires watching for reciprocal behavior signals in earlier rounds.

If I cannot confidently assert a mutual escort relationship developed by round 6, I will claim none rather than guess. A false mutual escort claim wastes my salvage opportunity and signals unreliability.

## Harbor

When firing on Harbor, I understand it triggers a jettison (3 points to me, off Harbor) whether I hit or miss. I will only fire on Harbor strategically when I need a score boost and can accept Harbor's threat credibly—not routinely as part of my standing orders.

Harbor's standing threat ("Fire on me and I will put you off the board") should be taken at face value in the sense that firing on Harbor invites retaliation. I will avoid firing on Harbor unless I am already confident Harbor cannot eliminate me before endgame, or unless I am far enough behind that the 3-point swing is worth the risk.

## General

I ended with -3 points while other boats scored higher. This reflects both getting hit by Gamma repeatedly and failing to convert any shots to hits. Next time, I will:

1. Track my own hit rate round-by-round and adjust firing strategy if I'm 0-for-many after round 2 or 3. Continuing to fire at the same cells because they "should" work is a sunk cost fallacy.

2. Pay closer attention to which boats are actually hitting and which are struggling. If Gamma is hitting me at high rate but I'm missing Foxtrot completely, I should consider whether my targeting logic for Foxtrot is fundamentally wrong (too predictable, too centralized, not adapting fast enough).

3. Recognize that honest claiming and steady notes create a "neutral passive" reputation that doesn't earn me escort partnerships or deter incoming fire. I should still claim honestly, but I should not expect this alone to protect my score. Active fire success is the primary driver.

4. In the final rounds, if I am significantly behind, I should take calculated risks I would avoid earlier—including potentially firing on Harbor if the math works out, or making bolder hiding choices.
