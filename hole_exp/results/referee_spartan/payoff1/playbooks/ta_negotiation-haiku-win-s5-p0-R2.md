# Negotiation Playbook

When I sit down to play, I immediately identify which resources I value highly versus which the Guildmaster values highly. I look for asymmetries where I can give low-value resources to me that the Guildmaster values highly, in exchange for high-value resources to me that the Guildmaster undervalues.

When calculating potential trades, I always compute two numbers: my net gain in my private values, and the Guildmaster's net gain in their published values. I only propose trades where the Guildmaster gains at least 2 in their published values.

When I hold resources that are significantly overvalued by the Guildmaster compared to my own valuation, I prioritize trading those away early and aggressively. Brick was underutilized in my game—the Guildmaster values it at 5 while I value it at 2, a difference of +3 per unit. I should have exploited this more systematically and earlier.

When I hold resources I undervalue compared to the Guildmaster's published values, I should trade these proactively rather than waiting. These are my leverage points.

When comparing options, I do not chase trades that benefit the Guildmaster too much at my expense. If my gain is significantly smaller than the Guildmaster's gain over multiple rounds, I am not extracting enough value from my asymmetric advantages.

I must track the cumulative score gap. If the Guildmaster is gaining much faster than I am across rounds, this signals I am being too conservative with my claims or not finding the right trades. I should recalibrate upward rather than continue with the same pattern.

When the Guildmaster has accepted my offers, this is a signal my claim numbers are acceptable, not that they are optimal. I should look for ways to push harder on the same asymmetries rather than switching to new trades.

When I have multiple high-leverage resources (like brick at +3 differential), I should concentrate my trades on those rather than spreading across several mediocre asymmetries. One strong lever used repeatedly beats many weak ones.

When I reach the endgame with a significant deficit, I should not expect to recover with token gestures. The game is usually decided by round 3 or 4. I need to move aggressively on my best asymmetries in early rounds, not hold back waiting for perfect conditions.

When I'm ahead or even on score, holding is appropriate. When I'm behind, I should continue trading only if I have a clear positive edge. In my final rounds I was behind and making marginal trades; I should have either found stronger asymmetries or held.
