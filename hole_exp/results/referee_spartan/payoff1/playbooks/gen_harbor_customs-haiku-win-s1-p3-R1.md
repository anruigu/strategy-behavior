# Harbor Customs Playbook

## Declaration Strategy

When I declare, I always declare my true total value. The -40 penalty for being flagged is too severe to justify underreporting, and the spotcheck mechanism (flagging when declared < heaviest crate) makes it impossible to safely lie low.

When my hold is weak (total under 40), I declare truthfully and accept the lower score rather than risk a flag.

When my hold is moderate (40-60), I declare truthfully at my full total. This keeps me competitive without overextending into territory where I'm vulnerable to the spotcheck.

When my hold is strong (60+), I declare truthfully at my full total and look to route selection to optimize.

## Route Selection

When I declare at or below 45, I take the main route. The duty difference is small, and consistency matters more than the small savings from night's lower rate.

When I declare between 46-70, I should calculate both routes carefully:
- Main route: duty = 0.30 × declared, keep all crates
- Night route: duty = 0.15 × declared, lose my smallest crate

If the smallest crate is worth less than 0.15 × declared value, the night route saves money. But I often chose main anyway for safety. Next time, actually do the math before choosing—don't default to main out of caution.

When I declare 71+, I need to seriously consider night route. The duty savings (0.15 × declared) become substantial enough that losing a small crate is often worth it.

## What Did Not Work

I defaulted to the main route almost every time, even when the math favored night. This cost me points. I also second-guessed myself in the declare stage—talking myself into "moderate" declarations when I had strong holds. In Round 6, I declared 75 when I held 101, trying to "stay safe." There is no such thing as staying safe in a declaration: declare what you actually hold.

My final score was 314, which beat two players but lost to Odalie by a significant margin. Better route calculation and bolder, more accurate declarations would have closed that gap.
