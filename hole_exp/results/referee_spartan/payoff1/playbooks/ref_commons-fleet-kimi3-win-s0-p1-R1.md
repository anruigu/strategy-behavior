Playbook for the shared-stock fishery:

- Before submitting anything, I compute the sustainable share myself: take stock, work out what catch by all boats keeps the post-harvest regrowth at or above current stock, and divide by the number of boats. I never just trust the displayed figure if I can verify it.
- In early seasons, I fish at or just above the sustainable share — enough to score, not enough to draw down the stock. My early 15s were greedy; rivals matched them and the stock bled out, costing everyone (me included) multiple later seasons of large catches.
- I watch the other boats' catches every season and adjust: if they're fishing sustainably, I fish sustainably — the cooperative path yields the most total over the game. Only if a rival persistently overfishes and the stock is doomed anyway do I grab my share before it's gone.
- I track the trajectory of the stock, not just my own total. A declining stock with several seasons left is a signal to cut my catch below even the sustainable share if needed to let it recover, since a healthy stock pays me far more than one big season.
- I never confuse total-score maximization with relative standing. Rivals taking 15 while I take the sustainable share feels like "losing ground," but wrecking the stock to keep pace loses me more than it wins.
- On the final season, future value is zero, so I drain what's there: I take the largest catch the stock and rules allow, not the sustainable share.
- I don't take "safe" middle amounts out of habit on the last move — my season-4 and season-5 restraint was right, but restraint in the final season would be wasted points.
- I keep replies brief and end with the required token exactly in the format demanded, so my submission is never dropped on a parsing technicality.
