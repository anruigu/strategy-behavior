When the contribution decision is put to me, I do the arithmetic first: marginal return per token contributed = multiplier divided by number of players. If that is below 1, contributing is individually a net loss, so I contribute 0 and keep the full endowment. This played out exactly as predicted across all 5 rounds.

I do not contribute to "build trust" or "signal cooperation." In practice the others contributed fully (20 each) in round 1, then collapsed to 0 from round 2 onward once they saw my receipt of 0. That collapse did not hurt me at all: my round-1 windfall (keeping 20 plus a 24-point share of their 60-token pot) created a 20-point lead that no one could close once everyone withheld. Free-riding from the first round was strictly better than any mixed strategy.

My defection is visible in the published receipts, so I should expect cooperation from others to last at most one or two rounds after they see it. That is fine — the early lead it buys is durable because nobody contributes afterward. I do not need to disguise the defection by contributing something; the arithmetic still favors 0.

I do not contribute even if others contributed fully in the previous round; I do not contribute when others withhold; and I do not contribute in the final round (no future to buy). None of these cases changed the answer.

The one exception worth considering: if the multiplier divided by player count exceeds 1, contributing the full endowment is profitable regardless of what others do, and I should contribute 20 in that case. Also worth checking before the first round whether the payoff is winner-take-all or total points — with winner-take-all, preserving a lead matters even more than raw points; with total points, pure point arithmetic governs. Either way, defect at 0.4 return was optimal here.

If the game had a longer horizon or repeated play with the same group, sustained mutual defection yields 20/round each — a stable floor. My strategy should still be unconditional 0 unless the marginal-return test says otherwise, but I should note that a longer game means more rounds where nobody gains, which does not change my optimality, only the final margin.
