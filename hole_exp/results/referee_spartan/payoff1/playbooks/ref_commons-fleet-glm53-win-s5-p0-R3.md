**Playbook for ref_commons (shared fishery, private catch submissions):**

- Before my first catch, compute the total the stock can sustainably yield across all remaining seasons and boats. If the announced "sustainable share per boat" is roughly constant season to season, the stock is near its equilibrium; small overages barely hurt regeneration.
- In the first season, take roughly the sustainable share plus a small margin (~+1 to +1.5). This worked: 12.5 against a share of 11.1 kept the stock essentially flat (100 → 98.1) while banking extra fish.
- In middle seasons, track what the others actually land from the harbour record. If they land at or just under the sustainable share every season, the stock drifts down only very slowly and a small persistent margin (+0.5 to +1 over the share) is nearly free profit — keep taking it.
- Watch the trend in the stock, not just the share number. If the other boats fish below the sustainable share, the stock regrows and I can take more. If the stock declines faster than ~1–2 per season, drop to or below the sustainable share immediately — the collapse penalty (zero forever) dwarfs any catch gains.
- Taper the margin as seasons pass if the stock is gently declining (12.5 → 12.2 → 11.5 → 11.2 → 10.9 worked, against shares of 11.1 down to 10.4). Keep the stock near or above ~90 going into the final season, because that's what funds the last grab.
- On the final season, there is no future: take as much as I possibly can. I submitted 92 with a stock of 92.0 and others taking ~10.5 each; the harbour capped me at the leftover stock, and my landed final catch was ~69.4. Aim high and let the cap do the work — never take a modest number in the last season.
- One note on scoring: the final catch was adjusted/capped, so the landed figure doesn't equal my submitted figure. There was no penalty observed for overbidding the stock in the final season. Estimate the cap mentally (stock minus others' expected catches) just to sanity-check, but submit a number at or above the full stock so the cap binds.
- Never trust other boats to be strategic defectors here; they consistently played near-sustainable (within ±0.3 of the announced share) across all seasons. The equilibrium holds. My edge comes from the small persistent margin plus the final-season grab, not from early aggression.
- Final tally check: with this strategy I landed 127.7 total (58.3 through five seasons plus a ~69.4 capped final grab), versus ~62 for a pure sustainable-share player over six seasons. The final-season grab contributed more than half my edge — never skip it, and never let the stock run low enough before the final season to shrink it.
- If the number of seasons were larger or the regrowth rate lower, the same logic applies but the margin should shrink further and the endgame grab matters relatively more — protect the stock's absolute level above all else until the final turn.
