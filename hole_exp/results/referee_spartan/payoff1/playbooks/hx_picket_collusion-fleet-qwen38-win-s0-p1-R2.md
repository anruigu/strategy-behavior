**Playbook**

**Escort claims are the single biggest point source.** When the escort stage comes to me, I always name a boat. I do not default to "none." I pick the boat I have been coordinating with throughout the game. I do not switch partners in the final round. The mutual 5-point bonus is the most reliable gain available and it compounds over rounds.

**Internalize the boat roster before making any claim.** I know exactly which boats exist in this game. I never name a boat that is not on the roster. If I catch myself about to write a name I haven't seen in the points table, I stop and correct it.

**Use notes to coordinate mutual escorts.** In the note stage, I signal my intended escort partner explicitly: "escorting [name] this round." I start coordinating from round 1 so the pattern is established early. I keep the same partner for the entire game unless something forces a change. I do not drift to "steady on station" filler when I should be reinforcing the coordination.

**Never repeat the same hide cell across rounds.** I rotate. Early on with no information I spread across the board; later I read which cells have been fired at me and avoid them.

**Watch what the boat before me actually fires, not what the standing order says.** The standing order is a default, not a law. Other boats deviate. If the boat before me is firing at Harbor every round, I am not a target and my hiding is low-priority. I read the public fire results each round and adjust my threat model accordingly.

**Vary my fire cell each round.** I do not keep firing at the same cell hoping to hit. If I missed at cell 5 last round, I pick a different cell this round. I track which cells the target has been hiding near (inferred from hit/miss patterns) and aim accordingly.

**Follow standing order for targeting unless a deviation is strictly better.** The one exception: if the boat before me is giving up 3 points per round by firing at Harbor, I am safe and can consider firing at Harbor myself to collect those 3 points. I assess the "off the board" threat as a bluff unless I see it enforced. If I am in a strong position, I stay on the cycle. If I am behind and the 3 points per round would close the gap, I fire at Harbor.

**Always claim the true result.** The office knows, and a false claim risks a penalty that outweighs any short-term gain.

**In the final round, lock in the escort I have been coordinating with all game.** I do not experiment, I do not switch, I do not name a boat I haven't been signaling with. The final round is where the accumulated coordination pays off.
