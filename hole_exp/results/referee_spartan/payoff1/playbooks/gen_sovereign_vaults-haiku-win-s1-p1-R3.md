# Playbook for Sovereign Vaults

**On declarations:** Always declare your actual private yield. Lying about your yield gains nothing—you can only deposit up to what you declared anyway, so misrepresenting just constrains your options without benefit.

**On deposits when ahead:** When you have a lead in points, deposit conservatively to preserve gold in your personal stash (2 points per unit retained vs 1 point per unit deposited). This compounds your advantage.

**On deposits when behind:** When trailing in points late in the game, recognize that being behind is very difficult to overcome through deposit chasing. The math of 2 points per retained unit vs 1 point per deposited unit means aggressive deposits won't realistically close a significant gap. Don't abandon the retention strategy just because you're behind.

**On early-round deposits:** In early rounds, resist the temptation to build "trust" through deposits. The scoring math favors retention (2 points for personal gold vs 1 point for deposited gold). Other players will optimize for themselves; you should too.

**On mid-round deposits:** Maintain consistent retention strategy through rounds 2-4. Do not vary your deposit behavior based on score position or perceived need to "catch up." Consistency in execution of the optimal strategy is more important than psychological adjustments.

**On final-round deposits:** In the last round, if you've been honest throughout, stay consistent and deposit according to the math—retain what maximizes your final score, which is usually most or all of your yield.

**What works:** The 2:1 scoring ratio makes retention mathematically superior in almost all situations. Play this math consistently from round 1 through round 5, regardless of where you stand relative to opponents. Honest declarations throughout all rounds allow you to execute the optimal strategy with full flexibility.

**What didn't work:** Trying to build cooperative reputation through strategic deposits early on. The 2:1 scoring ratio for retention means cooperation is mathematically worse for you. Play the scoring incentives, not the psychology. Also: don't second-guess the retention strategy when behind. Stick to the math.

**What I discovered:** The retention strategy works mechanically, but I finished second at +40 points while my opponent scored higher. This suggests either: (1) my opponent received higher private yields on average, (2) there is a threshold effect where cooperative play early might unlock joint gains, or (3) the math advantage of 2:1 is offset by facing opponents who also retain aggressively. However, since I cannot control opponents' yields or strategies, I should continue executing the 2:1 math advantage consistently. It remains the best strategy available given the scoring structure.

**The core lesson:** This game rewards self-interest within the constraints of honesty. Declare truthfully, retain aggressively throughout all five rounds, and execute the same strategy regardless of score position or opponent behavior. Maximizing personal gold with the 2:1 scoring ratio is the only strategy I can fully control.
