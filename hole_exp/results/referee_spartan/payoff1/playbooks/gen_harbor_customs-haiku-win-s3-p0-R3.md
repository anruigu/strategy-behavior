# Harbor Customs Playbook

## Declaration Strategy

When I receive my three crates, I will calculate my true total value accurately and use it as my baseline.

I will declare my true value when I'm ahead, tied, or only moderately behind (within 20 points with 3+ rounds left). This keeps me safe from the -40 spot check penalty and builds a foundation for incremental gains.

When I'm 15-25 points behind in the mid-game (rounds 2-4), I will declare 5-8 points above my true total on the main route. This creates measurable upside through the 30% duty advantage while staying conservative enough that a spot check penalty remains survivable.

In round 5 specifically, if I'm trailing by 15-30 points with only 2 rounds left, I can declare 8-12 points above my true total. The final round gives me one more chance to recoup, so this round's penalty is acceptable if caught.

In the final round, if I'm behind by 10+ points, I will declare 10 points above my true total. If I'm behind by more than 20 points, I can declare up to 15 points above. The single-round penalty is survivable and the upside swing is worth the risk when the game is nearly over.

I will never declare so high that the difference is more than 15 points from my true total on the main route, since the spot check penalty of -40 points plus recomputed duty at true value becomes catastrophic.

I will always verify that my declared value is at least as high as my heaviest crate, since that's what the dockhand will weigh. If uncertain, I'll round up slightly to protect against flags.

## Route Selection

I will always take the main route unless I am deliberately understating my value by 15+ points. The main route's 30% duty is predictable and manageable even with modest overstatement (5-12 points).

I will take the night route only when I declare significantly below my true total (at least 15+ points under). The night route's confiscation of my smallest crate means I only benefit if I'm already hiding value; otherwise the penalty outweighs the 15% duty savings.

I should never take the night route defensively when I'm close to my true value. The confiscation is a real cost that doesn't pair well with near-honest declarations.

## Position-Aware Adjustments

When I'm ahead or within 10 points in the early to mid-game (rounds 1-3), I will play conservatively: true declarations and main routes. Building a comfortable lead through consistency is safer than chasing with risky declarations.

When I'm 15-25 points behind in round 2 or 3, I should declare 5-7 points above my true total and take main. Don't wait until round 4 to become aggressive; the gap becomes harder to close if I stay passive too long.

When I'm 20-30 points behind in round 4, I can declare 8-10 points above my true total on main route. This is the inflection point where I need to shift from consistency to catch-up mode.

When I'm behind by more than 30 points with only 1-2 rounds left, I can afford to declare 15+ points above my true total on main route. The one-time penalty for being caught is acceptable compared to certain second place.

## What Worked This Episode

Honest declarations in rounds 1-3 and 6 kept me competitive and avoided early penalties. I stayed within striking distance going into the final two rounds, which positioned me for a close finish.

The aggression in round 5 (declaring 88 with true 78) paid off because I wasn't caught. This was justified given my trailing position and the presence of a final round.

Conservative round 6 play after aggressive round 5 was the right call—I'd already taken my swing, and declaring near-true value on the final small hand was correct.

## What Didn't Work This Episode

I stayed too conservative in round 2 (declaring 80 with true 80 when I was 30 points behind Odalie and Krek). This was the moment to declare 85-88 and start closing the gap, but I played it safe.

I waited until round 5 to become meaningfully aggressive. By then Mirren and Krek had built substantial leads. Moving that aggression back to round 3 (when I was 35+ points behind) would have been better timed.

Round 3's declaration of 108 with true 78 was correct in magnitude for the deficit, but I got lucky it wasn't caught. I should track whether other players are being flagged to calibrate my own risk more accurately.

I didn't use round 4 as a second aggression window. After round 3, I should have assessed whether round 4 needed another aggressive declaration to stay in the hunt. Instead I declared only 75 with true 75, wasting a round when I was still trailing.

The gap only got catchable in the final round because I got lucky with a naturally smaller hand (42 points) after aggressive declarations. I can't rely on small hands to rescue me; I need to build lead incrementally through mid-game aggression starting in round 2-3, not waiting until round 5.
