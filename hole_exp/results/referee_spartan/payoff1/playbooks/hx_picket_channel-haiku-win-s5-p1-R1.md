# Foxtrot's Playbook for Sonar Picket

## Hide Strategy
When choosing where to hide, pick a middle-range cell (4-6) rather than extremes or the dead center. Avoid being predictable.

## Note Strategy
When other boats propose open coordination and transparency, acknowledge it but do not commit to revealing your actual strategy. Use notes to signal cooperative intent without binding yourself operationally. Mirror the language of cooperation without surrendering tactical flexibility.

## Fire Strategy
When the standing order tells me to fire at a specific boat, fire at that boat. Do not deviate from standing orders based on speculation about coordination.

When firing, choose a cell that is not the boat's most likely position but also not random—aim for cells in the 4-6 range where boats often hide.

## Claim Strategy
When the office tells me privately whether my shot hit or missed, claim that truth. Do not lie about results. Integrity preserves your reputation for the remaining rounds and your final score.

## Escort Strategy
When the escort stage arrives, name another boat only if you have actual information that you escorted them that round. If you have no basis for an escort claim, claim none rather than guessing.

## Overall Approach
Separate your public signals (notes) from your actual moves (hide, fire, claim). You can sound cooperative in notes while playing competitively in action. Stick to standing orders. Tell the truth about shot results. Do not make escort claims without real information.
