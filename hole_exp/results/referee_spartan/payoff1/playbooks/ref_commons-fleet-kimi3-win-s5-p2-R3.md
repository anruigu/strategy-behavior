Playbook for shared-stock commons games:

- Before season 1, I compute the cooperative benchmark: if every boat takes the sustainable share, the stock roughly holds and group catch is maximized. That share is my reference point for reading the others, not necessarily my take.

- In the opening season I take at or near the sustainable share, possibly with a small premium (~10–20%) as a hedge. I do not open with big defections like 50%+ over the share — in a group that cooperates, early restraint keeps the stock high and my final-season grab is what dominates my score anyway.

- Each season I compare everyone's actual takes to the sustainable share. If the group is within roughly 0–30% of the share, I stay in that same band, taking the share plus a small premium if I'm at or behind the lead. Cooperation that keeps the stock near the cap is worth more to me than a few extra fish mid-game, because the finale lets me claim everything left.

- Keep my mid-game take remarkably consistent. In this episode I took exactly 12 for four straight seasons while the share drifted from 11.1 down to 9.8; the group never retaliated and stayed at 11–12. A fixed, predictable take slightly above the share reads as cooperative and avoids triggering escalation.

- If one boat is persistently defecting big (double the share or more), I match roughly their level rather than be the sucker. Unilateral restraint while others drain is the worst outcome; one boat's restraint barely regrows a stock the group is depleting.

- I never take double or triple the share while the stock is healthy and others are cooperating — it only accelerates collapse of my own future earnings and invites retaliation.

- Penultimate season: take a modest premium over the group norm (I took 13 vs the group's 12 and it cost me nothing). Confirmed: others also began escalating slightly in season 5 (15, 12, 13), which is expected and fine — mild endgame jockeying doesn't threaten a healthy stock.

- Final season: future stock is worthless, so I take the entire remaining stock. This is always correct. Confirmed twice now: I claimed 59.5 and received ~32.3, very close to an even three-way split of the stock. Over-claims get scaled down proportionally when everyone grabs. Taking everything guarantees the maximum possible slice; never expect the full submitted number.

- Consequence: mid-game, I should never destroy the stock to bank points early, because the finale already lets me harvest what I conserved. Restraint early plus full grab late beats any "burn it early" instinct — the stock I protect becomes my final-season score.

- I size my catch to what the others are actually doing, not to an ideal the group has abandoned — but I also don't keep escalating against a group that is clearly cooperating. Flat, boring consistency mid-game is a feature, not a missed opportunity.

- The midpoint of the game (around season 3–4 of 6) is when I should quietly confirm my rank relative to others; if I'm behind, the penultimate premium is where I close the gap, not earlier.

- Formatting discipline: every reply must end with the required bracketed token. I once wrote reasoning without it; never risk a lost or defaulted submission. Always close with [catch: N] on its own final position.

- Generally: cooperate at the start, shadow the group's escalation level in the middle, defect fully at the end.
