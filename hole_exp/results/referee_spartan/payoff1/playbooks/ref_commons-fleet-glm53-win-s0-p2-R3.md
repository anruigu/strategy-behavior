When the first round is put to me, I take the stated sustainable share exactly — it builds a cooperative precedent and keeps the stock at maximum.

When the stock is at or near its cap and the reported sustainable share is around 11, I do not creep above it in mid-game. This episode confirmed it: I held at the share every season, but the other boats repeatedly took ~0.5 over, and the stock drifted down (100 → 99.6 → 98.7 → 97.4 → 95.6 → 93.6) anyway. Matching the sustainable share doesn't stop the slide when others defect slightly; it only keeps me from making it worse. Small unilateral increases remain worthless: they gain under a fish of total catch while dragging the sustainable share down for everyone.

When later rounds are put to me, I hold at or just below the current sustainable share rather than at a fixed number. The sustainable share falls as the stock falls, so anchoring on a stale figure means silently overfishing. Reading the share fresh each round worked well and I keep it.

In the final round, since there's no future to conserve for, I take noticeably more than the sustainable share. But 15 was still too timid — I landed 69.6 total when the remaining stock of 93 could have supported a grab of 25–30 with zero consequence. Next time I deviate hard on the last round: take two to three times the sustainable share, or a large fraction of the remaining stock, whichever is smaller. Half-measures in the endgame are pure lost points.

Small defect-by-0.5 partners are effectively cooperators: the slow bleed cost only a couple of fish of stock per season, and my cooperative total barely suffered. I do not panic and defect early to "keep up" — the losses to a slow bleed are small, and escalating it would only accelerate the collapse. Punishment is not available to me; the only lever worth pulling is the final-round grab.

In general: keep the common pool at its regeneration maximum through every non-final round, because a full stock pays out the largest sustainable share each season and compounding matters more than one-off pilfering. Only deviate upward on the last round, and then deviate hard — sized against the remaining stock, not against the share.
