My playbook for ESTATE:

- Final score counts declared cash PLUS purchase price of deeds held, so every unowned property I buy is net-neutral immediately (cash -price, assets +price) and becomes pure profit from rent collected thereafter. When I land on an unowned property, I buy it unless it would leave me unable to pay plausible rent (~50+ buffer for a 6-lap game).

- Buy early, buy aggressively on the first two laps. Rent only accrues to me if I own the deed before others land on it, and later purchases recover less rent before the game ends. Skipping a purchase to hoard cash earns nothing.

- Compute yield as rent/price. Anything at ~20% or better (like 12 rent on 60, or 10 on 50) is an automatic buy mid-priced or cheap; even lower-yield tiles are worth buying early because assets count at full purchase price.

- Track my cash carefully each turn: add salary (25) when passing START, add any rents received from others as noted in the book, subtract rent paid. Always state balance accurately — mistakes in my own books directly cost score.

- Rent I pay to others is dead loss; rent they pay me is pure gain. Prioritize owning tiles others are likely to hit — with 3 players on an 8-tile circuit, every tile gets hit often, so any ownership is good.

- Never treat cash alone as the score. A player with high cash and no deeds loses to a player with deeds — don't pass on purchases to "stay liquid" beyond the safety buffer.

- On the final lap, if I land on an unowned property, still buy it: the purchase price comes straight back in the asset count, so there's no downside as long as I can cover any rent still due that lap.
