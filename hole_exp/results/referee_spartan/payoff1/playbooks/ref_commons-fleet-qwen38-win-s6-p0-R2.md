# Playbook: Ref Commons (Fishery)

**When I'm shown the stock and sustainable share, I note both and count which season it is out of how many total.**

**In the final season, I claim as much of the stock as I can.** The stock's health is worthless after the last catch, and if total claims exceed the stock, allocation is proportional to each boat's claim. This means my landing is (my claim / total claims) × stock. Claiming more always weakly increases my share, so I should claim a large fraction of the stock — at least 60–80% of it, or more. In practice, claim roughly 2× the equal three-way split. I made the mistake of only claiming 40% last time and likely left a large pro-rata share on the table.

**In the second-to-last season, I catch about 20–25% above the sustainable share.** I know the final season will be a free-for-all that drains the stock, so I front-load my extra catch here while the stock is still high enough to support it.

**In all other seasons, I aim for roughly 10–15% above the sustainable share.** Sticking to exactly the sustainable share leaves money on the table. The stock can absorb a modest surplus for several seasons without collapsing. In early seasons when the stock is near maximum, I can lean toward the upper end (15%) because there's more buffer.

**When I see what other boats caught last season, I calibrate.** If they're consistently at or above the level I'm taking, I match or slightly exceed them. If they're below me and the stock is holding, I hold my level. I never drop below the sustainable share while others are catching at or above it — falling behind early is hard to recover.

**I watch the stock trajectory, not just the current level.** If the stock is declining more than about 2–3 points per season, I pull back toward the sustainable share for one or two seasons to let it stabilize. If the stock is stable or growing, I maintain my slightly-above-sustainable target.

**I never let the stock drop below roughly 50% of the maximum.** Below that, the regrowth is too slow to recover, and a single bad season (or an aggressive rival) can wipe it out. If I'm approaching that threshold, I cut to the sustainable share or below.

**The core mistake to avoid: under-claiming in the endgame.** The final season is a one-shot extraction problem where claiming more always increases your expected landing under pro-ration. Holding back is pure loss.
