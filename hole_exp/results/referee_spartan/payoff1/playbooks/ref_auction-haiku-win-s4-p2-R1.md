# Playbook for Ref Auction

**On valuation and bidding basics:**
When a lot's value to me exceeds the standing bid by a significant margin, I bid to take control rather than pass. The gap between estimate and true value is my information advantage—use it.

When no one has bid yet, open at minimum to gather information cheaply and establish position without waste.

When I'm in a strong position on a lot (high value, low bid), bid the minimum raise to preserve budget while staying competitive. Don't jump aggressively when you don't need to.

**On budget management:**
Track my remaining budget carefully after each winning bid. I started with 30 but ended with significant leftover funds—this suggests I was too conservative on later lots.

Don't let remaining budget make me timid. If I have budget left and a lot offers positive value, I should spend it. Unspent budget at the end of the game creates no value.

**On competitive dynamics:**
When another bidder is accumulating multiple wins and building advantage, don't cede lots I can profitably win just to preserve budget for later. Lot 4 is an example—I should have competed harder there, not held back.

In the final round of the final lot, bid if the value justifies it. There's no future round to save for. I bid lot 5 round 2 correctly on this principle.

**On valuation edge:**
I only see my own values, not others'. When a lot is worth more to me than the estimate and bidding is light, assume I have an edge and pursue it. Don't second-guess good value.

When a lot's value to me is below estimate (like lot 2 at value 23, estimate 25), be prepared to pass or bid minimally if competition emerges. This lot went to bidder 0 anyway, so my minimal bid was correct.

**On scoring discipline:**
My final score of +30 came primarily from one lot (lot 1, won at 24 against value 54). I should have found more lots like this—high-value opportunities where the standing bid is low. Look for these asymmetries and commit to winning them.

Don't treat the game as just "not losing money." Seek positive spread opportunities and bid to capture them.
