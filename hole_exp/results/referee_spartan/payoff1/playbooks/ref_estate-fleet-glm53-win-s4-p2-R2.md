Playbook for ESTATE:

- Scoring is cash plus purchase price of deeds held, so buying property is roughly cash-neutral in scoring terms but earns rent on top. Property is the better use of cash whenever I can afford it without going broke.
- Buying is even more important than I first thought: with no property at all I paid rent every single turn and finished far below the buyers, who converted cash into deeds AND collected rent from me. If I stay propertyless, I bleed steadily.
- When I land on an unowned tile, buy it unless the price would leave me too little cash to cover likely rents (roughly one lap's worth, ~25-40) before my next salary. Given rent drains fast, lean toward buying even with a thinner cushion.
- Early laps matter most: opponents bought up Wharf, Foundry, Terrace, Mill and Depot within the first three laps, leaving nothing for me. On the first pass around the board, grab any tile I land on if I can possibly afford it.
- Rent is a constant outgoing, not an occasional one: over 6 turns I paid 90 total in rent and collected salary only once (small rolls meant few START passes). Budget on rent nearly every turn, and don't count on multiple salaries — with a short board, expect roughly one salary per lap at best, possibly fewer.
- On the final laps, drop the cash cushion entirely: a last-lap buy converts cash 1:1 into deed value plus any incoming rent, so buy on lap 5-6 even if it leaves me near zero.
- If rent exceeds my cash, pay my remaining cash and declare zero.
- Always include all three tokens exactly, with balance updated correctly (subtract rent and purchase price, add 25 salary when passing START).
- Read the bank's book each turn to track who owns what and update my rent-exposure estimate; prefer high-rent tiles (Exchange, Foundry, Wharf) when choosing, and note which tiles remain unowned so I know my chances of a future buy.
