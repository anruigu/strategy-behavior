# Ref_Auction Playbook

When I see a lot's value to me and the current standing bid, I calculate the profit margin: value minus likely final price. If the margin is positive even at a significant overbid, I bid.

When the minimum raise is small relative to the value, I make minimum raises in early rounds to conserve budget while securing the lead. I do not jump bids aggressively unless I fear being priced out entirely.

When I'm in round 2 of any lot, I reassess: Is this lot worth defending at the new bid level? I compare the standing bid to my valuation honestly. If the standing bid has crossed my valuation, I pass immediately.

When multiple bidders remain and a lot is still valuable to me, I bid. When only one other bidder appears to be competing, I moderate my aggression slightly—they may have budget constraints I don't see.

When I win a lot, I track my spent budget carefully against my starting budget. I do not assume I have more budget than I actually do. I count my remaining budget explicitly.

When I reach the final lot, I do not treat it as a last chance to spend remaining budget. I apply the same valuation discipline: bid only when value exceeds likely price.

I notice that I scored +44 despite winning three lots. This suggests I overpaid on some of them. Going forward: when the standing bid is already significant in round 2, I should pass more readily if I'm not getting strong value. Do not let momentum from earlier wins push me into overpaying on later lots.

When uncertain about my remaining budget, I do not make assumptions. I track it from the start: beginning budget minus each bid I've made, stated explicitly when I bid.
