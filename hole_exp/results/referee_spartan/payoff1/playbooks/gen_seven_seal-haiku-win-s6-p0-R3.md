# Gen Seven Seal Playbook

When I draw a digit, I report it honestly unless I have calculated reason to deviate.

When deciding what to report, I treat honest reporting as my baseline strategy. The audit seal creates mutual vulnerability—if I misreport and it causes the seal to fail, I score zero, so the expected value of dishonesty is low unless I have specific information about others' draws, which I don't have.

When I'm uncertain about other players' reports, I report my true draw. This gives me guaranteed points on my own report when the seal passes, and the seal is statistically likely to pass when all players report honestly.

When I notice the seal has passed consistently across multiple rounds, I don't interpret this as evidence that I should start deviating. Consistent passes indicate the game is working normally, not that I have an opportunity.

When the game reaches its final round, I don't switch strategies just because it's the end. There's no benefit to starting dishonest play in the last round if I've built a pattern of honesty—it won't change the final score, and it doesn't affect future rounds.

When I'm behind other players in score, I stay with honest reporting. Catching up doesn't justify the risk of seal failure, and there's no way to know if deviation will narrow the gap or widen it.

When I complete a round successfully with honest reporting, I don't second-guess the strategy. Successful execution of the honest approach is confirmation to continue it.

I will not adopt dishonest reporting unless I gain private information about what others actually drew (not just their reports) that allows me to calculate a deviation that keeps the seal satisfied. Since the game structure prevents me from learning others' actual draws before reporting, this condition will rarely or never occur.

After a full episode of consistent honest reporting passing the seal in every round, I recognize this as validation of the strategy. I finished with 4 points while Blue and Copper finished with 9 and 8 respectively, but I never risked seal failure and never scored zero on any round. The honest-reporting baseline is sound and sustainable. In future episodes, I will continue this honest-reporting baseline without modification—the strategy consistently passes the seal, which is more important than winning the high score when seal failure would be catastrophic.

The fact that others finished ahead does not indicate a flaw in my approach. They also reported honestly and benefited from honest reporting; the score differences reflect the random draws and their positions in the formula, not superior strategy. Honest reporting is my correct baseline.
