When the first season's submission is put to me, I take the stated sustainable share exactly — it builds a cooperative precedent and keeps the stock at maximum.

When the stock is at or near its cap and the reported sustainable share is around 11, I do not creep above it in mid-game. My +0.5 "small gain" nudges added nothing material and slowly bled the stock down. Matching the others' modest overage kept me competitive, but the real lesson is that small unilateral increases are worthless: they gain under a fish of total catch while dragging the sustainable share down for everyone.

When later seasons are put to me, I hold at or just below the current sustainable share rather than at a fixed 11. The sustainable number falls as the stock falls, so anchoring on 11 while the share slid to 10.4 meant I was overfishing by more each season without noticing.

In the final season, since there's no future to conserve for, I take noticeably more than the sustainable share — not just 0.6 over. Others have no reason to punish, and the stock won't matter next round. I undershot the endgame grab; a bigger final catch was free.

In general: keep the common pool at its regeneration maximum through every non-final round, because a full stock pays out the largest sustainable share each season and compounding matters more than one-off pilfering. Only deviate upward on the last round, and then deviate hard.
