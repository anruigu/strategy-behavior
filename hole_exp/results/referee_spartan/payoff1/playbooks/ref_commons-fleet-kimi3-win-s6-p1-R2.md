When the game opens with a regenerating shared resource, I commit to harvesting at or just above the sustainable share — the regrowth math makes conservation the dominant strategy for total score, not a moral choice. A small premium (1–2 fish over the share) from season 1 is safe and free value when the stock is at or near cap.

Before each submission I do the quick arithmetic: current stock, post-harvest regrowth, seasons remaining. I never take an amount that, combined with plausible rival takes, would push the stock toward zero — collapse is the only outcome that ruins me regardless of my relative position.

When I'm behind on total score, I increase my take modestly (roughly 1.2–1.5x the sustainable share), not aggressively. I size my aggression to what the stock can survive even if others grab too.

When I'm ahead, I defend by fishing exactly at the sustainable share or slightly below — there's no reason to risk collapse to widen a lead, and restraint pulls rivals toward restraint too.

I watch the landed history for each rival's pattern. Rivals stable at the sustainable share tell me the stock will hold, so I can safely take a small premium every mid-game season. If a rival spikes hard, I don't match — I wait for them to settle before pressing my own premium.

Through the middle seasons I bank a consistent small premium (share plus 2–4) rather than alternating restraint and spikes — against cooperative rivals this accumulates a lead without ever testing the stock.

I go heavy only when the endgame makes regrowth irrelevant: in the final season there is no future to protect, so I take a large share of what's left. In a clear final season with a known history of moderate rivals, I should estimate their likely takes from recent seasons and take close to (stock − expected rival takes), not a conservative round number.
