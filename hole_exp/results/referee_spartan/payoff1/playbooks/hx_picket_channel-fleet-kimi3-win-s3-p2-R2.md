When I hide, I pick a cell away from where my attacker fired last round; their miss tells me which number they aimed at. I favor mid-range cells (4–7) but rotate within that band and avoid repeating exact cells, edges, or the center often enough to become readable.

When the note stage comes, I post a short note that ACKs prior stations by tag, states my intent for the round, and commits to a verifiable pattern (source+round relays, verify tag). Credibility compounds; the trust built in notes is what makes later coordination execute.

When a sync or coordination plan is proposed, I ACK explicitly, state my conditions for executing, and hold until all stations give final confirmation — then execute fully when the condition is met. Conditional language like "execute only on all-station final confirmation" is good, but I must not announce one thing and do another in the last stage.

When firing, I follow the standing-order target (next station round the picket) unless an agreed sync overrides it. Obedient, varied fire keeps me trusted; unilateral off-plan fire without coordination earns little and costs credibility.

In the final round I reassess raids explicitly: firing on Harbor can be rational when retaliation cannot outlast the patrol and the guaranteed transfer beats likely escort income, but I only do it if it was clearly signaled or does not betray a pact I need others to honor.

When claiming a result, I always file it truthfully — it is required, and truthful claims underwrite the sync trust that pays off later.

When escorting, I use early notes to lock at least one explicit mutual pact by name and tag, then I always follow through. Filing "none" while others pair off leaves repeated 5-point mutual payments on the table; hoping for unannounced reciprocity in the last round is weaker than a locked pact.

My escort claim must match my notes: if I said I file "none" absent an ACK, I do not improvise a surprise escort at the end unless I explicitly announced the switch. Consistency between note, claim, and salvage is part of the payoff.

When someone threatens retaliation (Harbor's jettison warning), I never fire on that station before the final reckoning; the safe path plus escort points usually beats the raid unless endgame math clearly flips it.

When ahead late in the patrol, I keep hide cells unpredictable against the one boat ordered to fire on me, track each attacker's aim history and stated cell rules, and dodge both their last miss and their announced "varied 4–7" habits by occasionally stepping outside the band they expect.
