# Harbor Customs Playbook

**Declaration Strategy:**

When declaring, always declare at least the value of your heaviest crate to avoid the -40 penalty. The penalty is so severe that honesty or near-honesty is safer than aggressive understatement.

When I have a total hold value, I will declare it truthfully or very close to it. The -40 flag penalty makes dishonesty costly and unreliable.

In the final round especially, maintain the honest declaration strategy. A conservative declaration on a small hold (like 41 on 5+12+24) is perfectly valid and avoids all risk.

**Route Selection:**

When I've declared honestly and am in a competitive position, take the main route. The main route gives me 70% of my declared value as net gain (after duty), while night route sacrifices my smallest crate to save duty costs—only worthwhile if my smallest crate is worth significantly less than 15% of my declared value.

Calculate before deciding: main route nets (declared × 0.70), night route nets (total - smallest - floor(declared × 0.15)). Take whichever is higher.

Don't overthink route selection. Stick to the math. When in doubt, main route is the safer baseline choice for honest declarations.

**Positional Awareness:**

When I'm ahead in score or tied, prioritize consistency over risk. Don't try to extend a lead with aggressive declarations that could backfire.

When I'm behind, I can afford slightly riskier declarations, but still stay within the range I see others using—declarations seem to cluster between 45-85, and outliers are rare.

In the final round, if tied or slightly behind, an honest declaration that positions me for a reliable route choice is better than chasing a risky gamble. The game rewards consistency over heroics.

**Pattern Reading:**

Track what declarations other players make across rounds. Most players seem to declare in the 50-85 range, with main route being the predominant choice when players have solid holds.

Outlier declarations (very high or very low) are rare enough that I shouldn't assume others will make them. Play to the median.

Don't try to read opponents' hold compositions from their declarations—there's too much noise. Focus on the math of my own position.
