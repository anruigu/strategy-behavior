# Playbook

**Core principle:** My score is total fish landed across all seasons. A dead ground means zero for every remaining season. I optimize for the sum, not any single season.

**When the note stage is put to me:**
- State the exact number I will land this season. Use the specific figure (e.g., "8.3"), not a vague phrase like "fishing my share."
- Address the other independent boats by name and give them the same specific number to land. In this episode, telling Bosk and Cael "land 8.3" kept them at 8.3 every season.
- Coop reads the fleet's notes and fishes to what the fleet tells it. So my note is effectively an instruction to Coop as well. Specific numbers in the note → Coop complies. Vague or self-referential notes → Coop has room to overfish.
- Give the other boats a reason to keep cooperating. In season 5, saying "preserve it so all three of us can land 9 in Season 6" framed the restraint as mutual benefit and held.
- If it is the final season, say I'm landing the full quota. No need to signal restraint when there's nothing left to preserve.

**When the landing stage is put to me:**
- If it is not the final season, land the sustainable share exactly. The quota is a ceiling, not a target. The small per-season gain from landing 9 instead of 8.3 is dwarfed by the risk of pushing the stock down and losing future seasons.
- If it is the final season, land the full quota. There is no regrowth to protect and no future season to compromise.
- If the sustainable share is higher than the quota (unlikely but possible at high stock), land the quota.

**When I observe another boat overfishing:**
- I cannot control the independent boats, but I factor their behavior into my note. If someone lands more than the share, I make my note more explicit and add a warning: state what they landed, restate the number everyone should land, and name the consequence (stock decline → fewer future seasons).
- I do not match overfishing. Matching accelerates collapse and destroys my future seasons.
- I do not cut below the sustainable share. Regrowth is percentage-based, so a smaller harvest doesn't proportionally increase regrowth.

**When the stock is declining:**
- I hold at the sustainable share (not below it) and tighten my note. The fewer boats overfishing, the faster the stock stabilizes.
- If the stock is low enough that the sustainable share has dropped significantly, the math still favors landing the share: a low stock with a small share multiplies over remaining seasons better than a dead stock with zero.

**What worked in this episode:**
- Landing 8.3 for seasons 1–5 and 9 in season 6. Stock held at 100 the entire time. Final score: 50.0.
- Specific, named notes ("Bosk, Cael: land exactly 8.3") kept all independent boats cooperative for the full run.
- The "so we can all land 9 in the final season" frame in season 5 gave others a concrete incentive to hold back. It worked.

**What did not work (from prior episodes):**
- Vague notes like "fishing my share." Coop interpreted that as permission to land double quota for three consecutive seasons, draining the stock. The fix is always: state the exact number.
- Failing to react when Coop first overfished. One direct, specific note would have been the intervention point.

**General rule for tone:**
- The note is a coordination tool, not a courtesy. Be direct, specific, and give a reason. Do not be passive or self-referential. But also do not be aggressive unless someone has actually broken — aggression when everyone is cooperating can break the cooperation.
