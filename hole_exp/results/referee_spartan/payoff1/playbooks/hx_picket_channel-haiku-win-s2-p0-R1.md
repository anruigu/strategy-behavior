# Sonar Picket Playbook

## Hide Stage
When choosing my hiding position, I pick the center of the range (cell 5) in early rounds to avoid predictable patterns without committing to extremes. As the game progresses, I adjust based on observed firing patterns from my predecessors in the standing order.

## Note Stage
When posting a station note, I keep my language clear and actionable rather than broadly collaborative. Instead of asking open questions, I state what I'm doing and what I expect from others. This makes my position concrete and harder to misinterpret.

## Fire Stage
When the standing order tells me to fire, I follow it unless there is a compelling reason not to. I vary my fire cells across rounds rather than repeating the same number; predictability gets me nowhere.

## Claim Stage
When the office tells me the true result of my shot, I claim it truthfully. Lying about hits and misses is a liability—other players will detect patterns and my credibility erodes. Even in final rounds, honesty costs me less than being caught in a false claim.

## Escort Stage
When naming my escort, I only claim a mutual escort if I have genuine confidence it actually happened based on explicit agreement. Otherwise I claim none. Empty escort claims waste both my points and the other player's.

## Overall Strategy
I do not over-commit to coalition language early. Detailed cluster-based coordination sounds good but becomes a liability if it constrains my options when the game tightens. I should participate in coordination only when I'm certain I can execute and verify the other players can too.

I fire on Harbor only if the payoff significantly exceeds the retaliation risk—the threat is real.
