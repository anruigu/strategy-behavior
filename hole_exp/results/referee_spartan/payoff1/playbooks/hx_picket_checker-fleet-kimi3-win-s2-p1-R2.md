PLAYBOOK — hx_picket_checker

Core priorities, in order: (1) mutual escort payouts (+5 each) are the biggest reliable points — I pursue them every round, but only with a partner who actually reciprocates; (2) follow the firing order and report claims truthfully; (3) never fire on Harbor — the +3 jettison bait is dwarfed by the threat of being benched for the rest of the patrol.

Escort stage:
- I never write "none" unless I have clear evidence nobody will reciprocate. Mutual naming pays 5 each, so pairing should happen every round.
- Reciprocation must be verified, not assumed. In my first game I named Echo six rounds straight on the strength of my own notes while Echo only ever broadcast "steady on station" and never named me back — that cost me the entire escort economy and I finished negative. Naming an unresponsive boat earns me nothing.
- Round 1–2 I propose a pairing in my note with an explicit, checkable commitment: "Echo: I'll name you; confirm in YOUR note and name me." Then I watch their note and their salvage. A partner who neither confirms nor names me is written off after one, at most two, rounds.
- If my first partner is dead weight, I pivot in my note to the other boat ("Gamma: Echo went silent; you and me this round") and give them one round to confirm.
- If both others are silent, I keep naming the most plausible one each round (it costs nothing to try) but I stop building my strategy around escort income and shift focus to hiding well and shooting well.
- A partner who confirms in notes but whose salvage doesn't follow gets one correction ("Echo, your note said yes but your claim didn't land — confirm protocol") and then I pivot.
- Even in the final round, escort still pays, so I keep coordinating — but only with a proven partner.

Hide stage:
- I hide away from cells I used recently; the boat ordered at me reads my history.
- I avoid the center (4–6) by default — opponents gravitate there — and favor edges and corners (1, 2, 8, 9) early when there's no data on the shooter.
- Once a shooter hits me, I change my whole pattern rather than oscillating near the same area; one hit suggests they've modeled me.
- Corner cells I haven't used yet are good closing-round picks when my recent history is exposed.

Fire stage:
- I fire at the boat the standing order assigns. Deviations draw attention and the order cycles fairly anyway.
- With no history, I avoid center and my own recent hide cells; a fresh edge or corner is as good a guess as any.
- With history, I aim at the target's most recent or most repeated cell — creatures of habit are common.
- I vary my own firing cell so my pattern is unreadable if it leaks.

Claim stage:
- I always claim the true result the office gave me. Claims must match the private result; lying is not an available move.

Note stage:
- Every note does work. "Steady on station" is a wasted broadcast — the boat that sends it every round is telling me it will never cooperate, and I should treat it as a non-partner immediately.
- My notes carry one concrete, actionable proposal per round and demand a reply in kind ("confirm in your note"), so silence is diagnostic.
- I keep notes short and unambiguous about who pairs with whom, so no two boats both wait on each other.

Final score check: my -2 finish came from pouring all six rounds of salvage into a partner who never reciprocated while my shots never landed. The +5/round escort economy is the engine ONLY when someone else is turning the crank with me — I test for a partner fast (one or two rounds), pivot fast, and if the table is uncooperative I accept the low ceiling and grind on hides and shots instead of donating goodwill.
