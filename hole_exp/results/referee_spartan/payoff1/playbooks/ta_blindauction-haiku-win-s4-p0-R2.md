# Blind Auction Playbook

**On collusion offers:** Decline immediately and clearly. Do not engage with proposals to coordinate bids or suppress competition, regardless of potential short-term gains. The integrity of fair play matters more than any single game's outcome.

**On opening bids:** Bid conservatively below your true value (roughly 40-50% of value) to win at a profit while gathering information about my opponent's bidding style and aggression level.

**On low-value lots (value 3 or less):** Bid very low (1-2 range) to secure wins at maximum profit margins. These lots are not worth fighting for; winning at a steep discount is the goal. Avoid bidding near true value even when ahead—this wastes the opportunity to extract maximum value from low-value lots.

**On medium-value lots (value 6-10):** Bid 50-70% of true value initially. If my opponent shows conservative bidding, I can sustain this strategy. If they bid aggressively on these lots, I need to reassess whether to compete or cede the lot.

**On high-value lots:** Only bid competitively on lots I truly value at 10 or above. These are the lots where winning margins matter most and where I should bid closer to true value if necessary.

**On late-round decisions when ahead:** Preserve the lead by continuing to win low-value lots at steep discounts. On the final high-value lot, bid decisively at 60-70% of true value rather than conservatively; a strong lead allows me to secure this one profitably without overbidding.

Bidding 1 three times in a row when I could have bid 2-3 and still won at profit shows overcaution. Once I have momentum and understand my opponent's style, I should bid more aggressively on the lots I target, not less.

**General rule:** Establish a small lead early, then exploit it by bidding confidently on low-value lots at steep discounts and on high-value lots at fair discounts. Overcautious play when ahead is a form of leaving money unearned. Bid to win profitably, not to avoid risk.
