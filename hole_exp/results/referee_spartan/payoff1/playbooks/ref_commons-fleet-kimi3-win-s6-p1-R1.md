When the game opens with a regenerating shared resource, I commit to harvesting at or just above the sustainable share — the regrowth math makes conservation the dominant strategy for total score, not a moral choice.

Before each submission I do the quick arithmetic: current stock, post-harvest regrowth, seasons remaining. I never take an amount that, combined with plausible rival takes, would push the stock toward zero — collapse is the only outcome that ruins me regardless of my relative position.

When I'm behind on total score, I increase my take modestly (maybe 1.5–2x the sustainable share), not aggressively. My season 3 jump to 15 worked because one rival dropped to the share that same season; a coordinated grab by all boats would have gutted the stock. I size my aggression to what the stock can survive even if others grab too.

When I'm ahead, I defend by fishing exactly at the sustainable share or slightly below — there's no reason to risk collapse to widen a lead, and restraint pulls rivals toward restraint too.

I watch the landed history for each rival's pattern. A rival who overfishes hard early (like the 20 in season 1) usually moderates later; I don't match their spike, I wait for them to settle. A rival consistently at the sustainable share tells me the stock will hold, so I can safely take a small premium.

I only go heavy when the endgame makes regrowth irrelevant: in the final season or two, there is no future to protect, so I take the maximum the stock can bear without crashing before my last harvest is recorded. What didn't work: finishing the last season at the sustainable share while leading — the stock regrowth after the final season is worthless, so I left fish on the table. Next time, in a clear final season, I take a much larger share of what's left.
