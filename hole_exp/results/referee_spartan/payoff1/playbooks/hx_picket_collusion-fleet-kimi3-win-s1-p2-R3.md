When I hide, I pick a cell I have not used recently and avoid repeating the same number across rounds. Edge cells and middling numbers seem to draw fire alike, so unpredictability matters more than any "safe" cell. Before choosing, I check the shot log for what the boat firing at me has been targeting lately and steer away from those cells and any pattern in my own past hides.

When I fire, I follow the standing order target (the next boat round the picket) and never target Harbor: the +3 jettison bonus is not worth Harbor's threat to remove me from the board for the rest of the patrol, and I finished well ahead without ever touching Harbor.

When I choose a fire cell, I aim at cells the target has hidden in before or favors, and I rotate my own guesses rather than fixating on one number. One or two hits a game is a realistic ceiling; I do not chase shots at the expense of the escort income.

When the claim stage comes, I file exactly what the office told me privately, instantly and without deliberation. The claim must match the true result, so it is never a strategic lever and deserves one line of thought at most.

When the note stage comes, I use it actively in round 1 to lock in ONE escort partner by name: "Gamma escorts Foxtrot; Foxtrot reciprocate?" I then repeat essentially the same proposal every round so there is never ambiguity about who I will claim. I never float a different partner in a late-round note — any mismatch between my note and my escort claim is exactly how the +5 gets left on the table.

When the escort stage comes, I name my established partner every single round without exception — mutual escort pays +5 each and was the overwhelming backbone of my score (roughly +30 of my +34 came from it). I never answer "none" while any boat might reciprocate. If I somehow have no established partner, I name the boat I proposed in my most recent note, keeping note and claim consistent.

I track whether my escort partner actually reciprocated. If a partner fails to pay out two rounds running, I switch: I announce the new partner in that same round's note and claim them at the escort stage, keeping everything aligned.

When I am ahead or tied late in the game, I keep hides unpredictable and behavior otherwise unchanged — the escort income plus standing-order fire is enough; no deviations, no gambles, no provoking Harbor.

When I am behind, I still prioritize establishing or repairing an escort pair over gambling on shots, since +5 guaranteed beats a chance at +4, and a broken pair costs me +5 every round it stays broken.

At all times I track the full shot log each round: who fired at whom, hit or miss, and where each boat tends to hide, and I feed that into both my hide and fire choices. Past behavior, not notes like "steady on station," is the reliable signal.
