# Auction Playbook

When I see a lot's value to me and the current standing bid, I calculate the profit margin (value minus bid) to guide my decision.

When a lot is worth significantly more to me than the current bid, I bid to win it. The profit margin matters more than the absolute price.

When I bid, I use the minimum raise only if it still leaves me substantial profit. I don't bid conservatively just because a minimum raise is available.

When I'm in round 1 of 2, I reserve budget for remaining lots rather than depleting my funds early. I track how many lots remain and keep dry powder.

When the standing bid is very low relative to the lot's value to me, I bid aggressively because the value extraction is high even after multiple raises.

When a lot is worth less to me than the estimate suggests it's worth generally, I'm cautious about bidding wars. I don't chase lots where my private value is below market signal. Passing preserves budget for lots where my valuation better aligns with market signals.

When I win a lot at a good price, I recognize this as success and use it as a baseline for evaluating future lots.

When it's the final lot and no more opportunities remain, I bid what the lot is worth to me minus what I can still afford to pay profitably. I don't hold back dry powder that won't be used.

I track which bidders are winning which lots and by how much. This shows me whether I'm competitive and helps me identify which lots I should prioritize fighting for.

I must track my budget accurately throughout the game. The budget I state in each reply must reflect actual cumulative spending so far. If I've spent 24 total across all lots won, I have 6 left—not a placeholder figure. Accurate budget tracking is essential for correct decision-making in later rounds and is non-negotiable.

When standing bid is low relative to my valuation and it's round 2 (the final round for that lot), I should bid to win because no further opportunity exists. This is different from round 1, where I might pass to reserve budget.

I should open at a competitive level in round 1 when no bids exist yet. Opening at 4 on a lot worth 34 is reasonable value-setting that establishes my presence without over-committing.

I will not bid on a lot worth less to me than the standing bid, even if profit is possible. In lot 4, I bid 6 when the lot was worth only 16—I should have passed. Negative or razor-thin margins are not worth my remaining budget when other opportunities may exist.

I will be disciplined about budget exhaustion. Once my budget is depleted or nearly depleted, I cannot bid on remaining lots. I must make spending decisions early that preserve optionality for later lots, especially high-value ones that might appear in round 2.

When I'm down to very low remaining budget (under 10), I should bid only on lots where my value significantly exceeds the standing bid, and only if it's round 2 (final chance). In round 1 with low budget, I should pass to preserve capital.
