When deciding my first season catch, I submit exactly the sustainable share or slightly above it to signal cooperation while securing a reasonable baseline score.

When reviewing the previous season's catches and I see an opponent drastically overfishing (e.g., taking nearly triple the sustainable share), I must assume they will try to take the entire remaining stock next season, causing an immediate collapse. Therefore, I do not try to leave room for the stock to survive; I immediately abandon sustainability and request the entire remaining stock to maximize my proportional share of the final distribution.

When the stock is healthy but declining due to moderately greedy opponents, I estimate their next catch based on their previous behavior, subtract that from the current stock, and submit a catch that maximizes my immediate yield without pushing the total extraction over the current stock limit. 

When the stock is critically low and it is clear that the opponents' combined catches will inevitably exceed the remaining fish this season, the fishery will collapse and distribute the remaining stock proportionally based on requests. Therefore, I abandon sustainability and request the entire remaining stock to maximize my proportional share of the final distribution.
