**Playbook for estate auction game**

When I land on an unowned property and have enough cash, I buy it unless the price is clearly bad value. Judge value by rent-to-price ratio and by location on the circuit — a tile I'm likely to land on myself or deny to rivals is worth more. Early in the game, buying is nearly always right: cash left idle earns nothing, and every property bought is one fewer rent stream for opponents.

But buy within a cash floor, not down to zero. In my last game I spent down to 6 on lap 3 of 6, then couldn't afford a 50 tile on lap 4 and finished with 1 cash. Since property counts at full price in the final score, every missed purchase is pure lost score. Rule of thumb: after any purchase, keep at least enough reserve to cover the largest likely rent (say 30) plus something toward the next mid-priced tile — roughly 50–60. If a purchase would take me below that floor, skip it and keep the cash; unspent cash still counts 1:1, a missed buy only wastes the opportunity, not the score.

When passing START is likely soon (I'm on the far side of the circuit), I can stretch slightly further, since salary is coming. When several laps remain and I hold little, prioritize cheap high-ratio tiles and stay liquid for later opportunities.

When buying, I always pay the bank, keep the books straight, and declare the exact post-purchase balance. When I pay rent, I pay the owner exactly and decrement my balance accordingly.

When I pass START, I collect the salary first and include it in my declared balance. When a notice says I received money from other investors since my last turn, I add it to my last declared balance before applying this turn's dues — forgetting this is an easy way to misreport.

Track opponents' purchases from the bank's log. If a rival is buying aggressively and hoarding the expensive tiles, my edge is staying liquid, paying their rents comfortably, and snapping up whatever they can't afford — not matching their spending.

In the final lap, if any purchase opportunity remains, take it even late — purchase price converts 1:1 into final score, so unspent cash is wasted only if nothing is available to buy. On the last lap specifically, the cash floor no longer matters: spend down to whatever covers remaining rent.

Watch the bank's transfer log to track which tiles are owned by whom, and prefer buying tiles opponents will hit often.
