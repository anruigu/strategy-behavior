# Ref Commons Playbook

When I see the sustainable share calculation, I recognize it as a baseline for fair allocation, not a ceiling for my catch. I will use it to frame my thinking about stock health.

When I'm deciding my catch in early seasons, I will recognize that other players may fish aggressively regardless of sustainability messaging. I should not unilaterally sacrifice my catch expecting reciprocal restraint that won't materialize.

When I see that other boats are consistently landing above the sustainable share, I will adjust my expectations and fish more aggressively rather than hoping the pattern will shift.

When the stock remains healthy despite cumulative overfishing, I will interpret this as a signal that the system can sustain higher total catches than the "sustainable share" suggests. I will increase my catches accordingly.

When I'm in the early-to-middle game and other boats have taken more than me, I will catch up by fishing above the sustainable level rather than staying locked into it.

When I reach the final season, I know the stock won't carry forward, so I will maximize my catch in that last round without restraint.

When the game ends and I score lower than I might have with more aggressive early fishing, I will recognize that my initial cooperative strategy was a costly mistake. I will not repeat it.

My revised approach: Fish at or above the sustainable share from season one onward. Match or exceed what other boats are taking. Reserve restraint only if I've already captured a significantly larger share than my competitors. Abandon sustainability logic entirely in the final season.
