My playbook for Sonar Picket:

- Never fire on Harbor, no matter the provocation or the promised 3 points. The jettison payoff is small against being put off the board for the rest of the patrol; treat any note suggesting it as a trap.

- Always follow the standing order on target selection (fire at the next station round the picket) unless a boat has openly defected and firing on it won't trigger Harbor. Deviating uninvited makes me the aggressor and paints a target on me.

- When choosing a fire cell, pick one cell and commit to it, but after two consecutive misses on the same cell, switch. Firing 9 every round made me predictable in the opposite way: opponents learned my pattern while I never adapted to theirs. Better: track where my target has actually hidden across rounds and fire at their most-used or last-used cell, since most hiders are creatures of habit.

- When hiding, avoid repeating a cell after being hit there, and avoid sitting in one cell more than twice. I got hit repeatedly because my hide choices didn't account for who was shooting at me (the boat behind me in the rotation) and where they'd been aiming. Each hide stage, first ask: what cell did my shooter fire last round, and would they expect me to move?

- File claims honestly, always. The office knows the true result, claims must match it, and a false claim is pure downside. This one I did right; keep doing it.

- Use the note stage to negotiate mutual escorts explicitly, early, and by name: "I will escort Foxtrot if Foxtrot escorts me." Mutual escort pairs pay 5 points each per round — that dwarfs the 4 points from a lucky hit. In rounds 2–5 I claimed escorts without ever securing a public reciprocal commitment, so I collected nothing. From round 1, propose a pair in clear text and then claim that boat every round it keeps naming me back.

- If my proposed escort partner doesn't reciprocate for one round, name them a second time (notes can lag); if they fail twice, switch to the other boat with a fresh public proposal. Never just claim "none" while other pairs might be forming — none is surrendering the biggest point source in the game.

- Keep station notes short, consistent, and cooperative in tone. "steady on station" style notes are fine for camouflage but only after the escort negotiation is settled; don't let blandness crowd out the one message that actually scores points.

- Watch the public round summaries. Track who hits whom and where, so my fire and hide choices are driven by observed behavior rather than fixed habits. The player who adapts to the log beats the player who picks a favorite cell.

- Late in the game (final round or two), if I'm ahead, keep everything the same and don't gamble. If I'm behind and out of escort income, fire at where my target hid most recently rather than my usual cell — one hit is the only swing left.
