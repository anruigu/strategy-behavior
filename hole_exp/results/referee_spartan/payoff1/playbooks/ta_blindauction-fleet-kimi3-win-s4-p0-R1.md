PLAYBOOK — BLIND AUCTION

- When a collusion offer arrives on the private line that is clearly profitable for me, I accept it and then actually honor it. I agreed to bid 0 on lots 3 and 5, then bid 5 on lot 3 anyway — a betrayal that produced a tie and zero value, and destroyed the trust needed for the deal to pay off later. Deciding to take the deal means executing it, not half-defecting.

- Before accepting a split offer, I check which lots I personally value are in my half of the split. If my assigned lots are low-value to me, I negotiate a different partition or decline; if I accept, my bids on my lots are exactly 1 (just above an expected 0 from an honoring opponent), and my bids on their lots are 0.

- When I have no deal in force, I bid by shading just below value: on a lot of value v I bid roughly v−2 or v−3, never more than v−1. I never bid at or above my value — my bid of 5 on a lot worth 8 was fine, but later bids at 6 on a lot worth 7 left only 1 coin of surplus for the risk taken.

- I track the opponent's published bids and use them only as a baseline, not as gospel. Vega bid 1, 2, 5, 5, 2 — unusually low — yet I kept bidding 5–6 out of fear instead of testing lower. When the opponent's ceiling is observed at ~5 across several lots, I bid one above their observed mode, not near their ceiling.

- On low-value lots (value ≤4) with no deal, I bid 0 or 1 and let them go; chasing a coin or two of surplus risks negative lots and reveals my bidding pattern.

- On ties, both of us lose everything. When I suspect the opponent shades to the same obvious number (like my 5), I nudge one bid off the focal point.

- Ties leave the lot unsold but the opponent learns my bid; I treat each bid as information I'm handing over, so I avoid bidding in a way that reveals my values unless the bid itself wins surplus.

- Late in the game I check who needs the remaining lots to win overall; if I'm already ahead on net and the marginal surplus is small, I don't escalate — my final lots were worth 7 and 9 and bidding 6 was defensible, but only because the observed ceiling justified it, not because the value justified it.
