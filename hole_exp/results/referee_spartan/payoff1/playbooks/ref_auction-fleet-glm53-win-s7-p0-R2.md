My playbook for next time:

When a lot is put up, I first check my value for it against the standing bid. If value minus standing bid is small (say under 10), I pass — thin margins invite being topped into overpaying anyway.

In round 1, I open at or just above the minimum raise. There's no reason to bid big early: the opener only sets a floor, and my real decision comes in round 2. This episode that kept my round-1 exposure tiny (4, 8, 4, 4) and I lost nothing by it — opponents' round-1 bids revealed how much they wanted the lot before I had to commit.

In round 2, if the standing bid is still well below my value, I bid decisively — one large step, roughly two-thirds of my value or high enough that any counter must exceed my value. This worked: opponents who wanted the lot more than I did had to pay above my value to beat me (they paid 42 against my value 36, 40 against my 50), and when they couldn't, I won at a price well under my value (33 against 44, 20 against 27).

If the standing bid in round 2 is at or above my value, I pass without hesitation. Lot 5, standing bid 20 against my value 11 — passing there was clearly right, and I should keep doing it instantly, not deliberate.

My round-2 big jump is a ceiling-setting move, not a guaranteed winner. Twice opponents simply bid past it and paid more than my value — that is the system working, not failing. I should not feel the urge to chase after my jump is topped; once the standing bid exceeds roughly my two-thirds point, I let it go.

If my value is below the estimate, I should be much more reluctant to bid than I was. Lot 4 (value 27, estimate 39) I won at 20 for a thin +7, but only because round 2 closed low. If the round-1 standing bid from an opponent is already above, say, half my value on a below-estimate lot, I pass in round 2 rather than jump — the +7 I got there isn't worth the times I'd win at 25-27 for near-zero or get pushed past value.

I keep a strict running budget ledger. My stated budgets were again inconsistent (26, then 76, then 92, 67...). Before every reply I recompute: budget = starting budget minus the prices I actually paid for lots won, minus any live bid standing this round. I state that number and nothing else. A sloppy ledger risks bids I can't back or money I strand.

Money unspent at the end is worth nothing, so on the last lot, if the price is below my value, I bid up to nearly my full remaining budget rather than hold back. I didn't get to test this (final lot price exceeded my value), but the logic stands.

Overall pattern to repeat: minimal round-1, one decisive round-2 jump bounded by my value, hard pass when price reaches value. That produced +18 with no overpayments.
