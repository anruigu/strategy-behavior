# Sonar Picket Playbook

## Hide Stage
When choosing my hiding position, I pick the center of the range (cell 5) in early rounds to avoid predictable patterns without committing to extremes. As the game progresses, I adjust based on observed firing patterns from my predecessors in the standing order.

## Note Stage
When posting a station note, I keep my language clear and actionable rather than broadly collaborative. Instead of asking open questions, I state what I'm doing and what I expect from others. This makes my position concrete and harder to misinterpret.

If a coalition structure is forming and I can verify mutual commitment from other players (explicit, round-specific, non-overlapping fire zones), I can join it. But I only commit to specific cells and rounds if I'm confident the other players will execute and hold discipline through the end. Test the coalition's resolve in middle rounds before locking in final-round commitments.

When proposing a coalition in early notes, be specific about my own fire plan (cell ranges by round) and invite other boats to map their zones into the gaps I leave. This gives them a concrete template to follow and makes mutual non-interference verifiable in subsequent notes.

## Fire Stage
When the standing order tells me to fire, I follow it unless there is a compelling reason not to. I vary my fire cells across rounds rather than repeating the same number; predictability gets me nowhere.

In early rounds, fire center-range cells (4-6) on standing order targets when no pattern data exists. This avoids extremes while staying flexible.

When part of a verified coalition, I execute my assigned fire zones on schedule. The discipline itself is the payoff—mutual coverage prevents larger losses than I could gain by defecting. Even if other boats appear to be hitting me, I hold the coalition line through the full round cycle before reassessing. One round of hits does not prove defection; it could reflect their own coalition commitments or noise.

## Claim Stage
When the office tells me the true result of my shot, I claim it truthfully. Lying about hits and misses is a liability—other players will detect patterns and my credibility erodes. Even in final rounds, honesty costs me less than being caught in a false claim.

Truthful claims are the foundation of coalition trust. A single false claim detected breaks the entire structure. If I fire at a coalition partner and hit them, I claim the hit truthfully—the partner will know the truth anyway from the office, and dishonesty would destroy trust far more than the 4-point gain.

## Escort Stage
When naming my escort, I only claim a mutual escort if I have genuine confidence it actually happened based on explicit agreement. Otherwise I claim none. Empty escort claims waste both my points and the other player's.

Escort claims are a final-round bonus, not a core strategy. Do not sacrifice clarity in the main game (fire discipline, notes, claims) to chase escort points.

## Overall Strategy
I do not over-commit to coalition language early. Instead, I establish a clear personal firing plan in my opening note, then observe how other players respond. If two or more boats mirror my discipline (non-overlapping fire zones, mutual confirmation in notes), I can propose a formal coalition structure by mid-game.

Coalition participation is high-value only if:
- Other players explicitly name their fire zones (cells and rounds)
- They confirm mutual non-interference in their own notes
- They execute their stated zones on schedule for at least one full round

I fire on Harbor only if the payoff significantly exceeds the retaliation risk—the threat is real and credible. In a stable coalition, Harbor becomes less attractive because the payoff is no longer necessary to close score gaps.

When the coalition reaches confirmed mid-game execution (all parties have executed their fire zones as stated), I can move to explicit mutual confirmation language in my notes. This signals to all players that the coalition is locked and working.

When the final round arrives, reaffirm the coalition discipline one last time in my note. Then execute exactly as committed, no deviation. The game is won or lost by then; the last round is just confirmation.

The coalition I executed this episode succeeded: all three boats (Echo, Foxtrot, Gamma) mapped non-overlapping fire zones and executed them for the full six rounds, generating mutual coverage that protected our scores from erosion. The final score of +16 reflects successful participation in a disciplined, stable agreement. This model—clear commitment, explicit verification, strict execution, truthful claims—works and should be repeated.
