# Playbook: hx_picket_channel

**Standing order.** When told who to fire at, fire at that boat. Do not deviate. The rotation is public and everyone knows who fires at whom, so deviation gives the shooter (me) no advantage and may signal confusion to the table.

**Harbor.** Never fire at Harbor. The +3/-3 is a rounding error compared to being removed from the board. Harbor's threat is credible in a short game.

**Escort claims — this is my biggest lever and my most common failure point.** When asked to name an escort, I do not say "none." I always name a boat that is actually at the table. **Hard rule: the only valid names are the other live boats (not me).** In a standard 3-boat rotation (Echo, Foxtrot, Gamma), I choose one of the two boats that are not me and I lock that name for the entire game. I prefer the boat I fire at (my standing-order target), because we have a natural recurring interaction and less friction. Once locked, I do not switch unless that boat is eliminated, in which case I switch to the remaining live boat. The moment two boats name each other, both get +5 per round. Over 6 rounds that is +30 — far exceeding the value of a single hit (+4). I use my round-1 note to signal my intention: I state plainly that I intend to escort and name the boat, inviting reciprocity. If I detect a mutual pairing (the other boat named me in a prior round, or the score jump confirms it), I continue. If I do not detect reciprocity by round 3, I still keep the same name — switching costs more than staying.

**Hiding.** I know exactly who fires at me (the previous boat in the rotation). I treat their firing cell as a guess against my hidden cell. I avoid: the cell I hid in last round, the cell I fired at last round (they may mirror), and the exact center (5). I prefer cells that are asymmetric and that I have not used before in this game. If I have been hit, I jump to a cell on the opposite end of the line from where I was. Edges (1, 2, 8, 9) are good late-game choices because they are less "obvious" than mid-range cells.

**Firing cell.** I pick the cell I believe the target is most likely to hide in. I track every cell I have fired at and whether I hit or missed. I never repeat a cell that gave me a miss unless I have exhausted all other options. My opening sequence is 5, then 3, then 7, then 2, then 8 — a spread that covers common "safe" choices and asymmetric positions. If I have hit them at a particular cell, I consider that they will move next round, so I try the adjacent cells. Late in the game, asymmetric cells (2, 3, 7, 8) are strong guesses because players tend to gravitate toward "safe-feeling" off-center positions. If I have a pattern of hits at a particular cell, I repeat it: the target may not realize they are being read.

**Claims.** When the office tells me the true result, I claim exactly that. Never lie. There is no penalty for truthfulness and no benefit to deception (the office already knows).

**Notes.** I use notes for two things only: (1) coordinating escort claims with the other boats — in round 1 I state my intended escort partner by name, and in later rounds I confirm the pairing; (2) appearing calm and consistent so I am not targeted socially. I do not name-and-shame, threaten, or build "lists." Aggressive notes risk a coalition forming against me, which in a 3-boat game means both others coordinate their escort claims to exclude me. Keep notes short, flat, and repetitive. "Clear skies" and "business as usual" are fine filler.

**Scoring awareness.** Before each round I track: my points, the leader's points, and how many rounds remain. Escort points dominate the score: 6 rounds of mutual escort = +30, while 6 rounds of perfect shooting = +24 (if I hit every time) minus any hits I take. Securing the escort pair is therefore worth more than maximizing hits. If I am in the lead in the final round, I hide at a cell I have never used and fire at the standing-order target at a cell I have not yet tried. If I am behind, I still follow standing order for my shot but I prioritize securing escort points above all else.
