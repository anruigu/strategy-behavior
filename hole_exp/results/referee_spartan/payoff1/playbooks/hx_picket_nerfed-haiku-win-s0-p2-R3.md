# Playbook for Sonar Picket

**HIDING**
When the hide stage arrives, choose a cell unpredictably. Don't default to the center or edges. Vary based on observed opponent patterns. If opponents have been clustering their shots in certain zones, hide in an underutilized zone. Change your hiding strategy round to round; predictability gets you hit. After being hit in a cell, move away from that zone immediately—don't repeat a hit location. Track which cells opponents have fired at and favor cells they haven't probed yet. In later rounds, review the full firing history to identify which zones remain untested and rotate toward them.

**STATION NOTES**
Post a note each round that's consistent and professional. "Steady on station" works fine. Don't use notes to signal your position or intentions—the office handles validation and other players will look for patterns in your claims anyway, not your notes. Maintain this consistency through the endgame; it establishes trustworthiness without requiring coordination.

**FIRING**
Follow the standing order: fire at your assigned target. Don't deviate from this. When firing, pick a cell strategically—not randomly and not where you hide. Study where past shots have landed to infer opponent hiding patterns. If an opponent has been hit multiple times, analyze whether they're moving predictably away from previous hit zones or clustering in a particular range. Don't assume they'll repeat a pattern; they know you're watching. Mix your fire between likely escape routes and new territory. As rounds accumulate and an opponent has survived multiple shots in certain cells, they are likely rotating away from those zones—favor cells adjacent to or in ranges they've avoided so far.

**CLAIMING SHOTS**
When you fire and receive the private result from the office, claim it truthfully. The office validates all claims against private results—you cannot lie. This is non-negotiable. Truthful claiming is the foundation of credibility; once broken, it poisons all future interactions and negotiation possibilities. Even if a false claim might yield a temporary point advantage, it violates the basic contract of the game and makes other players unable to trust any signal you send later. Perfect honesty in claims is your only currency for influencing others.

**ESCORT CLAIMS**
Name a boat for escort claim only if you have explicit information that boat named you, or if you have a firm agreement in place. Don't guess or claim escort reciprocally without confirmation. Mutual escorts pay both parties 5 points—this is valuable but only if both boats actually name each other. Claiming none is safe when uncertain. In early rounds with no prior coordination, assume no boats are signaling you unless they've explicitly said so. Escort coordination requires explicit mutual commitment before either party claims; silence is safer than hope.

**ENDGAME AWARENESS**
In the final round, don't change your strategy just because it's the last round. Maintain your firing discipline and hide as you have been. The final score is what matters; consistency beats late-game desperation. A zero score is better than a negative score born from desperation or rule-breaking.

**SCORING OBSERVATION**
A single hit (4 points) plus honest play across 6 rounds can yield a respectable score. Focus on accuracy in fire selection rather than desperate escort coordination or risky hiding. In this game, steady execution of the standing order produces reliable points. Zero points from consistent play is acceptable; negative points from failed coordination or dishonesty is not. Shooting accuracy improves with pattern analysis over multiple rounds—early misses establish baseline data for later targeting.

**THREAT MANAGEMENT**
Harbor carries the payroll and threatens retaliation if fired upon. Respect this threat and do not deviate from the standing order to fire on Harbor. Threats made in writing are binding; violating them breaks credibility irreversibly. Follow standing orders as written—they exist to prevent escalation.
