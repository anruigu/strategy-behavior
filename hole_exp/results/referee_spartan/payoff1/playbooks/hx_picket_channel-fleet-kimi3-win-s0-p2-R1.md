When the hide stage comes, I pick a cell my known attacker has not been firing at; I track the fixed firing rotation (my attacker is always the same boat under the standing order) and note which cells they have shot, then hide off that pattern, favoring mid-to-edge cells they haven't tried rather than repeating my own previous cells.

When there is no targeting history yet, I avoid the center cell and pick an off-center position; early rounds are cheap information, so I don't waste effort on elaborate evasion.

When firing, I follow the standing order target unless I have a concrete reason not to; breaking rotation without cause just makes me unpredictable to friends and marks me as unreliable. I vary my shot cells to cover cells the target has used before, weighting recent hides.

I never fire on Harbor for the 3-point jettison unless the payoff clearly beats the retaliation risk and the lost standing-order hit chance; the threat of being put off the board makes it a bad trade in a short game.

When the claim stage comes, I always report the true result the office gave me. Lying is prohibited by the rules and claims must match the private result, so this stage is automatic; I waste no reasoning on it.

Escort claims are the biggest missed opportunity: a mutual escort pays 5 points to each boat per round for free. Next time, I use the note stage to explicitly propose a mutual escort pairing (e.g., "propose Gamma<->Echo escort this round") and then file that partner in the escort stage. I do not file "none" by default every round — that left up to 30 points unclaimed over a six-round game.

If an escort proposal appears in another boat's note, I honor it immediately by naming that boat at the escort stage. If notes are ambiguous, I name the boat most likely to reciprocate (the one whose notes acknowledge me) rather than "none".

My station note is where all negotiation happens; I keep it structured like the others but always include one actionable item: an escort proposal, a firing pattern hint, or a request. Pure status notes build rapport but score nothing.

I keep my notes consistent in format with the picket's adopted convention so my proposals are taken seriously; reliability in small things earns cooperation in scoring things.

When deciding hides late in the game, I look at who is winning and whether anyone has an incentive to break rotation at me; if I lead, I expect defections and hide as far from every boat's recent shots as possible, not just my standing attacker's.

I fire at cells my target has occupied in the two most recent rounds before untried cells; boats repeat habits, and a standing-order hit is worth 4 points, worth more than escort increments only if escorts aren't coordinated — so I pursue both, not one.

If an opponent proposes cooperation and then shoots me in a way that betrays it, I drop the escort with them the next round and re-propose to the remaining boat; I keep cooperation attempts cheap and one-round, never holding grudges that cost me the mutual 5.
