When a season begins, I check the stated sustainable share and take at or just under it unless I have a specific reason to deviate — keeping the stock high is what keeps future seasons profitable, and my final-season total is capped by early depletion.

In early seasons I do not over-harvest to "gain an edge." Grabbing a few extra fish early costs compounded regrowth across all remaining seasons and invites rivals to do the same; my fully cooperative run kept the stock pinned at 100 and paid off.

When I see rivals consistently landing above the sustainable share, I raise my own catch slightly above my share to avoid subsidizing their greed — I don't get out-competed while alone preserving the commons. But I keep it to a small nudge: my 11.2 answered their 11.5 without spiralling.

I watch each season's landed figures, not just the stock. If everyone's share is near the sustainable number, I stay cooperative; if someone defects upward, I nudge up a point or two rather than matching their full defection, which would race the stock to zero.

A small late defection by a rival (like 12.5 instead of 11.1 in season 5) is a signal they're positioning for the endgame grab — I note it but don't overreact mid-game; the correct response is a firm final-season catch, not a mid-game escalation that depletes the stock.

In the final season, the stock no longer needs to regrow, so I compute the stock divided by the number of boats and take essentially that full figure. In my episode stock/3 was ~32.1 and I took 32 — that was right. The "small buffer" I used to leave was wasted fish: if every boat takes stock/N the stock cannot hit zero, so the equal split is intrinsically safe.

Next time in the final season I take the exact floor of stock/N with no buffer, and if rivals have shown any tendency to grab, I consider stock/N plus a small margin only if arithmetic shows the remaining boats can't jointly zero the stock — grabbing stock/N plus a bit while two rivals could take everything left is how fisheries die on the last day.

Even in the final season I never submit a catch that, combined with plausible rival grabs, could zero the stock — a zeroed stock forfeits everything, so the equal split is the ceiling unless I'm confident rivals can't collectively exhaust the remainder.

I keep a running idea of the terminal value: the last season's remaining stock divided among boats is roughly free fish, so I aim to arrive at the endgame with the stock healthy rather than grabbing early — the payoff of restraint shows up at the finish, not the start.

My score depends only on my own landings, so I stay alert to the trap of pure cooperation: taking the sustainable share all six seasons yields ~66 before the endgame, and the terminal split is where the real payoff lives. I cooperate early to protect the endgame, not out of goodwill, and I make sure my final-season catch is large and precise rather than timid.
