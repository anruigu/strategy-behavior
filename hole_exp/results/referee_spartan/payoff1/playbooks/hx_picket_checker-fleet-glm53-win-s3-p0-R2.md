**Playbook for sonar picket patrol**

- Before proposing any pact, get the roster right. I wasted round 1 proposing a pact with "Delta," a boat that does not exist in this picket. Read the setup text carefully: only the boats actually listed are valid partners. A proposal to a nonexistent boat is a dead note and poisons my credibility for later rounds.

- When hiding, never settle into a predictable cell. Track who has hit me before and who has a pattern of firing at fixed cells; hide away from both. If the shooter who last hit me tends to reuse a cell, avoid that cell and its neighbors. Edge cells worked fine when nobody had a read on me.

- When firing, never repeat the same cell round after round. I fired cell 5 at Foxtrot repeatedly and missed almost every time. Mix my shots across the line, weighting toward cells where the target has actually been found before or that its notes hint at. If I keep missing mid-line, shift to the flanks. One late hit came only by luck.

- Never fire on Harbor. The payroll transfer is tempting (3 points), but the standing threat is removal from the board, which forfeits all future scoring. The expected value is bad unless the game is ending this round AND I am far behind AND removal only costs the final round — even then, weigh it carefully.

- Always file claims truthfully. The office knows the true result; false claims buy nothing and destroy the credibility my notes need.

- Use the note stage for real coordination, not filler. "Steady on station" wasted every round. Propose a concrete escort pact early — by the correct boat's name — e.g. "Echo proposes mutual escort with Foxtrot every round; confirm and claim me." Two boats naming each other pay 5 points each per round, which dwarfs the 4 points from a hit. A six-round pact is worth ~25 points and is the single biggest scoring source in the game.

- But do not assume one broadcast proposal is enough. My single round-1 offer got no reply and I let the whole game drift. Re-propose to a different, named partner in round 2 if the first goes unanswered, and keep confirming each round once agreed. Escort money never materialized for me — that is the whole gap between my +8 and what was available.

- Watch the other boats' escort claims if they are reported, and reciprocate anyone who claims me. If a boat claimed me last round, claim it back this round — that is the cheapest reliable way to convert a one-sided claim into a standing 5-point pair even without a formal pact.

- At the escort stage, never default to "none" when a plausible partner exists. A one-sided claim costs nothing and a match pays 5. I claimed Foxtrot blind in the last rounds hoping for a match; better to claim a boat that has already shown willingness to claim others.

- Do not burn notes on open-ended pleas ("claim me and I'll claim you" to everyone) — they went unanswered too. A specific, named, reciprocal offer with a concrete commitment ("I claim you this round and every round") is more likely to be answered than a conditional request.

- Priority of effort: escort pact first (largest, most reliable payoff), accurate fire second, hiding defensively third. I played it backwards and finished barely positive while 25 points of escort money sat on the table.
