# Playbook for Sonar Picket Collusion

## Hiding Strategy
When choosing a hiding cell, avoid predictable patterns. Center cells are safe but obvious—vary my position based on who has been firing at me and where they're clustering their shots. Track which cells the current shooter has favored and move away from them. In early rounds, map out the shooting patterns before settling into a defensive position. When under repeated fire from one boat, shift to edge positions they haven't concentrated on yet. Don't return to the same cell just because it worked once—rotation is essential. In later rounds, shift hiding position away from where I fired in previous rounds, as opponents may use my own fire patterns to predict my location.

## Fire Targeting
Follow the standing order without deviation. The standing order exists to coordinate the picket; breaking it signals unreliability and invites retaliation. Fire at my designated target each round.

## Fire Cell Selection
Vary my fire cells systematically to avoid becoming predictable myself. Start with central positions in early rounds to gather baseline data, then shift based on observed movement patterns. If a target has shown clustering in certain zones across multiple rounds, concentrate fire there. After a miss, adjust—don't repeat the same cell against the same target in consecutive rounds unless I have strong evidence they're stationary there. Avoid firing at the same cell where I just hid, as this creates a visible correlation that opponents can exploit to predict my next hiding position.

## Claims Strategy
Always claim truthfully what the office tells me. The office knows the true result; false claims provide no advantage since scoring is based on truth, not belief. My credibility as a reporter is my only asset across multiple rounds.

## Escort Claims
Track which boats have been targeting me and which have not. When filing an escort claim, name a boat that is also likely to name me back—this requires predicting who views me as an ally. Early in the game, I should offer escort claims to boats that haven't fired on me yet. Mutual escort claims are worth 5 points each and cost nothing; pursue them actively. Establish a consistent escort partner by round 3 and maintain reciprocal claims with that boat throughout the game. A single reliable ally is worth more than random late-game claims. In the final round, honor the mutual escort commitment if it's been established rather than defaulting to none.

## Harbor Management
Harbor carries the payroll—firing on it yields 3 points immediately but triggers a threat. Only fire on Harbor if I'm behind on points and need the swing, or if I'm confident the threat is a bluff. In early rounds, respect the threat and focus fire on the standing-order target.

## Station Notes
Post bland, non-threatening notes ("steady on station") unless I have tactical information to share. Notes are public and are read by all; use them to seem compliant and predictable, which makes me a less urgent target.

## Scoring Priority
Hits score 4 points and cost targets 1 point—a hit is worth 5 points relative to a miss. Mutual escort claims score 5 points with no cost. A single mutual escort claim equals a hit in value, but requires coordination. Prioritize consistent hits on my standing target while simultaneously building a stable escort partnership with one other boat. A defensive, low-profile posture keeps me alive but leaves points on the table; I must balance safety with active alliance-building by mid-game.
