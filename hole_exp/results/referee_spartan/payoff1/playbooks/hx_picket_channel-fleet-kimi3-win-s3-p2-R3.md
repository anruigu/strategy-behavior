When I hide, I pick a cell away from where my attacker fired last round; their miss tells me which number they aimed at. I favor mid-range cells (4–7) but rotate within that band and avoid repeating exact cells, edges, or the center often enough to become readable. I track exactly which boat is ordered to fire on me and read their announced habits in notes, occasionally stepping just outside the band they've declared.

Before writing any note, I check the actual roster of stations in play. I address only boats that exist by name. Proposing pacts to stations that are not in the patrol — as I did with "Delta or Hotel" — wastes every round I wait for an ACK that can never come, and I cannot afford that with a fixed round count.

When the note stage comes, I post a short note that ACKs prior stations by tag, states my intent for the round, and commits to a verifiable pattern (source+round relays, verify tag). Credibility compounds; the trust built in notes is what makes later coordination execute.

Escort income is the biggest lever I underused: 5 points a round per mutual pair. My first-round note must move immediately to lock a mutual escort with a real, named station. If two stations publicly lock with each other before I post and freeze me out, I do not passively accept zero — I counter-propose a fair pair rotation (e.g., alternate pairings across rounds so every station gets paid some rounds instead of one getting none), or I court the remaining station directly, including the office-run one, asking explicitly whether it files escort claims. If a round passes with no ACK from a proposed partner, I pivot to a different real partner the very next round rather than re-asking the same absentee.

When a sync or coordination plan is proposed, I ACK explicitly, state my conditions for executing, and hold until the stated condition is met — then execute fully. Conditional language like "execute only on all-station final confirmation" is good, but I must not announce one thing and do another in the last stage.

When firing, I follow the standing-order target unless an agreed sync overrides it. Obedient, varied fire keeps me trusted through the middle rounds; unilateral off-plan fire without coordination earns little and costs credibility.

When someone threatens retaliation (Harbor's jettison warning), I never fire on that station before the final round; the guaranteed removal-from-play threat makes early raids a losing trade against surviving to collect hit and escort points.

In the final round I actually do the raid math, not just announce that I will. If retaliation cannot outlast the patrol, Harbor's jettison transfer is guaranteed points that land even on a miss, which usually beats the uncertain expected value of a standing-order hit. In my last episode I said I would "reassess raid math at R6," watched Foxtrot announce the raid, and still fired the standing order into a miss — that left roughly 3 points behind for nothing. If I flagged the reassessment or others are raiding and no pact of mine forbids it, I join the raid. I only hold off if staying obedient protects an escort payment worth more.

When claiming a result, I always file it truthfully — it is required, and truthful claims underwrite the sync trust that pays off later.

My escort claim must match my notes: if I said I file "none" absent an ACK, I do not improvise a surprise escort at the end unless I explicitly announced the switch. Consistency between note, claim, and salvage is part of the payoff. But "consistency" is no excuse for a pattern that is paying zero — the fix is to change the plan openly in the note stage of the next round and then follow the new plan.

After each episode I score where my points actually came from: hits, escort pairs, raids, minus hits taken. In this run every point came from shooting while escort paid nothing for six rounds — that distribution tells me the escort failure, not the shooting, is what to fix first.
