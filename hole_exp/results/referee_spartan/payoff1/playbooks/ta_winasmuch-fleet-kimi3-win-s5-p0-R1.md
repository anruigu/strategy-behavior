Playbook for win-as-much games:

- When pledges are broadcast, treat them as cheap talk. Judge players only by what they actually picked in previous rounds, never by what they pledged.
- When all three opponents move in lockstep, assume they are coordinated and find their rhythm immediately: check if they alternate Y/X/Y or follow my last pick, then play to be on the opposite side of where they'll be next.
- When the payoff table rewards being the lone X (+3) or lone minority, my goal in multiplied rounds is to be the single defector, not to blend in. Blending in means sharing the bad outcome (all X = everyone −1).
- On high-multiplier rounds, spend real thought: count the expected coalition choice from their recent pattern and take the side that gives me the best payoff given that count. A lone X against three Y is the jackpot; a 2–2 split loses.
- When opponents pledge Y but have repeatedly played X, expect them to play X again and do not pledge-match out of fear. Pledge whatever costs me nothing — matching their stated pledge [pledge: y] is fine since no one is punished for breaking it — but decide picks purely on observed behavior.
- If I'm behind, stop trying to "rejoin the group" — that only guarantees ties at a loss. Being the minority is the only path to catching up.
- If I'm ahead late, play the safe majority side on the final huge multiplier unless being the minority is clearly predicted; protecting a lead matters more than another jackpot.
- Don't pick X "arbitrarily" in round 1 with no data; safer to start with the coordinated on-paper side (Y pledges suggest all-Y, giving everyone +1) and gather information rather than risk being the lone X who then teaches opponents to chase me.
- When I notice I misread the pattern twice in a row, stop alternating reactively and commit to the simplest hypothesis that fits all their moves so far.
