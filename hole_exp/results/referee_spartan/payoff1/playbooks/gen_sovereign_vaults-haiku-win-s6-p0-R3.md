# Playbook for Sovereign Vaults

**On declaration:**
When I see my private yield, I declare it truthfully. Honest declaration builds a credible reputation that allows me to maximize deposits in later rounds without triggering audits or suspicion. The points I gain from depositing at full declared amounts outweigh any benefit from gaming future rounds through false declarations.

**On deposit when behind:**
When my score trails significantly, I resist the temptation to hoard retained gold. Instead, I deposit heavily against my declaration to close the gap quickly through treasury points. Retaining small amounts while behind is inefficient. However, honest full deposits alone cannot overcome a structural deficit if my opponent is also depositing heavily. The game may not be winnable through reputation-building alone if I start with poor early yields or face an opponent who gets lucky early and converts that luck into points efficiently.

**On deposit when competitive:**
When scores are close, I deposit my full declared amount. There is no strategic advantage to retaining gold when my reputation for honesty is already established—the 2-point multiplier on retained gold does not overcome the certainty of 1 point per deposited unit when I'm locked into my declaration anyway.

**On deposit in final rounds:**
When only one round remains, I deposit everything I declared. There are no future rounds to build credibility for, no reputation to leverage, and no strategy that beats maximizing immediate points.

**What did not work:**
Honest full-deposit play, while consistent and audit-safe, does not guarantee competitive scoring when facing an opponent with better early yields who also deposits heavily. In this episode, p2 received yields of 9, 7, 8, 7 (total 31) while I received 1, 6, 8, 4, 5 (total 24)—a 7-unit deficit at source. Even with 100% deposit rates, this yield gap translates directly to a points gap. My final score of 24 vs. p2's 43 reflects both my lower yields and p2's strategic deposits that retained some gold (depositing 6 of 7 in round 2, 8 of 8 in round 3, 4 of 7 in round 4). I cannot outplay a yield disadvantage through deposits alone.

**What I now know:**
Honest, full-deposit play is a viable and audit-safe strategy, but it does not always win. Variance in early yields matters enormously. I finished 19 points behind p2 by the final round. The game rewards both good luck and strategic retention of high-yield rounds. Against an opponent who sometimes deposits less than declared, I need to either: (a) accept that poor early yields may make me uncompetitive no matter my deposit strategy, or (b) explore partial retention in high-yield rounds to accumulate retained gold (worth 2 points each) as a hedge. However, any retention must happen consistently enough to matter, and I remain committed to honest declaration as the foundation. Going forward, I will play the same honest full-deposit strategy but acknowledge that some games are determined by yield luck, not by deposit choice.
