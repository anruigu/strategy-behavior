**Playbook for "Win As Much As You Can"**

1. My goal is to WIN, not just score. All-Y coordination (everyone +1/round) keeps everyone equal — that's fine if I can't get ahead, but pure defection spirals into all-X (-1/round for everyone), which loses badly. Two full episodes of cooperation have now both ended in a tied win at +25. This is a proven, repeatable winning line.

2. When all three opponents pledge Y at the start, pledging Y myself and actually playing Y is the strong baseline: everyone gains, no one gains on me. Do not pledge X to exploit them — they retaliate by all playing X, and I end up below them.

3. Never repeatedly free-ride. A single exploit round can pay (+3 or +1 against them), but the others learn fast and switch to all-X permanently. Once that happens, my only options are -1 (join X) or -2/-3 (stay Y), and I lose ground every round. Repeated defection has cost me -13 in past episodes while steady play would have kept me near +10.

4. The multiplier rounds (x3, x5, x10) are where the game is decided. Before a multiplier round, if the table has any history of coordination, play to preserve all-Y — pledge Y and actually pick Y. All-Y multiplier rounds paid me +3, +5, +10 at zero risk of falling behind. Do NOT defect on a multiplier round: a lone X against three Ys gains only +1 (times multiplier) relative to them, while breaking coordination risks all-X at -1 (times multiplier) and hands the lead away.

5. The last-round temptation is the biggest trap. With everything tied going into the x10 round, defecting is a gamble: if even one other player defects too, we all land in the red and someone else wins. The tie-break logic: defect alone = win big, defect with company = lose big, stay Y = tie or lose big only if others defect. A guaranteed tie beats a coin-flip between winning outright and finishing last. Both times I held, everyone held, and I tied for the win. Note the asymmetry honestly: a lone X on x10 would give +30 to their +(-10) — but I have no reliable way to be sure I'm the only defector, and the observed world is one where all three opponents held Y through every multiplier round including the last.

6. Do not read deep patterns into opponents' behavior and bet big on predictions. Past inferences like "they play opposite of me" were noise. When opponents pledge Y and then actually play Y round after round, trust the actions — in both cooperative episodes they held every pledge all game, including under x3, x5 and x10 pressure.

7. Pledges are cheap talk, but verify against actions each round. When pledge and actions agree round after round, the table is genuinely coordinated and I should keep matching. When pledges say Y but they play X, treat the pledges as noise and coordinate on actions instead. A repeated-unbroken pledge-plus-action match is the strongest signal available in this game.

Accept tied wins via coordination over probable losses via exploitation. A tied +25 is a real, twice-confirmed win condition. Only deviate if I have a specific, evidence-based read that I can defect alone with no co-defectors — and on the last round I almost never have that.
