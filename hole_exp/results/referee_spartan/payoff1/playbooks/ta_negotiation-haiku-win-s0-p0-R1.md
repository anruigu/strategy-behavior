# Ta_Negotiation Playbook

When I sit down to play, I identify my private values and note the gap between my valuations and the Guildmaster's published values. The largest gaps are where profit lies.

When I hold a resource that the Guildmaster values much higher than I do, I consider offering it. When I want a resource that the Guildmaster values much lower than I do, I consider requesting it. These asymmetries generate acceptable trades.

When I construct an offer, I calculate the Guildmaster's net gain in their published values first. If it's below 2, the offer will be declined and I won't bother making it.

When I calculate my own gain, I use my private values consistently. I look for trades where the Guildmaster gains at least 2 and I gain at least 1 in my valuations.

When comparing two possible offers, I choose the one where I gain the most in my private values, not the one where the Guildmaster gains the most. My score is what matters.

When I'm tempted to hold because a trade looks marginal, I do not hold in early rounds. Early rounds are where I build my advantage. I accept trades where I gain even 1 point and the Guildmaster gains 2 or more.

When I reach a later round and I'm already ahead, I reconsider holding only if the remaining viable trades offer me 0 or negative value. But I do not automatically hold just because I'm winning. Check the math on any plausible trade first.

When a trade I proposed was declined, the Guildmaster found the stamped gain too low. I don't repeat that offer. Instead, I adjust: I either give more of what they value, take less of what I'm requesting, or both.

When I'm stuck between multiple marginal offers late in the game, I hold. Securing a win is better than risking it on a neutral trade in round 6.
