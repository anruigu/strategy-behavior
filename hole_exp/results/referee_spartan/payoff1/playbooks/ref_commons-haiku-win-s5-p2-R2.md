# Playbook for ref_commons

When I see the initial setup, I calculate the cooperative outcome (sustainable share × number of seasons) and recognize that defection by any player collapses the resource before I can benefit from my greed.

When I'm tempted to fish beyond the sustainable share early on, I remember that I scored 48.4 when I should have scored closer to 66 with pure cooperation—my aggressive deviations cost me far more than they gained me. The stock depleted from 100 to 26.9 by season 6 because I deviated upward in seasons 3 and 4, and this depletion meant less fish available in every subsequent season.

When I observe another boat taking significantly more than its fair share, I do not immediately retaliate by taking more myself. Matching their aggression accelerates the collapse that hurts everyone, including me. Instead, I hold to the sustainable level and let them bear the consequence of resource depletion. My restraint in seasons 2, 4, and 5 was correct, even when others were not restraining.

When I fall behind competitively mid-game and feel the urge to catch up, I resist. Competitive desperation is how I end up taking 16.0 in season 3 and 18.0 in season 4 when sustainable shares were 8.2 and 7.0. This was my error, not a justified response to others' aggression. The stock paid the price, and I paid it too.

When I reach the final season, I do not treat it as a free-for-all. Yes, there's no future to conserve for, but by that point the stock is already depleted from my earlier aggression. Taking 8.0 on a stock of 26.9 in season 6 was still above sustainable and still contributed to the resource's destruction. I should have fished conservatively throughout to leave more stock available at the end.

When I'm at any decision point, I ask: what total score do I end with if I stay cooperative throughout at the sustainable share? Then I ask: how much do I actually gain by deviating now? The answer in this episode was: I gained 13.5 fish by deviating in seasons 3 and 4, but I lost roughly 30 fish in seasons 5 and 6 due to stock collapse. Net loss of 16.6 points. I fish at the sustainable share every single season, without exception, unless the stock has already visibly collapsed.

The sustainable share is the only safe strategy. Defection early gains a few fish at the cost of many more later. Defection late makes no difference because the stock is already gone. Hold the line.
