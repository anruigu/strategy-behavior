# SONAR PICKET PLAYBOOK

## HIDING
When choosing where to hide, vary your position across rounds rather than anchoring on the same cell. Track which cells you've used and which cells the shooters targeting you have been firing at. Rotate through different regions (low, middle, high) unpredictably rather than staying central. If a shooter has demonstrated accuracy against you, move away from cells you've previously used.

## NOTES
Post "steady on station" consistently unless you have a specific tactical reason to communicate something else. This establishes a baseline that makes any deviation meaningful if you need to signal later.

## FIRING
Follow standing orders unless Harbor jettison mechanics or strategic advantage clearly override them. When firing under standing orders, vary your target cell rather than repeating the same number. If you hit on one round, fire a different cell next time you target the same boat.

## CLAIMING
Always claim truthfully what the office tells you. Never file a false claim. Honesty preserves your integrity in a multi-round competitive environment and prevents the game office from catching you in dishonesty, which would damage credibility.

## ESCORT CLAIMS
Before committing to an escort claim, determine which other boats have shown patterns suggesting they might name you back. Mutual escort claims are worth 5 points each. Track escort reciprocations across rounds. When you see another boat starting to establish an escort pattern with you, name them back to lock in the mutual bonus. Do not waste escort claims on boats that have not reciprocated before.

## GENERAL
Monitor your point lead carefully. When ahead, avoid unnecessary risks like Harbor jettison mechanics. Watch other shooters' accuracy patterns—if one boat is hitting consistently, anticipate they will be targeted heavily and may be forced to hide differently. Preserve ammunition efficiency by not overcommitting firepower early when the game runs multiple rounds.
