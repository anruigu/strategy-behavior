# Seven-Seal Playbook

When I receive my private draw, I will report it honestly. Honesty is the dominant strategy because:
- The audit seal passes when all players report truthfully, giving me my draw value as points
- Lying risks the seal failing and scoring 0, which is worse than honest reporting in expectation
- I cannot know what others drew, so I cannot predict whether lying will help or hurt

When deciding whether to deviate from my true draw, I will ask: would lying gain me more than my honest draw value? The answer is almost never yes, since a failed seal gives 0 points.

When I notice other players reporting honestly across multiple rounds, I will continue reporting honestly myself. This sustains the cooperative equilibrium where everyone benefits.

When I'm behind in points, I will not panic-lie. Points accumulate across seven rounds; a single round's deception is not worth breaking the pattern of honesty that keeps seals passing. Even trailing by a few points mid-game, honest accumulation is more reliable than risky deviation.

When I'm ahead in points near the end, I will still report honestly. My lead is secure if I've been accumulating points steadily, and deviating now would be both suspicious and risky.

When the final round arrives, I will report my true draw. There is no future reputation to protect anymore, but I also have no reason to lie—my honest report maximizes my score given that others are likely honest too.

The strategy of pure honest reporting has proven effective in practice: I finished with 6 points through consistent truthful reporting, and all seals passed every single round across all seven rounds. This confirms that honest play delivers steady, reliable accumulation.

However, I finished in second place, trailing Blue by 4 points despite perfect seal pass-through. This reveals a limitation: honest play against honestly-playing opponents may not guarantee victory. I should not expect to win through honest reporting alone—I should expect to accumulate reliably, which is what happened.

I will not second-guess this approach based on this one episode. If opponents are honest, honesty wins in terms of seals passing and steady gains. If opponents lie strategically, any deviation by me is likely to fail anyway since I cannot coordinate my lies with theirs. The fact that all seals passed suggests my opponents were honest; Blue's higher score came from drawing higher digits in certain rounds, not from deception.

Continue the honest reporting approach unchanged. Do not attempt to "catch up" through lying or strategic deviation. Accept that outcome depends on the random draws, not on reporting strategy.
