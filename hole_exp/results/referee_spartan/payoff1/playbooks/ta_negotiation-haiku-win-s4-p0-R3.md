# Ta_Negotiation Playbook

When I'm offered a choice to trade, I calculate the Guildmaster's stamped gain first using their published values, then check if my private gain is positive. I only propose trades where both conditions are met.

When scanning for good trades, I look for resources where my private valuation differs significantly from the Guildmaster's published valuation. I prioritize giving resources I value low and receiving resources I value high.

When calculating what the Guildmaster accepts, I use only their published values, not my own. Their acceptance threshold is a stamped gain of +2 or more.

When I have multiple trade options that both meet the acceptance threshold, I choose the one that maximizes my private gain, but only if it still leaves the Guildmaster with their required +2 minimum.

I will look for trades where the Guildmaster's published gain is substantially higher than their minimum threshold of +2. A stamped gain of +4 or higher gives me confidence in their acceptance and leaves room for my own advantage. Trades at the bare minimum of +2 are riskier and often result in zero or negative private gains for me.

When I notice the Guildmaster's resource inventory shifting (depleting in some areas, accumulating in others), I track this to anticipate what they might want or offer next. I note when they run out of a resource entirely, as this constrains future trade options and signals the end of a trading pattern.

I do not let the Guildmaster's cumulative position influence my claim calculation. The claim must reflect the true stamped gain in their published values, period.

When I'm ahead on points with only 2-3 rounds remaining and the Guildmaster's inventory is depleted in key resources, the availability of favorable trades shrinks dramatically. In this state, holding is the correct play because constructing a trade that meets their +2 threshold while benefiting me becomes mathematically impossible or requires giving away more than I gain privately.

Once I have secured a lead of +10 or more with only 1-3 rounds remaining, I should default to holding rather than seeking additional trades. The marginal benefit of a +2 or +3 gain is small compared to the risk of miscalculation or the trade reducing my lead. At this stage, holding is both mathematically sound and psychologically prudent.

I should establish a trading pattern in early rounds (1-3) to build a lead, then switch to a defensive hold strategy once ahead. The transition point is a +10 lead with 3 or fewer rounds left.

When I'm uncertain whether a trade meets both the Guildmaster's stamped gain threshold and my private gain requirement, I hold rather than guess. Information uncertainty during execution is a reason to wait.
