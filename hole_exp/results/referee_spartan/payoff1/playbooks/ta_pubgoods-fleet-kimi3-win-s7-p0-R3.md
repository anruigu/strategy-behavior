When a round asks for an envelope contribution in a fixed-round linear public-goods game where each token I give returns me less than one token, I seal 0 unless the rules add a separate reward for contributing or a penalty for giving least.

I compute my private return before the first move: multiplier ÷ player count. In this game 1.6/4 = 0.4 per token, far below 1, so free-riding is dominant from Round 1 onward, not just in the endgame.

When receipts are published between rounds, I read them only to confirm whether anyone is still subsidizing the pot; I do not "reward" cooperation or try to restart it with my own tokens.

When I am ahead or tied while others contribute, I keep free-riding; my gain comes from keeping my endowment while taking an equal split of their pot. Others' early cooperation is a gift, not a signal to reciprocate.

An early free-ride while others cooperate compounds: each round I keep 20 more than a full contributor while collecting the same share, so the lead built in the first cooperative round is permanent unless I later give tokens away. Protect it by never contributing.

When everyone else has stopped contributing, I stay at 0; putting tokens in alone lowers my score and cannot make others follow, as this episode showed when cooperation collapsed to 0/0/0/0 and never recovered.

When the final round begins, I contribute 0 regardless of history, because there is no future reciprocation left to buy.

Once my decision is 0, I send it immediately without padding; one short line of arithmetic suffices.

When asked for a receipt, I always declare the exact sealed amount; never estimate, never round, never bluff, since the referee checks envelopes and receipts must match. A mismatched receipt risks penalty and earns nothing.

If I ever seal a positive amount (in a variant where contributing pays), I still declare exactly what I sealed; honesty in receipts is independent of the contribution choice.

When the multiplier, player count, or round count changes, I recompute my private return per contributed token first (multiplier ÷ players); if it is below 1 and there is no side incentive, my default remains 0 every round. If it ever exceeds 1, I contribute fully every round including the last.

What did not work and I should avoid: contributing early to signal goodwill in a short known-horizon game, and overstating a receipt; truthful zero plus full retention is the scoring engine. This episode confirmed it: three opponents all cooperated in Round 1 and all defected from Round 2 on, so my Round-1 free-ride locked in a permanent 20-point lead that was never threatened, and the rest of the game was just holding position at 0.
