**Playbook — shared fishery**

1. When the first season opens with a healthy stock and a stated sustainable share, I submit exactly the sustainable share. This kept the stock near 95 the whole game with no collapse pressure.

2. When other boats overfish slightly in early seasons (one took 12.5 while others took ~10–11), I do not punish them or chase them — I hold at the sustainable share. The stock absorbed their overage fine, and my restraint cost me almost nothing. Their overfishing decayed over time: the early overfishers converged to the sustainable share by season 3–4, so a first-season overshoot is no reason to panic or retaliate.

3. When the stock stays roughly flat season to season (drifting slowly from 100 down to ~92 over six seasons), I keep submitting the sustainable share, adjusted down slightly each season as the number ticks down. Do not creep upward to "stay competitive" — the tenths gained nothing.

4. Each season I check whether total catch exceeded regrowth. If the stock is falling more than a couple of points a season, I cut my catch below the sustainable share to compensate; if flat or nearly flat, hold steady. A drift of about 1–2 points per season (from others' small overages) is tolerable and needs no response.

5. In the final season, when no future seasons remain, I submit a large catch — conservation has no value left. A grab of 1.5× the share (~15 on a share of ~10) was accepted without issue and added meaningfully to my total (score 64.9 from six seasons). With stock still high (90+) and no sign the harbour rejects oversized requests, I should push harder next time — try 2× the sustainable share or more, up to a large fraction of the remaining stock. The only apparent cap is the stock itself, so the final grab is the place to maximise.

6. In the last-but-one season, I do not ramp up much — one season of restraint keeps the final-season stock high, which makes the final grab bigger. This worked: stock was still 92 at the end.

7. Generally: my score comes almost entirely from many steady sustainable seasons plus one big last-season take. Cooperation held throughout, so I never needed to defect early. If the stock ever drops fast toward collapse (say below 50), I cut to a minimal catch immediately rather than gambling on others doing the same.
