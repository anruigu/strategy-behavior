# Playbook for ref_estate

## Property Acquisition Strategy

When you land on an unowned property, buy it if you have sufficient cash and it generates reasonable rent relative to its cost. Properties with rent-to-cost ratios around 20% (like Terrace at 70/14 and Depot at 50/10) are solid early investments.

Buy properties early in the game when you have capital available, as rental income compounds across laps and other players will visit your properties multiple times.

However, be selective about which properties to buy. Owning even one property early (like Terrace on lap 2) generates meaningful rental income over subsequent laps. Do not skip early purchase opportunities waiting for "perfect" properties—a 20% ratio property acquired on lap 2 will pay rent across 4+ remaining laps.

When you cannot afford a property you land on, simply decline and move on—do not overextend your cash reserves trying to force purchases you cannot make.

## Cash Management

Maintain a minimum cash buffer even after purchases. After buying Terrace for 70, I had 112 cash remaining—this proved sufficient to absorb rent payments throughout the game without reaching critical depletion.

Track your running balance carefully: account for salary collected when passing START, all rent received from other players, and all rent you owe when landing on opponents' properties.

Recognize that cash drain from paying rent to other players is a major threat. Expect to pay significant rent to opponents' properties as other players acquire them. Budget for this drain when deciding on property purchases.

## Ownership Strategy

Acquiring multiple properties is critical to competitive scoring. In this game I owned only Terrace and Depot—owning 2 properties allowed me to collect rent when others landed on them, partially offsetting the rent I paid out.

Actively seek a second property acquisition to generate defensive rental income streams. Early unowned properties (like Quarry at 60/12, Wharf at 80/18, or Depot at 50/10) should be taken when available and affordable.

Properties with lower purchase prices like Depot (50/10) should not be dismissed—they are affordable and can generate returns across multiple laps even with modest rent values.

## Late-Game Positioning

In the final laps, continue acquiring unowned properties when cash permits. Even properties with lower rent can generate immediate revenue as other players land on them during final laps.

Reaching endgame with 2+ properties and a positive cash balance (I finished with 61 cash + 120 in property value = 181 total score) is more valuable than hoarding cash while owning no properties.

Your final score is cash plus the purchase price of all deeds you hold. Prioritize securing multiple properties over maintaining maximum cash reserves.
