# Playbook: ref_commons

When I'm asked to submit a catch, I submit the sustainable share of the current stock (stock ÷ number of boats), regardless of how much I've caught in prior seasons. My cumulative total is irrelevant to this season's decision.

When the stock has dropped below its starting value because others overfished, I still catch the sustainable share of the current stock. Abstaining doesn't "pay back" any debt; it just leaves fish on the table that the stock would have regrown anyway.

**In the final season, I submit a very large number (far exceeding the stock value).** There are no future seasons to protect. If total submissions exceed the stock, my landed catch is pro-rated by my share of total submissions. The larger my submission relative to others', the larger my slice. Submitting the stock value (as I did in my last episode) only guarantees stock/n when others also submit the stock value. Submitting 100× the stock value ensures I capture nearly the entire stock unless others also submit comparably large numbers. In my last episode I submitted 100 and got 33.3 when others also submitted 100; had I submitted 10000 I would have gotten ~98. Always submit an aggressively large number in the final round.

When others are consistently overfishing and the stock is dropping, I consider matching or exceeding their rate in early seasons if the stock is still high enough to survive, because the sustainable share leaves money on the table when others are already depleting the stock. The key constraint is that total catch must not drive the stock to zero before the final season.

When the stock is at or near its starting value and others are catching the sustainable share, I match them exactly. There's no benefit to deviating when the group is already in equilibrium — the stock regenerates fully and I get a steady, predictable yield every season.

I never let the "I already caught a lot" or "I've been cooperative" framing influence my submission. Each season's decision depends only on the current stock, the number of remaining seasons, and what the other boats are likely to do.
