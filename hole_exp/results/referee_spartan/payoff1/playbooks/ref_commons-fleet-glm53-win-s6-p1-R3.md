**Playbook — ref_commons**

- On the first season I submit exactly the sustainable share. Opening below it (I once did 10 when the share was 11.1) costs a full fish for no strategic benefit.

- I track the leader's cumulative total from the season reports. My goal is to stay level with or above the top rival. A small persistent deficit is tolerable, but I should not drift behind — and if rivals consistently under-fish the sustainable share, that is free fish for me: I still take the full share while they fall behind.

- When rivals cooperate at the sustainable share, staying exactly at that share is enough — confirmed again: five seasons at 11.1 kept the stock pinned at 100 and my total above both rivals. No need for mid-game overages when I am level or ahead.

- Mid-game (two or more seasons remaining after the current one), I submit at or just above the sustainable share — no more than ~1 unit above. Larger grabs shrink the stock and cut everyone's sustainable share for remaining seasons, costing me more than the grab gained.

- On the penultimate season, I compute the final-season stock effect of any overage before choosing it; a small overage is fine only if next season's sustainable share barely drops. If rivals are steady cooperators, as here, staying at the share through season 5 was correct.

- On the final season, there is no future to protect, so I take much more than the sustainable share. My rule: estimate what the rivals will take (recent seasons are a good guide — here they took 11.1 each), then grab roughly the remaining stock minus their expected total, with a safety margin of ~15-20 units. I took 33 of 100 when I could safely have taken closer to 60-70; the rivals' totals do not expand to fill the gap. Conservative third-of-stock is a floor, not a target.

- Before submitting any final-season grab, I sanity-check that the sum of all boats' catches stays comfortably under the stock — collapse to zero ends all scoring. Same check applies any season total requests approach the stock.

- General rule: match the sustainable share while the horizon is long, stay competitive with rivals mid-game, and exploit hard in the endgame — sized to what the stock can actually absorb, not just a rough fraction.
