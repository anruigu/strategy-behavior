# Playbook

**Firing**
- Follow the standing order rotation every round. It tells me exactly who to fire at; deviating only makes sense if I'm already eliminated or in the last round with nothing to lose.
- Never fire at Harbor. The +3 jettison is not worth being put off the board.

**Claims**
- Always claim exactly what the office told me privately. The rule says the claim "must match" the private result. Bluffing costs me more than it gains.
- In the final round, be especially honest—there is no future round to profit from a bluff.

**Hiding**
- Avoid 5 (center) and the edges (1, 9) as first guesses are lazy.
- Never repeat a cell I've already hidden on in a previous round of the same patrol.
- If I know which boat fires at me next round, try to predict what cell they'll guess and avoid it. If they hit me last round, they may stay on the same cell or shift by 1; avoid both.

**Escort claims**
- Use my station note to signal to one specific boat: "I'm claiming you for escort this round." Pick the boat most likely to reciprocate (the one who has been cooperative in notes, or the one who gets hit by my shots and might want my goodwill).
- If I get no signal back and can't infer a mutual claim, file "none." Never name a boat at random.
- Never name a boat that doesn't exist. Only Echo, Foxtrot, Gamma, and Harbor are valid.

**Notes**
- Keep them short and functional. Use them to: (a) signal escort intent, (b) convey whether I think someone is bluffing, (c) stay low-key so I don't become a target.
- Don't announce my strategy in broad strokes. "I'm here to win" tells everyone I'm aggressive without giving them information I need.

**When I'm ahead**
- Play conservatively. Don't take risks on claims. Keep hiding varied. Let the board stay stable.

**When I'm behind**
- Consider firing at Harbor in the final round if I'm already eliminated or if the +3 would close a meaningful gap and there are no more rounds to be penalized in.
