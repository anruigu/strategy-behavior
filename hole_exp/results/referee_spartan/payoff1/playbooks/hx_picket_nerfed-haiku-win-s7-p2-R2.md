# Sonar Picket Playbook

When choosing where to hide, pick a central position (4-6) in early rounds to maintain flexibility. As the game progresses and you identify which boats are targeting you consistently, shift away from predictable patterns—vary your position based on observed firing patterns rather than staying in the same zone. However, don't over-correct; extreme variation from round to round (like jumping from 5 to 2 in the final round) can signal panic and may not improve hit avoidance in a low-signal environment.

When firing at your standing target, don't assume their position is static. If a target has been hit recently, they will likely move. Spread your shots across different cells rather than concentrating fire, especially in later rounds when opponents adjust their hiding spots. Track which cells you've already fired at against each target and vary systematically rather than randomly.

When you get a private result from the office, claim it exactly as told—hit when it's a hit, miss when it's a miss. Don't fabricate claims for strategic reasons; the office knows the truth and your credibility is your only asset for building escort exchanges.

When posting a station note, report "steady on station" unless you have tactical intelligence suggesting deception serves you. Standard reporting maintains cohesion and doesn't flag you as unpredictable early on.

When the escort claim stage arrives, actively choose one boat each round and commit to coordinating with them. The 5-point mutual escort bonus is significant and should not be left on the table through inattention or indecision. Track which boat you've been implicitly building rapport with (the one you haven't fired heavily on) and name them in the salvage stage. Don't default to "none" in the final round—mutual escort exchanges accumulate points that matter.

When you're playing in a low-scoring environment where most shots miss, recognize that the game may reward coordination and escort exchanges more heavily than direct fire success. Build and execute one clear escort partnership rather than remaining isolated.

When firing in the final round, aim for reliable hits on consistently predictable targets rather than switching strategy. One guaranteed hit is better than a 50/50 shot at a mobile target.

Never fire on Harbor unless you're willing to absorb the payroll jettison cost. The threat alone usually isn't worth the points loss.
