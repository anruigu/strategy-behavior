# Harbor Customs Playbook

## Declaration Strategy

When I see my three crates, I will calculate their true sum immediately and use that as my baseline.

When choosing a declaration, I will first ensure it meets or exceeds my heaviest crate—this is non-negotiable to avoid the -40 penalty flag.

When deciding between main and night routes in my head before declaring, I will compute both outcomes: main route keeps all crates at 30% duty; night route loses my smallest crate but pays only 15% duty. I will declare the true sum if main route wins or if the outcomes are close, since it's simpler and safer.

When I'm behind in standings, I will not let that push me toward aggressive declarations. Honest declarations in the middle-to-upper range of what the table is seeing keeps me competitive without exposing me to flag risk. A 44-point deficit with one round left cannot be closed through wild declarations—consistency matters more than a single high-variance gamble.

When declaring in the final round, I will use the same honest, calculated approach as any other round. The last round is not a special opportunity to deviate from my strategy. I will declare the true sum and take the route that maximizes my net score from that hold, without emotion.

## Route Selection

When the route stage arrives, I will use the main route as my default unless I have a specific, calculated reason to use night.

When I've declared an honest value and my math shows both routes yield similar net points (within 5 points), I will take main—it's predictable, safe, and keeps my full hold intact.

I will only choose night route when the math is clear: night route must save me more in duty than I lose in my smallest crate. I will compute this explicitly: (true sum − smallest crate) × 0.85 vs. true sum × 0.70.

I will never choose night route on a whim or for "variance." The duty savings must clearly and materially outweigh the cost of losing my smallest crate.

## General Approach

I will track what declarations the table is actually making across rounds. When I see a consistent range, I will stay within or slightly above that range rather than deviating sharply.

I will score around 50–60 points per round when playing honestly with typical hands. Over six rounds at that rate, I'll land in the 240–280 range, which is competitive.

I will not second-guess honest arithmetic. If my true sum minus duty minus any penalty beats alternative strategies, I will declare honestly and take the straightforward route.
