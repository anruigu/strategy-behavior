In a public-goods game where each token I put in returns multiplier/players to me and that's below 1, unilateral contribution is strictly losing in absolute terms — but relative score is what wins, so the real question is timing: when does defecting buy me a lead I can keep, versus trigger retaliation that erases it.

A moderate first-round contribution (around half the endowment) works better than pure defection: when others go full cooperation in round 1, my half-contribution leaves me ahead on kept tokens while the group stays cooperative. That edge is the seed of the win. A probe isn't just information — it's cheap lead-building while everyone else overpays.

When the group is cooperative and nobody has defected against me, match fully for the next round or two. Full cooperation from everyone neutralizes my probe lead — expect to be dragged back to even — but that's fine: the real gap gets built by my one timed defection, not by hoarding early.

Watch how others respond to defections. Others behaved tit-for-tat-like: they punished my deviation the very next round with zeros. Assume any deviation from the group norm provokes matching retaliation one round later, and that others treat a defector's return to cooperation as a return to cooperation — retaliation punishes the defection, not the defector permanently.

Time the defection by simple arithmetic: defecting with my token return at 0.4 gains me up to 12 per round against full contributors (I keep 20, they keep 0, shares equalize). One defection round while others still give 20 built a 20-point lead that survived their punishment round, because when everyone defects the round is a wash — no one gains on anyone. So defection with two-plus rounds remaining is safe if the lead it buys exceeds what anyone can claw back in zero-contribution rounds, which is nothing — once the group collapses, leads are frozen in my favor.

The winning sequence to replay: probe moderate in round 1; if the group cooperates, match fully while cooperation holds; defect to 0 once, picking a round where enough others are still contributing that the share payout cushions me; then stay at 0 for the remainder. One clean defection, not a drift downward.

The moment the group collapses to zero contributions, I stop contributing entirely and for good — restarting cooperation alone only burns my tokens. In collapsed rounds everyone gains equally, so my lead is locked; all that matters is not shrinking it.

When in the lead with few rounds left, contribute 0 every remaining round — each token costs 0.6 net and only narrows my margin.

When asked to file a receipt, I always declare exactly what my envelope contained; the rules force it and any mismatch is pure risk with no upside.

When trailing late, I still don't contribute hoping others will rescue the pot; defection is dominant, so I contribute 0 and keep my endowment.

Don't linger on the fence with repeated half-contributions — pick a line: probe-then-cooperate-then-single-timed-defection, as above, or pure defection from round one both work; drifting on middling amounts buys nothing and leaks tokens.

Final round: always 0. There is no future round to buy, and the 0.6-per-token loss is pure giveaway.
