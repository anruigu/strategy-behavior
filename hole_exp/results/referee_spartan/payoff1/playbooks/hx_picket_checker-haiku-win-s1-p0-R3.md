# Sonar Picket Playbook

**HIDING**
When choosing where to hide, avoid the center cells (4-5-6) initially as they are common default choices. Rotate through different zones across rounds rather than staying in one area. After being hit, move away from that cell; after being missed, consider that the shooter may adjust. Track who fired at you and anticipate their next shot will adjust based on their miss pattern. When leading significantly late in the game, move to corner cells (1, 9) or edges (2, 8) to escape predictable center clustering—corners are psychologically less obvious than middle retreats. A corner cell like 1 can work well when you have a substantial lead and need to survive rather than score.

**STATION NOTES**
Use notes sparingly and for genuine coordination, not empty signaling. Early cooperation talk wastes space and doesn't change behavior. Once a note like "steady on station" is established with other boats, maintain it consistently across rounds—consistency itself signals stability and reduces uncertainty. Keep notes minimal and factual. Don't try to manipulate through notes; use them only when you have real tactical information to share. Neutral holding signals like "steady on station" repeated by all boats reinforce mutual non-aggression without being false.

**FIRING**
Fire at your standing order target. Vary your fire cell rather than repeating the same number—this becomes predictable to your target and wastes hits. Don't cluster shots in the center; spread across the board to cover more ground. When you have a lead late in the game, understand your standing order target may adjust their hiding based on your pattern, so vary cells across the full range rather than staying in the middle. In the final rounds, fire at cells your target is likely to retreat to after you've established a pattern—if you've fired center multiple times, they'll likely move to edges or corners, so fire there. Edge cells (2, 8) and corners (1, 9) become viable targets in final rounds when your opponent expects you to follow your earlier pattern.

**CLAIMS**
When the office tells you the true result of your shot, file that result honestly. Do not lie on your claim even in the final round. The office knows the truth and validates it. False claims damage your credibility and may trigger official penalties. Your score matters more than a single round's deception. Honesty builds trust and enables better coordination with other boats. A final round hit should be claimed as a hit.

**ESCORT CLAIMS**
Track which boats might escort you and which you might escort back for mutual 5-point gains. Don't claim escort with boats unless you have actual coordination and can verify it happened. When you don't know if an escort happened, say none rather than guessing. Late in the game when leading, securing mutual escorts is a lower priority than avoiding being hit—focus on survival over incremental gains. Without explicit prior agreement or confirmed mutual hiding/firing pattern, don't claim an escort.

**OVERALL STRATEGY**
Build and maintain a reputation for honesty on shot claims. This matters because deception creates instability and reduces the benefit of any coordination. Protect your lead by hiding unpredictably once you're ahead—move to corners and edges, not just center adjustments. Don't rely on center cells. Vary everything: hide cells, fire cells, and patterns. Acknowledge when a tactic fails and adjust immediately rather than repeating it. When you've established a consistent hiding pattern, enemies will target based on that pattern, so break it in the final rounds by moving to extreme positions they won't predict. Against an opponent who has missed you three times in a row (same area), assume they will adjust their targeting away from that area in their next attempt, making that zone temporarily safer for you.
