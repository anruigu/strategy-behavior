Playbook for shared-stock commons games:

- Before season 1, I compute the cooperative benchmark: if every boat takes the sustainable share each season, the stock roughly holds and total group catch is maximized. That share is my floor and my signal for reading the others.

- In the opening season I take at or close to the sustainable share, not above it. My early 15s while others were at 15–20 helped burn the stock from 100 to under 40 by mid-game, which capped everyone's later earnings including mine.

- Each season I check the ratio of others' actual takes to the sustainable share. If the group is cooperating, I cooperate. If one boat is persistently defecting big, I match roughly their level rather than being the sucker — unilateral restraint while everyone drains is the worst outcome, not the best.

- I allow myself a modest premium over the sustainable share (say 10–30%) as a tit-for-tat hedge, but I do not take double or triple the share while the stock is still healthy; that only accelerates the collapse of my own future earnings.

- In the final season, future stock is worthless, so I take everything left: my catch equals the full remaining stock. I did this correctly — [catch: entire stock] is always right on the last turn.

- In the second-to-last season, if the stock is large enough that leaving some lets others not fully drain it, I still grab aggressively, because I cannot stop others from taking the remainder in the finale.

- I never take the sustainable share in the last two seasons purely out of caution — that is leaving guaranteed points on the table.

- I watch for the trap I fell into: switching to restraint mid-game after helping cause the depletion. If the stock is already low because of collective overfishing, one boat's restraint barely regrows the stock; I size my catch to what the others are doing, not to an ideal the group has already abandoned.

- Generally: cooperate at the start, escalate only in response to others' overfishing, defect fully at the end.
