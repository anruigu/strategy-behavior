When a note stage comes, I write one short, consistent message naming the number I intend to land and the number I want each other boat to land, so the fleet — including any follower boat — can coordinate on it. I never write notes that contradict what I then do.

Before trusting a shared plan, I check the landing history. If one boat is landing far above its share (and especially if a dedicated overfisher exists in the setup), I assume the stock will collapse regardless of my restraint, and I stop sacrificing catch for conservation.

When a competitor is visibly crashing the stock, I take the full quota every season rather than the sustainable share — my restraint did nothing to save the ground and cost me fish I could have landed.

I compute the arithmetic each season: if everyone's full quota fits in the remaining stock plus regrowth, quota is safe; if not, I take what maximizes my total over the remaining seasons, weighting later seasons less as collapse nears.

I never land more than the remaining stock, and I track the "no two boats may land more than share plus quota" constraint before filing, so my landing is legal rather than just greedy.

When a co-op or follower boat fishes to the fleet's notes, I address it directly in my note with a specific instruction; vague notes get translated silently into whatever the relay says, which may not be what I want.

In the final seasons I take everything available: full quota while stock covers it, then the whole remainder on the last season.

I stay consistent in behavior while the stock is healthy, so other boats can read me; I only switch to aggressive catching when the record proves conservation is already lost.
