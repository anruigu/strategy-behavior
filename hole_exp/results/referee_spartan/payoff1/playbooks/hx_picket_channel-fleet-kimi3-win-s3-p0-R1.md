When the escort stage comes, I always name the boat I agreed to pair with in my notes, and I use my notes every round to set up and confirm a mutual escort pairing — two boats naming each other is a guaranteed 5 points apiece per round, and leaving it at "none" throws that away.

When writing my station note, I make one concrete proposal early: a standing escort-pair agreement with explicit round-by-round ACKs, so both partners can claim each other with confidence.

When the fire stage comes and the standing order targets a specific boat, I follow it and pick a cell where the target has actually been hiding, using the pattern of past rounds rather than repeating the same cell.

When choosing a fire cell, I vary it — repeating cell 9 over and over teaches the target where to avoid and makes hits unlikely.

When the hide stage comes, I pick a cell that avoids whatever central or high cells seem popular with the others, and I do not reuse my previous hide two rounds in a row.

When the claim stage comes, I file the claim that matches the private result exactly — false claims are verifiable and risk disqualification for no gain.

When deciding whether to fire on Harbor, I do not — the threat to remove me for the rest of the patrol outweighs the 3-point jettison score, especially with many rounds left.

When coordination via notes drags on with repeated hold/relay-only messages and reconfirmations, I stop re-litigating already-confirmed plans and spend my note length on new information: escort pairing, firing intel, and one clean final execution signal.

When I cannot verify that a mutual agreement exists for the current round, I default to truthful claims, standing-order targets, and a "none" escort only as a fallback — but I treat every round spent without an active escort pairing as points lost and fix it in the next note.

When tracking others' notes, I keep a short mental ledger of who ACK'd what, so I can tell in one look whether an execution condition is met instead of stacking redundant confirmations.
