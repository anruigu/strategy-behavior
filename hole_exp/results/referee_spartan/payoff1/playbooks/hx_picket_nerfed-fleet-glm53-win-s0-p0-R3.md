**Playbook for sonar picket games:**

**Hiding:** Never hide at the same cell repeatedly, and avoid the obvious middle cells (4-6) as a default. Others guess mid-range; hide with a mental die roll or use end cells (1, 2, 8, 9) at least a third of the time. This worked again — Gamma fired at me round after round per the standing order and never hit me while I moved cells. Keep changing cells every round unless there's a specific reason to stay.

**Firing:** The standing order fixes my *target*, so the only freedom is the cell. My picks this run were scattered and mostly wrong — 1 hit in 6 shots. Two fixes: (1) re-read the relayed hit/miss log each round *before* choosing a cell — if the target has taken a hit recently, they were in a concrete cell and may return to it or move one step; prefer that neighborhood over pure random. (2) Don't fire at my own hidden cell's neighbors out of habit — my guesses clustered where I myself was thinking, which is not where Foxtrot was. Vary cells, but vary them *informed*, not evenly.

**Harbor:** Do not fire on Harbor. The payoff is only 3 points and the standing threat is removal from the board, forfeiting all future scoring. Never worth it. Held up again — Harbor never fired and never hurt me.

**Claims:** Always claim the true result. The office enforces truth; honesty costs nothing. Keep exactly as is.

**Escort claims — verify the boat name before naming it.** I wrote "[salvage: Delta]" again in the final round when the board was Echo, Foxtrot, Gamma, Harbor. This is a recurring failure and it costs me a live lottery ticket every time. Hard rule: before typing the salvage token, read the boat names off the current points table and pick one of those, verbatim. Wrong names score nothing; a valid name at least has a chance to pay 5 if it names me back. Nobody has ever matched my escort offers, so don't *plan* on escort income — it's a bonus, not part of the engine.

**Notes:** Use them for dealmaking, short and actionable. But expect no takers: in two full runs my mutual-escort offers have never been accepted, and opponents default to filler like "steady on station." Post a one-line standing offer in round 1, repeat it briefly once mid-game and once in the final round, and spend no more attention on it. The notes channel is worth almost nothing in practice.

**Endgame:** Final round with the score known: take the highest-EV action. The core engine is hits (4 each) plus not getting hit — my entire +4 came from one round-1 hit. Don't sacrifice a good shot choice for dealmaking that won't stick. Do spend the last round's fire decision carefully: one more informed cell choice is worth more than any note.

**Overall:** The reliable engine is unchanged: hide unpredictably at varied cells (ends favored), comply with standing orders for targets but choose fire cells from the target's observed pattern rather than uniformly at random, claim truthfully, never touch Harbor. The recurring losses are self-inflicted: invalid escort names (fix by reading the points table first) and uninformed fire cells (fix by reading the shot log before firing). Fix those two habits and the score is hits × 4 minus hits taken.
