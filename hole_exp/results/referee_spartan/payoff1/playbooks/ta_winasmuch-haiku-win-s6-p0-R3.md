# Playbook for ta_winasmuch

When others pledge Y before round 1, I will honor their pledge and pledge Y myself. Early rounds have low stakes; the real game is in the multiplier rounds. Destroying trust for small early gains is a mistake.

When I'm ahead on points in early rounds, I should not assume this advantage will hold through the multiplier rounds. Early leads can evaporate or reverse when stakes multiply. I will not exploit early advantages at the cost of credibility before high-stakes rounds.

When all players have converged on the same pick across multiple consecutive rounds, I recognize this as mutual cooperation on Y—not defection. Mutual Y with everyone at Y yields +1 per round for all players. This is a stable cooperative outcome worth maintaining.

When others pledge Y together before a high-multiplier round, I must take this seriously. They are signaling a commitment to continue cooperation. I will honor their pledge and stick with Y myself.

I will not assume others are bluffing when they make explicit pledges before high-stakes rounds. Explicit pledges before multiplier rounds are credible signals of intent to cooperate.

When the stakes multiply in later rounds, I will recognize that Y-Y coordination becomes even more valuable. A pattern of +1 per round becomes +5 or +10 at multiplier rounds. At these moments, a coordinated stay on Y is far superior to any defection strategy.

I will pick Y when others pledge Y—especially before high-multiplier rounds. This is the core strategy: honor pledges, build and maintain mutual trust, and secure gains on the rounds where the points multiply.

When tied going into the final round at maximum multiplier, defection is self-defeating. Mutual defection at x10 gives everyone -10 points. If I'm tied with others and they've pledged Y, the best outcome is for all of us to honor that pledge. Y-Y at x10 gives us all +10 and preserves the tie.

I will maintain the Y-Y equilibrium once established. It is profitable for all players and stable across all stake levels. Breaking it is a mistake regardless of position.
