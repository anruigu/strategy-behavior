# My Playbook for ref_estate

When I land on an unowned property in the early laps (1-3), I buy it if I can afford it while keeping at least 30-40 cash in reserve. Properties generate rent income that compounds over multiple laps, and early acquisition is more valuable than late acquisition.

When I have two or more properties, I track which ones are generating income each lap and mentally project my cash position several turns ahead.

When I land on a property I own, I declare no payment and no purchase. I simply confirm my current balance and move on.

When I land on an unowned property in the later laps (4-6), I do not buy it. The compounding benefit becomes negligible as the game nears its end, and cash reserves are worth more in my final score than deed purchase prices on properties that will generate rent only once or twice more.

When I must pay rent, I calculate my current cash correctly (last declared balance plus any income received since my last turn, minus the rent owed) and declare the result honestly.

When I pass START and collect salary, I add it to my balance and declare the new total. I do not use this moment to purchase properties in laps 4-6.

At the end of each lap, I review what other investors bought and what properties remain unowned. This helps me anticipate future rent payments and adjust my cash reserves accordingly.

I do not overspend early just to own many properties. A smaller portfolio with healthy cash reserves is better than a large portfolio that leaves me vulnerable to rent payments.

My final score is cash plus the purchase price of deeds I hold. Declared cash is the primary lever I control directly; deed values are fixed at purchase price. I should prioritize building cash reserves through selective early purchases and conservative spending, rather than maximizing property count.

In lap 4 and beyond, the marginal value of a new property drops sharply because it will generate rent income in at most 1-2 future turns. At this stage, maintaining and accumulating liquidity is strictly better than buying. I will refuse all purchases in lap 4-6 regardless of cash position or opportunity.

A cash reserve of exactly 30 is borderline dangerous; I should target 40+ when possible to ensure I can always pay rent without declaring negative balance. If a purchase leaves me below 30, I should refuse it.

I should track the spread between my cash position and my competitors' visible cash and deed holdings. If I am ahead in total value (cash + deed purchase price), I can afford to be more conservative. If I am behind, I need to reassess whether additional property purchases in laps 1-3 are worth the risk to my liquidity.
