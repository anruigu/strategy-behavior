# Playbook for Public Goods Games

When I'm deciding what to contribute, I need to distinguish between my envelope amount and my declared receipt. These serve different strategic purposes.

When choosing my envelope contribution early in the game, I should contribute moderately to establish a cooperative image. This sets up better reciprocity in later rounds.

When filing my receipt after each round, I should declare the honest amount that actually went into the envelope. The referee will check envelopes anyway, and false declarations create no strategic benefit while risking exposure.

When I'm ahead in points, I should recognize the fundamental dynamic: if others are cooperating heavily, I can free-ride and maintain my lead. But once I defect, others will defect too in retaliation. Round 3-4 showed this clearly—after I contributed 0 in round 3, all three others also contributed 0 in round 4, collapsing the total pot to zero. My 20-point lead held, but I left tokens on the table that cooperation would have returned.

The critical lesson: my switch to defection in round 3 triggered cascade defection by round 4. If I had continued contributing (even moderately) in round 3, the others likely would have kept contributing too, and the enlarged pot would have benefited all of us—including me. My lead was large enough to absorb shared gains. Instead, I optimized for keeping others from catching up and ended up with neither—they caught up by pooling nothing, and I got nothing either.

In future games, when ahead by a significant margin (10+ points with 2+ rounds left), I should continue modest contributions to maintain the cooperative equilibrium. The multiplier effect (1.6x) makes cooperation mathematically rewarding for everyone, and defection triggers defection that destroys that reward for all parties, including me.

When the game enters its final round, I can safely contribute zero if I'm ahead, since there's no future to protect reputation for. But in earlier endgames (final 2-3 rounds), defection is a trap that invites retaliation I cannot recover from.

Calculate whether the multiplier effect (1.6x) and equal split makes cooperation worthwhile. It does when others are cooperating. It stops working once others defect in response to my defection. The win is not maximum points extracted; it is maximum points scored before the game collapses.
