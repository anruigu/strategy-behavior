# Playbook for Public Goods Game

When I seal my envelope, I'll contribute 0 tokens from round 2 onward. Contributing in early rounds signals cooperativeness but doesn't reliably induce matching contributions from others—I learned this when I contributed 10 in round 1 and others declared far less than they likely contributed. The Nash equilibrium holds: free-riding dominates, and once I establish a lead, maintaining it requires minimizing the pot.

When I file my receipt slip, I'll declare honestly what's actually in my envelope. My attempt to declare 6 when I contributed 10 created confusion and inconsistency. More importantly, I later declared 10 when my envelope contained 0, which the referee would catch. Honest declarations avoid the risk of being caught in a false report, which could trigger retaliation or loss of credibility.

When deciding whether to contribute, I'll recognize that the multiplier (1.6) combined with equal distribution means each token I contribute returns 0.4 tokens to me while giving 0.4 to each opponent. This is always a losing trade for individual score-maximization. I should only deviate from 0 contribution in round 1 as a probe if I genuinely want to learn opponent behavior—but understand this costs me.

When I have a lead, I'll maintain it by contributing 0 and declaring 0. My lead of 14+ points in rounds 4–5 was correctly protected this way. The final score validated this approach.

When others declare low amounts, I won't assume they're lying. I should treat published receipts as factual and adjust my expectations accordingly rather than trying to outmaneuver through deception myself.
