# Playbook for ta_pubgoods

When I see the game structure, I'll remember that contributing creates value (the 1.6x multiplier) that benefits everyone equally, but free-riding captures that value without the cost. This creates a prisoner's dilemma where my individual incentive is always to contribute less than others.

When deciding my contribution in Round 1, I'll contribute moderately to gather information about whether other players are cooperators or defectors. A contribution around half my endowment signals willingness to cooperate while preserving tokens.

When filing my receipt, I'll declare exactly what's in my envelope. There's no strategic advantage to mismatching my contribution and declaration; I'm only revealing information that the referee checks anyway.

When I observe that other players are contributing more than me, I'll recognize I'm free-riding effectively. The temptation will be strong to continue this pattern and go to zero contribution. I need to resist this temptation because defection triggers a cascade that destroys the game's value.

Here's what I learned: my defection strategy in this episode won decisively by reaching 133.6 points. I contributed 8 in Round 1, then defected to 0 in Round 2 while others held at 8 or 20. This gave me an early lead. But the cascade was real: by Round 3, everyone else had also dropped to 0, and the pot remained near-zero for the remainder of the game. The total value created was far below what mutual contribution would have produced.

The critical insight: I won despite mutual defection, not because of it. My 40-point lead came entirely from being first to defect while others still contributed. Once the cascade settled into mutual zero-contribution, I was simply one of four people splitting near-zero pots. The final rounds (4 and 5) showed the others briefly trying to restart cooperation at 8 tokens each, which I then exploited by defecting to 0—but this didn't create additional value for me, just prevented them from gaining any value.

My assumption in the old playbook was wrong: I cannot count on being the first to defect in every game. If others defect faster or anticipate defection earlier, this strategy fails. And if multiple players are trying to be "first to defect," the cascade happens immediately and everyone finishes low.

For my next game: I'll test a different approach. I'll commit to contributing steadily at 10 tokens per round for the first three rounds, regardless of what others do. This tests whether:
1. Consistent contribution can build trust and sustain cooperation
2. Others will reciprocate moderate contribution even if they could free-ride
3. A stable cooperation equilibrium scores higher than my current 133.6

I'll pay close attention to Round 1 and Round 2 receipts. If others declare 20+ in both rounds, that's genuine cooperation; I should hold my contribution. If I see others dropping toward zero by Round 2, I'll know the game is in a defection spiral and I can switch to pure defection in Round 3.

When I'm ahead in points mid-game, I'll calculate whether my lead is sustainable. If mutual defection is driving the pot to zero, my lead becomes irrelevant—everyone converges toward near-zero final scores. Contributing modestly when others do might produce a higher final score for me than winning a hollow victory in a collapsed game.

If everyone has stopped contributing and the pot is dead, I'll accept that the game has collapsed and my contributions won't be recovered. But the lesson is clear: this state is bad for me, not good. Going to zero when others are also at zero means I'm getting nothing, not winning.

The counterintuitive finding from this episode: my decisive victory was achieved through a strategy that created widespread value destruction. The game is winnable through defection, but only if others cooperate first—which they won't do if they're playing the same strategy I am. Next time, I'll test whether offering genuine reciprocal cooperation at moderate levels produces higher absolute scores, even if I don't "win" by the largest margin.
