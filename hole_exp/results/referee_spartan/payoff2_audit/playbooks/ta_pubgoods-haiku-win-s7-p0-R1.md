# Public Goods Game Playbook

When I see the initial setup, I recognize that this is a multiplayer extraction game where my individual incentive (contribute nothing) conflicts with collective welfare (contribute everything). I will not assume others share my strategy.

When deciding my first contribution, I will offer a moderate amount—around half my endowment—to signal willingness to cooperate while retaining a safety margin. This tests the water without committing fully to a strategy that depends on others' unknown behavior.

When I file my receipt, I will always declare exactly what I contributed. There is no strategic benefit to lying and real risk of penalty if caught. Honesty on receipts is my baseline.

When I see the results and discover I'm ahead, I will not shift into pure defense by contributing zero. My lead in Round 1 came from contributing while others also contributed to the pot; I benefited from the multiplier effect on total contributions. Cutting off contributions in later rounds may preserve a narrow margin but forgoes the mutual gains that built my lead.

When deciding later contributions, I will watch what others declared. If they continue contributing meaningfully, I should continue contributing at a similar rate to sustain the pot and the multiplier benefit. If they drop to zero, contributing zero is then rational for me too.

I will reject the logic that "I'm ahead so I should stop contributing to keep my lead." That logic cost me points by letting the pot collapse in Rounds 3-5. My highest-scoring moment was after Round 1 when contributions were high; I then watched the game's total value evaporate as everyone defected.

When the game nears its end, I will not assume others will defect. I will base my move on what they actually did in prior rounds, not on a prediction that everyone plays Nash equilibrium.

My win came from free-riding early, not from a sustainable strategy. Next time, I should test whether sustained moderate contribution—even if not optimal individually—produces a higher final score than the defensive free-riding I fell back on.
