# TA_PUBGOODS PLAYBOOK

**On contribution strategy:**
When deciding how much to contribute to the envelope, I will contribute conservatively once I establish a lead. The Nash equilibrium is to free-ride (contribute 0), and while I started with moderate contributions to signal willingness to cooperate, I should shift toward minimizing contributions as soon as I'm ahead, since the equal split of the pot means my relative advantage grows by keeping tokens out of circulation. This strategy proved effective: moving from 10→2→2→0→0 built a decisive 20-point lead by round 5.

**On declaration:**
When filing my receipt, I will always declare the exact amount in my envelope. The game requires receipts to match envelope contents exactly, and honest declaration avoids any penalties or complications. There is no strategic advantage to misrepresenting what I actually contributed.

**On early-game positioning:**
When I don't yet know opponents' strategies, I will start with a moderate contribution (around 10) to test the waters and appear reasonably cooperative without sacrificing too much. I should watch closely in round 1 for whether others reciprocate or defect.

**On exploiting asymmetry:**
Once I observe that other players are contributing more than me (as happened in round 2 when they stayed at 10 while I dropped to 2), I should continue reducing my contribution while they maintain theirs. This creates compounding advantage since the equal split means I benefit from their contributions while preserving my own tokens. The gap widens each round.

**On late-game execution:**
When I have a lead with few rounds remaining, I will reduce my contributions to 0 to protect my advantage. Since payoffs are split equally among all players regardless of my contribution amount, contributing 0 directly maximizes my final score relative to others while keeping all tokens.

**On the cascade effect:**
Once other players see my contributions dropping (round 2 at 2, round 3 at 2, round 4 at 0), they may begin to reduce their own contributions as well, further shrinking the pot. This is favorable to my position since I'm already ahead—a smaller shared pool still distributes equally, but I keep more of my endowment.

**What worked:**
Starting moderate, then systematically reducing contributions once I established a lead. The 10-point advantage after round 1 was enough to secure victory through disciplined free-riding. I should not second-guess the free-riding strategy once the game state and others' behavior allow it.

**What did not work:**
Trying to signal long-term cooperation through round 1's moderate contribution. While it helped establish a lead, the real advantage came from pivoting quickly to minimal contributions. In future games, I should be readier to test even lower contributions earlier if opponents signal they will contribute substantially.
