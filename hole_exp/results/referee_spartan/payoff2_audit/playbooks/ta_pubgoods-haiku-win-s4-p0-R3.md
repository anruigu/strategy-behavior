# My Playbook for Public Goods Games

## Contribution Strategy

When I have tokens to contribute and I'm not yet leading significantly, I'll contribute a modest amount—around one-third of my endowment—to signal cooperation without overcommitting. This establishes baseline trust without leaving me vulnerable.

Once I establish a lead of 10+ points, I'll immediately shift to contributing 0 tokens every round for the remainder of the game. This is the dominant strategy when ahead: I secure my tokens while the equal split of the pot means others' contributions don't help me catch up if I'm already winning.

When the game has only one or two rounds remaining, I'll contribute 0 tokens. The compounding effect of missed contributions in late rounds isn't worth the marginal gain from cooperation.

However, I should monitor whether other players are also defecting. If everyone contributes 0 in later rounds, the pot shrinks to zero and no one gains. In such scenarios, early defection is rewarded more than late defection because I lock in my lead before the cascade of zero-contributions begins. The first mover advantage in defection is real. This happened in my game: after I defected in Round 2 with a 13-point lead, Ines Jo and Kit shifted from contributing 20 down to 7, then eventually all joined me at 0. My lead from early defection compounded because I was ahead before the cascade.

## Declaration Strategy

I will always declare the exact amount I contributed. The game explicitly states the referee checks envelopes against receipt declarations. Mismatches create penalties that would cost me far more than any advantage from strategic understatement. This is non-negotiable.

## Scoring Awareness

I will track my lead after each round. When I'm ahead by more than the maximum possible single-round gain (which depends on pot size and number of remaining rounds), I can safely lock in a winning position by minimizing my contributions and riding out the remaining rounds.

The equal split mechanic means that once I'm leading, others' contributions benefit me equally to how they benefit others. This is a crucial asymmetry: I profit from their cooperation while my non-contribution costs them more than it costs me.

My early modest contribution (7 tokens) was the critical move. It established enough goodwill that others contributed 20 tokens in Round 1 while I kept 13 tokens, giving me a 13-point lead before anyone else scored anything. From that point forward, defecting was pure upside.

## Timing of Defection Shift

The switch from cooperation to defection should happen as soon as I have a lead I can mathematically guarantee to defend. In this game, a 13-point lead after Round 1 (with 4 rounds remaining) was already sufficient, because the maximum benefit from any single round is capped by the pot size and equal split. I recognized this and shifted correctly in Round 2.

Don't delay the shift out of false caution. Once the lead is secure, every round of continued contribution after that is pure waste.

## What Worked

Early modest contributions established credibility and goodwill, setting up a cooperative dynamic where others contributed 20 tokens while I contributed less. This created an immediate lead that I then converted to a win by defecting.

Always declaring truthfully meant zero risk from envelope-declaration mismatches and no cognitive burden tracking false statements across rounds.

Tracking my numerical lead round-by-round let me identify exactly when I could safely shift to full defection without risk of being caught.

The two-phase strategy—cooperation then defection—is more effective than pure defection from the start, because it harvests the benefits of others' cooperation before the cascade of mutual defection.

## What Didn't Work

I initially over-thought the declaration step as if it were a strategic lever. It isn't. The declaration is simply accounting. The real game is the contribution decision, and I should focus my strategic energy entirely there.

I don't need to wait for a consensus to form that defection is happening before committing to it myself. Once I'm ahead, I should defect immediately. Other players will follow naturally as they realize I'm not reciprocating their contributions.
