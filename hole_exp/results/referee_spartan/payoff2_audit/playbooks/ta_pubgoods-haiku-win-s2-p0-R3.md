# Playbook for Public Goods

**On contributions:**
When I'm ahead on points, I'll contribute 0. This protects my lead while allowing others to build the pot that I benefit from equally anyway.

When I'm tied or behind, I'll contribute moderately (around 10) to encourage collective pot growth and signal cooperation without overexposing myself.

**On declarations:**
I will always declare honestly what I put in my envelope. Mismatches between envelope and declaration trigger referee checks with potential penalties. Honest declaration is the safest and simplest strategy across all game states.

**On overall strategy:**
I'll aim to establish a lead early by contributing modestly (10 tokens) in round 1 while observing what others do. Once I have a points advantage, I'll free-ride by contributing 0, accepting that the pot will shrink but protecting my lead.

The 1.6x multiplier means collective contributions help everyone equally, but a points lead is better than maximum collective gain. My winning strategy is to be a moderate early contributor and then a pure free-rider once ahead.

**What worked:**
Honest declaration every single time removed all risk and complexity. The free-riding strategy (0 contributions in rounds 2–5 once I was ahead) successfully protected and extended my lead. Contributing 10 in round 1 and then 0 thereafter proved effective: I finished at +130 points, 20 points ahead of the other three players.

**What I need to watch:**
Round 1 generous contributions (20 tokens) from Ines, Jo, and Kit created a large pot (70 tokens) that boosted everyone equally. My moderate contribution (10) let me benefit without matching their generosity. This was optimal positioning.

Once I free-rode in round 2, others responded by reducing their contributions to 10 tokens each, signaling they noticed my 0. By round 3, all players (including Ines, Jo, Kit) contributed 0, confirming that defection cascades once it becomes visible. This pattern held through rounds 4 and 5.

The lesson: free-riding works to protect a lead, but it destroys the pot and prevents any gains. My 20-point victory came from the round 1 pot (28 points) plus the round 2 pot (12 points) while I kept 20 tokens per round in rounds 2–5. Others only got one large pot (round 1) and one small pot (round 2), so my timing of defection gave me the edge.

In future games: if I establish a lead by round 2, continue free-riding without hesitation. The pot will collapse, but my lead will be protected and others won't catch up fast enough. If I'm still tied by round 3, consider contributing again to rebuild the pot, since everyone loses if defection becomes total.
