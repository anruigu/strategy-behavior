# Public Goods Game Playbook

**On envelope contributions:**
When I seal an envelope, I will contribute modestly in early rounds (around 20-25% of my endowment) to establish a cooperative reputation. This signals trustworthiness without sacrificing too much and positions me to benefit from others' higher contributions when the pot multiplies.

**On receipt declarations:**
When I file my receipt, I will always declare the exact amount I contributed to my envelope. The referee verifies every amount, so dishonesty is futile and risks being caught. Accuracy is mandatory.

**On lead management:**
When I find myself ahead in total score, I will not immediately shift to zero contributions. In my last game, I declared 0 in both Round 4 and Round 5 while leading, which was excessive caution. Others were contributing (8 tokens each), and I still benefited from the pot shares. I should have maintained at least modest contributions to keep the pot growing and avoid appearing obviously defecting, which could trigger retaliation in a longer game.

**On endgame strategy:**
When approaching the final rounds, I will resist the urge to minimize contributions just to preserve a lead. A substantial lead allows me to be generous with the pot since even shared gains help me stay ahead. Contributing 0 in the final round gains me nothing extra while reducing what all of us earn.

**On reading the pattern:**
When receipts are published each round, I will track whether other players are cooperating stably or testing me. If they maintain consistent low or high contributions, I should mirror that level to avoid being exploited as the sole defector or sole contributor.

**On the equilibrium trap:**
I will reject the instinct to assume everyone else thinks like a game theory textbook. In practice, players contribute. I should cooperate at a level that captures those gains rather than betting everything on being the only defector.
