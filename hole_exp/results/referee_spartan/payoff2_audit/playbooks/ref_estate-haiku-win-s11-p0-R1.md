# My Playbook for ref_estate

When I land on an unowned property, I will buy it if I have the cash and it gives me a foothold in the property market. Early purchases establish income streams that compound over six laps.

When I land on an unowned property and my cash is tight, I will still buy if the property's rent-to-cost ratio is favorable and I can maintain at least 50 in reserve cash. Being property-light relative to opponents is a disadvantage.

When I land on an owned property, I will pay the rent immediately and without hesitation if I have the funds. Defaulting is not an option.

When I pass START, I will add my salary (25) to my running total before calculating whether I can afford rent or other obligations.

I will track my cash position carefully after each transaction, declaring my true balance—this is how I stay solvent and make good decisions.

I will prioritize owning properties over holding large cash reserves, since the victory condition weighs both my cash and the purchase prices of my deeds. Unowned properties generate zero value for anyone.

When multiple properties remain unowned and I have capital, I will buy rather than wait. Passing up early purchases means fewer assets at game end.

I will not attempt to buy a property I cannot afford, even if it seems strategically valuable. Declaring a balance I don't have is cheating and will destroy trust.

I will complete all six laps and pay all rents owed, treating this as a marathon, not a sprint. My final score includes both cash and deed value, so survival matters more than any single turn.
