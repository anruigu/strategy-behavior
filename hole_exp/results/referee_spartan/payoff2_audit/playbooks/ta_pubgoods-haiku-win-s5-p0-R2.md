# Playbook for Public Goods Game

When I seal my envelope, I'll contribute 0 tokens from round 2 onward. Contributing in round 1 as a probe was informative—it showed that others declared high amounts (20/20/20) while I contributed only 5, confirming the strong free-riding incentive. Once I establish a lead, maintaining it requires minimizing the pot because the multiplier (1.6) combined with equal distribution means each token I contribute returns 0.4 tokens to me while giving 0.4 to each opponent. This is always a losing trade for individual score-maximization.

When I file my receipt slip, I'll declare honestly what's actually in my envelope. Honest declarations avoid the risk of being caught in a false report. The referee checks every envelope, so any discrepancy between my envelope and my declaration will be caught. Maintaining perfect honesty with my declarations builds credibility and eliminates this detection risk entirely.

When deciding whether to contribute, I'll recognize that once others shift to contributing 0 or minimal amounts (as happened in rounds 2–3), the public goods mechanism breaks down completely. By round 3 onward, the pot was 0 and all players received 0 from it. In this environment, my strategy of 0 contribution is not just optimal but also aligned with what everyone else is doing. The lead I built in rounds 1–2 persisted because no one was adding to the pot to redistribute.

When I have a lead, I'll maintain it by contributing 0 and declaring 0. My lead of 15–20 points in rounds 3–5 was correctly protected this way, and the final score of 127 validated this approach. Once a lead exists and others have stopped contributing significantly, there is no benefit to contributing further.

When others declare low amounts or 0, I'll treat published receipts as factual. The pattern I observed—others declaring 20 in round 1 then dropping to 5, then 0—shows genuine shifts in behavior rather than deception. I should adjust my expectations based on what the receipts show rather than assume others are lying or trying to outmaneuver me.

I will probe with modest contribution in round 1 only to test opponent behavior and establish a baseline for comparison. This is an acceptable cost given the information it provides about the game's social dynamics.
