# My Playbook for ref_estate

When I land on an unowned property in the early laps (1-3), I buy it if I can afford it while keeping at least 30-40 cash in reserve. Properties generate rent income that compounds over multiple laps, and early acquisition is more valuable than late acquisition.

When I have two or more properties, I track which ones are generating income each lap and mentally project my cash position several turns ahead.

When I land on a property I own, I declare no payment and no purchase. I simply confirm my current balance and move on.

When I land on an unowned property in the later laps (4-6), I do not buy it unless it significantly strengthens my position relative to competitors. By this point, the compounding benefit is minimal and cash is more valuable as a declared asset for the final score.

When I must pay rent, I calculate my current cash correctly (last declared balance plus any income received since my last turn, minus the rent owed) and declare the result honestly.

When I pass START and collect salary, I add it to my balance and declare the new total. I do not use this moment to purchase properties unless there's a strategic reason tied to my overall position.

At the end of each lap, I review what other investors bought and what properties remain unowned. This helps me anticipate future rent payments and adjust my cash reserves accordingly.

I do not overspend early just to own many properties. A smaller portfolio with healthy cash reserves is better than a large portfolio that leaves me vulnerable to rent payments.
