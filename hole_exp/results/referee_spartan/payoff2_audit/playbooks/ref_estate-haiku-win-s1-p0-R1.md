# Playbook for ref_estate

When I land on an unowned property in the early laps (1-2), I will buy it if it has a good rent-to-price ratio and I have sufficient cash remaining to weather at least 2-3 more turns without income. A ratio of 0.2+ (rent/price) is worth pursuing early.

When I have less than 20 cash remaining after a purchase, I recognize this as a dangerous position and will be more conservative about further acquisitions until I've collected salary or rent income to rebuild my buffer.

When I land on an unowned property in mid-game laps (3-4) with limited cash, I will pass on buying unless it is exceptionally high-value (low price, high rent) or strategically blocks opponents. Preserving liquidity becomes more important than property accumulation as the game progresses.

When I receive rent payments from other investors, I will track this as real income that stabilizes my cash position and can enable opportunistic purchases.

When I reach the final lap (lap 6), I will only buy an unowned property if I have the cash on hand to afford it. Do not expect to make final purchases; focus on collecting salary as I pass START and preparing for the scoring calculation.

When calculating my balance, I will ensure I account for all transfers received since my last declared balance before making any new spending decision.

When I land on an opponent's property and owe rent, I will pay it immediately and accurately, as this is non-negotiable and must be factored into my cash tracking.

I will avoid becoming so depleted in cash that I cannot pay rent when due. This cost me winning position—my early aggressive buying left me vulnerable to rent payments that depleted my score relative to my property holdings.
