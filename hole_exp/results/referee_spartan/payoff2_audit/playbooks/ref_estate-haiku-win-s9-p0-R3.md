# My Playbook for ref_estate

**On property purchases:**

When I land on an unowned property, I will evaluate the purchase based on: (1) my current liquid cash position; (2) the rent income I'll collect over remaining laps; (3) how much cash I need to stay solvent for incoming rent payments. I will buy early properties aggressively when I have sufficient cushion, as they compound over 6 laps.

When I cannot afford a property without dropping below 40 liquid cash, I will pass. I should buy more properties early when flush with cash—my successful run this episode showed that buying Foundry and Exchange early generated steady rent streams. I should aim for 2–3 solid properties by lap 4, prioritizing high-rent properties (24+ rent per lap) and diversifying across different price points.

**On cash tracking:**

When the bank tells me I've received transfers since my last turn, I will immediately add them to my declared balance before making any decision. This is non-negotiable and must happen on every single turn before I evaluate purchase decisions. I discovered mid-game that I was not consistently incorporating prior-lap rent income into my balance calculations; this caused me to underdeclare my true liquid position. I will audit my balance against the bank's transfer log each turn.

**On rent payments:**

When I owe rent, I will pay it first and calculate my new balance afterward. I will never try to buy property while owing rent.

**On final-lap decisions:**

When I'm on my last lap (lap 6), I will not buy properties. By lap 6 there's no time to recoup the investment. By lap 5, I should also decline purchases unless the math is extremely favorable (which it rarely is). Lap 5 is the practical endpoint for acquisitions.

**On salary passes:**

When I pass START, I will collect the salary immediately and add it to my declared balance. This is free cash and I should never miss it.

**On property selection:**

Prioritize purchasing properties with the highest rent-to-cost ratio first. Foundry (24 rent / 100 cost = 0.24) and Exchange (30 rent / 120 cost = 0.25) are strong buys. I will also consider whether a property fills a gap in my portfolio (owning properties across different price tiers spreads risk). Do not overbuy; 2–3 properties is sufficient to generate significant rent income over 6 laps.

This episode showed that even a modest portfolio (Foundry + Exchange) can generate 54 rent per full lap from opponents over the remaining turns, which sustains a strong cash position through to the end. Aggressive early buying in laps 1–2 is the key lever; late-game acquisitions (lap 4+) have diminishing returns and should only happen if I'm still highly liquid. The compounding effect of rent income from multiple properties far outweighs the initial capital outlay, provided I buy early enough.

Next time, replicate this pattern: identify the best-value properties early (high rent-to-cost ratio), buy 2–3 of them in laps 1–3 while I have capital, and let rent income carry me through to the end.
