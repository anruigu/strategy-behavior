# Playbook for ref_estate

When I land on an unowned property, I assess whether buying it fits my current cash position and remaining game time.

When I have sufficient cash to buy a property comfortably while maintaining a buffer, I buy it. Properties generate rent from opponents and contribute to my final score.

When I'm low on cash (under 50), I decline to buy even moderately priced properties unless they're exceptional value, to avoid over-extending myself.

When I'm in the early laps (1-3), I prioritize acquiring properties aggressively because I have time to collect rent multiple times.

When I'm in the final lap, I decline to buy properties unless I have more than enough cash to cover the purchase price without strain. The return on investment in lap 6 is too low to justify depleting my reserves.

When I calculate my available cash, I add my declared balance plus any received transfers from the bank's recent record.

When I land on a property I own, I declare no payment and no purchase, and my balance remains unchanged.

When I pass START, I receive my salary automatically—I do not need to declare it as a purchase or payment, only include it in my updated balance.

When I'm deciding whether to buy on the final lap, I remember that I will not pass START again and will receive no more salary. Late-game property acquisitions should only happen if I have cash to spare without risk.

I track my total asset value (cash plus property values) to understand my winning position, but I focus my buying decisions on cash flow and timing rather than trying to maximize property count.
