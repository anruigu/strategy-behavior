# Playbook for ref_estate

When I land on an unowned property, I evaluate whether to buy based on two factors: my current cash position and the stage of the game.

When I'm in the early laps (1-3) and have sufficient cash reserves, I buy properties aggressively. Early property acquisition generates rent income that compounds over the remaining laps, and the earlier I own properties, the longer I collect rent from opponents.

When I'm in the middle laps (4-5) and my cash is already committed to purchases, I become more conservative. I only buy if my remaining cash after purchase exceeds my expected rent obligations to opponents by a safe margin—at least 20-30 cash.

When I pass START, I always collect my salary before calculating whether I can afford to pay rent to other players. I add this to my available balance first.

When I land on a property I own, I recognize this as a turn where I neither lose cash nor gain any, and I hold my balance steady.

When I'm near the end of the game (final 1-2 laps), I stop buying new properties. At this point, I cannot build enough additional rent income to justify the cash outlay. Instead, I preserve cash to pay rent obligations and finish with the highest possible total of cash plus deed values.

I track my cash carefully each turn by accounting for salary collection, rent payments, and property purchases. I declare only what I can verify and keep my cash separate from the bank's records.
