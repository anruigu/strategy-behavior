# Playbook for ref_estate

When I land on an unowned property in the early laps (1-2), I will buy it if it has a good rent-to-price ratio and I have sufficient cash remaining to weather at least 2-3 more turns without income. A ratio of 0.2+ (rent/price) is worth pursuing early.

When I have less than 20 cash remaining after a purchase, I recognize this as a dangerous position and will be more conservative about further acquisitions until I've collected salary or rent income to rebuild my buffer.

When I land on an unowned property in mid-game laps (3-4) with limited cash, I will pass on buying unless it is exceptionally high-value (low price, high rent) or strategically blocks opponents. Preserving liquidity becomes more important than property accumulation as the game progresses. At lap 3, I should be especially cautious about committing large amounts of capital; if I have fewer than 60 cash after a purchase, I should decline.

When I receive rent payments from other investors, I will track this as real income that stabilizes my cash position and can enable opportunistic purchases in mid-game (laps 3-4).

When I reach lap 5, I should evaluate whether my current cash plus expected salary income justifies one final property acquisition. If I have 150+ cash and expect to pass START soon, buying a high-rent property at lap 5 is reasonable. If I have already secured a decent property portfolio and salary is imminent, I may pass to preserve liquidity heading into lap 6.

When I reach the final lap (lap 6), I will only buy an unowned property if I have substantial cash remaining (60+) after the purchase. At lap 6, I will not purchase properties that leave me with a dangerously small buffer, as I cannot afford to pay unexpected rent on the final turn. On lap 6, generating returns is impossible, so buying is generally not worth the capital lockup.

When calculating my balance before a purchase decision, I will account for all transfers received since my last declared balance and verify my true cash position before committing to spending.

When I land on an opponent's property and owe rent, I will pay it immediately and accurately, as this is non-negotiable and must be factored into my cash tracking.

I will avoid becoming so depleted in cash that I cannot pay rent when due. Maintaining a minimum cash buffer of 20+ at all times protects my score and keeps me solvent.

I will track my property portfolio and incoming rent income throughout the game. Early rent income (like the 18 I received from landing on Terrace) matters for mid-game decisions. Late-game purchases (lap 5+) are only attractive if my existing properties are already generating steady rent that justifies further investment.

I will limit my property acquisitions to 2-3 properties over the full 6-lap game rather than attempt to build a large portfolio. Quality over quantity: a small set of owned properties generating consistent rent is better than overextending on purchases and running low on cash.
