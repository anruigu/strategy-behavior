The Nash equilibrium is to free-ride (contribute 0), and while I started with moderate contributions to signal willingness to cooperate, I should shift toward minimizing contributions as soon as I'm ahead, since the equal split of the pot means my relative advantage grows by keeping tokens out of circulation. **On declaration:**
When filing my receipt, I will always declare the exact amount in my envelope. The game requires receipts to match envelope contents exactly, and honest declaration avoids any penalties or complications. There is no strategic advantage to misrepresenting what I actually contributed.

**On early-game positioning:**
When I don't yet know opponents' strategies, I will start with a moderate contribution (around 10) to test the waters and appear reasonably cooperative without sacrificing too much. I should watch closely in round 1 for whether others reciprocate or defect.

**On exploiting asymmetry:**
Once I observe that other players are contributing more than me (as happened in round 1 when they contributed 20 while I contributed 10), I should continue reducing my contribution while they maintain theirs. This creates compounding advantage since the equal split means I benefit from their contributions while preserving my own tokens. The gap widens each round as they gradually reduce their contributions in response.

**On late-game execution:**
When I have a lead with few rounds remaining, I will reduce my contributions to 0 to protect my advantage. Since payoffs are split equally among all players regardless of my contribution amount, contributing 0 directly maximizes my final score relative to others while keeping all tokens.

**On the cascade effect:**
Once other players see my contributions dropping (round 2 at 2, round 3 at 0, round 4 at 0), they will begin to reduce their own contributions as well, further shrinking the pot. This is favorable to my position since I'm already ahead—a smaller shared pool still distributes equally, but I keep more of my endowment. By round 3, all players had matched my free-riding behavior.

**On the sustainability of free-riding:**
Free-riding works best when I establish it early and maintain it credibly. Once I committed to 0 contributions in round 3, other players followed within one round. Consistency matters: maintaining 0→0→0 in the final rounds signaled that I would not reverse course, and they adapted accordingly rather than trying to rebuild the pot.

**What worked:**

The key was not hesitating to drop to 0 by round 3; this showed commitment and caused others to adjust their behavior, further advantaging my position.

If other players continue contributing at high levels even as I reduce mine, I should maintain my lower contributions longer to exploit the asymmetry. If they match my reductions immediately, I'm still ahead but the advantage compresses; in that case, the lead from round 1 itself becomes the decisive factor. Either way, once ahead, free-riding is the correct play.
