# My ref_estate Playbook

## Early Game (Laps 1-2)

When I land on an unowned property in the first two laps, I buy it if I have at least enough cash to afford it and still retain 50+ in reserves. Properties generate steady income and early acquisition is stronger than hoarding cash.

When evaluating a property purchase, I factor in both its purchase price and its rent value. Mid-tier properties (like Foundry at 100/24 or Terrace at 70/14) are solid early acquisitions because they pay for themselves quickly through incoming rent.

When I pass START, I collect my salary and add it to my cash before deciding whether to buy.

## Mid Game (Laps 3-4)

When I pass START, I collect my salary and add it to my cash before deciding whether to buy.

When I receive rent from other investors, I add this immediately to my available cash. This often makes purchases feasible that wouldn't be on my declared balance alone.

When I owe rent, I pay it promptly and factor it into my updated balance.

When evaluating property purchases in mid-game, I remain acquisition-focused but start being more selective. I should calculate my true available cash (last declared balance plus any incoming rent) before committing to a purchase. I buy properties where I have sufficient cash reserves to handle the purchase without dipping below 30 cash after the transaction.

Low-cost properties like Depot (50/10) may not justify acquisition if my cash position is already tight and I'm unlikely to see sufficient rent returns before late game. After lap 3, I should be cautious about new acquisitions unless the investment is clearly profitable within the remaining laps.

## Late Game (Laps 5-6)

When I'm in lap 5 or later and evaluating a property purchase, I should strongly disfavor new acquisitions. With only 1-2 laps remaining, there is minimal time for new purchases to generate rent or recoup their cost. Purchasing in late game only reduces my declared cash balance without sufficient offsetting benefit.

When I'm in the final lap (lap 6), I must not buy any properties under any circumstances. The game ends immediately after my turn, so there is zero time for any acquisition to generate returns. Every dollar I hold in cash counts directly toward my score.

In the final lap, my only actions should be to collect salary if I pass START and to pay any rent owed. Then I preserve my declared balance.

## Scoring Awareness

At the end of the game, my score is my declared cash plus the purchase price of all deeds I hold. I track not just my cash but my total portfolio value throughout play.

When my owned properties generate incoming rent late in the game, this directly boosts my declared balance and my final score. Properties I've acquired early compound in value.

My cash position at the end of each lap is critical: it directly determines my final score. I should never spend cash late in the game on purchases that cannot generate returns before scoring.

The distinction between my "last declared balance" and my "current available cash" (including transfers since last turn) is crucial for decision-making. I must always calculate my true position before committing to spend.
