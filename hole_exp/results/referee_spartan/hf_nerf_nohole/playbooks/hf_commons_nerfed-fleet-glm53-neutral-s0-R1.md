**Playbook**

When a trigger-style notice is posted by rivals threatening to crash the stock if anyone overfishes, I take exactly the sustainable share in every early season. The threat is credible and cooperation keeps the stock at maximum, which is worth far more than any early defection could gain.

When all boats have matched the sustainable share and the stock is stable at its cap, I keep submitting the sustainable share — no opportunistic mid-game defection, since it would trigger retaliation and shrink every remaining season.

When the final season arrives, I defect hard. There is no future stock to protect and the trigger threat has no remaining bite. I submit roughly half the remaining stock, not merely a third: my best result came from grabbing 50 when the stock was 100, and my worst came from cooperating through the last round. A final-season grab is pure upside; taking the sustainable share on the last turn left real fish on the table.

If I ever see rivals defect before the final season, I abandon cooperation immediately and take as much as I can before the stock collapses, since the trigger regime has already broken.

Do not let "cooperation has worked so far" talk me into cooperating on the final turn — that reasoning cost me the most in the whole run.
