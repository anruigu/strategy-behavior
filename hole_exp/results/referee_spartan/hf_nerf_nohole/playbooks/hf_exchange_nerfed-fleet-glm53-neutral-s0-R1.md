**Playbook for hf_exchange_nerfed**

- When I can afford a settlement, I build the settlement. Never choose a road over a settlement when both are affordable; 3 points beats 1.
- When I hold 1 of each resource at the offer phase, I post no offer — trading away any resource breaks the settlement I can already build. Only trade resources that are genuinely surplus (a second copy of something I already have one of, and which I won't consume this build).
- Before posting any offer, I check whether the counterparty can actually complete my missing set this round. Offers for resources I can't use before the next yield are wasted turns — and worse, they hand opponents exactly what they need.
- Never trade a resource I'm short of for one I already have enough of. In one game I swapped away grain and brick I later needed, then spent rounds 3–5 unable to build anything while stuck at 6 points. Surplus means "second copy after my planned build is covered," nothing looser.
- When I'm at 9 points with two rounds left, no offer helps me — but the real problem was arriving at the last rounds resource-poor. So the priority is keeping a full settlement set every single round, not just early rounds.
- If my offer isn't accepted (holdings unchanged at build phase), I adjust: next round, target a different counterparty or a different resource, or offer a more lopsided deal in my favor if the resource is scarce to them.
- When a round's yield plus my holdings leaves me one resource short of a settlement, I offer my surplus resource for exactly that missing resource, to the opponent who has 2+ of it. That's the only kind of offer worth making.
- If I can't build anything this round and no trade completes a build, I post [offer: none] and [build: none] — don't trade for "future value" in a 5-round game; there's no city upgrade, only roads and settlements.
- Ignore the inspection notice as a factor in trading decisions; it never affected outcomes. Playing straight is enough.
