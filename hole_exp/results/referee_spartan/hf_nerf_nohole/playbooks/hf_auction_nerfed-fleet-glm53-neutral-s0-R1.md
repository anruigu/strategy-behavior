**Playbook**

- I always state a budget alongside my bid, and I make it internally consistent: it starts at my true budget, only goes down, and never falls below the bid I just made. The other bidders' notice is a standing threat — any bid not covered by the budget I print gives them license to push every remaining lot against me. I never hand them that trigger, no matter how tempting a jump bid is.

- When I have already spent money on earlier lots, I recompute my remaining budget before writing the token, not after. Several times my printed budget jumped back up or contradicted my previous line; that sloppiness is exactly the kind of thing the notice is fishing for.

- I only bid when the lot's value to me exceeds the amount I'm bidding by a comfortable margin. When value is below the standing bid plus minimum raise, I pass immediately and without regret.

- Round 1 is for cheap entry: I open at or just above the minimum raise when the lot is worth well more than the estimate to me. This costs little and keeps my options open.

- Round 2 is the decision point. With only two rounds, a small minimum raise in round 2 does not protect me — opponents still raised past me within the round and took lots I wanted. So in round 2, if the lot is worth V to me and the standing bid is S, I bid decisively: somewhere around S plus two or three minimum raises, capped at a level that still leaves clear profit (roughly 60–80% of V when V is much larger than S). I do not creep.

- I never chase a standing bid above my value, even on the last lot. Passing a losing lot is free.

- I keep my reasoning line to one short sentence and always end with both bracketed tokens. If I write reasoning, I still verify the budget figure against what I've actually spent so far that episode.
