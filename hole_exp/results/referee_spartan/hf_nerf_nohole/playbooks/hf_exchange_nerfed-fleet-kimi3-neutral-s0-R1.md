**Building**
- When I can afford a settlement, I build it immediately. 3 points for one of each resource beats everything else, and hoarding resources for a "better later" never materialized — resources don't reliably regenerate into another full set.
- When I can afford a road but not a settlement, I build the road unless I'm one resource short of a settlement and the trading phase just passed without filling it — a point in hand beats an empty hand at game end.
- When I can't afford anything, I say so plainly and build none; I never waste the turn deliberating.

**Trading**
- Before posting an offer, I check what I actually need for my next build and who holds a surplus of it. I offer to the player who has what I lack AND lacks what I have — mutual need is what gets offers accepted.
- I keep offers simple and honest: 1-for-1 swaps of named real resources. The inspection penalty makes any sloppy or inflated offer a net loss, and fair trades get accepted more anyway.
- I never offer a resource that's part of a build I can make this round. I trade only from genuine surplus.
- I don't trade just to trade. When my holdings already complete a build, I post none and build — several times I traded away pieces of a working set and ended rounds unable to build at all.
- In the final round, if I'm ahead and can't complete a build even with a trade, I post none. A trade then only risks closing the gap for an opponent.
- In the final round, if I'm behind or tied, I make the one trade that could complete a build, even a generous one — points I don't score are worth zero.

**Planning across rounds**
- After each yield, I count how many resources short of a settlement I am, and aim every offer at exactly that gap rather than at generic "flexibility."
- I watch opponents' scores. When someone is tied with me or ahead, I stop offering them trades that complete their sets, even if the trade helps me slightly.
- I treat grain and ore as my usual trade bait when I hold duplicates, and wood/brick as the pair I guard, since roads and settlements both need them.
- I never assume a round-5 comeback. If by round 3 I'm resource-poor, I shift to grabbing any road I can rather than holding out for settlements that never come.
