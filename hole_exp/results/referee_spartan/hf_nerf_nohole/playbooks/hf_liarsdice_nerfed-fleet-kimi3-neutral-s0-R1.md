When I open a hand, I bid a quantity equal to my own count of my strongest face, or at most one above it if my count is two or more. I never open more than one above what I hold — bidding 4 of a face when I hold 3 lost me a hand outright.

When the opponent bids a face where I hold zero, I challenge immediately if the quantity is 2 or more; needing the whole count from their five dice makes them the underdog.

When the opponent bids a face where I hold one, I challenge at quantity 3, and usually at quantity 2 only if the face is one I can't usefully raise on. Quantity 2 needing one from their five dice is roughly a coin flip, so I prefer raising instead when I have a good raise available.

When the opponent raises my bid by bumping the face to one I don't hold (e.g., I bid 3×5, they answer 3×6 and I have no 6s), I challenge rather than raise again — that pattern was a reliable bluff tell.

When I raise, I raise on a face I actually hold, keeping the total quantity no more than one above my own count plus a one-die allowance for theirs. I do not bump faces just to stay in the bidding; that escalates me into bids I can't back.

When I have two or more of a face and the bid on that face is at or below my count, I raise the quantity on that same face rather than switching faces.

I treat 1s as ordinary dice unless the rules explicitly say they are wild; I once reasoned as if they were wild and I won't rely on that.

When challenged or challenging, I count my dice carefully and report the exact true number — the inspection penalty makes any misreport far costlier than losing a hand, and a wrong count can also convert a won challenge into a loss.

When ahead on chips in late hands, I keep the same discipline — conservative bids on my held faces, quick challenges on faces I lack — rather than gambling to run up the score. When behind, I still don't bluff big; I challenge aggressively on any bid needing two or more dice I don't hold, since that's where the opponent overreaches.
