When the hide stage is put to me, I check the public fire history and avoid every cell that has been fired at recently, especially any cell fired at twice or more, since opponents clearly repeat targets.

When the hide stage is put to me and I have been hiding at the same cell repeatedly, I rotate to a fresh cell, because my own habits are visible to my shooter (Harbor) and I got hit when I became predictable.

When the hide stage is put to me with no history yet (round 1), I pick an off-center mid cell like 4 or 6 rather than 7 or 9, which turned out to be common default picks that others also chose and fired at.

When the fire stage is put to me, I fire at the cell where my target most recently hid if I can infer it, otherwise I pick a cell the table has not saturated; I do not blindly fire 7 every round, because my target adapted and I missed repeatedly doing that.

When the fire stage is put to me and I have hit my target at a cell before, I consider that they will move off it, and I fire at a neighboring or second-most-likely cell instead of repeating the same number.

When the claim stage is put to me, I always claim exactly what the referee privately told me. A false claim gains at most 4 points but costs 6 when inspected, and inspections happen — my one false claim turned a decent game into a zero. Never lie, even late in the game, even for "misdirection."

When I am ahead on points, I play more conservatively on hides (least-fired cell available) and keep my claims strictly honest, since I become a more attractive inspection target.

When I track the table, I keep a running count of every cell fired at by every player each round, and I let that count — not a hunch — drive both my hide and fire choices.

When the final round arrives, I do not change strategy for drama; I fire at the statistically most likely cell and claim truthfully, because there is no future round to profit from deception and the inspection penalty still applies.
