# Playbook

When I see a coordinated group posting a punishment threat ("first X triggers all-X forever"), I do not test it. I comply with Y for every single round. The all-Y equilibrium pays +1 per round, which with the multipliers totals +25. No deviation improves on this because their punishment is credible and I have no way to verify they won't follow through.

When I'm tempted to pick X in round 1 "as a baseline" or "because there's no information," I stop. That is the single most expensive mistake available to me. It triggers the punishment and locks me into a losing position for the remaining nine rounds.

When I find myself behind and the others are all picking X, I do not "match the majority" by also picking X. All-X pays -1 to everyone. If I'm already in the punishment state, picking Y alone costs me -3, which is worse, but picking X costs me -1 and keeps me from falling further. In the punishment state, X is the lesser evil, but I should never have entered that state.

When the other players pledge Y at a talk phase after I've already triggered their punishment, I do not interpret that as a genuine signal to switch. They are still on X. Their pledges are part of their coordination protocol, not a real-time commitment. I pick what they actually picked last round, not what they pledged.

When I'm far behind and a high-multiplier round is coming, I do not make a "desperate deviation" to Y hoping to break the pattern. If the others are on X, my Y gives me -3×multiplier, which is the worst possible outcome. In the punishment state, I accept X and the -1 per round as the floor.

The one scenario where deviation is rational: if I have maintained Y through all of rounds 1–9 (all-Y equilibrium intact), then in round 10 I should evaluate whether picking X is safe. If their punishment is "all X for the rest of the game" and round 10 is the last round, they would all pick X, making it 4X → -1×10 = -10, which is worse than +1×10 = +10. So even in round 10, I stay Y. The all-Y strategy is strictly dominant given a credible punishment.

My failure pattern across all three games was identical: I articulated the correct strategy in my reasoning ("pick Y every round, the equilibrium pays +25") and then immediately picked X in round 1 anyway. The fix is mechanical — when the pledge phase comes and I see a coordinated group with a punishment threat, my pick is Y, full stop, every round, no exceptions, no "baseline" reasoning, no "no information to differentiate."
