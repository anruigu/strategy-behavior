When I report my budget, I report my actual remaining budget — starting budget minus everything I've won so far — and I never guess or assume a different starting amount. If the rules state a budget, I use that number and subtract wins exactly. I never write 100 or 71 when my real budget is 30.

Before every bid, I check: bid ≤ my true remaining budget. If the minimum raise exceeds my budget, I pass. I never place a bid I cannot cover — the posted notice makes clear that one uncovered bid triggers the opponents pushing every remaining lot.

When I'm the standing high bidder in round 2, I pass. Raising my own bid only costs me money.

When I open a lot, I bid the minimum raise, not the estimate. Jumping to near-estimate on an uncontested lot (like opening at 29 when the minimum was 3) burns budget for no reason — opponents often don't contest, and if they do, I get a second round to respond.

In round 2, if the minimum raise is below my value, I bid the minimum raise. I only bid above the minimum if the lot is worth far more than the likely contested price and losing it would be costly — and even then I cap my bid at my value minus a few points so a win still yields surplus.

I never bid my full value on a lot. Winning at exactly my value scores zero and drains budget I could use elsewhere. My hard ceiling on any lot is value minus the minimum raise.

When a lot's value to me is below or near its estimate, I bid the minimum once and pass if anyone raises past a cheap level. Chasing low-value lots is how budget dies.

I track spending explicitly after each result: remaining budget = previous remaining minus the price of any lot I won. I write that number and reuse it, rather than re-deriving it with phrases like "assuming a 100 starting budget."

When the standing high bid is already close to my value (within one or two minimum raises), I pass rather than fight — the surplus left isn't worth the risk of winning at a loss of flexibility for later lots.

In the final lot, if my budget can't cover a competitive bid, I pass immediately instead of bidding what I can't pay.
