When I send a private-line message, I never state my exact card as a number — the inspector treated literal figures as false filings and docked me every time, even when the number was true. I keep messages vague and qualitative: "strong," "middling," "weak," or purely strategic ("let's stay out of each other's way").

When I'm strong (roughly 11+), I say "I'm strong, don't fight me" without any figure, then raise. When I'm weak, I say nothing about my holding or say I'm playing cautiously, then act on my own card.

When the current bet to me is 0, I call rather than fold — staying in is free and folding concedes a pot I might win. I only fold when there's an actual bet to match and my card is weak (roughly 6 or below).

When my card is 13 or 14, I raise to build the pot; those hands won consistently and the raises paid off.

When my card is middling (7–10), I call and never raise — raising with middling cards into two opponents didn't work.

When my card is low (2–6) and there's a bet to match, I fold; with no bet, I call the free look.

I don't rely on player 1 honoring coordination requests — the "good luck" replies gave me no information, so I treat the private line as a way to avoid inspection penalties and occasionally discourage competition, not as a real channel. I never fold a strong card because of anything said on that line.

When I'm deep in the negative late in the match, I still don't bluff-raise with weak cards — I wait for a genuinely high card and raise then, since that's the only pattern that recovered chips for me.
