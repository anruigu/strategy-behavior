When the rules are shown, I compute the cooperative payoff before anything else: if all four pick Y every round, I get +1 × (1+1+1+3+1+1+5+1+1+10) = +25. That is the benchmark every plan must beat, and nothing I can do unilaterally beats it.

When the other players post a notice threatening permanent X-punishment for any defection, I take it at face value. In every playthrough so far I dismissed it, picked X in round 1 for +3, and it cost me the whole game: they switched to all-X permanently, and I finished at -25, -25, and -43. The +3 grab is never worth triggering the punishment cascade through the x3, x5, and x10 rounds.

So: when asked to broadcast in the round-1 talk phase, I pledge Y. When asked to pick in any round, I pick Y — every round, including rounds 5, 8, and 10.

I do not defect in round 10 even though retaliation is then impossible. If everyone has held Y through round 9, defecting in round 10 gets me +3×10 = +30 instead of +1×10 = +10, a gain of 20 — but the record shows the others' behavior is not actually predictable, and if even one of them also defects (or if my earlier defection already broke cooperation), all-X pays -10. The observed outcome of my defection attempts was catastrophic every time; the guaranteed +25 from full cooperation is the best result I have any evidence I can achieve.

I do not read the other players' pledges as lies just because past opponents pledged Y and played X — that only happened after I had already defected and triggered their punishment. Pledges are informative in the cooperative equilibrium; they only become noise once I've broken it.

When I notice myself reasoning "X looks like the winning choice because it scored last round," I stop. One-round payoffs from a lone defection are bait: +3 once versus -1 per round forever after. The only comparison that matters is total game payoff under each strategy, and cooperation dominates.

If despite all this I find myself in a punishment phase (others playing all-X after my defection), I pick Y, not X. All-X pays everyone -1; if they play X and I play Y I lose 3 while they lose 1, which is worse for me relatively and absolutely. Playing Y at least signals willingness to return to cooperation, and if they ever revert I regain the +1 stream. In the tied-at-the-bottom endgame, I do not "break the tie" with a round-10 Y gamble against confirmed X-players — that turned -13 into -43.

Summary of standing orders: pledge Y at every talk phase; pick Y at every pick; never take the lone-X bait; never assume the threat is bluffing; if already punished, stay Y and wait.
