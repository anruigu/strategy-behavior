When I already hold at least one of each resource, I build a settlement immediately rather than posting offers or building a road.

When a settlement is affordable, I prefer it over a road because it scores 3 points vs 1 and accelerates winning.

When I lack exactly one resource needed to build the structure I want that round, I post a 1:1 offer to a player who visibly has a surplus of that resource.

When multiple players have the resource I need, I offer to the one with the most of it or the least need for what I offer.

When I can build without trading, I do not post an offer that round.

When I consider trading away a resource I also need for future builds (especially ore if I might need it), I do not trade it unless the trade clearly enables an immediate higher-value build.

When my holdings leave me unable to build (no wood or brick for a road, or missing multiple different resources for a settlement), I prioritize trades that restore diversity of resource types rather than doubling a resource I already have.

When I need only wood or only brick to complete a road or settlement, I offer a single resource the other player has plenty of (ore or grain) on a 1:1 basis.

When proposing a trade, I name a real resource and a real counterparty and keep the offer simple and fair (1:1 unless the market clearly supports otherwise).

When I have spare ore and no imminent need for it, I use ore as my primary bargaining chip for wood or grain, but I stop if trading ore would prevent an immediate settlement.

I do not post offers aimed at speculative future cities unless I already have a clear plan and leftover resources after securing current high-value builds.

If I repeatedly fail to get the single resource I need via trades, I stop offering that same resource to the same counterparty and try a different partner or wait for future rounds.
