When asked to declare, I always state my true private yield.

When my private yield is 0–2, I deposit 0 (keep all rounds with tiny yields because the audit risk of lying isn’t worth 1-point-per-unit deposits).

When my private yield is 3–5, I deposit 1 (signal minimal cooperation while preserving most units for the 2-point retained payoff).

When my private yield is 6–10, I deposit 2–5 depending on how much I need to signal trust: default to deposit 3, increase to 4–5 only if I am trying to build credibility quickly or if my opponent is consistently contributing visibly high amounts.

When I am behind on total points and need to influence opponents’ behavior, I modestly increase deposits within the ranges above to signal cooperation; when I am ahead, I reduce deposits toward the minimum.

When an opponent declares high but consistently deposits zero, I stop matching their apparent cooperation and revert to the deposit rules above (they are not providing social returns).

When any audit/inspection threat is explicit or credible, I do not lie and I moderate my deposits upward within the above bands to avoid drawing attention.

When tempted to over-contribute for group benefit, I act on the numeric fact: retained units yield 2 points each vs deposits 1 point each, so I prioritize personal retention unless strategic signaling justifies the lower per-unit return.

What didn’t work: overgenerous deposits cost me points without securing reciprocation; being honest about yields avoided audits and is my baseline.
