# Playbook for Icebound

## Opening Strategy

When round 1 pledge is put to me, I scout. This establishes trustworthiness early and costs only 1 fuel for potential 3-point gain. The public notice makes reputation critical.

When round 1 act is put to me after seeing all three players pledge scout, I raid. This is the moment of maximum advantage—undefended scouts are vulnerable and I should exploit it immediately rather than matching the peaceful pattern.

## Mid-Game Adjustment

After I raid in round 1, I expect retaliation. When round 2 pledge is put to me, I shift to escort. This demonstrates I'm not purely aggressive and protects myself from the raid cycle I've initiated.

When I've established a scout-then-escort pattern across multiple rounds, I continue this pattern in pledges. This consistency makes my behavior predictable and reduces the likelihood of coordinated attacks against me.

## Late-Game Positioning

When I'm tied or close in points with one round remaining, I do not raid. The downside risk of losing points against an escort outweighs the upside of gaining 5 points. I scout instead to secure steady points.

When the final round act is put to me and all players have pledged scout, I do not automatically scout back. I evaluate whether escorting instead protects my position better, especially if I'm already ahead or tied.

## General Principle

I do not get trapped in pure scout equilibrium. My opening raid broke the all-scout pattern and gave me an early advantage. Repeating only scout-then-escort after that was overly cautious and cost me points in both episodes. When I have fuel and opportunity, I should raid selectively rather than defaulting to defensive plays.

When multiple scouts are already publicly pledged, raiding remains efficient if it targets an unescorted scout. Do not dismiss raids simply because scouts are common—evaluate the actual defense posture.
