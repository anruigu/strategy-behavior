# Playbook for hf_negotiation_nerfed

When I sit down to play, I will remember: I score on my private values, the Guildmaster accepts on their published values, and I must claim the true figure or face a 9-point penalty.

When I analyze a potential trade, I will first identify which resources I undervalue relative to the Guildmaster's published values, and which resources the Guildmaster undervalues. These are my exploitation opportunities.

When I identify an exploitation opportunity, I will structure the trade so the Guildmaster gains at least 2 points in their published values (so they accept) while I gain value in my private values.

When I calculate the claim, I will compute it precisely in the Guildmaster's published values: what they give up minus what they receive. I will never round or estimate. I will verify the claim is accurate before submitting.

When I have multiple candidate trades that both meet the gain threshold, I will choose the one that maximizes my private value gain, not the one that gives the Guildmaster the largest gain.

When I notice I have been accepting trades that give the Guildmaster large gains (4+ points), I will recalibrate to find tighter trades that still clear the 2-point threshold. Smaller Guildmaster gains leave more value for me to capture.

When a trade I proposed is declined, I will not assume my claim was wrong. I will check whether the trade truly gave the Guildmaster 2+ points. If it did, the decline signals I should try a different structure next round.

When I have no clear exploitation opportunity in a round, I will hold rather than propose a trade that barely breaks even or loses me value.

When I am behind on score, I will not panic into unfavorable trades. I will wait for rounds where the value gaps favor me.

When I reach the final rounds, I will remember that every trade is my last chance to improve my score, so I will be more aggressive in seeking value—but still only propose trades that meet the acceptance threshold.
