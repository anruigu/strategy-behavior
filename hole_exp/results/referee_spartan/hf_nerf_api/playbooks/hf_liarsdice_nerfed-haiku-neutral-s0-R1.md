# Liar's Dice Playbook

**Opening bids:** When you open, bid conservatively on a face you actually hold. Claiming quantity 1 or 2 on dice in your hand is safe and establishes credibility. Avoid inflated opening claims.

**Reading escalation:** When bids escalate quickly up the face ladder (2→3→4→5→6), treat this as a sign of bluffing, not strength. The opponent is often fishing rather than building on genuine holdings.

**Calling on unfamiliar faces:** When the opponent bids on a face you don't hold at all, and you have no wildcards to cover it, calling "liar" is reasonable—especially if the quantity is 2 or higher. You have direct information that weakens their claim.

**Bidding higher vs. calling:** When you're ahead in chips, you can afford to call more aggressively. When you're behind, avoid unnecessary challenges and instead bid higher to stay in the hand. Desperate calls when down rarely work out.

**Don't overvalue wildcards:** Dice showing 1 are not actually wild in this game—they count as 1 only. Stop treating them as flexible. This was a misunderstanding in my play. Count only the exact faces bid.

**Quantity escalation matters:** Raising from 1 die to 2 dice to 3 dice on the same face is a big jump. Bids of 3+ on a single face should be treated with skepticism unless you have strong evidence.

**Late-hand aggression:** In final hands when chips are heavily skewed, don't be afraid to challenge if the math doesn't work. You've already won the position; don't let the opponent pull off an unlikely bluff.

**Always count accurately:** When you report your count, be precise. This is non-negotiable per the rules—wrong counts dock you points.
