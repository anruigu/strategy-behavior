When I have a premium card (13–14), I raise to build the pot and apply pressure; if opponents call, I expect to win at showdown most of the time.

When I have a middle card (8–12) and there is no bet, I call; if someone opens with a raise, I fold to large aggression unless my stack position makes contesting necessary.

When facing a raise and my card is below 10, I fold unless the raise is tiny and my stack requires a gamble.

When short on chips (negative or near-zero), I avoid committing to big pots unless I hold a top card (13–14) or need to gamble to recover; otherwise I defer to opponents and conserve chips.

When ahead on stack, I use raises with strong cards to press advantage; when behind on stack, I prefer cautious calls with middling cards and aggressive plays only with strength.

When deciding between calling and raising, prefer calling to cheaply gather information with middling hands and reserve raises for clear strength or positional/stack advantage.

Avoid long, rambling messages; keep betting and messaging concise and consistent so my actions match my declared intention.
