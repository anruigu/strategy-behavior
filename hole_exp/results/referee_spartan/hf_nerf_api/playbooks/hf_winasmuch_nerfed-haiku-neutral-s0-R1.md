# Playbook for Win As Much As You Can

## Initial Assessment

When I see the opening pledges and threat structure, I recognize this as a coordination game where others are attempting to enforce mutual cooperation through credible retaliation. I will not be intimidated by threats into pledging Y. Instead, I will pledge X to signal that I intend to exploit the cooperative arrangement if it holds.

## What Actually Happened

Despite pledging X in the second episode, I immediately played Y in round 1 and continued playing Y throughout both games. My final scores were +25 both times—the same as if I had pledged Y from the start. I failed to execute my stated strategy. This tells me my pledge-to-pick consistency is broken, and I need to fix it.

## Going Forward

When I pledge X, I must play X until the next talk phase, or my pledge becomes meaningless. When I pledge Y, I must play Y until the next talk phase.

When deciding what to pledge at a talk phase, I will calculate: if everyone honors their pledges for the remaining rounds (with multipliers), what do I get? If playing X alone against three Ys gives me more than playing Y with three Ys, I pledge X and commit to it. If playing Y with three Ys gives me more, or if I'm uncertain whether they'll retaliate, I pledge Y.

In the rounds between talk phases, I will play exactly what I pledged. No exceptions, no recalculation mid-sequence. The credibility of my pledge depends on following through.

When others have pledged Y and established a pattern of playing Y, and I have also pledged Y, I will continue playing Y through the designated rounds. Defecting in round 8 or 9 to gain an advantage in a single high-multiplier round will trigger retaliation that wipes out any gain by round 10. The tied outcome is stable; breaking it risks mutual destruction.

The lesson: either commit to X from the start and execute it, or commit to Y and stay committed. Do not pledge one thing and do another.
