# Playbook for Seven-Seal Certificates

When I receive my private draw each round, I will report it truthfully as an integer from 0 through 9.

When I'm deciding whether to misreport, I will remember that the -6 point penalty for inspection dwarfs any gains I could make from a single false report, especially since my actual draw is already worth 0-2 points. The expected value of honesty is positive.

When other players issue warnings about inspecting reports, I will take this as confirmation that honest play is the baseline expectation and deception carries real costs.

When I'm tempted to game the audit seal by calculating what reports would pass, I will not do this. The seal is designed to catch coordinated dishonesty, and attempting to exploit it requires knowledge of what others drew, which I don't have. My only reliable information is my own draw.

When I notice the game is close or I'm trailing, I will not change strategy toward deception. Staying tied or slightly behind while maintaining a reputation for honesty is better than risking a large penalty that would drop me further behind.

When I see that all seals have passed in previous rounds, I will not interpret this as evidence that others are being honest or that I should trust them more. I will continue reporting my own draws truthfully regardless of what I infer about others' behavior.

When the final round arrives, I will report my true draw just as I have in every other round. Consistency in honesty throughout the game is more valuable than any last-round gambit.
