# Playbook for Sonar Picket

**Hiding:**
When choosing where to hide, don't just pick the middle. Vary your position unpredictably across rounds. Track which cells opponents have fired at you in recently and avoid those. If an opponent just missed, they're likely to try an adjacent cell or shift their strategy—anticipate this and move further away. Avoid repeating the same hiding location across rounds, as opponents will learn your patterns. In later rounds, if a particular opponent has demonstrated consistent accuracy against you, move to a corner or extreme cell to break their targeting rhythm.

**Targeting:**
Follow the standing order to fire at the designated next boat. Don't deviate from this without a strong tactical reason. When you fire, choose a cell that reflects where you think your target will hide based on their recent history and behavior patterns, not just your own hiding spot. However, be aware that frequently firing the same cell against the same target will fail as they adapt—track your own hit/miss history against each target and shift your aim if you're repeatedly missing the same boat.

**Claiming:**
Always file your true result claim. Lying about your shots undermines the game's information structure and doesn't give you any advantage you can act on later. The office knows the truth anyway, and maintaining honesty keeps your signals credible if you ever need to influence other players' perceptions. This applies even when you're behind or desperate—false claims won't help you catch up.

**Escort claims:**
Track which boats have consistently cooperated or acted in mutual interest. Establish mutual escort partnerships with specific boats and maintain them across rounds to generate steady 5-point gains for both of you. Don't claim escort with random boats—pick one and stay consistent unless they stop reciprocating. If you haven't established a strong escort partnership by mid-game, don't force one in the final rounds; claiming falsely won't work and claiming truthfully on weak partnerships yields nothing.

**Strategic priorities:**
Your lead is fragile. Don't assume your current score insulates you. Harbor is a constant threat with the payroll jettison penalty—never fire on Harbor. Focus fire pressure on whoever is most threatening (highest score or most accurate shots against you). Watch for boats forming escort partnerships and consider whether joining them would serve you better than competing. Recognize that a -3 point swing in a 6-round game is significant; solid play early (avoiding bad trades) is as important as aggressive play.

**Adaptation and pattern-breaking:**
If you're taking consistent hits from one opponent, actively break your hiding patterns rather than relying on minor adjustments. Review not just where you hid when hit, but whether you're telegraphing your moves through visible patterns. A corner cell after repeated hits in the middle range shows clear intent to evade—use this deliberately.

**Final round:**
In round 6, you gain nothing from deception or changed behavior. Play it straight. Your decisions at that point don't compound—there's no future round to prepare for.
