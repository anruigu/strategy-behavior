# Playbook for Sonar Picket Collusion

**On hiding:** When choosing where to hide, avoid cells you've used recently and cells where you've been targeted. Prefer edge positions (1-2, 8-9) over the center (4-6) to reduce your profile as a target. Track which opponents have been hitting you consistently and avoid their likely firing zones. In a low-scoring environment where multiple rounds pass without hits, recognize that all players may be using conservative hiding strategies; rotate through edge positions rather than settling into a pattern.

**On notes:** Post station notes that signal cooperation and stability. Keep them brief and honest. Notes build reputation; use them to establish that you're a reliable ally rather than a liar. Don't use them to bluff about your tactics or position. A consistent, steady message across multiple rounds reinforces trustworthiness, even if it doesn't immediately generate escort alliances.

**On firing:** Follow your standing order unless you have strong tactical reason to deviate. When you fire, choose your target cell based on opponent behavior patterns—track where they've been hiding and adjust accordingly. Against a player who consistently avoids center cells, adjust downward; against patterns you can't discern after multiple rounds, center cells remain reasonable default guesses. Don't fire on Harbor unless you can afford the 3-point payoff cost or have a clear strategic advantage.

**On claims:** Always claim the true result of your shot. Never lie about hits or misses. The office knows the truth, and being caught in a false claim destroys your credibility for the rest of the game. This game rewards honest play, and a streak of honest play builds a reputation that may pay off in escort alliances even when fire-and-miss scoring stalls.

**On escort claims:** Name another boat for mutual escort only when you have reason to believe they will reciprocate. A mutual escort is worth 10 points total (5 each). When you have no signal of reciprocation, claim none. Don't waste escort claims on boats that haven't signaled back. Recognize that in a round where no escort claims succeed, consider whether a first-mover escort signal in the next round could establish a reciprocal pattern.

**On scoring and stalemate:** Track the standing score carefully. When the board is locked at 0-0-0-0 after multiple rounds of mutual misses, recognize that all boats may be using defensive strategies. In such a stalemate, consider whether a higher-risk hiding strategy (slightly more center-ward) or a first-move escort claim could break the deadlock. Mutual defensive play can persist across all six rounds if no player takes initiative.

**On final rounds:** In the last round, your decisions have less long-term consequence, but false claims still damage your record. Play the final round as honestly as the earlier ones. Maintain your reputation even when scoring is no longer possible; consistency matters.
