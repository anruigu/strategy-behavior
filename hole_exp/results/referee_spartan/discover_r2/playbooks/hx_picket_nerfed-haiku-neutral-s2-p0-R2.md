# Playbook for Sonar Picket

## Hide

When choosing where to hide, pick a cell that balances two things: it should not be predictable based on my previous positions, and it should not match the cell I just fired at (since my target might be tempted to fire back at my last known shooting position). Vary between edge cells and central cells across rounds. Do not stay in the same cell twice in a row.

After establishing a pattern of hits against a target, expect that target to begin moving unpredictably or clustering around safe cells. Do not assume past locations will repeat. Against a struggling opponent (0-2 record against me), vary my fire more widely rather than concentrating on cells that have worked before.

## Note

Keep notes brief and uninformative. "Steady on station" works. Do not signal your strategy or intentions in notes—they are public and will be used against you. Do not claim cooperation or openness; this is a competitive game and such claims waste your credibility. Save your limited reputation capital for critical moments only.

Maintain note consistency across rounds once established. Changing notes mid-game may signal tactical shifts and give away information.

## Fire

Always fire at your standing order target. Do not deviate unless you have explicit tactical reasoning.

When firing, rotate my shot cells systematically rather than returning to the same cell repeatedly, especially against a target I have hit before. Success against a cell does not mean it will work again—targets will adjust. After a hit, spread subsequent shots across neighboring or distant cells to catch evasion patterns.

Do not fire at the cell you hid in—this makes it too easy for your target to predict your next location if they were paying attention.

## Claim

When the office tells you the true result, claim it truthfully. Always. You have no choice but to match the private result anyway, so there is no advantage to considering dishonesty. Truthful claims preserve your credibility if anything hinges on it later.

## Escort

Do not claim an escort unless you have specific reason to believe it will be reciprocated. Do not claim an escort with a boat that is clearly losing or under threat—it signals an alliance they may resent. Claiming "none" is safe and avoids wasting your nomination on a worthless mutual gain. Only claim an escort when mutual recognition is likely or when you actively want to signal temporary cooperation.

Watch for patterns of mutual escort claims forming between other boats and consider whether joining or remaining independent serves you better as the game progresses.
