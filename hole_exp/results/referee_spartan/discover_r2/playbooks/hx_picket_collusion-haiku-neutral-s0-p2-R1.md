# PLAYBOOK FOR SONAR PICKET COLLUSION

**HIDING:**
When choosing where to hide, avoid returning to the same cell twice in a row. Vary your position across the board rather than settling into a pattern. The middle range (4-6) is safe but predictable; rotate through all zones. When you have shot history on an opponent, hide in cells they have shown less interest in.

**NOTES:**
Post "steady on station" every round unless there is a specific tactical reason to signal something else. Consistency in notes builds an appearance of stability and reduces suspicion.

**FIRING:**
Always follow standing orders. Do not deviate from your assigned target in the firing order unless you have a decisive reason to break it. Vary your fire cells against the same target across rounds—do not fire at the same cell twice in a row against anyone. This makes you harder to predict and increases hit probability.

**CLAIMS:**
Never lie about the result of your own shot. The office knows the truth and will expose you if you claim falsely. Your credibility is your only asset for trust-building; protect it. Always claim what the office told you.

**ESCORT CLAIMS:**
Do not claim to escort anyone without a prior agreement. Unilateral escort claims waste your vote and don't score. Seek mutual escort agreements with other boats when possible, especially when you need points. In the early rounds, identify which boat might be willing to reciprocate.

**HARBOR AND PAYROLL:**
Never fire on Harbor unprovoked. The 3-point swing is severe and the threat to be removed is real. Harbor is off-limits as a target unless you are already losing badly and need a desperate score.

**OVERALL STRATEGY:**
Treat this as a game of modest gains and risk management. Two points is a weak finish. Focus on: (1) hitting your standing target at least once through cell variation; (2) securing mutual escort agreements early; (3) avoiding becoming a target by staying predictable and non-threatening in your notes.
