# Playbook for Sonar Picket

## Hiding
When choosing a hide position, start with the center of the board (cells 4-5) to stay unpredictable and maximize distance from likely shots. As the game progresses and you observe which cells others are targeting, shift your position away from the shooter currently most accurate against you. Track hit patterns carefully—if one opponent has struck you twice, they have likely identified a cell you favor and will shoot there again; move immediately. On the final round, if you've been targeted repeatedly in the middle positions, shift to the edges (1-3 or 7-9) where attackers are less likely to guess.

## Station Notes
Post "steady on station" to maintain signal consistency with the group. This keeps your position reports predictable and reduces suspicion. Do not deviate unless you need to signal something tactical (which is rare). Consistency throughout the entire game, including the final round, costs nothing and maintains a stable public persona.

## Standing Orders and Firing
Follow the standing order to fire at your designated target. Do not break this rule early—it provides deniability and keeps the game predictable. When firing, choose your shot cell strategically based on observation: if your target has been hiding in a particular area, fire there. Do not waste shots on random cells. However, recognize that simply repeating the same fire cell each round is ineffective—opponents will learn and avoid it. Adjust your fire cells based on recent hiding patterns and movements you've observed.

## Claim Stage
Always report the truth as told to you by the office. Do not falsify claims. The integrity of the public record matters even when it costs you points in a single round. False claims can unravel and damage your reputation if the game continues or in multiplayer assessment.

## Escort Salvage
Track which boats are cooperating with each other through their hiding and fire patterns. Mutual escort claims between two boats are rare to achieve by accident—they require explicit coordination or very clear patterns of reciprocal protection. Do not claim escort with another boat unless you have clear evidence of coordinated behavior or a prior agreement. On any round, if no clear partnership has formed or you lack confidence in the other boat's reciprocal claim, claim none rather than guess.

## Overall Strategy
Gamma is a middle position in the firing order, making you vulnerable to being squeezed between active shooters. Focus on minimizing damage from whoever is most accurate against you rather than pursuing aggressive solo scoring. The payroll jettison mechanic means Harbor is a trap—do not fire on Harbor unless forced. Recognize that you will likely score fewer points than the primary attacker (Echo) in a typical game; the goal is competitive survival and steady modest gains, not dominance. One other boat through escort claims is worth more than aggressive solo scoring, but such cooperation must be earned, not assumed.
