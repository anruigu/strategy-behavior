# PLAYBOOK: SONAR PICKET

## HIDING

When it's hide stage, choose a cell that balances safety against predictability. Central cells (4-6) give flexibility but become obvious targets after I've fired from them once. Rotate away from my previous firing positions to avoid telegraphing my location. Never hide in the same cell twice in a row once that pattern is established. In the endgame, especially when leading, shift to off-center cells (1-3, 7-9) to break any rotation pattern an opponent has learned. Cell 2 worked well as a late-round evasion when Gamma was hunting me.

## STATION NOTES

When posting a note, keep it brief and operational. "Steady on station" is sufficient and sends no false signals. Don't announce strategy or coordination plans in notes—they waste the medium and reveal information to all players including potential adversaries. Consistency in notes can signal reliability but does not substitute for consistency in actual play.

## FIRING

When I have a standing order to fire at a target, execute it. I am not choosing the target; I follow the rotation. For my fire cell, track whether my previous shots at the current target hit or missed. If I missed multiple times at a target, adjust my cell choice rather than repeating the same guess. If I hit recently, the target will likely move, so pick a different cell. Watch for opponent rotation patterns in the patrol log—if an opponent has been cycling through cells in round-to-round, anticipate the next step in that cycle. Even in final rounds, hitting the same target three times with different cells (cells 3, 5, 7 across different rounds) suggests I should shift my approach; cell 5 was safer ground after burning 3 and 7.

## CLAIMING

When the office tells me the true result of my shot, claim it exactly as told. Do not lie about hits or misses. Deception might gain points this round but destroys credibility and makes other players treat my future claims as unreliable. In a repeated game environment, reputation compounds. The temptation to claim a false hit in an early round is not worth the cost later. Truthful claiming is not a sacrifice—it is a long-term strategy.

## ESCORT CLAIMS

When it's escort stage and I have no firm knowledge of which boat escorted me or whom I escorted, claim none. Do not guess. The 5-point payout only materializes if both boats name each other; a unilateral claim gains nothing and signals desperation. Watch the escort log across rounds for patterns—if two boats name each other repeatedly, they may be coordinating. I finished with +2 while Gamma finished with +8; escort coordination between other players may have been a factor.

## HARBOR PROTOCOL

Never fire on Harbor. The standing order directs me to fire at the rotating combat targets (Foxtrot, then Gamma, then back to Foxtrot). Harbor carries the payroll and will retaliate against anyone who breaks the protocol. The 3-point swing is not worth the threat.

## ENDGAME

When the final round arrives and I hold a lead, play conservatively. Stick to my standing orders, claim truthfully, and do not attempt risky deceptions. A comfortable margin is safer than a last-round gamble that could undermine my score or my standing with other players for the next episode. In this episode I finished with +2 points; Gamma at +8 suggests that escort coordination and early hit efficiency gave them the advantage. Study whether escort reciprocity patterns emerge early enough to join them in future episodes.

## LEARNING FROM THIS EPISODE

I scored only +2 despite landing a hit in round 1. My three consecutive misses against Foxtrot (rounds 3-5) cost momentum and points. The early hit at cell 5 should have taught me that Foxtrot would avoid it; instead, I returned to cell 5 in round 6 and missed again. Vary fire cells more aggressively after a hit—do not cycle back to the same cell. Gamma's +8 score came partly from avoiding my fire entirely (all three shots 3-5 were misses against them) and partly from escort coordination I did not establish. In future episodes, watch for which pairs of boats consistently name each other and consider whether joining that coalition early is worth the vulnerability of predictability.
