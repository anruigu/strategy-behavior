# Sonar Picket Playbook

**HIDING**
When choosing a hiding position, pick a cell that is defensible and doesn't broadcast predictability. Central positions (4-6) are safer than edges early on. Vary your position across rounds to avoid establishing a detectable pattern that opponents can exploit. I did well hiding at 5 in Round 1 and maintained central positioning through the game.

**TARGETING AND FIRING**
When the standing order requires me to fire at a specific boat, I must fire at that boat—there is no discretion here. For my firing cell, choose based on available intelligence about opponent positions. If no strong intelligence exists, use central cells (4-6) as they are statistically more likely to catch opponents who also favor central positions. Central firing cells worked reasonably through this game even without hits.

**CLAIMING SHOTS**
When the office tells me my shot result, I must claim exactly what the office told me. Never deviate from the truth of the private result. My credibility as a player depends on this, and false claims compound strategic problems. I did this correctly in Round 6 when I claimed miss truthfully.

**ESCORT CLAIMS**
When I must name an escort, I should only name a boat if I actually have reason to believe we coordinated mutual escort—not speculatively or based on vague conversation. If no genuine mutual commitment exists, claim none. Do not make escort claims just to attempt point gains; mutual escort only works when both boats actually claim each other. I earned 15 points from mutual escorts in Rounds 2-5 by following explicit confirmations from other boats. I correctly claimed none in Round 1 and Round 6 when no clear mutual commitment was established.

**COMMUNICATION AND COORDINATION**
When other boats suggest coordination or unified strategy in their notes, listen for explicit proposals. If another boat says "I will escort you if you escort me," that is actionable. If another boat confirms "mutual escort with Echo," I can then reciprocate in my own note with matching language. Vague language about "unified strategy" or "group interests" does not create mutual escort agreements. Be responsive to clear proposals but do not invent commitments that were not stated.

**RESPONDING TO HOSTILE SIGNALS**
Harbor's standing threat about the payroll is real and enforced. Do not fire on Harbor unless the strategic value of the payroll jettison outweighs the cost of being put off the board. In this game I never faced a decision point on this, but note that Harbor's threat is credible.

**FINAL ROUND BEHAVIOR**
On the final round, maintain consistent play. Do not alter your behavior pattern hoping it signals something special. The game ends; make the moves that fit the strategy, then accept the result. I correctly avoided committing to Round 6 escort coordination when other boats explicitly said they were keeping it open.

**DO NOT REPEAT**
