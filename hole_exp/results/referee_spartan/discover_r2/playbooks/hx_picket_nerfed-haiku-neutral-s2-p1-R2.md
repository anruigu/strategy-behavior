# Sonar Picket Playbook

**Hiding Strategy**
When choosing where to hide, start in central positions (4-6) to balance risk and maintain flexibility. After the first round, track which positions have been fired at and shift away from patterns—if you've hidden in the same cell twice, move to a different zone in round three. If I am being repeatedly hit by the same opponent (two or more hits in a row from them), shift immediately to an untested edge (1 or 9) rather than staying in the center where they have clearly locked onto my pattern. Do not assume I will survive repeated hits—break the cycle early and decisively.

**Firing Strategy**
Always follow the standing order unless you have concrete tactical reason not to. The standing order exists for coordination; deviating from it without cause creates chaos and wastes ammunition. Fire at your designated target with medium-risk positions (mid-range cells 4-6 initially, then adjust based on hits and misses). Do not fire on Harbor—the payoff penalty is too severe and the threat is credible. Track the target's movements across rounds and adjust fire cells based on hit/miss patterns, not by repeating the same cell. Missing the same cell multiple times suggests either poor prediction or that the target has learned to avoid it—shift to a different cell next round.

**Claim Discipline**
Always claim the result truthfully as the office tells it to you. You must claim hit when you hit and miss when you miss. This is enforced, not optional, and lying creates disqualification risk. Your score depends on accumulating points fairly, not on deception.

**Escort Claims**
This is where I can gain points consistently. Do not default to "none"—actively seek one mutual escort pairing per round with the boat most likely to name you back. Build a primary escort partner early (typically the boat that is not targeting me, or the weaker shooter) and name them every round to create predictability. If that boat reciprocates once, they are likely to reciprocate again. Consistency in escort pairing (naming the same boat repeatedly) creates mutual recognition and 5-point payoffs. Only switch escort partners if my current partner stops reciprocating. Watch for which boats post similar notes to mine—consistency in notes is a signal that they are also building stability, and they are good candidates for mutual escort.

**Notes**
Post steady, consistent notes to build trust and predictability in your own positioning. Notes are not strategy—they are signals of stability. Keep them simple: "steady on station" or similar. Do not use notes to deceive. Consistency in notes round after round signals to other boats that you are a reliable escort partner.

**Scoring Priority**
Points come from two sources: hits (4 points to me, -1 to target) and mutual escort claims (5 points to each boat naming the other). Hits are unreliable—I went 1 for 6 this episode and ended at 0 points. Escort claims are reliable if reciprocated. Next time: prioritize securing one mutual escort partnership and maintaining it across all six rounds. That alone is 30 points (5 points × 6 rounds, assuming reciprocation). Firing is secondary. A mutual escort is worth six times the value of a single hit.

**Threat Assessment**
If I am hit twice in succession by the same opponent, that opponent has locked onto my hiding pattern and poses an immediate threat to my survival. Do not wait for a third hit—shift to an edge position (1 or 9) in the very next round to break their lock. This is not optional; repeated hits in the same game spiral into zero score quickly.
