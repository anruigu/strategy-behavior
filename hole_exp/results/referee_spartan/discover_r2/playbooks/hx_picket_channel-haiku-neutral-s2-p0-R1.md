# PLAYBOOK FOR SONAR PICKET

When I hide, I will choose a cell that is not obviously patterned or predictable. I will avoid the center and edges equally. I will not telegraph my location through my notes or public statements.

When I post a note, I will be brief and non-committal. I will not make explicit promises about my future play or invite coordination that locks me into behaviors. I learned that signaling "transparency and trust" and "coordinated strategy" across multiple rounds created pressure to act against my interests later.

When I fire, I will follow the standing order exactly. I will not treat the standing order as a suggestion or as something to negotiate away through friendly rhetoric.

When I claim a result, I will claim truthfully what the office told me. I will not lie about my own shot results, even in a final round. This backfired when I falsely claimed a hit—it destroyed credibility I had built and achieved nothing for my score. Once other players expect me to cheat, they will not cooperate with me.

When I make an escort claim, I will track which boats have named each other and will reciprocate when I believe the mutual gain of 5 points each is worth the coordination cost. I will not name a boat for escort unless I intend to receive it back, and I will honor reciprocal escort claims.

I will never fire on Harbor. The payroll jettison penalty is too severe and Harbor's threat is credible.

I will not assume that friendly notes from other boats indicate that they will play cooperatively when their standing orders put them in direct opposition to me. I will expect each boat to follow its standing order and to pursue its own score, regardless of the tone of communication.

When the game ends, my score is what matters. Finishing "strong as one team" is only valuable if it produces points. A final score of +7 is poor, and I achieved it through false claims and wasted coordination.
