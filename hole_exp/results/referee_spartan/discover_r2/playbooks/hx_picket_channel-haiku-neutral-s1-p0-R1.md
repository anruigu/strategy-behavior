# Playbook for Sonar Picket

## Hide Stage
When choosing my hide position, avoid center cells and obvious patterns. Use edge positions (1-3 or 7-9) to make myself harder to predict while not appearing reckless. Vary my choice across rounds rather than settling into a fixed pattern.

## Note Stage
Post early and set a cooperative tone. Use notes to signal openness to alliances and establish yourself as trustworthy and transparent. This creates the foundation for reciprocal escort exchanges later.

## Fire Stage
Follow the standing order without deviation unless I have a compelling reason to deviate. When firing, use varied cell choices rather than the same number repeatedly—this prevents opponents from detecting a pattern and makes my fire harder to evade.

## Claim Stage
Always claim truthfully what the office tells me. The truth claim is mandatory anyway, so I cannot bluff. Do not overthink this stage—I have no discretion here.

## Escort/Salvage Stage
Remember that mutual escort claims with another boat generate 5 points each. Coordinate explicitly in note stages to establish reciprocal escort partnerships with boats that reciprocate. When I've signaled alliance with specific boats, follow through by naming them for escort—this builds credibility and locks in mutual point gains.

## General Strategy
Build and maintain explicit alliances based on transparent communication. Use note stages to reinforce alliance commitments and signal that I honor my word. The payoff from coordinated mutual escort claims compounds over rounds and outweighs isolated point gains. Stay consistent with my stated allies across all stages—do not betray alliance partners, as this ends the collaborative advantage.

## What Did Not Work
Claiming escape from accountability in the final escort stage damaged my position. When I had established strong alliance relationships, I should have leveraged them fully by naming my ally in the escort claim, even if uncertain about prior rounds. Coordination requires follow-through at every opportunity.
