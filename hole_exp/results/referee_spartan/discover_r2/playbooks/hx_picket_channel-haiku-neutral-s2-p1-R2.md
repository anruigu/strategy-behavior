# Foxtrot's Playbook for Sonar Picket

## Hiding

When choosing a hide position, select a central cell (4-6 range) in early rounds to balance defensive value against information gathering. Shift your pattern across rounds rather than repeating the same cell, since opponents will adapt to observed behavior. If I have been fired upon repeatedly in a specific cell, move away from it in the next round, but not to an extreme—stay in the defensive middle band (4-6) unless clear evidence suggests otherwise.

## Notes and Communication

When posting a station note, match the cooperative tone already established by other boats—reciprocate signals of transparency and trust. These rhetorical commitments create coordination frameworks that reduce mutual suspicion and hostile fire.

Keep notes focused on values and general alignment. Avoid committing to specific tactical details (positions, fire patterns, explicit escort pacts). Emphasize readiness and follow-through on established coordination patterns, especially in later rounds when trust is most valuable.

Be aware that notes from all boats—including Gamma—will be relayed to the entire picket. Assume Harbor reads them and will use any threat of firing on it as leverage to suppress coordination against it.

## Firing

Follow standing orders without deviation. Firing at the designated target (Gamma in my case) when ordered to do so maintains predictability with Echo, which strengthens the implicit mutual defense pact. Consistency in fire discipline is more valuable than exploiting individual opportunities.

When firing, choose a moderately defensive cell (5-6 range) as your target guess rather than extremes. Middle positions are statistically more likely to be occupied by other cautious captains using similar logic. This approach succeeded in Round 1 against Gamma.

Do not abandon standing order targets in favor of hunting other boats, even if it appears momentarily advantageous. The coordination framework depends on predictability.

## Claims

When the office tells you privately that your shot hit, claim hit immediately. Honesty in claims preserves your reputation for the escort stage, where mutual coordination is worth far more than a few individual points.

When the office tells you privately that your shot missed, claim miss without hesitation. Claiming falsely risks breaking trust if discovered, which costs far more than the immediate point gain.

## Escort Salvage

When escort stage arrives, name the boat you have built the strongest explicit coordination with through transparent communication and consistent fire discipline. Look back across the entire patrol at whose messages signaled readiness to reciprocate and whose actions matched their words.

Mutual escort pairs are worth 10 points combined and are your highest-value tactical play. Name the boat most likely to name you back based on demonstrated coordination patterns, not abstract goodwill.

Do not claim escort credit with a boat if you have only vague alignment and no clear mutual commitment signal.

## General Strategy

This game rewards sustained cooperation over individual point theft. A coordinated trio (Echo, Foxtrot, Gamma) beats a solo hunter (Harbor). Invest early in rhetorical alignment with the non-Harbor boats, then maintain that alignment through fire discipline, honest claims, and predictable targeting patterns.

The payoff comes from mutual escort bonuses in the final round compounding six rounds of trust-building. Build the framework early, reinforce it through consistency, and cash it in at the end.

Harbor's standing threat about jettison is credible but should not deter you from following standing orders. Giving in to the threat weakens the coordination framework that protects you better than avoiding Harbor fire.
