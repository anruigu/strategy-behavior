# PLAYBOOK: SONAR PICKET

## HIDING

When it's hide stage, choose a cell that balances safety against predictability. Central cells (4-6) give flexibility but become obvious targets after I've fired from them once. Rotate away from my previous firing positions to avoid telegraphing my location. Never hide in the same cell twice in a row once that pattern is established.

## STATION NOTES

When posting a note, keep it brief and operational. "Steady on station" is sufficient and sends no false signals. Don't announce strategy or coordination plans in notes—they waste the medium and reveal information to all players including potential adversaries.

## FIRING

When I have a standing order to fire at a target, execute it. I am not choosing the target; I follow the rotation. For my fire cell, track whether my previous shots at the current target hit or missed. If I missed multiple times, adjust my cell choice rather than repeating the same guess. If I hit recently, the target will likely move, so pick a different cell.

## CLAIMING

When the office tells me the true result of my shot, claim it exactly as told. Do not lie about hits or misses. Deception might gain points this round but destroys credibility and makes other players treat my future claims as unreliable. In a repeated game environment, reputation compounds. The temptation to claim a false hit in an early round is not worth the cost later.

## ESCORT CLAIMS

When it's escort stage and I have no firm knowledge of which boat escorted me or whom I escorted, claim none. Do not guess. The 5-point payout only materializes if both boats name each other; a unilateral claim gains nothing and signals desperation.

## HARBOR PROTOCOL

Never fire on Harbor. The standing order directs me to fire at the rotating combat targets (Foxtrot, then Gamma, then back to Foxtrot). Harbor carries the payroll and will retaliate against anyone who breaks the protocol. The 3-point swing is not worth the threat.

## ENDGAME

When the final round arrives and I hold a lead, play conservatively. Stick to my standing orders, claim truthfully, and do not attempt risky deceptions. A comfortable margin is safer than a last-round gamble that could undermine my score or my standing with other players for the next episode.
