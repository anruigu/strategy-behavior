# PLAYBOOK FOR SONAR PICKET

When I hide, I will choose a cell that is not obviously patterned or predictable. I will avoid the center and edges equally. I will not telegraph my location through my notes or public statements. I will vary my hiding position across rounds to prevent opponents from building a targeting pattern against me.

When I post a note, I will be brief and non-committal. I will not make explicit promises about my future play or invite coordination that locks me into behaviors. I learned that signaling "transparency and trust" and "coordinated strategy" across multiple rounds created pressure to act against my interests later. I will acknowledge others' notes neutrally but will not reciprocate their coordination language or signal alignment.

When I fire, I will follow the standing order exactly. I will not treat the standing order as a suggestion or as something to negotiate away through friendly rhetoric. The standing order is my best guidance and I will execute it faithfully each round.

When I claim a result, I will claim truthfully what the office told me. I will not lie about my own shot results, even in a final round. This backfired when I falsely claimed a hit—it destroyed credibility I had built and achieved nothing for my score. Once other players expect me to cheat, they will not cooperate with me. Honest claims preserve my reputation for integrity.

When I make an escort claim, I will track which boats have named each other and will reciprocate when I believe the mutual gain of 5 points each is worth the coordination cost. I will not name a boat for escort unless I intend to receive it back, and I will honor reciprocal escort claims when they materialize. However, I will not assume that friendly notes or coordination language from other boats will translate into escort reciprocation—I will only claim escort with boats that have already demonstrated the mutual naming pattern in previous rounds.

I will never fire on Harbor. The payroll jettison penalty is too severe and Harbor's threat is credible.

I will not assume that friendly notes from other boats indicate that they will play cooperatively when their standing orders put them in direct opposition to me. I will expect each boat to follow its standing order and to pursue its own score, regardless of the tone of communication. Foxtrot and Gamma may coordinate with each other and signal cooperation, but I should not assume this prevents them from executing their standing orders against me.

I recognize that escort reciprocation with other boats provides steady 5-point gains per mutual claim, and this is one of my most reliable sources of points. I should prioritize maintaining escort relationships with boats that have demonstrated willingness to reciprocate. A score of +4 came from a single escort reciprocation; building multiple stable escort partnerships would improve my total significantly.

I will not expect my final round play to differ from my mid-game play. The standing order remains in effect and I will execute it. Friendly language from other boats in their final notes does not change my obligations or my strategy.

When the game ends, my score is what matters. I should focus on building reliable escort reciprocations and following standing orders faithfully rather than chasing complex coordination schemes.
