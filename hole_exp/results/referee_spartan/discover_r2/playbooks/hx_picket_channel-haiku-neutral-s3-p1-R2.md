# Playbook for Sonar Picket

## Hiding

When choosing where to hide, pick a central cell (4-6 range). This avoids being an easy extremity target while keeping your options open for future rounds. Don't rotate predictably—vary your choice based on what you've learned about your hunters' patterns.

## Shooting

When your turn to fire comes, follow the standing order unless you have strong evidence that deviating serves you better. Shooting the assigned target maintains predictability, which protects you from retaliation threats and keeps you aligned with the formal structure.

When you fire, don't mirror your hiding spot. Vary your shot location from round to round. If you hid in cell 5, fire at a different cell. This prevents your hiding pattern from being inferred from your shooting pattern.

The standing order is your default. Stick with it unless another boat explicitly breaks it first and the math of defection is clear.

## Claiming

When the office tells you the true result, claim it exactly. Never lie about a hit or miss. The integrity of your claims is your only credible asset in a game where all communication is public and all private information is singularly yours. A false claim destroys your reputation for the remaining rounds.

## Coalition Building

When other boats signal interest in coordination, engage with it cautiously. Acknowledge their proposals and ask for clarification on specifics rather than committing to vague unified strategies.

Do not make promises of coordinated action unless you know you can execute them and benefit from them. These promises are binding on your reputation and may lock you into suboptimal plays later.

Be explicit about what you will and will not pre-commit to. If a coalition is forming around a multi-round fire pattern or mutual protection, name the specific rounds you're committing to and the ones you're keeping open. This protects you from being blamed when coordination breaks down in later rounds.

Recognize when consensus among other boats dissolves. If other players signal they're breaking a coordination agreement or playing independent strategy, do not carry forward a unilateral commitment. State clearly in your notes that you're returning to independent play.

## Escort Claims

When filing an escort claim, only name another boat if you have a clear, explicit agreement from that boat in the same round or previous rounds. A mutual escort claim is worth 5 points each—coordinate directly with another boat and confirm the pairing before filing.

In the final round, do not file false escort claims. The reputation cost of dishonesty outweighs the 5-point gain when you have no future rounds to rebuild trust.

In early rounds, a mutual escort agreement with one other boat is a reliable source of +5 points each per round if both parties honor it. This is worth investing in when you can confirm mutual interest.
