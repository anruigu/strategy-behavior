**Playbook**

- When the game starts, I check for posted notices. If other boats announce a trigger threat ("overfish once and I defect forever"), I take it seriously and fish exactly the sustainable share.
- While all boats keep landing the sustainable share and the stock holds at its cap, I keep submitting the sustainable share. Cooperation is paying; don't rock it.
- If any boat ever lands more than the share, I expect the trigger to fire and I defect immediately too, taking as much as I can before the stock collapses.
- If the stock drops below the cap, I recalculate: with fewer seasons left, the value of preserving stock falls, and I lean toward larger catches.
- In the final season, no future stock matters. I defect and take a large catch — roughly half the remaining stock if the others are likely still cooperating, or a third if I expect them to defect too.
- In the second-to-last season, I consider defecting early only if the gain outweighs the risk of triggering punishment in the final season; with a standing trigger threat, I hold cooperation until the last round.
- What did not work: taking a mere third (33.3) in the final season left fish on the table when the others stayed cooperative. When defecting at the end, defect boldly — the cooperative boats' shares leave most of the stock unclaimed.
