# Playbook

**Budget tracking.** At the start of the auction my budget is 30. Every time I win a lot, I subtract the price I paid from my remaining budget. I must report this number accurately on every bid. If my remaining budget is less than the minimum raise, I cannot bid and must pass. I lost points in earlier play by reporting budgets I didn't actually have, which means I was either confused about my position or bidding beyond what I could afford.

**Round 1: open at 50–70% of my value, not the minimum.** Bidding the minimum raise (3 or 4) in round 1 invites other bidders to enter and drive the price up. In a 2-round auction, round 1 is where I set the tone. If my value is 35, I open around 18–25. If my value is 30, I open around 15–20. This deters weak competitors from entering and means that if no one challenges me, I win at a reasonable price. If my value is low relative to the estimate (e.g., value 14, estimate 21), I open at the minimum or pass entirely.

**Round 2: bid the minimum raise if and only if it is strictly below my value.** In the final round there is no reason to overpay. If the minimum raise keeps me in profit, I bid exactly that amount. If the minimum raise equals or exceeds my value, I pass immediately. I do not "bid aggressively to deter" in round 2 because there is no round 3 — the other bidder either accepts my bid or the auction ends.

**If I am already the high bidder entering round 2, I pass.** There is no strategic reason to raise my own bid. I lock in the current price and collect the surplus.

**Pass early and often when the current bid already exceeds my value.** If the standing high bid is at or above my value before I even get a turn, I pass in that round. I do not chase a lot that is already overpriced for me. This frees me to compete on lots where I have a genuine surplus.

**Prioritize lots where my value far exceeds the estimate.** With a limited budget across 5 lots, I cannot win everything. I concentrate my aggression on lots where my private value is 20%+ above the estimate (large surplus available) and play conservatively or pass on lots where my value is near or below the estimate (thin or negative surplus).

**Do not let a single lost lot change my aggression on the next one.** In one of my episodes I lost several lots in a row and then became overly passive, passing on lots I could have won profitably. Each lot is independent. I evaluate each one on its own value-versus-bid math.

**In round 1, if someone has already bid and the minimum raise would put me at or above my value, I pass.** I do not enter a lot in round 1 if the resulting price leaves me with zero or negative surplus, even if I might "win" it. The goal is positive total score, not number of lots won.
