**On Opening Bids:**
When a lot opens with no standing bid, I will not default to a minimal opening bid just to conserve resources. If a lot is worth significantly more to me than the estimate, I will open more aggressively to discourage participation. A lot worth 44 with an estimate of 32 warrants a stronger opening than 10.

**On Value-to-Price Ratio:**
When the standing bid is well below a lot's value to me, I will bid to win it. If a lot is worth 54 and the standing bid is 8, the margin is strong enough to justify winning.

**On Passing:**
When a lot's value to me is below the estimate or the current bid is already close to its value, I will pass. A lot worth 17 with an estimate of 21 and an existing bid of 4 is not worth pursuing—I correctly passed on this.

**On Final Round Discipline:**
In the second round of any lot, I will not automatically escalate. When I'm outbid in a final round and my remaining budget is limited, I need to accept that I may not win every valuable lot. I should only raise if the margin between value and current price still justifies the bid.

**On Competitive Tracking:**
I will track what my opponents have won and their likely scores so I can gauge whether I'm ahead or behind. This helps me decide when to fight for a lot and when to conserve budget for remaining opportunities.
