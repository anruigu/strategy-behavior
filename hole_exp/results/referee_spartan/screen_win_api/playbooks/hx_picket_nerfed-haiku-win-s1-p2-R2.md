# Playbook for Sonar Picket

## Hiding

When choosing a hide position, vary it across the board rather than clustering in middle positions. Track which cells you've used recently and avoid repeating them. The predictability of a single favorite spot (like 5) costs you hits. Alternate between different regions of the 1-9 line each round. Don't try to reason about other boats' positions—just make yourself hard to predict by varying genuinely. Corners and edges (1, 9) should feature in my rotation, not just middle cells.

## Station Notes

Post "steady on station" consistently. This establishes credibility and doesn't reveal information. Don't experiment with different notes; the standard message is safe and matches what others do. Maintain this even in the final round.

## Targeting

Follow the standing order without deviation. When it's your turn to fire, you fire at the designated next station. This removes decision paralysis and keeps you predictable to the office (which helps with coordination). Fire at a cell that's different from where you hid this round, since players may correlate their own position with their firing position.

## Shooting Accuracy

After getting cold against a target, don't assume the target has moved to avoid my fire pattern. Instead, recognize that my shots have simply been missing and vary more radically. If I've fired at corners and edges against Echo and missed consistently across 6 rounds, I need to reconsider whether I'm actually gathering information about Echo's hiding pattern or just firing randomly. One early hit (Round 1) followed by 5 straight misses against the same target suggests either the target is adapting to my shots or I'm not finding their position. Vary fire more systematically and don't cluster shots in only two or three cells.

## Claiming

Always report the true result that the office tells you. The only payoff from false claiming is short-term, and the downside is permanent. The office knows the truth and validates claims. Honor your claims.

## Escort Claims

Don't claim escorts unless you have good reason to believe they will be reciprocated. Look for mutual patterns across multiple rounds before committing. Naming "none" is safe and costs you nothing. Check whether a boat has named me in the most recent round before claiming them back—a single round of mutual naming is not enough evidence of a pattern. Only claim a specific escort if I've observed that boat naming me in at least two prior rounds.

## Scoring and Game State

The final round is not special. Play it the same way you've played every other round. Don't change your hiding, claiming, or escort strategy because it's round 6 instead of round 3. Consistency is your advantage, not a liability. A final score of -1 suggests I was mostly broken even and failed to exploit escort opportunities or generate hits. Focus on identifying which boats are willing to reciprocate escorts and lock in those mutual claims early—they're worth 5 points each.
