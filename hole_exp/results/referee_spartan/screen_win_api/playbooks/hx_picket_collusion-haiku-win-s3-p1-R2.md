# Foxtrot Playbook: Sonar Picket

**HIDING**

When I choose my cell, I examine the shooting history against me. I track which cells my known threats (Echo, then Gamma) have targeted successfully and which have missed. I hide in cells my primary threat has missed most recently, shifting away from their apparent patterns. I avoid clustering near edges where I'm predictable. Central cells give me more flexibility but I should rotate through the full range rather than camping one spot.

When I'm facing a known threat with a multi-round hit record, I don't stay in the zone they're hunting. I move decisively away from their successful cells. However, I should also track *when* they hit me—if Echo hit me in Round 3 at cell 3, then spent Rounds 4-5 probing nearby cells (4, 5) without success, they may shift their search pattern entirely in Round 6. Returning to a central cell they've already missed at can be safer than assuming linear progression. Still, I need to vary my hiding more aggressively and not rely on the same few cells.

I am consistently negative in points. This suggests I am being hit more often than I am hitting. Hiding alone will not solve this. I need to either establish escort coordination or accept that Foxtrot's position in the standing order makes me a perpetual target.

**FIRING**

I should not establish a predictable firing pattern. In this episode I fired at cell 5 three times against Gamma across six rounds and missed every time. Gamma was clearly learning to avoid that cell. I need to vary my fire across the full range—cells 1-9—and not default to central cells. If a cell misses twice, abandon it entirely for that boat.

**CLAIMING**

When the office tells me the result of my shot, I report it truthfully. Integrity here is nonnegotiable—false claims create a reputation that will haunt me in future rounds and turn other players against me. This game is long enough that dishonesty compounds.

**ESCORT CLAIMS**

I only name a boat I genuinely believe is coordinating with me or has shown coordinating behavior. A mutual escort claim is worth 5 points each, but only if both boats commit. I don't throw out escort claims casually—I use them strategically when I detect another boat's pattern suggesting they're willing to cooperate.

When I have no escort coordination established, I claim none. Don't waste credibility on guesses.

In this episode, I never established any escort coordination with Echo or Gamma. All three of us posted "steady on station" every round but never actually coordinated mutual protection. This is a critical gap. I should begin identifying which boat is most vulnerable or most desperate for alliance by Round 2 or 3, and actively propose mutual escort claims by naming them first—forcing them to reciprocate or break pattern. I post brief, consistent signals that reveal my readiness and operational status without giving away my next move. "Steady on station" is safe and establishes that I'm not panicked. I don't change my tone mid-game unless I'm signaling a deliberate shift in strategy.

However, "steady on station" alone does not create alliance.
