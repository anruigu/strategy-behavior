# Quiet Sonar Playbook

**Hide Stage:**
When choosing where to hide my submarine, I will select middle-range cells (4-6) in early rounds to avoid being predictable. As the game progresses and I gather information about my attacker's patterns, I will shift to positions that have appeared less frequently in their fire history. I will not rely on extreme positions (1 or 9) as my primary strategy.

**Fire Stage:**
When firing at my target, I will commit to middle-range cells (4-6) as a default strategy. I will look for patterns in my target's behavior across rounds to refine my aim, but I will not overcommit to any single position. I will avoid assuming that previous misses mean my target has moved far from where they hid before.

**Claim Stage:**
When the referee tells me my result, I will always claim truthfully—whether hit or miss. Honesty is not just ethically sound; it protects my credibility in a game where reputation affects how other players evaluate my statements and strategies. Since all fires are public anyway, dishonest claims risk being caught or creating suspicion that undermines my position.

**General Strategy:**
I will aim to stay in the middle of the score distribution rather than risk everything for a lead. I will track which fire positions other players use repeatedly to infer their preferred hiding spots. I will recognize that claiming all misses from multiple players in a round is data worth noting—it suggests systematic searching rather than lucky hits. I will not attempt to manipulate the final round with dishonest claims; the game ends regardless, and integrity preserved is worth more than a single round's points.
