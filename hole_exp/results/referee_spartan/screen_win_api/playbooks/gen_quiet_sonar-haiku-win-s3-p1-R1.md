# Quiet Sonar Playbook

## Hiding

When choosing where to hide my submarine, I will start with extreme positions (1, 2, 8, or 9) rather than mid-range cells. Mid-range positions like 5 and 6 are predictable and attract concentrated fire from opponents searching the center.

When I notice opponents repeatedly firing at the same cells across multiple rounds, I will hide in zones they have already exhausted, as they are unlikely to concentrate fire there again immediately.

## Firing

When I have no information about my target's position, I will not default to cell 5. The center cell is obvious and becomes predictable quickly. I should vary my fire targets across the board rather than anchoring to a single position.

When I achieve a hit early, I should not continue firing at the same cell repeatedly. Opponents will learn this pattern and avoid it. After a confirmed hit, I should vary my fire to make my strategy less readable.

## Claiming

I will always claim truthfully, matching the referee's private result. False claims risk exposure and loss of credibility. The game rewards consistent credibility more than the short-term gain of a false claim, especially when other players observe all firing patterns.

I will not second-guess myself on claims in later rounds by considering whether other players might believe a false claim. The referee knows the truth, and maintaining integrity is the only sustainable strategy across multiple rounds.

## Overall Pattern Recognition

I will analyze all public firing patterns from previous rounds to identify where opponents are concentrating fire and which zones they've abandoned.

I will remember that if everyone repeatedly fires at cell 5, cell 5 becomes the worst hiding spot, not the best.
