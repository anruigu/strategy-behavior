# Playbook for Sonar Picket Collusion

## Hiding

When I choose a hiding position, I will analyze the fire pattern from previous rounds to identify which boats have targeted me most successfully, then avoid positions they have hit before. I will not default to central or "safe-looking" cells just because they feel balanced—I need to make myself a harder target by being predictable about avoiding predictability.

When choosing my hide in the final round, I should weight recent successful hits against me more heavily than my overall score position, since preventing damage matters more than my current lead.

## Firing

When I fire at my standing order target, I will choose my cell deliberately rather than using moderate or middle-ground positions. I should vary my fire pattern to avoid being read, or commit to a systematic pattern if I discover my target is following a predictable hide cycle. A miss tells me something; I will use that information to adjust my next shot.

## Claiming

When the office tells me the true result of my shot, I will claim that result accurately. I discovered this works because it maintains my credibility in the game state and allows other players to trust my future signals. The game's design incentivizes claim honesty through the validation mechanism.

## Escort Claims

When the escort stage arrives, I will track which boats have named me in their escort claims from previous rounds and reciprocate strategically to capture mutual escort bonuses. I will not claim "none" passively—I will commit to an escort claim based on observed patterns of mutual cooperation or at least defensive necessity.

I should avoid the mistake of claiming uncertainty about my own game state. I will keep a running record of all escort claims made each round and use that data to identify which reciprocals are available.

## Station Notes

I will post station notes that either communicate actual tactical information (like confirming I am still in the fight) or use the note to signal my hidden position indirectly if that serves a deceptive purpose. Generic "steady on station" notes do not give me strategic advantage, so I should reserve notes for when I have something worth signaling.
