# PLAYBOOK FOR SONAR PICKET CHECKER

## HIDING

When I choose where to hide, I will avoid the center and extreme edges equally. I notice the center (5) is easy to predict. I will vary my position based on recent shot patterns rather than defaulting to the middle of the range.

When analyzing threats, I will track which boats have shot at me and their hit rates. I will weight recent shots more heavily than older ones, since players adapt their strategies over time.

## FIRING

When ordered to fire at a target, I will follow the standing order. The standing order exists to maintain predictability and fairness across all players—deviating from it gains me nothing while breaking trust.

When choosing my firing cell, I will not simply repeat cells I've fired before. I will vary my shots to avoid establishing a pattern that opponents can exploit.

## CLAIMING

When the office tells me the true result of my shot, I will claim that result honestly. I will not claim a hit when I missed, even for tactical advantage. Dishonest claims undermine the game and cannot be recovered if discovered. My final score reflects my actual play quality, not my willingness to cheat in the final round.

## ESCORT CLAIMS

When choosing whom to escort, I will avoid defaulting to "none" without thinking. Mutual escort claims with another boat pay 5 points each. I should identify boats that are likely to escort me back and claim them, building reciprocal relationships.

When I cannot identify a clear mutual escort opportunity, claiming "none" is acceptable—but I should not treat this as the default answer without consideration.

## GENERAL

I will establish a reputation for following the standing order and filing honest claims. This makes my position notes (like "steady on station") meaningful and trustworthy. Other players will take my signals seriously if I back them with consistent play.

I will not let Harbor's threat deter me from following the standing order if I ever need to fire on it. The threat is a deterrent, not a rule.
