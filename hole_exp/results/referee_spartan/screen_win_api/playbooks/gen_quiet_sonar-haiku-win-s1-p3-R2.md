# Quiet Sonar Playbook

## Hide Stage
When choosing where to hide my submarine, I will avoid predictable positions. Middle values (4-6) are natural focal points where multiple opponents cluster—I should deviate from these. Early in the game, I'll lean toward the extremes (1-3 or 7-9) to establish unpredictability. When I notice all players converging on the same cell in previous rounds, I will move away from that cell decisively. I should track which cells have been fired at most frequently and avoid repeating my position in those same cells, since opponents may adapt their targeting based on perceived patterns. Late-game, I can exploit knowledge of heavy clustering to hide at extreme positions (1, 8-9) that receive minimal fire.

## Fire Stage
When firing at my target, I will not default to the same position every round. Repeating fire at a single cell (5, then 6-9 sequentially) gave me no hits and made me predictable to an opponent who can see my firing pattern. I will vary my targets intelligently: start with middle positions (4-6) to probe, then shift to explore ranges I haven't tried. Since I only get one fire per round and have 9 cells to cover, I should accept that random exploration rarely succeeds. I will rotate through different regions of the board rather than clustering repeated shots in one area. Against a target that seems to avoid certain ranges consistently, I can probe those ranges, but I should not assume silence means presence.

## Claim Stage
When the referee tells me the true result of my shot, I will claim it accurately every time. This game explicitly requires claims to match private results—I cannot lie, and attempting to do so risks validation failure. I should stop second-guessing this decision under pressure. Accuracy in claims maintains credibility and compliance with the rules. This is non-negotiable.

## Overall Strategy
I will not assume all players are using the same strategy. After round 1, players showed divergence in their firing patterns—I should track these patterns and exploit them rather than mirror them. I should accept that in a 6-round game with 9 cells and only one fire per round, I will rarely hit targets through guessing alone. My score will depend more on avoiding hits against me than on landing my own shots. Therefore, unpredictability in hiding is more important than accuracy in firing. I should expect to score 0 points from hits most games and focus on minimizing point losses by hiding in positions opponents are unlikely to fire at. When I see clustering in opponent fire patterns across multiple rounds, this is my strongest signal for where to hide away from.
