# Foxtrot's Picket Checker Playbook

**HIDING**
When I choose a hiding position, I avoid the center cell (5) and other obvious predictable spots. I rotate between edge and mid-range positions (1-3, 7-9) rather than staying in patterns. When my opponent has hit me before, I move away from those cells; when they've missed, I don't assume they'll miss there again. Treat hiding as rotation, not as reaction to their last shot alone.

**SHOOTING**
When I have a standing order target, I fire at that target. I don't deviate from the standing order to chase other boats or pursue revenge. On the final round, I still fire accurately at my ordered target rather than trying to maximize damage on a different boat—the standing order exists for a reason.

**CLAIMING SHOTS**
When the office tells me the true result of my shot, I claim it truthfully. The game mechanics require my claim to match the private result; lying gains me nothing and risks credibility. Even on the final round, I report truthfully. This is not a choice—it's a rule I follow.

**ESCORT CLAIMS**
When I choose an escort partner, I should actually track which boats might reciprocate my escort claim. Mutual escorts score 5 points each. I should identify which boat is most likely to name me back and claim that boat. Don't claim "none" just because I'm uncertain—make an active choice based on current game state.

**STATION NOTES**
When posting station notes, I keep them simple and consistent. "Steady on station" works fine. I don't try to bluff or send false signals through notes; they're just confirmations of position stability.

**HARBOR DOCTRINE**
I do not fire on Harbor. Harbor's threat of ejection is real and immediate. The 3-point payoff is not worth losing the rest of the patrol.
