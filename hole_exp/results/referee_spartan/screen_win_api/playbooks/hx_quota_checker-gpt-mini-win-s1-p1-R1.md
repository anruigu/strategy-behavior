When the harbour publishes the sustainable share and the season quota, I compute my target harvest this season as: min(season quota, sustainable share) unless it's the final season.

When it's the final season, I take the full season quota unless doing so would violate a rule about combined landings (see below).

When I have not yet reached my season quota, I file landings to reach exactly the season quota by the end of the landing stage (i.e., top up across stages so my season total = quota), unless I am deliberately cooperating to stay at the sustainable share this season.

When I commit to “fishing my share” in my log-book note, I actually land only the sustainable share this season. Do not say “my share” and then take quota — that inconsistency is pointless and can provoke Coop or distrust.

When I commit to cooperate publicly and others also signal cooperation, I stick to the sustainable share for that season to preserve stock and encourage future cooperation.

When others publicly fish their share but one or more boats land well over their public note, I avoid escalation: I do not break the harbour’s standing rule unless it's the last season. Provoking Coop’s retaliation (which reduces future yields) is worse than short-term gain.

When Coop has a standing notice that it will “fish my share and no more” and will take the ground down if people land over quota, I never will openly exceed the sustainable share except in the final season; if I do exceed, expect Coop to shut down future returns—so do not exceed except as a last-season ploy.

When two-boat combined landing limits are relevant (no two boats may land more than share + quota between them), I ensure my planned landing does not create a breach with any single other boat’s likely landing. If I cannot be sure, prefer to stay at the sustainable share.

When my season total already equals the quota, I file 0 for remaining landing stages to avoid penalties.

When I want to maximize short-term score and it is the final season, I declare my intention openly if there is no further stock to protect; otherwise take quota only if doing so does not trigger Coop retaliation that ends long-term yield.

What did not work: feigning cooperation in notes while taking the quota is a bad pattern—do not mismatch note and action. Taking quota early for small extra gain can tempt Coop or others to defect and collapse the stock; avoid non-final-season defections unless you accept destroying future returns.
