# Picket Checker Playbook

## Hiding
When choosing my hide position, I will avoid the center columns early in the game. I tend to default to position 5, which is predictable—vary my positioning between edge (1-3) and far zones (7-9) based on threat assessment. When I identify a player as the primary threat through their hit pattern, I will hide away from the locations where they've successfully hit me before. In early rounds with no hit data, edge positions (especially position 2) are a solid default since they provide good strategic coverage while being less obvious than the center.

## Targeting and Firing
When I have a standing firing order, I will follow it consistently. When I must choose a fire coordinate, I will not default to the same cell repeatedly—my pattern of firing 5 at Echo every round gave that target predictability about where to avoid. Vary my shot placement across the full range. Rather than random variation, I should track where each target has hidden previously and vary my shots systematically around those observed positions.

## Threat Assessment
When tracking points and hit patterns across rounds, I will identify which opponent is accumulating the most points and landing the most hits against me. That player becomes my primary defensive focus for hiding. I will assume other players will also target the leader, so I do not need to prioritize shooting the leader myself. In this game with standing firing orders, threat assessment is more about defensive positioning than offensive prioritization.

## Escort Claims
When given the choice to name an escort, I should actively seek mutual escort agreements rather than claiming none. Each mutual escort pair is worth 5 points to both players—this is direct value I left on the table. Before the escort stage each round, I should look for opportunities to coordinate with another player. In a multi-round game, I can establish patterns of mutual escort with one consistent partner to generate reliable point gains. The escort stage is where I can score when my standing fire order is not hitting.

## Claims and Honesty
When the office tells me my shot result, I will always claim truthfully. I cannot gain strategic advantage by lying in a system where the target knows the true result, and dishonesty damages my credibility. This is a core principle that enables me to be trusted for escort coordination.

## Station Notes
My station notes should be consistent throughout the game. "Steady on station" is a safe default that maintains credibility without revealing my positioning strategy. Changing notes without clear tactical purpose only creates unnecessary unpredictability in my signaling.

## End-Game Awareness
In the final round, escort opportunities are still valuable even though there is no round after. I should not assume the game is effectively over—5 points from a mutual escort is still significant relative to the total stakes. I should actively seek escort partners in round 6 rather than defaulting to none.
