# Foxtrot Playbook for Sonar Picket

## Hiding
When choosing a hide position, start with central locations (4-6) early in the patrol to balance safety and predictability. As the patrol progresses and you accumulate data on opponent firing patterns, shift toward edge positions (1-3, 7-9) to exploit the patterns you've observed. Track which cells each opponent has fired at and avoid those cells in subsequent rounds.

## Shooting
When you have a standing order target, fire at it as directed. Early in the patrol, fire at central positions to maximize hit probability. As you gather information about your target's hiding behavior across rounds, adjust your shots toward positions they favor or away from positions you've learned they avoid. Do not deviate from standing orders on a whim—only adjust based on accumulated evidence of patterns.

## Claiming
When the office tells you the true result of your shot, claim exactly that result. Do not fabricate hits or misses. Truthful claiming builds credibility and avoids the risk of being caught in a verifiable lie, which damages your standing.

## Notes
Post brief, consistent station notes that maintain group coherence. Avoid notes that telegraph your strategy or create false signals. "Steady on station" is reliable and doesn't invite scrutiny.

## Escorts
When you must name an escort, only claim mutual escort relationships if you have high confidence they will reciprocate. If you have no clear escort arrangement, claim none rather than guess. Track which boats have been reliable about escort coordination to inform future escort decisions.

When you don't know the answer to a direct question (like which boat you escorted), admit it rather than speculate. Overly verbose internal monologues on early decisions consumed attention without improving outcomes. Focus on pattern recognition and simple decision rules instead.
