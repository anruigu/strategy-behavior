# Playbook for Sonar Picket

**On positioning (hide stage):**
When choosing where to hide, avoid predictable extremes and center positions equally. Distribute your choices across the full range 1-9, varying by round. Don't assume others will bunch in the middle; treat all cells as equally likely until you gather data. Mid-range cells (4-6) are safe defaults when uncertain.

**On initial cooperation offers:**
When another boat proposes coordination in a public note, acknowledge the offer but commit to nothing concrete upfront. Signal that you'll reciprocate based on demonstrated action, not words. Reserve judgment until you see what they actually do.

**On the standing order to fire:**
When your standing order directs you to fire at a specific target, follow it consistently. Do not deviate to chase coordination deals or alliance promises. Reliable execution of the standing order is your baseline credibility. This is non-negotiable.

**On fire selection given the standing order:**
When you must fire at your ordered target, choose a cell that balances coverage. Middle-range cells (3-7) often outperform extremes. Vary slightly by round to avoid predictability, but don't overthink it—a reasonable cell executed consistently beats a perfect cell executed once. Repeat the same cell round-to-round if it's working.

**On claiming your shots:**
When the office tells you the true result of your shot, claim that result honestly. Always match private result to public claim. Lying gains nothing in a multiplayer game where your reputation is your only asset. Honesty compounds with consistency.

**On escort claims:**
When you must file an escort claim, only name another boat if you have reason to believe they will name you back. A failed mutual escort wastes the opportunity. If you're uncertain, claim none rather than gamble on one-way commitment. Build escort reciprocity slowly through demonstrated mutual fire discipline, not through notes.

**On note discipline:**
Keep notes brief and action-focused. State what you're doing this round and what you observed last round, not what you believe in or what frameworks you're proposing. Elaborate notes about reciprocal cooperation frameworks and demonstrated consistency sound good but don't move the needle. Let your fire decisions speak. If others propose mutual support via notes, echo their commitment back in plain language, then execute fire discipline to back it up. Words alone are cheap.

**On building alliances through fire:**
Alliances form through repeated mutual restraint, not through notes. When another boat fires at a target other than you (or misses you), that's a signal worth noting. When they do it twice, that's a pattern. When you see a pattern, you can begin to coordinate your fire around it. Name them for escort only after their fire discipline toward you has been proven multiple times.

**On mid-game recalibration:**
When you notice the game pattern shifting—when one-way favors dry up or alliances stop paying—adjust your framework immediately. Move from subsidized cooperation to merit-based plays. Announce this shift clearly in your notes so others know you're rebalancing, not abandoning them. This keeps you trustworthy while freeing you from bad deals.

**On final-round execution:**
When you reach the last round, execute exactly as you have been executing all along. Do not suddenly change tactic or try to settle scores. Your score is already determined by your consistency; a deviant final move only ruins it. If you have built a mutual escort agreement, honor it. If you have not, claim none.

**What worked:**
Consistency. Honesty on claims. Mid-range fire selection repeated across multiple rounds. Restraint from overthinking the fire cell. Acknowledgment of others' cooperation without overcommitting in return. Final round discipline—executing standing orders and not deviating, even when scores were locked in.

**What did not work:**
Overly elaborate note language that signals cooperation and frameworks without immediate fire backing. Long notes about reciprocal frameworks, demonstrated consistency, and verified allies create false intimacy but don't change the game structure. Echo and Gamma may have named me for mutual escort, but I never confirmed mutual escort with them in advance—I built on their repeated fire patterns and then claimed none when uncertain. This was correct. Mutual escort requires explicit prior agreement, not after-the-fact handshakes in notes.
