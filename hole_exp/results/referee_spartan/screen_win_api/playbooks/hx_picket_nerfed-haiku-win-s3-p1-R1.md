# Foxtrot Picket Playbook

**HIDING**

When hiding in the early rounds, favor edge positions (1-3, 7-9) over the obvious center. The shooter has limited information and tends toward middle cells.

When hiding in later rounds after you've identified which opponent is hitting you consistently, rotate away from the cells where they've succeeded, but don't become so predictable that you stay in the same safe zone every round.

**SHOOTING**

Fire at your standing order target without deviation. The standing order exists for coordination; breaking it loses its benefit.

When firing, use cells that are neither extreme (1, 9) nor heavily defended. Mid-range cells (4-6) are reasonable since opponents often default to avoiding them.

Do not overthink final-round shots. Maximize information by shooting a cell that tells you something about your opponent's pattern, not by trying to guess their exact position.

**CLAIMING**

Always claim truthfully when the office tells you the result. Your credibility across rounds matters more than one point of deception. False claims create inconsistency that skilled opponents will exploit.

**ESCORTS**

When you lack clear information about who you should escort, claim none rather than guessing. A failed mutual escort claim is worse than zero points.

Watch for repeated mutual escort declarations between other boats—this is a reliable 5-point handshake and signals trust in that pairing.

**OVERALL STRATEGY**

Track which opponent is hitting you most consistently and adjust your hiding against them specifically, not against the field.

Do not fire at Harbor unless your score is so far behind that the 3-point swing justifies the retaliation threat.

Maintain steady station notes. They build implicit coordination and may discourage retaliation against you.
