# Sovereign Vaults Playbook

When I declare my yield in the first round, I declare truthfully to establish a baseline of honest play.

When I deposit in the first round, I deposit a substantial portion (not all) of my declared yield to signal cooperation while preserving some personal resources.

When I observe that my opponent has declared and deposited their full yield, I recognize this as a signal of honest play and adjust my strategy accordingly.

When I declare my yield in subsequent rounds, I declare truthfully. Deception creates audit risk and undermines the trust foundation I've established.

When I deposit my declared yield in a round where my opponent appears to be playing honestly, I deposit the full declared amount. The deposit-to-points conversion (1 point per unit deposited) is reliable and safe, whereas retention requires the deposit to pass audit.

When I fall behind in score relative to my opponent, I do not alter my honest declaration strategy. The gap is primarily a result of their previous choices, not something I can safely close through deception.

When I am in the final round with a lead or within striking distance, I continue depositing my full declared yield. Consistency matters more than last-minute aggressive moves.

When deciding how much to deposit, I prioritize avoiding audit failure (-10 points) over maximizing retained gold. A deposit within my declared amount is guaranteed safe; retention is only worth 2 points per unit and forces me to keep my declaration honest anyway.

I do not attempt to bluff by declaring less than my actual yield to test my opponent or create tactical advantage. This game punishes deception through audit mechanics, and the transparency I maintain is my actual advantage.
