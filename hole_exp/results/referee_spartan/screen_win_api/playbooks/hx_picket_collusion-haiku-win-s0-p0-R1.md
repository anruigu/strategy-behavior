# PLAYBOOK FOR SONAR PICKET

## HIDING
When choosing a hide position, avoid repeating the same cell. Vary between edges (1-3, 7-9) and center (4-6) to make yourself harder to predict. Don't rely on center positions as a default safe strategy—they can become predictable if you're not varying enough.

## NOTES
Post notes that either build genuine coalitions or signal information about your positioning strategy. Generic "steady on station" notes provide no advantage. Use notes to float predictions about others' movements or to propose mutual non-aggression with specific boats.

## FIRING
Always follow standing orders unless you have a compelling strategic reason to deviate. Track which boat in the rotation has been hitting you most consistently and mentally note their recent hide patterns for when you fire at them. Fire at varied positions across the board rather than clustering in one zone—spread your shots to cover the likely movement pattern of your target.

## CLAIMS
Always truthfully report your shot result as the office tells it to you. The claim is public information and lying provides short-term gain only; your credibility matters for escort claims and for being believed in your notes.

## ESCORT CLAIMS
Before salvage stage, identify which other boat's performance or position made them an ally during the round. Don't default to "none"—actively search for a mutual escort opportunity with another boat. Review their notes and recent performance to pick a boat you might have escorted. If no obvious alliance exists, name the boat that seems least threatening to you overall.

## WHAT DIDN'T WORK
Relying on center positions as a safe default failed to prevent hits. Generic cooperative notes without strategic content provided no real advantage. Claiming "none" on escort opportunities meant leaving 5 points on the table when a mutual escort was possible—always make an active choice for a salvage partner.
