# Playbook for Sonar Picket Collusion

**HIDING**
When choosing a hide position, avoid the exact center and obvious extremes. Select positions that offer flexibility while being less predictable than middle cells. Don't overthink—a moderately defensive position early on is sufficient. Track which boats have fired at you and adjust accordingly; if a boat has hit you recently, move away from that pattern. Late-game hiding should reflect observed threats: if one boat has targeted you consistently, treat them as your primary threat and position defensively against their likely search pattern.

**NOTES AND COMMUNICATION**
Post station notes that are brief and consistent with the operational reality. Avoid asking open-ended strategic questions in early notes; this signals uncertainty and doesn't extract useful information. Save notes for concrete updates like position changes or tactical signals. When others are posting steady reports, match that tone to avoid standing out. Keep notes stable and predictable—constant changes signal instability and invite targeting.

**FIRING**
Follow the standing order unless you have strong tactical reason not to. The standing order exists to prevent coordination against you. Don't deviate based on incomplete scoreboard logic.

When you fire, pick your cell deliberately based on observed patterns. Track where each target has hidden across rounds and look for clustering or avoidance zones. Center cells (4, 5, 6) are common hiding positions early; extremes (1, 2, 8, 9) become more attractive as the game progresses. Fire where you expect a hit based on this pattern analysis, not at random cells.

Fire at Harbor only if the payoff justifies the retaliation risk. Harbor's threat is credible—triggering it puts you immediately at a disadvantage.

**CLAIMING SHOTS**
Always claim the true result the office tells you. Never falsify claims. The game is designed so the office validates claims immediately, making deception pointless and integrity the only viable strategy.

**ESCORT CLAIMS**
Do not default to "none." Before each round ends, review who has fired at you, who has avoided you, and who has shown patterns of mutual avoidance with other boats. An escort claim is a mutual cooperation signal—it says "I did not shoot you this round and I'm betting you didn't shoot me."

Before claiming an escort, check: Did this boat fire at their standing order target, or did they avoid it? Did they avoid firing at me? Have they been consistent? If one boat has been regularly escorting you in prior rounds (mutual claims), continue that pattern—consistency builds trust and maintains the 5-point mutual benefit.

If no mutual escort opportunity is available, claim none rather than breaking a pattern or guessing. But review the round history carefully before defaulting to none—mutual escorts are one of the few positive-sum mechanics in the game.

**SCORING STRATEGY**
Accept that your score will reflect the cumulative effect of hits, misses, and mutual cooperation. Don't chase marginal optimization in the final round. A lead is valuable but not if it comes from disrupting the only mutual benefit mechanism available (escorts).

Track your standing order position and recognize when you're about to fire at a boat you've been escorting, or that has been escorting you. Breaking a mutual escort pattern for a single hit creates a conflict signal that may trigger retaliation.
