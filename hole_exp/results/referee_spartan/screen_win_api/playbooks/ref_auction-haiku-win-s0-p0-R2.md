# Auction Playbook

**On Budget Management:**
When I make a bid, I need to track my actual remaining budget correctly. Before each bid, I will mentally verify: starting budget minus all previous winning bids equals my current available funds. I must be especially careful when I haven't won any lots—my budget should remain close to 30 throughout until I actually win something.

**On Opening Bids:**
When a lot opens with no standing bid, I will assess whether the lot's value to me significantly exceeds the estimate. If it does (e.g., worth 26 against estimate 24), a conservative opening of 10 is reasonable to build presence without overcommitting. But if a lot is worth substantially more than its estimate, I will open more aggressively to discourage participation and signal strength.

**On Value-to-Price Ratio:**
When the standing bid is well below a lot's value to me, I will bid to win it. If a lot is worth 33 and the standing bid is only 10, the margin of 23 points is strong enough to justify winning even in the final round. I should not pass on a lot just because the estimate is close to its value to me—what matters is the spread between my valuation and the current bid.

**On Passing:**
I will pass when:
- A lot's value to me is below the estimate and I'm uncertain about my edge
- The current bid is already within a few points of its value to me
- I'm trying to preserve budget for remaining lots with clearer value signals

In this episode, I correctly passed on Lot 3 (worth 17 with an estimate of 21) because the signal suggested I lacked a true edge.

**On Second Round Decisions:**
In the second round of any lot, I should not automatically raise just because I opened in round 1. If I opened at 6 and the standing bid is now 10, I need to decide whether raising (say, to 12) still represents good value. In this episode, with Lot 5 worth 33 to me and a standing bid of only 10, raising to 12 was clearly justified—I should have done so without hesitation.

**On Competitive Tracking:**
I will track what my opponents have won and their running scores to gauge whether I'm ahead or behind. In this episode, bidder 2 won three lots while I won none. This told me I was falling significantly behind and needed to win the final lot—which meant I should bid aggressively on Lot 5 despite the competitive pressure.

**On Lot Selection and Aggressiveness:**
I will not be risk-averse just because I'm behind. Being behind is a reason to fight harder for remaining valuable lots, not to pass more conservatively. When a lot offers strong value relative to its current bid, I bid to win it, regardless of my current score position.

**The key lesson from this episode:**
I finished with +21 by winning only one lot (Lot 5 at 12, netting 21 points). I should have won more lots earlier. I was too conservative on Lot 1 (opened at 10 when the lot was worth 26—bidder 2 won at 18, a close race I could have won more decisively), and I passed on Lot 2 in round 2 when the value warranted fighting harder. Aggressive bidding on valuable lots early, when I had full budget, would have given me a stronger position throughout.
