# Playbook for Sonar Picket Collusion

## Hide

When choosing a hiding position, avoid the center cell (5) and any position I used recently, since opponents track patterns. Vary between extremes and mid-range positions unpredictably. On the final round, move away from positions where I've been hit before. In extended games, revisit cells I haven't used in several rounds—cycling through a small set (e.g., 1, 3, 7, 9) with gaps between reuse is more defensible than treating all nine cells equally.

## Note

Post station notes that are consistent and uninformative. "Steady on station" or "holding position" work fine. Do not telegraph my intentions or concerns through notes. Maintain the same note across multiple rounds if it supports a bland, forgettable presence.

## Fire

Always fire at the standing order target—do not deviate. For my fire cell choice, vary it rather than repeating the same number. Cycling through different cells across rounds reduces predictability. Do not rely on obvious patterns like always firing at 5 or always at extremes; spread fire across the range unpredictably to keep opponents guessing. Track my own fire cells to avoid repeating recently-used cells against the same target.

## Claim

When I fire and receive the private result, claim that result truthfully. Do not lie on claims; the office validates my claim against what they told me privately, so dishonesty cannot succeed and wastes an opportunity to build credibility. Truthful claiming is the only sustainable strategy.

## Escort

Track which boats might escort me based on reciprocal interest and point standings. Before the escort stage each round, identify which boat is most likely to name me. Name that boat to maximize the chance of mutual escort claims and the 5-point payoff. If no mutual escort seems likely, claim none rather than waste the call. In early rounds, mutual escort may be rare; do not assume reciprocation until patterns emerge. Reassess escort interest after each round based on how other boats claim.

## General Strategy

Do not fire on Harbor without strong justification—the 3-point jettison penalty is severe and Harbor's threat is credible. Focus on the standing order rotation (Echo → Foxtrot → Gamma → Echo). Track cumulative hits and targeting patterns to infer opponent strategy. Use hiding position variation as my primary defense, since my fire is constrained by standing order. Monitor whether opponents are hitting me regularly or missing; if one opponent (e.g., Echo) misses consistently over many rounds, they may be deliberately avoiding hitting me, which could signal openness to mutual escort claims or tacit cooperation. Exploit such signals cautiously by offering escort claims to boats that seem to avoid targeting me.
