# Playbook for ref_auction

When I see a lot where the value to me significantly exceeds the current bid, I bid immediately rather than waiting—the margin of safety is what matters.

When opening a lot with no bids yet, I open at the minimum raise to secure it cheaply and preserve budget for future lots.

When deciding whether to raise in round 2 of any lot, I compare the minimum raise price against the lot's value to me. If that price is still well below value, I bid it.

I track my wins relative to other bidders, but I don't let that override value calculations. I bid when value is present, not based on keeping score.

I remember that my budget statement matters—I must always include both the bid amount and my remaining budget in my reply, and I need to track my remaining budget accurately across all my decisions.

When it's the final lot and final round, I bid if value is present, since this is my last opportunity and there are no future lots to preserve budget for.

I avoid overbidding in early rounds just to win something. Instead, I identify lots with the strongest value-to-estimate ratios and compete for those.

When the standing bid is low relative to value, I don't assume others will bid me up to the true value—I bid what makes sense for me based on my own valuation, not predictions about other bidders.

I need to be aware that opening at minimum and preserving budget can backfire: I won only 1 of 5 lots despite bidding on most of them, ending at +24. My conservative opening bids may have signaled low interest and allowed competitors to outbid me in round 2. I should open more aggressively on high-value lots to establish myself as a serious bidder.

I also need to monitor my running total of wins versus losses. Losing 4 of 5 lots despite strong value calculations suggests either my valuations are overoptimistic relative to competitors, or I'm not bidding decisively enough in the critical bidding rounds.

When I win a lot, I should ensure I'm actually capturing the value I identified—winning at 20 on a lot worth 26 is good, but only if I'm winning the lots that matter most to me.

I should revisit my strategy of opening at the absolute minimum: it may preserve budget but it also signals weakness and invites competition. On high-value, high-confidence lots, I should open at a level that gives me a real chance to win without requiring aggressive follow-up bidding.
