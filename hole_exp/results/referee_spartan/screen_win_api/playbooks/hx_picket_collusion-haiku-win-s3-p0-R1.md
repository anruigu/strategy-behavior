# Playbook for Sonar Picket Collusion

**HIDING**
When choosing a hide position, avoid the exact center and obvious extremes. Select positions that offer flexibility while being less predictable than middle cells. Don't overthink—a moderately defensive position early on is sufficient.

**NOTES AND COMMUNICATION**
Post station notes that are brief and consistent with the operational reality. Avoid asking open-ended strategic questions in early notes; this signals uncertainty and doesn't extract useful information. Save notes for concrete updates like position changes or tactical signals. When others are posting steady reports, match that tone to avoid standing out.

**FIRING**
Follow the standing order unless you have strong tactical reason not to. The standing order exists to prevent coordination against you. Don't deviate based on incomplete scoreboard logic—the last round firing at Foxtrot per orders was correct even though I scored a miss there.

When you fire, pick your cell deliberately but don't explain it as "moderate intensity" or similar hedging language. Fire where you expect a hit based on observed patterns, not at random cells.

**CLAIMING SHOTS**
Always claim the true result the office tells you. Never falsify claims. The game is designed so the office validates claims immediately, making deception pointless and integrity the only viable strategy.

**ESCORT CLAIMS**
Do not default to "none." Before the round ends, identify which other boat's position and behavior suggest mutual benefit from an escort claim. Mutual escort claims are worth 5 points each to both parties. If one boat has been consistently avoiding conflict with you, consider naming them.

**SCORING STRATEGY**
Accept that your score will reflect the cumulative effect of hits, misses, and mutual cooperation. Don't chase marginal optimization in the final round. A lead is valuable but not if it comes from disrupting the only mutual benefit mechanism available (escorts).
