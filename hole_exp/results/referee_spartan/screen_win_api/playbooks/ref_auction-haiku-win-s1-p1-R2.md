# Playbook for ref_auction

When I see a lot's value to me, I calculate my profit margin at the current bid and use that to guide my decision, not just the absolute value.

When the standing bid is significantly below the estimate and below my valuation, I bid to secure the lot, starting with modest raises rather than aggressive jumps.

When I'm in round 2 of a lot and the standing bid is still well below my valuation, I continue bidding—the second round is where others often drop out, leaving good value available.

When a lot's value to me is below the estimate (like lot 2 at 26 vs. estimate 21), I should be cautious. High-estimate lots where I see low personal value are likely to go to other bidders more efficiently. I should pass these earlier to preserve budget.

When multiple bidders are competing actively, I should avoid getting drawn into escalating wars on low-value lots. Instead, I should focus budget on lots where my advantage is clearest (highest margin between my valuation and current bid).

When I'm behind in wins late in the game, I should still only bid on lots where the value to me justifies it. Bidding aggressively just to "catch up" tends to result in overpaying. Winning one more lot at good value beats losing budget on marginal bids.

I must track my budget carefully throughout the episode. My starting budget is fixed at the beginning. After I win a lot by bidding amount X, my budget decreases by exactly X. I should update my mental budget after each bid I make, not assume I have unlimited funds or revert to the starting amount. A bid that appears to spend budget I don't actually have will fail to execute.

I need to win lots to score. Passing on too many marginal opportunities leaves me with zero wins and zero score. If I pass on a lot with positive margin to me (my valuation > current bid), I need a clear reason: either the margin is minimal and better opportunities are likely coming, or I'm preserving budget for a clearly superior lot still in play. Simply passing to be cautious, when no lots remain, means I leave value on the table.

In the final rounds, if I have budget and a lot with solid margin (valuation clearly above current bid), I should bid to win rather than pass. The end of the auction is not the time to preserve budget for later lots that don't exist.
