# Sovereign Vaults Playbook

**Declaration Phase:**
When I receive my private yield, declare it truthfully. Honest declarations build credibility and eliminate audit risk, which is worth more than the minor flexibility of false declarations.

When I have a strong yield (7+), declare it fully—the points from deposits will exceed any benefit from retention.

When I have a weak yield (1-3), still declare truthfully. My opponent's pattern suggests they do the same, so deception is easily spotted and punished.

**Deposit Phase:**
Deposits score 1 point per unit. Retention scores 2 points per unit. This means retention is mathematically superior—I should retain more than I have been.

When I declare an amount, I should deposit less than my full declared amount and retain the difference. The 2-point retention bonus outweighs the 1-point deposit bonus in most cases.

When I declare a high amount (7+), I should retain roughly half and deposit the rest. This captures the superior retention multiplier while still building credibility through deposits.

When I declare a moderate amount (4-6), I should retain roughly half as well. Even at yields of 5, retention yields 10 points for 5 units retained while depositing yields only 5 points for 5 units deposited.

When I declare a low amount (1-3), I should still deposit most or all of it, since retention of very small amounts doesn't accumulate quickly enough to matter.

**Score Management:**
I ended my first episode at 36 points by depositing almost everything. My opponent scored only 13 points by doing the same. This suggests my pattern was exploitable—I was leaving points on the table through retention.

If I'm ahead by a significant margin (more than 10 points), I can afford to retain more, since my lead is already secure.

If I'm behind or tied, depositing full amounts keeps me competitive in the short term, but over a full game, moderate retention is the stronger long-term play.

**Long Game:**
My opponent played honestly and deposited fully, following my same pattern. They scored much lower. This confirms that honest high-deposit play is not optimal—I need to retain more.

Don't overthink the multiplayer dynamics. The key insight is: retention's 2-point multiplier beats deposits' 1-point multiplier. Maximize retention within the constraints of truthful declaring.

Never false-declare. The 10-point audit penalty is too severe and deposits are scoring well enough when paired with high retention.
