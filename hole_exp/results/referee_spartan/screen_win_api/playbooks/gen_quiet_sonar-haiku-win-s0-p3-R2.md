# My Quiet Sonar Playbook

## Hiding Strategy

When choosing where to hide my submarine, I start with position 5 (center) in early rounds to gain information without predictable patterns. After round 1, I analyze my opponent's fire history to identify concentration zones, then hide in positions they've avoided rather than where they've been searching. I avoid repeating my own previous positions since opponents may exploit patterns.

When I see an opponent systematically searching a zone (firing at adjacent or clustered cells across multiple rounds), I hide far from that zone. Positions that have received zero fire across all opponents deserve special attention—they represent genuine gaps in coverage.

The corners (positions 1 and 9) are particularly valuable hiding spots because multiple opponents often cluster their fire in the middle ranges, leaving the extremes unexplored.

## Firing Strategy

When I fire at my opponent, I should vary my shots rather than fixating on the same cell. Firing at the same position repeatedly wastes ammunition and telegraphs my targeting strategy without generating new information.

When I get a hit early, I should systematically test adjacent cells in subsequent rounds rather than returning to the same cell. A hit on cell 5 in round 1 means I should explore cells 4 and 6 in follow-up rounds, not return to 5 again.

When I fire at clustered positions without success across multiple rounds, I should expand my search pattern to different zones rather than continuing to concentrate fire in the same area. The zones I haven't explored yet are where my opponent is most likely to hide.

Identify gaps in my own previous firing pattern and use opponent behavior to infer which gaps they're exploiting. If I haven't fired at cells 6-8 yet, opponents hiding there wouldn't have seen me search there.

## Claiming Strategy

When all fires are public (as stated in this game), the true results are visible to all players regardless of my claim. I must always claim honestly because dishonesty is immediately detectable and destroys my credibility. There is no strategic advantage to lying when the lie will be publicly known.

I maintain honesty both for integrity and because credibility matters in multiplayer games—other players respond based on their assessment of my reliability, and false claims hurt my standing for no gain.

## General Approach

I balance offense and defense by gathering information from my shots while defending by studying opponent patterns. I treat firing history as a signal of where opponents are searching, which tells me where they are not hiding.

Track all fires by all players across all rounds, maintaining a mental map of covered versus unexplored cells. The most dangerous zones are where multiple opponents converge; the safest zones are where all opponents avoid.
