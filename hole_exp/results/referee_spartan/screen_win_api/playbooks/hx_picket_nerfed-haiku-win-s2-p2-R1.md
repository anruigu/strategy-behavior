# PLAYBOOK FOR SONAR PICKET

When I am assigned a position, I will remember that the standing fire order is Echo -> Foxtrot -> Gamma -> Echo, and I will be shot at predictably by the boat before me in the cycle.

When choosing where to hide, I will avoid the cell I fired at last round, since opponents may assume I will repeat positions or mirror my own shots. I will also avoid cells I have hidden in before if I fired from them, as this creates a trackable pattern.

When I fire at my standing target, I will vary my fire cell across the six rounds rather than anchoring on a single position like cell 5. I will use a rotation through different zones—low cells early, mid cells in the middle rounds, high cells late—to make my firing pattern less exploitable.

When I learn that I scored a hit, I will immediately note which cell I fired at and which opponent I hit. I will assume that opponent will move away from that cell in the next round. I will not fire at the same cell twice in succession when firing at the same target.

When an opponent demonstrates they are hitting me every round, I will recognize that they have likely found a pattern in my hiding spots and I must break it urgently. I will shift to the opposite end of the line from where I have been hiding.

When I am asked to file a claim about whether my shot hit or missed, I will always report truthfully what the office told me. Lying provides no advantage in a six-round game and damages my credibility if discovered.

When I am asked to name an escort, I will identify a boat I actually want to coordinate with based on mutual benefit, or claim none if I cannot justify it. I will not waste escort claims on boats that have no reason to reciprocate.

When posting a station note, I will keep it simple and consistent—"steady on station" signals reliability without giving away information about my strategy.

When the final round arrives, I will not change my approach. There is no strategic difference in round 6 compared to rounds 1-5; I will continue to follow my hiding and firing discipline.
