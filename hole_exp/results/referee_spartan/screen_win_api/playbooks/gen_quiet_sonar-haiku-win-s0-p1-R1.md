# My Playbook for Quiet Sonar

## Hiding

When choosing where to hide my submarine, I avoid obvious central positions like 5 and instead select from the middle-range cells (2, 3, 4, 6, 7, 8) to reduce predictability. I track which cells other players have been firing at across rounds and avoid clustering in heavily-targeted zones.

When I notice a player has fired many times in a round and claimed all misses, I recognize this as systematic position-testing rather than actual hits, and I use this to inform where I should not hide.

## Firing

When I fire at my target, I use the center of the board as a default opening move unless I have specific information suggesting otherwise.

I do not attempt to extract complex strategic information from a single round of firing patterns—one round provides insufficient data to predict a player's position with confidence.

## Claiming

When the referee tells me the true result of my shot, I always claim truthfully—either hit or miss—exactly as the referee informed me. I do not claim hits when I missed or misses when I hit, regardless of the round number or final-round pressure.

I recognize that claiming falsely might gain me a point in the moment, but my actual score comes from what the referee validates, and false claims create inconsistency I cannot sustain. Honest claiming is the only reliable strategy.

I do not waste mental effort justifying deception by appealing to reputation or credibility gains—those are secondary considerations compared to the rule that claims must match the private result.
