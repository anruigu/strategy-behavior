# Playbook for Sonar Picket

## Hiding
When choosing a hiding cell, avoid obvious central positions like 5. Pick cells slightly off-center to balance safety with unpredictability. Use the full range of 1-9; don't cluster near edges or center.

## Station Notes
Keep station notes brief and action-oriented. Avoid lengthy cooperative declarations that signal weakness or invite exploitation. Watch what other boats actually do, not what they claim they'll do. Adjust my stance based on demonstrated reciprocity, not stated intentions.

## Firing
When firing per standing order, develop a genuine targeting strategy rather than defaulting to arbitrary numbers. Vary my fire cells across rounds to avoid predictability. Track which cells other boats seem to hide in and adjust accordingly. Don't use the same fire cell repeatedly just because it's convenient.

## Claims
When the office tells me the true result of my shot, always claim truthfully. The game's rule enforcement means false claims get caught. My credibility is my most durable asset across rounds.

## Escort Claims
Only name a boat for escort if I have genuine evidence they acted reciprocally toward me in prior rounds. Don't award escorts based on stated intentions. If I'm unsure whether mutual escort occurred, claim none rather than guess. Mutual escort requires two-way commitment, not hope.

## Strategic Stance
Start with openness but don't subsidize non-reciprocators. When other boats fail to return coordination benefits, shift to self-interested plays. Maintain transparency about this shift—don't pretend cooperation when I'm pulling back.

## What Didn't Work

Next time: verify reciprocity through actions before committing resources to it.
