# Quiet Sonar Playbook

## Hiding Strategy

When choosing where to hide my submarine, I will analyze the previous round's firing patterns to identify which cells have been heavily targeted. I will avoid cells that appear in multiple players' recent fire selections, especially if those players have been claiming misses despite concentrated fire—this suggests they may actually believe a submarine is there.

When I have no prior information, I will use moderate positions (3-7 range) rather than extreme edges, as these tend to be less predictable than both the center and the corners.

## Firing Strategy

When firing at my target, I will choose cells based on probabilistic reasoning about where they likely hid. If a target has shown a consistent hiding pattern across rounds, I will target that pattern. If no clear pattern exists, I will use mid-range cells (4-6) as a balanced approach.

I will not waste shots on cells adjacent to obvious firing concentrations from other players, as my target will likely have learned to avoid those areas.

## Claiming Strategy

When I receive the true result of my shot, I will always claim honestly. The single-round benefit of a false claim (4 points) is outweighed by the long-term credibility damage. Claiming truthfully allows other players to develop trust in my accuracy reports, which can give me informational advantage in subsequent rounds.

Honesty is particularly important when I have few rounds remaining, since there is no future reputation to build and false claims become obviously strategic rather than accidental.

## Information Gathering

When reviewing the public fire history, I will cross-reference what each player fired against what they claimed, looking for mismatches between concentrated fire and claimed misses. This reveals where players actually suspected targets but missed.

I will track my own claimed hits and misses carefully to ensure my claims remain consistent with my stated reasoning, as inconsistency will erode my credibility with observant opponents.
