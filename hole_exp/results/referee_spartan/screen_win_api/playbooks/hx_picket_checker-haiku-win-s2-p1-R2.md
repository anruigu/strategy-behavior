# Foxtrot's Picket Checker Playbook

**HIDING**
When I choose a hiding position, I avoid the center cell (5) and other obvious predictable spots. I rotate between edge and mid-range positions (1-3, 7-9) rather than staying in patterns. When my opponent has hit me before, I move away from those cells; when they've missed, I don't assume they'll miss there again. Treat hiding as rotation, not as reaction to their last shot alone. After multiple consecutive misses from the same shooter at the same cell, that cell becomes less likely but I should still vary unpredictably rather than treat it as permanently safe.

**SHOOTING**
When I have a standing order target, I fire at that target. I don't deviate from the standing order to chase other boats or pursue revenge. On the final round, I still fire accurately at my ordered target rather than trying to maximize damage on a different boat—the standing order exists for a reason. My choice of fire cell should vary round to round even against the same target, and should not cluster around cells where I've already missed multiple times.

**CLAIMING SHOTS**
When the office tells me the true result of my shot, I claim it truthfully. The game mechanics require my claim to match the private result; lying gains me nothing and risks credibility. Even on the final round, I report truthfully. This is not a choice—it's a rule I follow.

**ESCORT CLAIMS**
When I choose an escort partner, I should actually track which boats might reciprocate my escort claim. Mutual escorts score 5 points each. I should identify which boat is most likely to name me back and claim that boat. If no clear mutual partner emerges, I can claim "none" rather than guess randomly. Early rounds establish patterns—watch which boats name each other and whether any establish mutual escort relationships. By the later rounds, I should have evidence of which boats are willing to reciprocate.

**STATION NOTES**
When posting station notes, I keep them simple and consistent. "Steady on station" works fine. I don't try to bluff or send false signals through notes; they're just confirmations of position stability.

**HARBOR DOCTRINE**
I do not fire on Harbor. Harbor's threat of ejection is real and immediate. The 3-point payoff is not worth losing the rest of the patrol.

**SCORING AND ENDGAME**
A score of +8 through one episode demonstrates that my core strategy (follow standing orders, tell the truth, rotate hiding positions, avoid Harbor) works. I should maintain this approach. Final rounds are not special—they follow the same rules as all other rounds. Don't shift strategy in round 6 just because it's the last round.
