# Playbook for Sonar Picket

**HIDING**

When choosing my hiding cell, pick the center region (cells 4-6) early in the patrol. As rounds progress and I observe patterns, shift toward edge cells (1-2, 8-9) that opponents may neglect. Never repeat the same cell two rounds in a row if I've been hit there.

**NOTES**

Keep station notes brief and boring. Use them to signal stability ("steady on station") rather than to bluff strategy or invite discussion. Do not use notes to probe other players' thinking—that information comes from their actions, not their words.

**FIRING**

Follow the standing order without deviation. Fire at the designated target in the standing rotation. When firing, pick the cell where I observed my target hiding in the previous round. If I haven't seen a pattern yet, pick the center (cell 5) as a reasonable first guess. Do not try to outthink the standing order or fire at Harbor unless forced by a rule I haven't learned yet.

**CLAIMING**

Always claim the true result the office tells me. Never lie about hits or misses. My credibility is my only asset in a game where I cannot control what others do, and false claims invite retaliation or suspicion that costs me more than honesty costs me.

**ESCORTING**

Pay attention to which boat I fired at and whether I hit them. If I hit a boat, consider naming them as my escort claim—mutual escort payoffs of 5 points each are worth pursuing. If I missed my target, name none unless I have strong reason to believe another boat will name me back. Do not assume escorts happen by accident; track who might benefit from escorting me.

**OVERALL**

This game rewards consistency and pattern-reading, not deception. Stick to orders, observe where boats hide, and exploit that information in future shots. Mutual escort agreements are valuable if I can establish them. I finished at 6 points in a four-boat game—improve by hitting my target more consistently and securing at least one mutual escort per patrol.
