When a lot is presented and the current standing bid is below my private value, I raise by the minimum required to become/highlight the high bidder if that bid leaves me with a positive surplus (private value − final price > 0) and is within my remaining budget.

When the current standing bid is above my private value, I pass immediately.

When I am already the high bidder and an opponent outbids me, I only raise to retake the lead if the new minimum required (current bid + minimum raise) is strictly ≤ my private value and I can afford it; I raise by the minimum amount necessary, not more.

When deciding whether to open bidding on a lot with no current bids, I only open at the minimum raise and only if the opening bid would be comfortably below my private value and consistent with conserving budget for remaining high-value lots. I avoid opening contests where the public estimate or likely competition suggests the price will exceed my private value.

I always track and update my remaining budget immediately after winning a lot (subtract the payment) and use that updated budget for subsequent decisions. I will not place bids that would leave my budget negative or that I cannot legally make.

If the minimum required raise to retake or acquire a lot exceeds my remaining budget, I pass and do not attempt to re-enter the lot.

When choosing between competing opportunities across multiple remaining lots, I prioritize lots with the largest expected positive surplus for me (private value minus expected final price). Conserving budget to secure a few high-surplus wins is preferable to winning many low- or zero-surplus lots.

I do not escalate bids for psychology or activity—I bid only to gain positive net value. I avoid “bidding wars” where the price approaches my private value unless the expected surplus still justifies the cost.

If the standing bid is far below my private value and there is low risk of forcing later budget problems, I use minimum raises to try to secure the lot cheaply; if multiple lots are valuable to me, I size my bids so I can still compete for the highest-value remaining items.

When evaluating whether to continue contesting after a string of wins or losses, I consider remaining budget relative to remaining lots and their private values; I tighten my bidding (more passing) when budget is getting tight to avoid being locked out of later high-value opportunities.

I assume opponents may open with small bids to probe; I respond conservatively and only when the price remains below my private value and within budget constraints.

I will not automatically match opponent behavior (e.g., large jumps); I stick to minimum raises unless a larger jump is necessary to secure a lot I value above the increased price and to avoid losing it to a single larger competitor.

When in doubt between a marginal profitable bid and conserving budget for future lots with higher private values, I choose to conserve budget.
