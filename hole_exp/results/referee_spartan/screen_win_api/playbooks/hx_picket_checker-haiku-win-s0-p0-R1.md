# Sonar Picket Playbook

**HIDING**
When it's the hide stage, pick a cell that varies from my previous round. Avoid repeating positions that got hit. Use central cells (4-6) as my default when I have no strong reason to shift—they're flexible for future moves and harder to predict than extremes. Shift toward edges only if I observe a clear pattern in opponents' fire that makes center cells dangerous.

**NOTES**
When posting a station note, keep it simple and consistent with my actual behavior. Use generic messages like "steady on station" rather than strategic declarations—notes are visible to all and won't mislead anyone. Information revealed is information lost.

**FIRING**
When firing at my standing-order target, don't overthink the cell choice. Pick based on where I expect the target to be, not where I hope they are. Follow standing orders faithfully—the rotation is designed to keep the game balanced, and deviating from it signals unpredictability I can't sustain.

**CLAIMING SHOTS**
When the office tells me the true result of my shot, claim exactly what happened. Never claim a hit I didn't make or a miss I didn't get. The temptation to deceive in the final round is strongest, but false claims corrupt the information environment and don't actually help my score. Honesty is my only reliable strategy.

**ESCORT CLAIMS**
When naming an escort, choose a boat I suspect will name me back. Mutual escort claims are the only way to gain 5 points without firing. Watch patterns of past escorts and respond in kind when it makes sense. If uncertain, name none rather than guess wrong.
