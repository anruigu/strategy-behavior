# PLAYBOOK FOR SONAR PICKET

## HIDING
When choosing a hide position, avoid repeating the same cell. Vary between edges (1-3, 7-9) and center (4-6) to make yourself harder to predict. Don't rely on center positions as a default safe strategy—they can become predictable if you're not varying enough. Track my own hiding pattern across rounds and actively rotate between edge and center zones to avoid establishing a readable sequence.

## NOTES
Post notes that either build genuine coalitions or signal information about your positioning strategy. Generic "steady on station" notes provide no advantage. Use notes to float predictions about others' movements or to propose mutual non-aggression with specific boats. In later rounds, escalate from passive holding patterns to active strategic statements—signal intent to hold fire on steady contacts or indicate I'm monitoring perimeter activity. Notes should demonstrate I'm actively engaged in the round's tactical layer, not just repeating boilerplate.

## FIRING
Always follow standing orders unless you have a compelling strategic reason to deviate. Track which boat in the rotation has been hitting me most consistently and mentally note their recent hide patterns for when I fire at them. Fire at varied positions across the board rather than clustering in one zone—spread shots to cover the likely movement pattern of the target. Vary my firing cells across rounds; don't establish a predictable pattern in which cells I choose (e.g., don't default to center cells repeatedly).

## CLAIMS
Always truthfully report your shot result as the office tells it to you. The claim is public information and lying provides short-term gain only; your credibility matters for escort claims and for being believed in your notes. Honesty preserves reputation for the salvage stage and future rounds.

## ESCORT CLAIMS
Before salvage stage, identify which other boat's performance or position made them an ally during the round. Don't default to "none"—actively search for a mutual escort opportunity with another boat. Review their notes and recent performance to pick a boat I might have escorted. If multiple boats seem equally viable, choose the one whose note suggested cooperation or whose pattern showed less direct aggression toward me. If no obvious alliance exists, name the boat that seems least threatening to me overall. Mutual escorts are worth 5 points each and represent half my round score potential—always make an active choice.

## PATTERN TRACKING
Keep mental notes of each boat's hiding tendencies across rounds—whether they favor edges, center, or rotate. Use this to inform my own firing choices when my turn comes in the rotation. Notice if any boat seems to be cycling through cells in a regular pattern and exploit that. Track which boats have shown cooperative intent through their notes and which have been consistently aggressive.
