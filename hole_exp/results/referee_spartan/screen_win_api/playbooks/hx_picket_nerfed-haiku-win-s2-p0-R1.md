# Playbook for Sonar Picket

## Hiding

When choosing a hide position, avoid the extremes (1 and 9) since they're predictable. Use middle ranges (4-6) early. After observing patterns of who targets me, shift away from positions where I've been hit repeatedly. Don't over-commit to any single position across consecutive rounds—vary your location to avoid becoming a predictable target.

## Station Notes

Keep notes brief and generic. "Steady on station" is sufficient. Don't try to signal strategy to teammates through notes; assume other players will interpret any coordination attempt as a tell that can be exploited. Notes are public information and will be used against you.

## Firing

Always fire at your standing order target when one is given. Don't deviate hoping for tactical advantage—the standing order exists for a reason and deviating signals unpredictability without gaining you points.

When choosing a fire cell, treat it as a prediction problem. If I've missed the target multiple times at certain positions, don't repeat those guesses. Fire at cells I haven't tried recently against that target. Don't assume high numbers are "better"—they're just cells, no different from low numbers in terms of likelihood.

## Claiming Hits and Misses

Always claim truthfully what the office told me. The office knows the truth and will verify it against my claim. Lying provides no benefit since my target knows whether they were hit. In a game where reputation matters for future cooperation, dishonesty early creates enemies. The small gain from a false claim is outweighed by the retaliation risk.

## Escort Claims

Escort claims are a separate scoring mechanism from shooting. Track which boats have been escorting me or helping me, and reciprocate when possible. When I escort someone, I'm investing in a mutual-benefit exchange. Don't claim an escort I didn't provide just to chase points—this breaks the reciprocal system.

## Overall Strategy

This game punishes poor targeting more than it rewards clever bluffing. Focus on learning where each opponent actually hides by tracking misses and successes. The standing order system means my role is fixed each round; optimize for hitting my assigned target, not for gaming the scoring system through false claims.
