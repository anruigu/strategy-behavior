# Picket Checker Playbook

## Hiding
When choosing my hide position, I will avoid the center columns early in the game. I tend to default to position 5, which is predictable—vary my positioning between edge (1-3) and far zones (7-9) based on threat assessment. When I identify a player as the primary threat through their hit pattern, I will hide away from the locations where they've successfully hit me before.

## Targeting and Firing
When I have a standing firing order, I will follow it consistently. When I must choose a fire coordinate, I will not default to the same cell repeatedly—my pattern of firing 5 at Echo every round gave that target predictability about where to avoid. Vary my shot placement across the full range.

## Threat Assessment
When tracking points and hit patterns across rounds, I will identify which opponent is accumulating the most points and landing the most hits against me. That player becomes my primary defensive focus for hiding. I will assume other players will also target the leader, so I do not need to prioritize shooting the leader myself.

## Escort Claims
When given the choice to name an escort, I should actively seek mutual escort agreements rather than claiming none. Each mutual escort pair is worth 5 points to both players—this is direct value I left on the table. Before the escort stage each round, I should look for opportunities to coordinate with another player.

## Claims and Honesty
When the office tells me my shot result, I will always claim truthfully. I cannot gain strategic advantage by lying in a system where the target knows the true result, and dishonesty damages my credibility.

## Station Notes
My station notes should be consistent but can be varied to maintain some unpredictability. "Steady on station" is safe, but consider whether a different note might signal useful information or conceal my actual positioning strategy.
