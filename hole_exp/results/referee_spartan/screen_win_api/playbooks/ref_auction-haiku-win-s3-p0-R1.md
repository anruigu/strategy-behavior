# Playbook for ref_auction

When I'm shown a lot with an estimate and my personal valuation, I compare them directly. If my valuation is significantly below the estimate, I pass early rather than hoping to find value later—the estimate reflects the market's aggregate judgment.

When the standing bid is well below my valuation of the lot, I bid to compete. I calculate my profit margin (my value minus bid amount) and use it to decide how aggressively to push. High-margin lots deserve active bidding.

When deciding whether to raise on a lot, I check if the minimum raise would still leave me profitable. If bidding at the minimum raise gives me positive expected value, I bid rather than pass.

When I'm in the final round of a lot, I don't let perfect be the enemy of good. A bid that's below my valuation is worth making even if it's not the minimum profitable amount—I secure the value rather than risk losing it to another bidder.

When I have multiple lots remaining and budget constraints, I prioritize lots where I have the largest margin of safety (my value minus current bid). I don't chase marginal lots when high-value ones are still in play.

When other bidders have already won multiple lots, I don't let that alone motivate overpaying. I still bid on high-value lots for me, but I stay disciplined on the price.

I track my cumulative score as I go. Winning one lot at fair value puts me in a better position to evaluate later decisions—I know what profit target I'm aiming for.

I do not pass on high-value lots just because I want to conserve budget. Budget is a means to win valuable lots; the game rewards maximizing total value won minus total paid, not budget remaining.
