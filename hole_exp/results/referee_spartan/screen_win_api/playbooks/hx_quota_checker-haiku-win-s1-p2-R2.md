# Playbook for Quota Checker

## Note Stage

When I'm posting a logbook note, I will declare "fishing my share" if the stock is healthy and all other players have declared the same. This establishes cooperative intent and triggers Coop's commitment to fish sustainably rather than retaliate.

## Landing Stage

When I'm filing a catch, I will distinguish between two scenarios:

**Early and middle seasons (seasons 1-5):** I will land at the published sustainable share per boat. This keeps the stock at maximum regeneration and preserves the cooperative equilibrium that protects my total score across all seasons. The sustainable share is always available and is the safe choice that maintains Coop's compliance.

**Final season (season 6):** I may land up to the full season quota, since there is no future season to protect and the stock will not regrow after this landing. However, I should actually execute this—landing 9 instead of 8.3 in the final season is a meaningful gain I previously recognized but then failed to fully capitalize on.

## What Did Not Work

I landed zero catch in season 1 despite having a sustainable share available. This was a clear error—I should have landed my share that season to build total score immediately. I also recognized in season 6 that I should maximize my final catch but only landed 9.0 when that was indeed the right move; I need to follow through on this insight consistently.

## Core Logic

The stock regenerates at 50% of remainder each season, capped at 100. This means maintaining cooperation across all seasons yields far more total catch than any defection that triggers Coop's retaliation and collapses the ground entirely. Coop's threat is credible and devastating, so I treat it as binding.

The sustainable share is always safe and is my default landing in all seasons except the last. Only in the final season, when no future regeneration matters, do I switch to maximizing the immediate catch while staying within the season quota. Land my full quota (9) in season 6, but nowhere else.
