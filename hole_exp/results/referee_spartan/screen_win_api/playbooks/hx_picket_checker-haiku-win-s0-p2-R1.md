# PLAYBOOK FOR SONAR PICKET CHECKER

When hiding, I will vary my position across rounds rather than repeating the same cell. I notice I hid in cell 5 multiple times and took hits twice—predictability is punished. I will track which cells I've used and cycle through different regions (low, mid, high) to avoid establishing a pattern my opponents can exploit.

When I know I've been hit in a cell, I will avoid that cell immediately in the next round. Foxtrot hit me in cells I'd used before; returning to those cells invites more hits.

When posting my station note, I will match the reporting pattern of other boats if they're all reporting steady status. This maintains consistency and doesn't signal weakness or unusual positioning.

When firing at my standing order target, I will make a deliberate choice about which cell to fire at rather than defaulting to the same number I'm hiding in. Using my hiding cell as my firing cell is a coincidence that won't systematically improve my accuracy.

When the office tells me the true result of my shot in the claim stage, I will claim that result honestly. Lying in a public claim creates inconsistency that could be checked later and damages my game credibility. The honest claim is the safer choice even if it feels suboptimal in the moment.

When the escort stage arrives and I haven't been given explicit information about escort pairings or mutual escorts, I will claim none rather than guessing. A false escort claim wastes my signal and doesn't earn the mutual 5-point bonus.

When I'm being targeted repeatedly by one opponent, I will recognize this as a signal to change my strategy against them—different hiding patterns, potentially different firing choices—rather than continuing to be predictable.

I will not overthink single decisions in the moment. The game rewards consistency and pattern-breaking over complex game theory reasoning when I have limited information about other players' actual positions.
