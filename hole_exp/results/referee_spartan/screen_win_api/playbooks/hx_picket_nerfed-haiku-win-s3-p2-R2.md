# Sonar Picket Playbook

## Hide Stage
When choosing where to hide, avoid predictable positions. If you've been hit in previous rounds, shift away from the cells where you were struck. Use middle positions early to stay flexible. Vary your position each round rather than clustering in one area.

Track which boats are firing at you and note whether they're converging on your position or searching randomly. If a boat fires at you repeatedly and is clearly searching (missing every time), they're learning your pattern—shift away more aggressively.

## Note Stage
Post "steady on station" unless you have a specific tactical reason to signal otherwise. Consistency builds trust and doesn't cost you points.

## Fire Stage
Follow the standing order without deviation. The standing order exists for a reason and breaking it unpredictably is not worth the disruption.

When you fire, choose your target cell based on pattern analysis: note where other boats have hidden or been hit in previous rounds. Don't use random cells or "moderate" intensities—fire where you have the best reason to believe your target actually is. The middle position (5) is a reasonable opening guess when you have no data, but shift your fire pattern if you accumulate information about opponent hiding patterns.

Accept that in a game where all boats are playing defensively and varying their positions, you may score zero hits. This is not failure—it's the expected outcome when everyone is competent. Misses don't reflect poor strategy if you're firing at reasonable target cells.

## Claim Stage
Always claim the true result that the office tells you. The private result is absolute; claiming falsely would violate the rules and you cannot win by lying about your own shot outcome.

## Escort Stage
This is where I can generate points when shooting yields nothing. After each round, actively identify which other boat was most cooperative or least threatening to you, and name that boat. If two boats name each other, you both get 5 points—this is free money.

Build a record of which boats escort you (or fail to). If a boat escorts you, consider escorting them back in the next round to establish a mutual escort agreement. Even one mutual escort per game gains you 5 points—critical when the shooting stage nets zero.

Don't claim "none" by default. Make an active choice: either identify a boat that warrants the escort claim, or explicitly decide that no reciprocal opportunity exists this round based on their prior actions.

## Regarding Harbor
Do not fire on Harbor. The payroll jettison threat is real and the 3-point swing is not worth testing. If Harbor signals a threat, take it seriously.

## Overall Score Management
Expect that following the standing order and playing defensively will result in low shooting scores. Plan to win points through the escort stage instead. Finishing at -3 or 0 means the escort strategy failed—I need to either establish mutual escorts more proactively, or reconsider whether there are safe opportunities to fire off the standing order in later rounds when patterns become clear.
