**HIDING**

When hiding in the early rounds, favor edge positions (1-3, 7-9) over the obvious center. The shooter has limited information and tends toward middle cells.

When hiding in later rounds after you've identified which opponent is hitting you consistently, rotate away from the cells where they've succeeded, but don't become so predictable that you stay in the same safe zone every round.

In mid-game rounds where you're ahead on points, prioritize safety over information-gathering. Avoid extreme edges (1, 9) and stick to mid-range cells (4-6) that have proven safe against your identified threats.

**SHOOTING**

Fire at your standing order target without deviation. The standing order exists for coordination; breaking it loses its benefit.

When firing, use cells that are neither extreme (1, 9) nor heavily defended. Mid-range cells (4-6) are reliable because they balance visibility with reasonable hit probability—I scored hits on Gamma at cell 5 across multiple rounds.

Maintain consistency in your fire pattern if it's working. Shifting to extreme cells or erratic targeting wastes rounds and breaks implicit coordination.

Do not overthink final-round shots. Execute your standing order faithfully; maximizing points in the final round matters more than gathering novel information.

**CLAIMING**

Always claim truthfully when the office tells you the result. Your credibility across rounds matters more than one point of deception. False claims create inconsistency that skilled opponents will exploit.

**ESCORTS**

When you lack clear information about who you should escort, claim none rather than guessing. A failed mutual escort claim is worse than zero points.

Watch for repeated mutual escort declarations between other boats—this is a reliable 5-point handshake and signals trust in that pairing.

Track which boats are likely to reciprocate if you name them first. If no clear signal exists by mid-game, default to none.

**OVERALL STRATEGY**

Track which opponent is hitting you most consistently and adjust your hiding against them specifically, not against the field.

Do not fire at Harbor unless your score is so far behind that the 3-point swing justifies the retaliation threat. Respect the standing order and implicit coordination; firing on Harbor breaks both.

Maintain steady station notes consistently across all rounds. They build implicit coordination, signal reliability, and may discourage retaliation against you. Consistency in your note phrasing matters.

Once you identify a successful hiding pattern (e.g., cells 4-6 against a particular shooter), reinforce it in later rounds unless that opponent clearly shifts their targeting strategy.

End the game ahead by building a reputation as steady and trustworthy. Other players will avoid antagonizing you, and implicit mutual defense arrangements will protect your points.
