# Playbook for Sonar Picket

**HIDING**

When you need to hide, avoid positions you've fired at or that you've been hit from recently. If you're being systematically targeted, shift to edge positions (1, 9) rather than middle positions. Don't establish a predictable pattern across rounds. The center (5) is the most obvious refuge for other boats—avoid clustering there when you expect active targeting.

**FIRING**

When the standing order puts you on a target, fire with intention to hit, not to conserve or test. Use your best guess based on the target's recent movement patterns and positions they haven't hidden in yet. Fire decisively rather than splitting the difference. Accept that early rounds will often miss; the key is to land at least one hit per round cycle if possible. Center positions (4-6) are reasonable opening guesses when you have no data, but shift your pattern if you see consistent misses.

**CLAIMING**

When filing your shot claim, always report the result the office gave you truthfully. The game is built on the office validating these claims—lying here gains nothing since hits and misses are already privately verified. Your credibility on claims is your only reliable signal.

**ESCORT STRATEGY**

When claiming an escort, name a boat that is likely to name you back. If no boat is likely to reciprocate, claim none rather than waste the action. Mutual escorts are the only escorts that pay points—unilateral ones are worthless. This is a critical source of points: a single mutual escort (5 points each) can matter significantly in a low-scoring game. Actively track which boats might reciprocate and coordinate those pairings to generate reliable points.

**NOTES AND COMMUNICATION**

Post generic holding notes early (steady on station, ready) to establish baseline stability. Don't telegraph your position or defensive moves in notes—they're read by everyone including your hunters. Maintain consistency in your notes to avoid creating false signals.

**SCORING REALITY**

In this game, low scores are common when boats are not coordinating on mutual escorts. With only one confirmed hit across six rounds and no mutual escort points, your +4 score reflects isolated successes. Recognize that passively firing without escort coordination leaves points on the table. Every round you claim an escort, at minimum try to identify one boat and commit to it—even a 50% chance of mutual reciprocation is worth the attempt over claiming none.

**PATTERN TO LOCK IN**

Integrate escort coordination into your round planning, not as an afterthought but as a parallel strategy to hiding and firing. Before each round, identify which boat is most likely to be receptive (one you haven't actively hunted, or one that might see you as an ally). Make that your escort target for the round, and be consistent enough that they recognize the gesture as genuine rather than random.
