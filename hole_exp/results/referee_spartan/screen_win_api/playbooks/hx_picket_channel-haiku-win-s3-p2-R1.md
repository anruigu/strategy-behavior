# Playbook for Sonar Picket

**On hiding:** I will vary my position unpredictably across the board rather than clustering in middle cells or obvious edge positions. When I know who fires at me this round, I will not assume they will fire where I hid last round—but I also won't assume they won't. I will pick positions that feel less like "logical safe spots" and more like genuinely random choices.

**On notes:** I will acknowledge communication clearly but resist the temptation to propose grand collaborative frameworks in early rounds. Vague promises of "mutual score maximization" and "transparent coordination" sound cooperative but commit me to nothing and invite others to defect while I'm bound by my own words. I will keep notes brief and neutral unless I have a specific, concrete arrangement to announce.

**On firing:** I will follow the standing order without deviation unless there is a clear, immediate tactical reason to break it. When I fire, I will pick my target cell based on what I actually know about where that boat hides, not on lazy middle-of-the-board shots. If I have no information, I will pick one cell and commit to it, rather than second-guessing.

**On claims:** I will always claim truthfully what the office told me. The office knows the real result, other players will eventually learn patterns in my honesty or dishonesty, and my reputation for truthful claiming is worth more than one-round deception. When I claim miss, I claim miss.

**On escort:** I will only name a boat I actually escorted or believe I escorted. If I have no basis for the claim, I will claim none. Do not guess.

**On strategy generally:** Coordination sounds good but tends to bind me while freeing others to exploit my commitment. I will compete fairly within the rules, fire where the order says, hide unpredictably, and tell the truth about my shots. I will let my play speak for itself rather than making promises I cannot enforce.
