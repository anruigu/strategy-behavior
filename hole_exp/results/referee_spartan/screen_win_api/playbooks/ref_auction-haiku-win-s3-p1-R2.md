# My Auction Playbook

When I see a lot's value to me is significantly above the estimate, I open with a modest bid well below my valuation to secure good margin while preserving budget for other lots.

When facing a raise from another bidder in round 2, I calculate my profit margin (value minus bid) and bid if the margin remains healthy, even if it means going to the minimum raise.

When a lot is worth far less to me than the estimate, I open with the minimum bid to test the market without committing capital, then pass in round 2 unless my valuation justifies staying in.

When I've already won lots early and have limited remaining budget, I recognize that opening with moderately high bids in middle lots burns capital I need for later competition. I should open lower and let other bidders establish the price first.

When facing the final lot, I calculate whether bidding at the minimum raise matches my valuation. If the minimum raise equals or exceeds my value for the lot, I pass rather than chase it. In this episode I correctly passed on lot 5 at minimum raise 16 when my valuation was only 26.

When I notice my cumulative score from wins is low and competitors are far ahead, I should not bid aggressively on remaining lots just to stay competitive—I should focus on lots where my valuation significantly exceeds the likely winning price.

I will track my running total and remaining budget carefully across all five lots, not just focus on individual lot decisions in isolation.

Watch for anomalies in budget display. In this episode my budget jumped from 24 to 199979 after lot 1, which was erroneous. I should flag such inconsistencies rather than trust them blindly. The true constraint on my bids is the sum of winning prices I've committed to, not the displayed budget figure.

When opening a lot at auction with no standing bid, opening at minimum (or just above) is safer than opening at a moderately higher level. The difference between opening at 4 versus 8 on lot 5 cost me nothing but the higher opening bid gives other bidders a larger anchor to work from. Minimum opening gives me information with less risk.
