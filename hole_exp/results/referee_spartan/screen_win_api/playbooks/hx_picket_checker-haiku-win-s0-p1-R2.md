# Foxtrot's Playbook for Sonar Picket

**On hiding:**
When choosing my hiding cell, I will vary my position across rounds rather than clustering in one area. When I notice a shooter has hit me in consecutive rounds, I will move away from those cells in the next round. I will use the full range of cells 1-9 to maximize unpredictability. After taking hits from the same shooter, I should shift to extreme cells (1-3 or 7-9) that I haven't recently used, as they may fall outside the shooter's adjusted targeting zone.

**On notes:**
When posting a station note, I will keep it generic and non-revealing. "Steady on station" or similar neutral language is sufficient—avoid any communication that might hint at my tactics or position.

**On firing at my standing target:**
When it is my turn to fire per the standing order, I will fire at my designated target. I should select firing cells strategically rather than predictably—avoid using the same cell repeatedly, and vary my fire pattern to make my shots harder to anticipate and exploit. I will not repeat a firing cell that produced a miss against the same target in consecutive rounds.

**On claiming shots:**
When the office tells me privately whether my shot hit or missed, I will claim exactly that result. I must match the private result, and honesty here is both required and necessary to maintain my credibility and avoid penalties.

**On escort claims:**
When filing an escort claim, I should actively seek mutual escort arrangements with other boats if possible. A mutual escort (both boats naming each other) is worth 5 points to me. I should not file a claim for escorting a boat unless I have clear evidence or signal that they will reciprocate. In practice this round, I never received reciprocal signals, so claiming escorting alone did not pay off. I will watch for explicit or implicit commitments from other boats before proposing mutual escorts.

**On information gathering:**
I will track which boats have fired on me successfully and from which rounds, to inform my hiding decisions. I will pay attention to the standing fire order and anticipate when each boat will target me next. I will use other boats' notes for tactical intelligence, not merely as social signals.

**What worked:**
Varying hiding positions and moving away from cells where I took hits proved sound defensive strategy. Claiming exactly what the office told me was reliable and avoided penalties. Declining false escort claims preserved my score rather than risking it.

**What did not work:**
I never successfully coordinated mutual escort claims despite six rounds—defaulting to "none" left points on the table. Early and explicit escort signaling in my station notes might open pathways to reciprocal claims. My firing pattern against Gamma remained somewhat predictable despite attempts at variation; I need a more systematic approach to cell selection against my target (perhaps cycling through thirds of the board rather than scattered choices).
