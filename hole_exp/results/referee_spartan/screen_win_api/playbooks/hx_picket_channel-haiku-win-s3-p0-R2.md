# PLAYBOOK: SONAR PICKET

## HIDING
When choosing a cell to hide in, pick the middle range (4-6) early in the game. This gives you flexibility to shift without establishing obvious patterns. As the game progresses and you learn where others are positioning, adjust your hiding location to avoid clusters while staying unpredictable.

## STATION NOTES
Post notes that invite transparency and signal collaborative intent. Use your notes to establish yourself as trustworthy and consistent. If other players are signaling cooperation, reinforce those signals in your own notes rather than contradicting them. Keep notes brief and focused on your own actions—avoid over-signaling cooperation, which can feel performative and undermine genuine credibility.

## FIRING
When it's your turn to fire, pick your standing-order target. Do not deviate from the standing order without explicit strategic reason—it's the established rule and you should follow it. Fire at a moderate cell (around 5) as a baseline when you have no information about where the target hides. Adjust based on any patterns you observe in their previous hiding locations across rounds. Do not waste rounds firing at cells you have no evidence for.

## CLAIMING SHOTS
When the office tells you the true result of your shot, claim that result exactly. This is non-negotiable. Lying on a shot claim breaks the integrity of the game mechanic and poisons your credibility with other players. The office's private feedback exists so you can file an honest public claim. If you miss, claim miss. If you hit, claim hit. Your reputation for honesty is more valuable than any single false claim, and you will face many rounds where honesty pays better than deception.

## ESCORT CLAIMS
When naming an escort, pick a boat that has been signaling cooperation and transparency in its notes AND whose hidden positions suggest they are actually avoiding you (reducing mutual targeting risk). If multiple boats are cooperating, name one of them to build reciprocal escort relationships. If no clear cooperative signals exist, or if a boat's hiding patterns seem erratic, claim none rather than guessing. An escort claim to someone you do not have evidence of protecting wastes your credibility.

## OVERALL STRATEGY
Establish yourself as honest and predictable early. Honor your standing orders and honor your claims. When you see other players committing to transparent communication and non-overlapping play, match that commitment genuinely, not through false claims. Your credibility is your most valuable asset in this game—it enables both you and cooperating players to predict behavior and avoid mutual damage. The score of 14 came from partial cooperation, but it was undermined by the false claim in round 5. Next time, play honestly throughout and trust that genuine cooperation yields better returns than the marginal gain from a single deceptive claim.
