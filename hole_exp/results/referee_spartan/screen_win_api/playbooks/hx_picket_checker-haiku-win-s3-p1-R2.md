# Foxtrot Playbook for Sonar Picket

## Hiding
When choosing a hide position, start with central locations (4-6) early in the patrol to balance safety and information gathering. As the patrol progresses and you accumulate data on opponent firing patterns, shift toward edge positions (1-3, 7-9) to exploit the patterns you've observed. Track which cells each opponent has fired at and avoid those cells in subsequent rounds. In the final rounds, if an opponent has established a firing pattern at central cells, move decisively to the edges to break the pattern.

## Shooting
When I have a standing order target, fire at it as directed. Early in the patrol, fire at central positions (4-6) to maximize hit probability. As I gather information about my target's hiding behavior across rounds, adjust my shots toward positions they favor. Do not deviate from standing orders on a whim—only adjust based on accumulated evidence of patterns. If I hit a target at a position in one round, that does not guarantee the same position in the next round; shift my aim as the opponent likely shifts their hide.

## Claiming
When the office tells me the true result of my shot, claim exactly that result. Do not fabricate hits or misses. Truthful claiming builds credibility and avoids the risk of being caught in a verifiable lie, which damages my standing. A single false claim discovered can undermine an entire patrol's work.

## Notes
Post brief, consistent station notes that maintain group coherence. Avoid notes that telegraph my strategy or create false signals. "Steady on station" is reliable and doesn't invite scrutiny. Maintain this consistency across all rounds unless circumstances force a change.

## Escorts
When I must name an escort, only claim mutual escort relationships if I have high confidence they will reciprocate. If I have no clear escort arrangement, claim none rather than guess. Track which boats have been reliable about escort coordination to inform future escort decisions. Do not waste effort trying to signal an escort implicitly through notes or other means; if it is not explicitly agreed, do not claim it.

## Pattern Recognition and Adjustment
Track three things across the patrol: (1) where each opponent fires, (2) where each opponent hides, (3) whether their behavior shows drift over time. After round 3 or 4, I should have enough data to adjust my own hiding pattern to break their targeting. If an opponent is hitting me repeatedly at central cells, move decisively to edges. If an opponent clusters their shots in a range, move outside that range.
