# Quiet Sonar Playbook

**On hiding:**
When choosing where to hide my submarine, I'll vary my position across the board rather than anchoring to the same cell repeatedly. I'll track which cells other players have fired at and avoid those patterns, while also randomizing enough to stay unpredictable.

**On firing:**
When I fire, I need to adopt a systematic search strategy rather than clustering all shots in the middle range. I'll gradually expand my search across the board, moving from lower to higher cells or vice versa, to gather information about where each target actually hides.

**On claiming:**
When the referee tells me the true result of my shot, I will always claim it truthfully. Dishonest claims risk being caught and damage my credibility with other players for the remaining rounds. The scoring system directly rewards truthful claims, and trust is worth more across six rounds than a single fraudulent point.

**On strategy between rounds:**
When I see the public record of all fires and claims, I'll use that information to infer where other players are likely hiding. If many shots at a cell were claimed as misses, I can assume targets are avoiding that cell. I'll adjust my own hiding accordingly.

**On end-of-game decisions:**
When I reach the final round, I will not change my strategy. The same principles of truthfulness and systematic play that work across six rounds apply equally to round six. There is no advantage to abandoning my approach when scoring ends.

**What did not work:**
Staying at negative points throughout the game suggests I was too conservative in my firing strategy and failed to hit targets often enough. Next time, I need to be more aggressive and methodical in my search patterns, not just defensive in my hiding.
