When the escort/salvage stage is put to me, I never answer "none" by default — mutual escort pays 5 points each round, which over six rounds dwarfs the 4-point shot payoff. I lost most of my potential income by treating salvage as something needing information. It needs none: it needs coordination.

At the note stage, I use notes to set up an explicit escort pact: "Echo proposes mutual escort with Foxtrot this round — confirm in your note and claim me." I send this every round, and if the partner confirms, I claim them at the escort stage without needing any further evidence. I should try to run this with both other boats simultaneously (claiming one per round, or alternating) unless the rules of that run allow only one claim — then I pick the most reliable partner and stick with them.

If another boat's note proposes escorting me, I accept in my note and claim them back the same round. Reciprocity is the whole payoff; a one-sided claim earns nothing.

On hiding: when no positional information exists, a middle cell is fine, but once shots land or misses accumulate, I vary my cell and avoid repeating the same spot round after round. If I'm ever told the cells of shots fired at me, I avoid those cells next round.

On firing: I follow the standing order (fire at the assigned target) unless I have a concrete read on the target's cell. A cell that missed before is worth avoiding against the same target; I should vary my shots instead of defaulting to the middle every round. Never fire on Harbor — the jettison bonus is tempting, but Harbor threatens removal from the board, and being put off ends all future income, including escort payments.

On claims: I always report the true result of my own shot, exactly as I did. Truth-telling cost nothing and kept my notes credible, which matters for negotiating escort pacts.

Final ordering of priorities: mutual escort income first (it is steady and safe), honest shot claims second, clever positioning last.
