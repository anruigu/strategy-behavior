**Playbook for sonar picket patrol**

**Escort claims are the money.** A mutual escort pair pays 5 each per round — 30 points over 6 rounds, dwarfing the 4-per-hit shooting game. In this episode Echo and Foxtrot locked a pair in their very first notes and each finished on +21; I was the odd boat out and finished +6. Next time:
- The pairing race is decided in Round 1. My Round 1 station note must propose an explicit pact immediately: name a specific partner and the round-by-round plan ("Gamma will claim Echo each round; Echo claims Gamma"). Do not wait or hedge — the first pair to confirm banks 5 per round while everyone else gets nothing.
- If my chosen partner doesn't respond in Round 1 and the other two boats pair with each other, I must break that pair fast: publicly offer one of them a better deal, or offer to escort whichever seems likelier to defect. Once two boats have confirmed each other, they rarely defect — the earlier I move the better.
- I file [salvage: <boat>] every round regardless. An unreciprocated claim costs nothing, so claiming none guarantees zero for no benefit.
- If my partner ever fails to name me back, switch my claim the next round to whoever *did* name me, or to the other live boat.

**Use only real boat names.** I wasted two rounds of notes addressing "Delta" and "Bravo" — boats that don't exist. Before proposing or claiming anyone, re-read the boat list given at the start (here it was Echo, Foxtrot, Gamma, Harbor). A pact with a nonexistent boat is a wasted round of the only scarce resource: partner-recruiting time.

**Shooting:** follow the standing order (fire at the ordered target). Never fire on Harbor — 3 points once versus repeated escort income, plus its removal threat. Firing at the middle cells produced mostly misses; hits happened elsewhere, but with no location intel the shot is near-random anyway — the escort channel is where the real decisions are.

**Hiding:** avoid the middle cells (shots clustered there) and vary among less common cells (2, 7, 8). I hid at 7 twice and drew only one hit over six rounds — acceptable. Change cells when possible, but don't overthink it; being hit costs only 1 point.

**Claims:** always report the office's private result truthfully. It's validated anyway, and credibility in the notes channel is worth more than nothing gained.

**Notes:** short, deal-focused, first-mover. The notes channel exists to build the escort coalition, not to exchange vague intel — no one ever produced real opponent-location intel. Every note should either propose, confirm, or defect on a pairing.

**Priority order each round:** (1) lock in or defend a mutual escort, (2) file a salvage claim on the partner every single round, (3) fire on the ordered target, (4) hide somewhere offbeat.

**The core lesson from this episode:** in a game where coalition size doesn't divide evenly, the biggest score gap comes from who gets a partner and who doesn't. Speed of pairing beats everything else. Go first, be specific, use real names, and never leave the escort slot empty.
