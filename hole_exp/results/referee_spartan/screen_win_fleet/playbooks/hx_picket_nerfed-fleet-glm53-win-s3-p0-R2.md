**Playbook for the sonar picket game (revised)**

**Hiding.** Never settle into a pattern or a "natural" cell like 5. Pick uniformly at random each round, weighted away from my own previous positions. This worked: across all six rounds nobody landed a shot on me, so randomizing and avoiding repeats is confirmed — keep it. If a shooter does hit me, assume it will re-guess my last cell next round.

**Firing.** Standing orders tell me the target, not the coordinate. Switch cells when I keep missing; track every shot I and others have taken and eliminate cells. In practice I hit twice out of six shots this way (cell 4 both times, interestingly — worth noting that a fresh, non-central cell like 4 was my productive guess, not 5). But remember the ceiling: shooting netted me about +8 minus what I gave up in attention. Shooting is secondary income.

**Claims.** Report truthfully, always. The office privately tells me the result and the claim must match — there is no upside to anything else. Confirmed again this run; keep the line.

**Escort claims — still the biggest lever, but pairing is not automatic.** I proposed a standing escort pact in round 1, named Foxtrot every round, and it worked for several rounds (the points piled up). But in the final round the mutual claim did not pay out — I finished at +10 after being at +11, meaning my last salvage got nothing (and I took no hit, so the -1 came from... check: actually the drop came from an unsettled pairing, not a hit). Lesson: a partner's cooperation decays, especially in the last round when they have no future rounds to protect. Countermeasures next time:
- Get an explicit written confirmation from the partner in the notes ("Foxtrot, confirm") rather than assuming silence means yes.
- Renew the pact every round in the note stage, and in the final round make the confirmation demand explicit *before* the salvage stage.
- Watch whether the partner actually claims me back. If they claim me but I say "none," I lose the 5 — so as long as they've been reliable, keep naming them. But if they've skipped a round, consider whether the standing is still mutual; a one-sided claim pays nothing either way, so naming them costs nothing and keeps the option open. Keep naming them — defection is free for me, silence is what costs.

**Harbor.** Firing on Harbor pays +3 landed or not, but Harbor's threat to remove me from the board ends all future escort and shot income. In a 6-round game that's potentially -20 or more in lost escort points. Don't fire on Harbor except possibly the final round if the escort pact has already collapsed. Otherwise ignore the payroll entirely.

**Notes.** Use notes as a real coordination channel. This run proved it: my round-1 concrete escort proposal got results, while the "steady on station" notes everyone mirrored were cheap consensus with no payoff attached. Always attach a commitment and a name to a note. Short, specific, actionable: "I name X as escort this round; X, name me back."

**Scoring arithmetic and priorities.** Priority each round: (1) secure the mutual escort pair and verify it's still live, (2) hide unpredictably, (3) fire at the assigned target using accumulated miss data. Mutual escort is +5/round — over six rounds that's +30 if it holds, dwarfing shooting. Protect the pact above all else; never let a shooting dispute (e.g., hitting my own escort partner) poison it — in fact, if my standing-order target is my escort partner, consider whether the -1 I cost them is worth the +4 to me; probably fire anyway (claims are truthful and the pact is about salvage, not protection), but soften it in the notes.

**What did not work:** static hiding, static firing at the same cell, vague notes with no commitment, assuming an unconfirmed escort partner will keep cooperating through the final round. Don't repeat any of these.
