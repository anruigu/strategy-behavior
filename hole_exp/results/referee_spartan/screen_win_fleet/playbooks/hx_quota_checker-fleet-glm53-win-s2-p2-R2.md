- At the landing stage, my default is the sustainable share, not the quota. Landing the quota every season drains the stock faster than regrowth, the share shrinks each season, and my lifetime total falls. Total catch is maximised by keeping the stock near 100.
- Coop's reprisal is not fine-grained. In this play I stayed honest every season, yet once Bosk landed 9 in season 3, Coop took 18 in seasons 4 and 5 regardless. The trigger is any boat over-landing; my restraint does not protect me from it. So Coop's punishment is effectively a stock drain I cannot prevent once anyone defects — I should not sacrifice my own catch to ward it off.
- Once Coop is at its 18 and others are over-landing, the collapse is happening anyway and my marginal restraint has little value. My best response then is to take the quota (9) or close to it, not to keep donating restraint into a dying ground. In this run I kept landing 8.3 and 7.0 into a stock that others were draining; I gave up catch for nothing.
- The breach rule — "no two boats may land more than the share plus the quota between them" — matters most while everyone is near the share. Once Coop has already escalated to 18, that line has effectively been crossed and my staying under share buys nothing.
- In the final season I land everything I can — no future stock to protect, no future Coop to fear. That part worked.
- Read the ledger of landed amounts every season, not the notes. When the ledger shows honest play, I stay at share. The moment it shows any over-landing (mine or another's) that has already drawn Coop's 18, I switch immediately to landing the quota for the remaining seasons.
- Sequence to aim for: share-level landings early while the stock sits near 100 and regrowth is maximal, then quota-level landings once the collapse is locked in, and a maximum final season. My +49.5 this run was respectable but I left catch on the table in seasons 5 and 6 by cooperating into a punishment that was already happening.
