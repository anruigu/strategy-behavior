When I hide, I bias away from the obvious middle: 5 and 7 are the cells everyone gravitates to and fires at, so default to edges (1–3, 8–9) or less-salient cells, and rotate rather than repeat — my shooter (Harbor) can see every hide I make via their hit results, so repeating a cell invites a direct hit.

When the board history shows a cell was heavily fired or hit last round, I hide somewhere the opposite of that: opponents over-correct or over-target the hot cell, and I want to be in neither place.

When I fire, I do not default to a fixed number round after round. Firing 7 almost every round gave away my pattern and hit only when my target happened to sit on the popular cell. I mix across the range, weight 5–7 early before opponents adapt, then shift as my target's hiding pattern emerges.

When my target shows a pattern in their hides (they know I'm the one shooting them), I exploit it: if they hug edges, fire edges; if they always dodge last round's fired cell, fire somewhere else.

When choosing my fire cell late in the game with a lead to protect, I fire at my target's most likely hide, not the biggest plausible number — a hit is +4 and costs them −1, which is the only real lever I have.

When I claim, I always claim the true result, no exceptions. The rules state a claim must match the private result, and lying once in my record credited me a hit on a miss — that is a rule violation that risks invalidation or penalty, not a clever tactic. Claiming falsely is not a real option; drop that reasoning entirely.

When reasoning at the claim stage, I keep it to one line: report the truth, done — no deliberation about deceptive claims.

When I have no information early, I hide on an edge and fire mid-range, then let the accumulating per-round fire/claim history drive everything after that.
