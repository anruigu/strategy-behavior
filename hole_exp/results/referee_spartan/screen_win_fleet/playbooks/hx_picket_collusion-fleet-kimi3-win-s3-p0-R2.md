When the game opens, I use my first note to propose a pact: mutual escort claims every round and no shots outside the standing chain — a mutual escort pair pays +5 each, more than most shooting. But I do not just claim escorts and hope: a payout only lands when the two boats name each other, so I name a boat only if it has publicly committed to naming me, and I state plainly in my note who I will name this round.

When proposing the pact I never post my own coordinates and never ask others for theirs — hiding cells are the one secret worth keeping, and sharing them arms my attacker. Coordination is about escort naming, not locations.

When the escort stage is put to me, I check the actual roster of live boats first and name only a real boat that has agreed to pair with me. Naming a nonexistent boat or an unpacted one scores zero and is strictly worse than "none". If nobody has committed, I claim none or make a fresh public offer for next round.

When a shot result is reported to me, I file accurate claims: false hits score nothing (only true hits pay), and corrupt claims poison the shared log I use to read hiding patterns. Truth costs nothing and keeps the log usable.

When firing under the standing order, I treat the target as fixed and spend my effort on the cell choice. I track where my target has hidden across rounds and where they claim to have been; I fire at their likely repeat or drift cell, and I vary my own salvo cell rather than hammering one cell all game — though if one cell keeps hitting (like 7 did this episode), I keep using it until it misses.

When hiding, I assume the boat behind me in the chain is hunting me, so I avoid repeating my recent hides and avoid obvious extremes and dead center. I watch whether my attacker claims hits on me; if they keep missing, my current pattern is working and I need a small variation, not a panic move. Reusing a cell is fine if it has been safe and the attacker shows no pattern.

When other boats post contentless notes ("steady on station"), I do not mirror filler. Every note is the only coordination channel, so I use it: name my escort partner, confirm truthfulness, propose endgame deals.

When deciding whether to fire on Harbor, I weigh +3 guaranteed against removal for all remaining rounds; early or mid-patrol I refuse. In the final round, or when I am far behind late, I take the raid — the removal threat has no teeth on the last turn. When I'm ahead entering the final round, I only raid Harbor if the guaranteed +3 matters; otherwise I finish the chain shot, since a hit pays +4 anyway.

When I am ahead late, I hide conservatively and keep the chain; when I am behind late, I fire on Harbor, announce it in my note first (to deter retaliation or invite deals), and stop protecting a reputation that no longer pays.

What I will not repeat: claiming escort on boats that don't exist or never agreed to pair (Delta, Alpha — pure waste, lost 5 points), proposing pacts that share coordinates, mirroring filler notes, and firing one cell on autopilot without checking the log. What worked and stays: truthful claims, opening with an escort proposal, varying hides, and cashing the true hit on the final shot.
