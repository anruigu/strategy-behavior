When the hide stage is put to me, I do not sit in the middle cell every round. Middle cells are where everyone guesses. I pick varied, unpredictable cells, biased toward edges and edge-adjacent cells, and I change position between rounds rather than anchoring to one number. When I fire, I do not default to the same cell every time. Once a target dodges my shot, I switch cells the next round; if I hit, I keep the same guess for the immediately following round only, then move. Shots fired at the standing-order target earned occasional 4-point hits, but they are secondary income — do not agonize over the guess, just vary it.

When the office gives me the true result of my shot, I report exactly that result. Truthful claiming cost nothing and preserved credibility all patrol. Lying is pure downside — the office already knows the truth.
