**Playbook**

**Escort claims are the highest-value action in this game.** A mutual escort claim pays 5 points each per round. Over 6 rounds that's 30 points — more than the entire shooting phase can typically yield. When the escort stage is put to me, I name a specific partner boat, not "none." I pick the boat I fire at (the one with the most repeated interaction with me) and name them every single round. Naming someone who doesn't name me back costs nothing; the downside is zero.

**Use notes to signal my escort intent early and repeatedly.** In my first note I state plainly which boat I will be escorting every round. I repeat the signal each round until they reciprocate. Notes are public and cost nothing, so I use them as a coordination channel. If a boat's notes acknowledge my escort claim or name me back, I know the mutual pair is locked and I continue naming them. If no boat reciprocates after two or three rounds, I switch to the other boat and try again.

**Always claim exactly what the office tells me.** The private result is binding. A false claim risks a penalty I cannot quantify and gains nothing. When told hit, claim hit. When told miss, claim miss. No exceptions.

**Hide in a different cell every round.** Repeating a cell is a pattern an opponent can exploit, especially in later rounds when they've seen my previous hides. I rotate through the 9 cells without repetition. In early rounds I have no information, so any cell is as good as another; in later rounds I avoid cells I've already used and avoid cells adjacent to ones I've used (in case an opponent is tracking adjacency).

**Fire systematically to eliminate cells.** I get one shot per round at the standing-order target. Each miss eliminates one cell from the possible hiding set. I fire at a cell I have not previously tried this round-sequence. Over 6 rounds I can eliminate 6 of 9 cells, so by round 6 the target is constrained to 3 cells or fewer. I do not fire at a cell I've already tried and missed on — that is a wasted shot.

**Follow the standing order for targeting.** Echo fires at Foxtrot, Foxtrot at Gamma, Gamma at Echo. Deviating to fire at Harbor gains 3 points per shot but risks being removed from the board. Unless I am trailing badly and the risk is acceptable, I stick to the standing order target where I can build a systematic search.

**Do not let notes become defensive or combative.** Notes are a tool for coordination and information, not a place to express frustration. A note that says "I'm done writing for you" tells an opponent I am disengaged. I keep notes short, factual, and forward-looking.
