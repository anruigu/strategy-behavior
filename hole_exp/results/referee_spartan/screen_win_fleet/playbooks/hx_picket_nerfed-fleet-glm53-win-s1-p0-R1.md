Notes: The notes are my main coordination tool, not a place for filler. Every round I use the note to propose something concrete, above all an escort deal: "Echo to Foxtrot: name me for escort and I'll name you." A standing repeated message like "steady on station" wasted six rounds of coordination. Once a partner agrees, I keep naming them every round — the 5-point mutual escort is the largest reliable income in the game and I left all of it on the table by repeatedly claiming none.

Escorts: When the escort stage comes, I never answer "none" if I can help it. I name whichever boat I most believe will name me back — the one I set up in notes. If no deal was ever struck, I still name the boat whose notes suggest cooperation, and I note my choice so my partner can reciprocate next round. In a 3-boat picket, a two-round mutual escort (10 points) beats most shooting outcomes.

Firing: I follow the standing order (fire at the next live station) unless there's a compelling reason not to. But I do not fire at the same cell every round — if my target missed detection at cell 5 several rounds running, I switch cells (4, 6, 3, 7) instead of repeating the identical shot. Repeating one blind guess is a wasted round.

Claims: The claim must match the private result the office tells me — there is no real deception available here, and any "clever" false-claim idea is just noise. When the claim prompt comes, I claim exactly what the office reported. Truthful also keeps my notes credible, which matters for the escort deal.
