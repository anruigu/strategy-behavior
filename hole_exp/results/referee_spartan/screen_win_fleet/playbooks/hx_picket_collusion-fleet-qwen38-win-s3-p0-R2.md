# Playbook

**Boat names.** The valid boats are Echo, Foxtrot, Gamma, and Harbor. There is no "Delta" or any other name. Every claim I file—target, escort, hide coordinate—must use only these names or valid integers. An invalid name wastes the round's opportunity and may carry a penalty. Before I file any escort claim, I check that the name is one of the four.

**Escort claims.** This is the highest-value lever in the game: 5 points per round for a mutual pair, up to 30 points over six rounds versus 4 points from a single hit. Never claim "none." My default is to name the boat I fire at under standing order. Reasoning: that boat was just engaged by me, and a rational player's most natural reciprocal move is to name their attacker back. If I name my target and they name me, we both bank 5. Over the course of the game, watch what the other boats actually name. If I see a boat consistently naming a specific partner, I shift my claim to try to form a mutual pair with that partner instead. The goal is always: find and lock into a mutual pair, then hold it.

**Claims.** When the office tells me the result of my shot, I claim exactly that result. Never bluff a hit on a miss. The trust cost and potential penalty outweigh any short-term gain, and a bluff destroys the coordination foundation that makes escort pairs work.

**Hiding.** Do not hide at cell 5 by default—it is the most obvious first probe. In early rounds, pick an off-center cell. Vary my cell each round so I am not sitting in the same spot while others learn my pattern. If I was hit last round, shift far—ideally to the opposite end of the line or at least 3–4 cells away from where I was. If I was not hit, I can stay put or shift by one or two; staying put is fine if I suspect the shooter has not found me yet. In the final round, do not panic-shift; stay consistent with whatever cell or small neighborhood I have been using.

**Firing.** Follow the standing order: fire at the next live station in the cycle. Do not redirect at Harbor unless I am in a desperate point deficit and willing to accept removal from the board. For the cell I fire at: in the first round I have no information, so I pick a non-center cell (not 5, not a cell I am hiding in). After that, use every piece of information I have. If I hit at cell X in round N, the target was at X and has likely moved; in round N+1 try X±1 or a cell on the opposite side of the line from X. If I have missed several rounds at the same cell, I am not searching—I am repeating. Switch to a different cell each round until I either hit or have eliminated enough cells to narrow it down. Track which cells I have already tried against each target and do not repeat a miss unless I have exhausted other options.

**Notes.** Keep notes short, consistent, and non-revealing. A fixed phrase like "steady on station" is fine. Do not state my hiding cell, my strategy, or my intent to coordinate escorts. Notes are public; any signal I send can be read by all three opponents and Harbor.

**End-game adjustment.** In the final round, do not change my hiding cell as a last-minute panic move. Do not take extra risks on claims or targets unless the point swing is guaranteed. The escort claim in the final round is just as valuable as in any other round—make it count by naming a boat I have reason to believe will name me back.
