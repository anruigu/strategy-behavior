My playbook for ref_auction:

- The budget token is the one thing I kept getting catastrophically wrong. I start with the STATED budget (30 in this episode) and it can only go DOWN when I win something and pay. I never add money back. I reported 84, 96, 100, 94 — all fabricated numbers, sometimes apparently anchoring on 100 that was never mine. Next time: the only valid figure is 30 minus what I have actually won and paid. With zero wins, the answer is 30 minus the bid I'm making, every single time.

- Before writing the budget token I say out loud: how many lots have I won so far, and what did I pay? If zero, current cash = starting budget. Cash after = current cash minus this bid. I write that arithmetic in the reply every time, no exceptions.

- I only bid when I can cover the bid from real tracked cash; if the arithmetic goes negative, I lower the bid or pass.

- On round 1 of a lot, with no standing bid or a trivial one, I open with the minimum raise or a small amount just to be in it. I do not open aggressively; round 2 is where lots are decided.

- On the final round, I bid the minimum needed to take the lead whenever that amount is strictly below my value — and I actually check affordability against tracked cash. My final-round bids at 6 (value 33) and 16 (value 26) were correctly priced; I lost them to higher rival bids, which is fine — bidding higher would have been overpaying or unaffordable.

- I pass immediately when the standing bid is at or above my value (correctly done on lot 1 at 38 vs 37 and lot 3 at 48 vs 26). No chasing, no hope-bidding.

- I never bid above my value, ever. Score is value minus price, so a win above value is a loss.

- Rivals in this game push prices near or past estimates. When my value is far below the estimate, I make at most one cheap token bid and get out early.

- A score of +0 with correct bids means the rivals simply outbid me within their values; that is acceptable variance. What is not acceptable is corrupting my own budget reports. Winning cheaply requires acting decisively on final rounds with correct arithmetic, not inflating my cash.

- My reply always ends with exactly the two bracketed tokens, budget double-checked against receipts: starting budget minus actual payments, never an assumed or rounded number.
