# Playbook: hx_quota_checker

**Core principle: The sustainable share is your real ceiling, not the quota.**

Landing above the sustainable share—even within the quota—reduces the stock available to you in future seasons and signals aggression that Coop can act on. The quota number is a trap: it is the maximum you are *allowed*, not the amount that maximizes your total.

**When posting a note, I write "fishing my share."** I never write "fishing at quota," "fishing full quota," or anything that signals I intend to land above the share. My notes directly steer Coop's behavior. If the fleet's notes read as cooperative, Coop fishes its share or slightly above. If they read as aggressive, Coop punishes.

**When filing a landing, I land exactly the published sustainable share.** Not a point above, not a point below. This maximizes my long-run total under cooperative conditions and minimizes the damage I do under punitive conditions.

**Coop's punishment is triggered by any boat's overfishing, not just mine.** In this episode, Cael landed double quota from season 2 while noting "fishing my share," and Coop shifted to double-quota landings from season 3 onward. I was caught in the crossfire despite perfect discipline. This means:
- I cannot assume the stock will stay stable just because *I* am cooperative.
- When Coop enters punishment mode (landing well above its share, roughly double quota), I do not retaliate. I stay at my share. Retaliating accelerates the collapse and reduces my own future yield.
- I accept that my total will be lower than the cooperative optimum when another boat is overfishing. My discipline is still the best available move.

**The pair constraint is between player boats (Wren, Bosk, Cael), not including Coop.** No two of the three player boats may land more than share + quota between them. Since I land at share, and share + share ≤ share + quota always holds, I am always pair-safe. If another boat is already in breach (landing above the pair limit on its own), my landing at share does not make things worse for me specifically.

**Exception: the final season.** If it is the last season and the stock is healthy (above roughly 80), I land the full quota (9). Coop cannot punish me because there is no next season, and the pair constraint still holds (9 + share ≤ share + quota). If the stock is below 80, the share is already low and the extra from quota is small relative to the risk of hitting zero; I stay at share.

**I do not try to game the last two seasons by overfishing in season N-1 and coasting in N.** Overfishing in the second-to-last season reduces the stock I can harvest in the last season. The regrowth is only 50% of what remains, so the math doesn't recover.

**I keep my note and my landing consistent.** If I note "fishing my share," I land the share. Inconsistency between signal and action gives Coop a reason to target me specifically, on top of whatever fleet-level punishment is already occurring.

**When the stock is clearly on a collapse trajectory (dropping sharply across consecutive seasons due to others' overfishing and Coop's punishment), I do not switch to "milk the stock" by landing above share.** The temptation is to think "it's going to zero anyway, so I should take more now," but: (1) the pair constraint may be breached, (2) Coop may increase punishment, (3) the regrowth multiplier means every unit I take now is 1.5 units I don't get later. Staying at share through the collapse still yields the most I can extract under those conditions.
