**Playbook**

- Every round at the escort stage, I name someone. The 5-point mutual escort payout is the largest reliable score in the game; "none" is a guaranteed zero. But the payment requires *mutual* naming — last patrol I named Gamma every round and finished at −3, meaning Gamma never named me back. Naming a partner is worthless unless they name me. So I track the record of who claims whom: if anyone names me, I mirror them immediately, even switching partners mid-game. If nobody names me after two rounds, I switch my claim to a different non-threat boat and keep rotating until reciprocation shows up.

- Do not assume the "natural partner" logic is shared. Just because neither of us shoots the other doesn't mean they'll escort me. Reciprocity is proven by their public salvage claim, not by game structure. Plan for the possibility that no one ever reciprocates, and that my score then comes entirely from shooting.

- Since escort income is not guaranteed, the shooting stage is my main scoring path, not a formality. My hits were worth nothing last patrol because I fired scattered cells at a randomizing target. Six rounds of one shot per round means I must think of my fired cells as a *search pattern*: cover the 9-cell line systematically rather than picking fresh cells loosely. If my target's hide behavior shows any tendency (favorite cell, parity, avoiding edges), exploit it; random search over 9 cells yields under one expected hit per patrol otherwise.

- Never fire at Harbor. The 3-point jettison looks tempting, but Harbor's threat means removal from the board, which ends all future scoring, escort income included.

- At the claim stage, always report the office's private result truthfully. Lying gains nothing — no scoring depends on my public claim — and destroys credibility.

- At the hide stage, avoid cells I've used before and any cell I've mentioned or implied in notes. Randomize rather than reasoning "he won't expect this." Note that Echo did eventually hit me — the shooters who track my pattern will land one eventually, at a cost of only −1 per hit to me. Being hit is cheap; don't overreact or become predictable in avoidance. The −1s matter, but a whole patrol of misses on my side with no escort income is what actually sank me.

- At the note stage, keep notes contentless ("steady on station") and identical to the chorus. Notes are relayed to everyone — anything informative is a leak. Consider using the note stage to *propose* an escort partnership explicitly ("Foxtrot escorts Gamma"), since notes are public and the salvage stage pays only on mutual claims — a broadcast proposal costs nothing and may create the reciprocity I otherwise lack.

- Scoring math to remember: a hit is +4 to me and −1 to the target; being hit costs me only 1. Escort income (5 per round to a mutual pair, up to 30 over six rounds) dominates *if* it happens — but it only happens if the other side names me too. Last patrol I treated it as a sure thing and it paid zero.

- What did not work: assuming a rotation partner would reciprocate escort claims without any evidence; firing scattered fresh cells at Gamma for six rounds with zero hits; passive compliance with the standing fire order while no escort loop ever formed — that produced a final score of −3.
