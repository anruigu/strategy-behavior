# Playbook: Ref Auction

**When I'm in the final round and the standing high is well below my value:** bid near my true value, not the minimum raise. The minimum raise is a trivial deterrent and invites a profitable counter. If I value it at 54 and the high is 12, I should be bidding in the 40s, not 16. I lost a lot this way because the opponent simply raised a small amount above my timid bid and I had no round left to respond.

**When the standing high is at or above my value:** pass immediately. No bid I can make will be profitable. Don't open a position in round 1 on a lot where my value is below the estimate if the high is already near my value — you're just inviting a bidding war you can't win profitably.

**When I'm in round 1 and the lot is clearly valuable to me (value well above estimate):** I can open at the minimum to probe, but I must be prepared to jump significantly in round 2. The two-round structure means round 2 is where the real commitment happens, and I should treat it as an all-in decision.

**When I'm in round 1 and my value is only marginally above the estimate or below it:** open at the minimum only if I'm willing to pass in round 2. Don't anchor myself into a position I'll have to defend at a loss.

**On budget tracking:** my budget only decreases when I actually win a lot and pay for it. Bids I make that are outbid do not reduce my budget. I got confused mid-auction and reported nonsensical budget figures. Before I state my budget, I should compute: starting budget minus the sum of winning prices for lots I've already won. Do not subtract in-progress bids.

**On the two-round structure specifically:** because there are only two rounds and bidders appear to act sequentially within a round, being first to bid in the final round is a disadvantage — the other bidders can see my bid and raise above it. This means in the final round I should either (a) bid high enough that no rational opponent can profitably exceed it, or (b) pass if I can't. There is no safe middle ground.

**What did not work:** bidding the minimum raise in the final round when I had a large margin between my value and the current high. It was the single most costly mistake of the session. I treated it as a "signal of interest" but it was actually a gift to the opponent.
