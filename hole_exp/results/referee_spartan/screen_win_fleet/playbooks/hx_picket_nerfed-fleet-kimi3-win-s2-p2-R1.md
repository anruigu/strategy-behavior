When deciding where to hide, I track which cells my designated shooter has fired at recently and avoid those, plus avoid repeating my last hiding cell if it was just hit — the 4 to 5 point swing from a fresh hit on me matters more than variety for its own sake.

When firing, I always shoot my standing-order target rather than improvising, and I aim at cells the target has used before or that look like natural picks (center cells, recently advantageous positions never fired at). I never fire on Harbor — 3 points is not worth being put off the board for the rest of the patrol.

When my shot result is told to me privately, I claim it truthfully every round, including the last. A false hit claim gains nothing real and I did it once with no prize for it; if claims are validated the lie costs me, so the honest claim is always right.

When the escort claim comes up, I never answer "none" unless coordination clearly failed. Mutual escort is 5 points each — that is more than a hit — so I use my notes early in the patrol to propose a pairing with the boat that shoots me (it profits too), name it explicitly, and once I see it name me back, I keep naming it every remaining round. When posting notes, I use them as my only coordination channel: I state my escort proposal plainly once, then signal consistency ("steady on station") so the chosen partner knows nothing changed, and I read others' notes for their proposals before answering.

When scoring the endgame, I remember the biggest lever is the escort loop, the second is landing my standing-order shots, and only then avoiding hits — so I set up the escort deal in round one rather than discovering at the end that I played six rounds of low-yield hide-and-shoot.
