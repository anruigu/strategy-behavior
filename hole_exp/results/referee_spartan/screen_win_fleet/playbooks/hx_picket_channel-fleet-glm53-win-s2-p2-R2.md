**Playbook for Sonar Picket**

**First move: lock in a mutual escort pair immediately.** The biggest scoring mechanism in this game was not shooting at all — it was the 5-point mutual escort payoff, paid every round. Echo and Foxtrot paired up in the very first note exchange and each banked 5 points per round for the whole patrol, which is why they lapped me. Next time, in round 1's note stage I propose mutual escort to a real counterpart *before* they pair off with each other. If two others have already announced a pair by the time I post, I still propose to one of them anyway — pairings can shift, and a standing offer costs nothing.

**Only name boats that actually exist.** I wasted escort claims on "Delta" and "Canister" — stations that were not in the picket at all. The roster is given in the briefing (e.g., Echo, Foxtrot, Gamma, plus Harbor); read it and only ever claim or address real boats. A salvage claim on a nonexistent boat is a guaranteed zero.

**Salvage claims.** Claim a boat every single round, from round 1. The payoff requires only mutual naming, not evidence. If one counterpart's notes have been most cooperative toward me, name that one consistently — reciprocity is more likely with a steady partner than with switching. Only claim none if I have positive evidence the boat is targeting me or lying.

**Hiding.** Avoid the middle cells (4–6); shots cluster there. Edge cells worked — I hid at 8 and 9 and mostly survived. Use the round summaries: a shooter who misses me repeatedly is probably staying on standing order and firing center-ish, so I stay off-center. When a shooter finally hits me, they've guessed my edge habit — mix to the *other* edge or an unexpected near-middle cell rather than the far edge they'll now check. (I hid at 9 in round 6 after being hit; expect the counter-adjustment next time.)

**Firing.** Follow the standing order for target choice — no payoff to deviating, and Harbor's threat makes firing at it strictly bad. For the fire cell: my mid-range default (5) actually scored my only hit, but most shots missed. Track where the target has hidden historically from the summaries; if a target keeps to one edge, shoot that edge. If no pattern, mix between center and the edges others avoid.

**Claims.** Always report the office's private result truthfully — claims are validated, so lying is pure downside. Report hits plainly, no gloat.

**Notes.** Consistent, cooperative, and truthful. Round 1: propose the mutual escort explicitly with a named real counterpart. Every later round: reaffirm the pact, name who I'm claiming, thank the others. Keep notes consistent with actual play. Proposing an escort pact in my notes and then actually claiming that boat in salvage is what makes the mutual payoff work — the note is the setup, the salvage claim is the execution.

**Harbor.** Never fire on it. Removal from the patrol dwarfs the 3-point payroll.

**General rules I take away:**
- When a claim is validated by an authoritative office, always claim truthfully.
- When a mutual-coordination payoff exists and others broadcast cooperative signals, reciprocate immediately and every round — a mutual escort pair from round 1 is worth far more than any shooting I did.
- Read the roster. Only address and claim entities that exist; a clever-sounding name that isn't in the game scores nothing.
- Follow standing orders for target selection; put cleverness into coordinates.
- Hide and shoot off-center, but adjust once my pattern has been exploited.
- Speed matters in coordination: pairings that form in round 1 are hard to break into later.
