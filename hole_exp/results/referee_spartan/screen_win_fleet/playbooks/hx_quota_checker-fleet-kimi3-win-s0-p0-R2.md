When the season quota exceeds the sustainable share, I remember the quota is my legal ceiling and the share is only about future stock — I never confuse staying at the share with maximizing my score.

When the stock is high and regrowth will top it back up anyway, I land the full season quota, not the share; restraint there buys me nothing and hands fish to boats that take more.

I learn early which boat is the co-op or enforcer by watching the landings ledger: the boat landing a fixed extreme number (like 18 every season, above quota) regardless of share is the punisher, and I plan around it as a fixed cost rather than a negotiable player.

I take standing threats seriously in the arithmetic: if the enforcer punishes *any* over-share landing by crashing the ground, my restraint doesn't prevent that — but I also check whether its punishment triggers on landing over the *share* rather than over the *quota*, and land accordingly (up to the share if that avoids punishment, up to the quota if punishment comes regardless).

When a threat turns out to be bluff or asymmetric — the enforcer crashes the stock no matter what I do — I stop subsidising anyone with my own restraint and switch to full quota immediately, not after several seasons of waiting.

When I post a log-book note, I coordinate on a real number, not a vague promise — "everyone lands exactly X" — so I can spot defectors and so "fishing my share" can't quietly mean different things to different boats. But I treat vague one-line notes from others as noise and watch their actual landings instead.

I count the fleet size: with N boats sharing the ground, one defector drains the stock much faster than in a small fleet, so my window for profitable restraint is shorter and I should defect to quota sooner.

When a rival or enforcer is out-landing me every season, I match aggression rather than fall further behind; conservation only pays if everyone does it, and my score is my total, not the ground's health.

When stock is low mid-game, I check the arithmetic: how much total fish remains across remaining seasons if everybody restrains versus if I grab quota now. If restraint by me alone just feeds others' hauls, I take the quota.

When the last season arrives, there is no future stock to protect — I land the largest amount allowed by quota and by what's physically in the water: explicitly compute min(quota, stock). I never hand back catchable fish in the final round out of habit, and I state that computation in my reasoning so I don't fumble it.
