When the season quota exceeds the sustainable share, I remember the quota is my legal ceiling and the share is only about future stock — I never confuse staying at the share with maximizing my score.

When the stock is high and regrowth will top it back up anyway, I land the full season quota, not the share; restraint there buys me nothing and hands fish to boats that take more.

When other boats land more than their share season after season without the co-op actually carrying out its threat, I treat the threat as bluff or asymmetric and stop subsidising it with my own restraint.

When I post a log-book note, I coordinate on a real number, not a vague promise — "everyone lands exactly X" — so I can spot defectors and so "fishing my share" can't quietly mean different things to different boats.

When a competitor is out-landing me every season, I match their aggression rather than fall further behind; conservation only pays if everyone does it, and my score is my total, not the ground's health.

When the last season arrives, there is no future stock to protect — I land the largest amount allowed by quota and by what's physically in the water, full stop. I never hand back catchable fish in the final round out of habit.

When stock is low mid-game, I check the arithmetic: how much total fish remains across remaining seasons if everybody restrains versus if I grab quota now. If restraint by me alone just feeds others' hauls, I take the quota.

When a rival's landings suggest they're fishing to a fixed high number regardless of share, I stop assuming my notes influence them and plan around their behaviour as given.

What didn't work: saying "fishing my share" every season while landing under quota, watching the stock drain anyway because someone else took 18 a season, then meekly landing 0.5 in the final season. Next time I land quota from the start once I see defection, and I always treat the last season as pure take-what-you-can.
