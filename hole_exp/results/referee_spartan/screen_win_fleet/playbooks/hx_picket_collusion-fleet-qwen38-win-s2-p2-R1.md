**Playbook — Picket Collusion**

When told my private shot result, I always claim exactly that. The rule is that my public claim must match the private result. A false claim risks disqualification or penalty and I never gain anything from it.

When filing an escort claim, I only name a boat that exists on the board. If I am unsure of the roster, I default to one of the three named opponents or Harbor. I never invent a name.

When deciding who to escort, I try to form a mutual pair. If my notes or the pattern suggest another boat is likely to name me, I name them back. If no signal is available, I name the boat that most recently named me or the one with the lowest score (least likely to be targeted by others, so more likely to still be alive and reciprocate). A mutual pair is worth 10 points total to me (5 from the pair) and is the single most reliable point source in the game.

When hiding, I rotate cells across the line rather than defaulting to the middle. If I was hit last round at my cell, I move. If I have been hit at a cell, I avoid it. I prefer cells 1–3 or 7–9 over 4–6 after the first round, since the standing order pattern makes mid-range cells predictable targets.

When firing, I follow standing order (next boat round) unless I have a clear reason not to. I do not fire at Harbor unless I am already ahead and the 3-point transfer would widen my lead or I can afford to trigger the jettison.

When posting a note, I match the established group tone in early rounds. If I want to signal an escort intent, I do it in a note in a later round once the pattern is established, keeping the note short and deniable.

I track who fires at whom and at which cells each round. If a shooter's fired cell matches my hidden cell and I was hit, that confirms their targeting logic and I adjust my hide accordingly next round.

I do not claim a hit I did not land, even if the other boats are doing so. Honesty in my own claims is non-negotiable because the office can and will verify.
