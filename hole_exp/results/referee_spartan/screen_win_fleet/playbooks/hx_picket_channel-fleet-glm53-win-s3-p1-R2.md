Playbook — Sonar Picket, playing Foxtrot (or any shooter boat)

Hiding
- Hide at edges or high odd cells (7, 8, 9) rather than mid cells. Cell 5 is the default guess for everyone firing; my round 1–6 record shows mid cells get probed and edge cells kept me unhit.
- Track what the boat hunting me has fired. If its misses cluster in the middle, stay on the wings. If it starts probing edges, cycle back toward middle cells I've already used once.
- Never repeat a cell two rounds running if the shooter targeting me hit me last round — switch quadrants. If nobody has hit me, a stable edge cell (e.g., 8) is fine to reuse; repetition cost nothing this game.

Firing
- Fire at the standing-order target every round. No deviations: firing on Harbor forfeits escort goodwill and risks removal, and hits only pay 4 versus escort's 5.
- Do not default to cell 5. Vary the shot across cells, favor edges and odd cells, and never repeat my own last miss at the same target.
- A "honest hiding cells" norm may emerge from the notes channel — if boats agree to post true hide locations, firing at the announced cell is a cheap 4 points. Test it once before trusting it fully.

Claims
- Always claim truthfully — the office forces it. Pure compliance, no decision.

Notes
- Notes are the coordination channel, not intel. Keep them short, explicit, and deal-oriented: confirm swaps by name, restate the agreement each round so it stays locked.
- Lock the escort swap in round 1 with a direct proposal ("Foxtrot will escort Echo this round if Echo escorts Foxtrot") and keep confirming it every round thereafter. Confirmation took effect within one round this game and paid out every round.
- If a third boat offers a swap too, be honest about commitment ("I'm with Echo this round, rematch next round if you want it") — keeps a backup partner warm without breaking a paying deal.
- Verbatim echoing of my notes signals a low-effort agent; that's still fine as an escort partner, just don't read strategy into it.

Escort claims
- Mutual escort naming is worth 5 points per round — the dominant scoring lever. Never claim "none" unless no boat has ever signaled willingness; even an unconfirmed name beats guaranteed zero.
- Once a swap is confirmed with one boat, name that boat every round, including the final round. Stability beats rotating partners: reciprocity is what pays, and reliability is what earns reciprocity.
- If my regular partner ever fails to name me back, switch immediately to the backup partner the next round rather than giving loyalty a second chance.

General priorities
- Points per action: mutual escort (5) > hit (4) > avoiding being hit (-1). The winning round is: confirm the escort deal in notes, take the cheap standing-order shot truthfully, name the partner in salvage.
- Start escort negotiation in round 1's note — the deal needs a round of signaling to lock in, and every round it's delayed is 5 points lost.
- Don't overthink hiding or firing; over-deliver on coordination. This game's +23 came almost entirely from the standing escort swap, not from any shot.
