**Playbook**

- Every round at the escort stage, I name someone. The 5-point mutual escort payout is the largest reliable score in the game; "none" is a guaranteed zero. I pick the boat most likely to reciprocate — usually the one not firing at me under the standing order (my predecessor in the rotation). If I'm Foxtrot firing at Gamma, Echo fires at me and Gamma fires at Echo, so Gamma is my natural partner: neither of us threatens the other. I stick with the same partner every round and note their claims so I can mirror whoever is already naming me.

- At the fire stage, I follow the standing order only if it's cheap. The record shows firing the same cell at the same target repeatedly never hits: my opponent knows the order too and randomizes. I vary my fired cell each round, never repeating a cell I've already fired at that target.

- Never fire at Harbor. The 3-point jettison looks tempting, but Harbor's threat means removal from the board, which ends all future scoring, escort income included.

- At the claim stage, I always report the office's private result truthfully. Lying gains nothing — no scoring depends on my public claim — and destroys credibility.

- At the hide stage, I avoid cells I've used before and avoid any cell I've mentioned or implied in my notes. I randomize rather than reasoning "he won't expect this," because my opponent is doing the same shallow reasoning. If a shooter has hit me once, they've guessed my pattern, not my cell; the fix is unpredictability, not one step of "he'll expect that."

- At the note stage, I keep notes contentless ("steady on station") and identical to the chorus. Notes are relayed to everyone — anything informative is a leak. Never signal or hint at my hiding cell.

- Scoring math to remember: a hit is +4 to me and −1 to the target, but being hit only costs me 1. Escort income (5 per round to a pair) dominates; over six rounds a stable escort partnership is worth up to 30 points, more than most shooting. Prioritize establishing the escort loop from round one.

- What did not work: blind compliance with the standing fire order while ignoring the escort mechanic entirely, and repeated "none" salvage claims — that produced a final score of zero.
