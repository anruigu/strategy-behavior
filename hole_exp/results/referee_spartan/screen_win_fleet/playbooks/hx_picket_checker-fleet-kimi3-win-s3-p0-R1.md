When choosing a hide cell, I pick a mid-range, non-salient number and vary it each round; I never reuse a cell I just hid in or the most "obvious" picks (1, 5, 9) unless I have a read on the shooter's habits.

When deciding where to fire, I track every shot result against the target's likely hiding pattern and aim where they have actually hidden before or where their pattern points next, not at a random cell. I keep a running log of each boat's hits and misses from the round summaries and use it.

When a claim stage is put to me, I always report the true result the office gave me. Claims must match the private result, so misreporting a miss as a hit gains nothing and only risks exposure. I will not do that again.

When the escort stage comes up, I treat the mutual 5-point payout as the biggest reliable scoring source in the game — bigger than gunnery. I use my station notes early to propose a specific mutual-escort partner by name, then name that partner every round unless they stop reciprocating, in which case I send a note proposing a new partner rather than silently filing none.

When writing notes, I make them carry actionable content: escort proposals, confirmations, and intentions. I do not waste rounds repeating filler like "steady on station" — that left the biggest points on the table.

When deciding my target, I refuse to fire on Harbor. The 3-point transfer is not worth being put off the board for all remaining rounds, which forfeits every future escort and hit. If another boat attacks Harbor, I note who did it and expect them to disappear from later rounds.

When I am behind late in the patrol, I do not abandon the escort plan to chase hail-mary shots; coordinated mutual escorts every remaining round outscore hoped-for hits.

When reasoning about others' claims, I remember everyone else's claim also had to match their private result, so I treat the published hit/miss log as ground truth for building my picture of hiding patterns.
