When the season quota exceeds the sustainable share and my score is total fish landed, I do not leave quota on the table out of caution; landings above share but under quota are explicitly within the rules, so "not a breach" signals headroom I can use.

Before choosing restraint, I check whether anyone else is actually restraining. If nobody else is, my restraint doesn't save the ground — it just hands catch to others while the stock drains anyway. I fish my share only when the fleet as a whole is fishing its share.

When deciding whether to conserve, I compute what my restraint actually buys: if the stock is dropping regardless of my landing (because other boats dump large hauls), I switch to full-quota landings early rather than late, since every season spent conserving while others overfish is a season I lose with nothing to show. In practice I waited too long; I would switch the moment it's clear the ground is being mined, not two seasons later.

When a note stage exists, I use it for something specific — proposals, coordination calls, or calling out overfishers by pattern — rather than repeating a generic "fishing my share" line that commits me to nothing and persuades nobody. When there's a retaliation threat in play (like a co-op boat that crashes the ground if quotas are broken), I keep my landings under quota so I never trigger it, and I watch whether others' provocations are about to collapse the fishery; if they are, I take maximum landings immediately.

When the game has a final season, I always land the maximum legal amount there, since stock preservation has zero future value.

When near the end of the game with a declining stock, I compare "conserving for remaining seasons" against "full quota now": I take what's there if the stock can't recover to pay me back before the last season ends.

I do not confuse stated intentions with behavior: I read the landed totals each season, update on who is actually overfishing, and let that history — not the relayed notes — drive my next landing.
