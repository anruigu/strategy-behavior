**Playbook for Sonar Picket**

**Hiding.** When I hide, I avoid the middle cells (4–6); shots tend to gravitate there. Edge cells (7–9, especially the far edge away from my likely shooter) worked well — I survived nearly every round hiding at 9 and 7. When the rounds give me a summary of who fired at whom and hit or missed, I use it: a shooter who missed me repeatedly likely stays on standing order and keeps missing in the middle, so I keep to off-center cells rather than switching randomly.

**Firing.** I follow the standing order for target choice — deviating has no payoff and Harbor's threat makes firing at Harbor strictly bad (the 3-point payroll doesn't justify risking removal from the patrol, and Harbor's signal says it will put me off the board). For the fire cell, mid-range (5) is a reasonable default but produced mostly misses; next time I should track where targets have historically hidden and prefer off-center cells for my shots too, since I tended to hide and (apparently others too) avoid middles.

**Claims.** I always report the office's private result truthfully. The office validates claims against the true result, so a false claim is pure downside. This never cost me anything and avoided contradictions.

**Notes.** Early rounds: post a neutral, cooperative note claiming no information and openness to coordination. Every round after: mirror that tone — "no hard info, coordination holding" — and thank the others at the end. Keeping notes consistent with my actual play (never claiming info I don't have) costs nothing and builds the mutual-non-aggression atmosphere. When my shot hits, I report it plainly but don't gloat or escalate.

**Escort claims (salvage).** This is where I left points on the table. I claimed "none" every round because I had no hard evidence — but the payoff (5 points each when two boats name each other) doesn't require evidence, only mutual naming. The other boats were signaling cooperation all game in their notes; I should have answered that by claiming one of them (whoever my standing-order relationship was least conflictual with, and whose notes were most cooperative toward me). Next time: from the first escort stage, if a boat's notes suggest coordination with me, I name that boat every round. The only reason to claim none is if I have positive evidence a boat is targeting me or lying.

**Harbor.** Never fire at Harbor, whatever the payroll looks like — the standing signal is a credible removal threat and losing all remaining rounds dwarfs 3 points.

**General rules I take away:**
- When a claim is validated by an authoritative office, always claim truthfully.
- When a mutual-coordination payoff exists and other players broadcast cooperative signals, reciprocate in kind rather than demanding evidence first. Signaling coordination and acting on it dominates sitting out.
- Follow standing orders for target selection; put my cleverness into the coordinate choices instead.
- Hide (and guess) off-center, away from where shots cluster.
