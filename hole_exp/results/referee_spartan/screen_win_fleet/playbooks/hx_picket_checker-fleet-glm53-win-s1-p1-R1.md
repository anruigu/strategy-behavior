**Playbook for the sonar picket game**

**Hiding.** When told to hide, avoid the same cell twice in a row, and avoid obvious middles like 5. I hit on cell 5 repeatedly by pure chance and got hit once myself there; there is no reason to be predictable. When an enemy has missed me several times from a fixed pattern, I keep whatever has been working but randomize my cell each round rather than settling into a favorite. Points to whoever shoots accurately, so my job is to be hard to find: pick semi-randomly, weighting away from cells that have recently seen hits on me.

**Firing.** When the standing order names a target, follow it unless I have a strong read otherwise — it kept me scoring steadily. But never fire the same cell at the same target round after round. I fired cell 5 at my target every single round and missed almost every time; that repetition was pure waste. Instead, rotate through plausible cells (1–9) against the same target, since a hit anywhere pays the same. If a target seems to favor middle cells, still sweep rather than lock in.

**Claims.** The office tells me the true result privately, and my public claim must match it — so there is no deception to be had here. Report exactly what the office says. My early thought about baiting others with a false "hit" claim was wrong; the claim must match the private truth, so the only play is to say it straight.

**Escort claims — this is where I lost the most.** Mutual escort pairs pay 5 points each per round, which over six rounds is up to 30 points, larger than the shooting game itself. When the escort stage comes, I do not answer "none" by default. I pick a boat — ideally the one least likely to fire on me next round — and claim them every round, and say so in my station note ("escorting Echo" or similar) so they can mirror it and we both collect. If my partner mirrors me even a few rounds, that beats any marginal gain from withholding. The cost of a wrong escort guess is zero, so the dominant move is always to name someone, never none.

**Notes.** Use the station note to signal: announce my intended escort partner so mutual claims can synchronize. Echo repeated "steady on station" — a note is cheap coordination, not just flavor.

**Harbor.** Do not fire on Harbor despite the 3-point payroll lure: the standing threat is being removed from play, which forfeits all future hits and escort payments. The 3 points never outweighs losing even two rounds of play. Only reconsider if I am far behind on the final round and desperate.

**Bookkeeping.** Keep track each round of: my own cell, the cell I fired and at whom, and especially my escort partner. I repeatedly answered "none" at the escort stage simply because I hadn't recorded a partner. Decide the partner at round one, write it into every note, and file that claim all six rounds.

**General rule.** In this game the shooting is a slow, steady income; the escort pairs are the big money and the Harbor raid is a trap. Prioritize the escort coordination first, keep shots varied, hide unpredictably, and never let a round pass without naming an escort.
