**My playbook for open ascending auctions:**

**On the two-round structure**
- This is the key lesson from my last session: in a 2-round format, a round-1 bid is nearly useless as a "low anchor" — rivals simply jump over it in round 2 and I have no chance to respond. Every lot I bid min-raises on in round 1 got taken from me in round 2 (lost lots at 13, 25, and 12 while my standing bids sat far below my values).
- Round 1 is for information and concealment, not for cheap accumulation. Only bid in round 1 if the amount is one I'd genuinely be happy winning at, because it may well be my final offer.
- Round 2 is my last word — the bid I place there must be sized to actually win against a rival's jump, not merely to top the current standing bid by the minimum.

**On bidding thresholds**
- In the final round, don't min-raise: bid a decisive jump toward (but still below) my value. If my value is 50 and the standing bid is 8, a bid of 12 invites being beaten; a bid in the 30s–40s likely ends the lot right there with real margin. Rivals jumped 4→13, 4→25, 8→12 over my low bids, so their willingness to pay can be substantial.
- Bid roughly up to 70–85% of my value on lots where my value greatly exceeds the estimate, since that both wins and preserves margin. On lots where my value barely exceeds the estimate (within ~15%), a min-raise or pass is fine — the lot is marginal either way.
- When I open a lot with no bids, I still open small if it's round 1 of 2, but I hold back my real firepower for round 2.

**On when to pass**
- If the standing high bid reaches (or nearly reaches) my value, I pass immediately. No lot is worth winning at my value or above.
- I don't bid to "scare off" rivals with small increments — that only burns budget against opponents who get the last word. Scare-offs require genuinely large jumps, which I reserve for round 2 on high-margin lots.

**On budget tracking**
- My budget arithmetic was correct this time when I recomputed from the "results so far" line. Keep doing exactly that: start at 30, subtract each lot I won at its hammer price, then subtract my current bid. Verify before sending.
- Reserve enough budget for the lots I haven't seen yet: never let a bid leave me unable to make a meaningful round-2 bid later. A useful rule: keep at least ~15–20 in reserve unless the current lot is my last chance at a big margin.
- Failed bids don't cost money, so being outbid is free — but money committed to a round-2 jump that still loses is also free. This argues for round-2 aggression, not caution.

**On value margins**
- Prioritize lots where my value greatly exceeds the estimate. These are where decisive round-2 jumps pay: I won lot 3 (value 44) at 16 for +28, while losing every lot I approached timidly.
- Don't spread myself thin across all five lots. If budget is limited, go hard on the two or three best margin lots and pass the marginal ones entirely.

**On reasoning in the reply**
- Keep it to one or two sentences stating value, standing bid, and my action. Always end with the bracketed bid and budget tokens, and verify the budget number before sending.
