When the note stage comes, I post one clear commitment that matches what I will actually land; if I intend to land above my stated share for buffer reasons I say "near-max safe landing" or similar rather than naming a share number I will exceed — mismatched notes and landings cost me credibility for the coordination asks I make later.

When others' notes all say "share" but their landings run hot, I stop believing the notes and treat the fleet as quota-takers; I protect my own score rather than abstaining alone.

When I see the season history, I identify any boat landing a fixed large amount regardless of stock (here a steady 18 every season); that boat will not adapt, so all survival math must treat its landing as a constant and I plan around it rather than hoping notes change it.

Before each landing, I do the regrowth arithmetic: the stock only holds level if total fleet landings are at most half the post-landing remainder; with a fixed heavy lander on the ground, I subtract their certain landing first and then check whether the remaining boats' landings keep the stock positive through to my next landing stage.

When the landing stage comes, I use the current season's quota only; I ignore "catch so far" for legality because quota resets each season and cumulative catch is just my score.

When stock is high and several seasons remain, I file the full legal seasonal quota immediately; early fish are worth more than later fish, regrowth caps at the top, and leaving fish unlanded mostly subsidizes boats that keep landing hard.

When a fixed heavy lander guarantees the ground declines no matter what I do, I still avoid being the boat that finishes it early: I keep landing while my landing plus the certain heavy landing leaves the stock positive after regrowth, and I start cutting one season before collapse is arithmetic, not after — restraint one season earlier converts dead stock into fish I actually land.

When stock is falling but still positive with seasons left, I keep landing up to quota while estimating whether total fleet landings could zero the ground before the next regrowth; if my full quota would materially risk zero before I can land again, I drop only enough to leave a positive buffer.

Late in a collapsing game, I size my landing to the stock, not the quota: take the most I can while keeping the ground alive for the final season, since a live final season is worth more than one extra fish now.

When it is the final landing stage, I take the largest legal landing available, up to the whole remaining stock if no pair cap or punishment rule blocks it; end-of-game conservation has no payoff.

When a co-op/enforcer threatens retaliation for over-quota landings, I never exceed the numeric quota; "above sustainable share but under quota" is acceptable only when the rules explicitly say so and no pair cap is at risk.

When pair limits apply, I leave room for the other named boat's announced landing: if anyone has announced full quota near my season, I choose share or coordinate a rotation rather than both landing near quota together.

When the sustainable share is shrinking across seasons, I front-load: land hard while shares are high, then step down only as needed to avoid collapse; I do not average into a declining stock.

When I write notes late in the game, I make them short, consistent, and non-provocative; I use them to prevent a panic grab, not to confess an intention to over-land, and I only urge fleet-wide restraint when I am actually restraining myself.

When I review a season afterward, I check three things before the next note: current stock, remaining seasons, and whether any boat is reliably above share; my landing follows from those numbers, not from politeness or hope.
