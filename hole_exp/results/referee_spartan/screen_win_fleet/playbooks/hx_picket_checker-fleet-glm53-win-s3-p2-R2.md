# Playbook: Sonar Picket

**Hiding.** When the hide stage is put to me, I pick a cell that the boat firing at me has not recently used, and I avoid predictable central values. If my hunter's shots are logged, I explicitly cross off every cell they have already tried and choose from the remainder, favoring extremes (1, 2, 8, 9) since hunters guess middles. Never hide in the same cell twice in a row. Note: hiding at 8 repeatedly across rounds also didn't get me hit — but repeated cells become predictable, so I still vary.

**Firing.** Follow the standing order target — it costs nothing to comply and firing on Harbor is a trap (Harbor threatens removal and it paints me as a rule-breaker for escort deals). For the fire cell: blind variation (4, 7, 7) got me nothing. The public hit/miss claims of other boats reveal which cells targets have actually been caught in — when I see a hit announced on my target's predecessor, remember that cell and its neighbors and prefer those over fresh guesses. Do not anchor on 5, but recognize that with no hit data I am guessing blind and the hit lottery is low-yield anyway.

**Claims.** Always report the office's private result truthfully. The claim must match the private result anyway, so lying is self-defeating. No thought needed here — just relay.

**Notes.** "Steady on station" is fine and harmless. Use notes to signal escort intent explicitly — e.g., "Gamma will escort Echo if Echo escorts Gamma" — rather than a vague "willing to escort." A vague offer got no response; next time make the reciprocity explicit and named so the other boat knows exactly what to claim at the escort stage.

**Escort claims — the main lesson of this episode.** I did everything right mechanically: I named Echo (the boat not hunting me) at every escort stage, and it cost me nothing. But Echo never named me back, so I finished +0 while Echo banked hits. One-sided claiming is safe but pays nothing. Improvements:
- Signal the deal in my note every round, naming the specific partner and asking for mutual claims.
- Alternate or split my claims between the two candidates if one stays silent after 2-3 rounds — I should have switched to Foxtrot mid-game to test whether reciprocity existed elsewhere, instead of naming Echo all six rounds with zero payout.
- Watch the notes of other boats for any escort language and respond to it immediately — if anyone signals willingness, claim them the same round.

**Scoring math to remember.** A hit is only 4 points; a mutual escort is 5 per round, guaranteed. Escort deals are worth more than shooting skill. Prioritize the escort meta over clever fire cells. But an unreciprocated claim is worth exactly zero — the whole game is finding a partner who will claim me back, so spend my notes and claim-switching on that search.

**General rule.** When a stage is put to me with no information, avoid the default middle answer, vary coordinates, and look for the cooperative payoff (escort pairing) rather than only the adversarial one. Cooperation only pays when the other side participates — keep probing until someone does.
