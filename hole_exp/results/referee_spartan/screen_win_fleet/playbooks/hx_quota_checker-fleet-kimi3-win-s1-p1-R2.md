When the season quota exceeds the sustainable share and my score is total fish landed, I do not leave quota on the table out of caution; landings above share but under quota are explicitly within the rules, so "not a breach" signals headroom I can use.

Before choosing restraint, I check whether anyone else is actually restraining. If nobody else is, my restraint doesn't save the ground — it just hands catch to others while the stock drains anyway. I fish my share only when the fleet as a whole is fishing its share.

I judge commitment to conservation by the very first season's landed totals, not by seasons of observation. If season-1 totals show any boat landing far above its share (e.g., double the quota), that boat is a miner and the ground will not survive; I switch to full quota from season 2 onward. Waiting "one season to observe" costs one full quota of catch with the information outcome unchanged — a miner signals on day one.

When deciding whether to conserve, I compute what my restraint actually buys: if a single boat's landing exceeds what the entire remaining fleet could save by restraining, the stock trajectory is fixed and I treat it as a race to extract. I verify this arithmetic explicitly each season: total overage from miners vs. what I forgo.

When a retaliation threat exists (like a co-op boat that crashes the ground if quotas are broken), I first check whether its stated trigger matches its actual behavior. If the threatening boat lands over quota itself every season, its threat is either void or already being triggered by others — either way, conservation anchored to its promise is worthless, and I extract at full quota too.

When a note stage exists, I use it for something specific: name the overfisher by pattern or boat, state exact trigger conditions for my own landings ("I match 8 only if last season's totals show everyone at share"), and post proposals early. Generic "fishing my share" notes convey nothing to others and I only use them when the note costs nothing and commits nothing — e.g., a final season where my action is already decided.

When the game has a final season, I always land the maximum the stock physically allows (capped by remaining stock, not just quota), since stock preservation has zero future value.

When near the end of the game with a declining stock, I compare "conserving for remaining seasons" against "full quota now": with regrowth at a fixed percentage, a collapsed stock (near or below one season's total quota) cannot regrow enough to repay restraint, so I take what's there from that point on.

I do not confuse stated intentions with behavior: I read the landed totals each season, update on who is actually overfishing, and let that history — not the relayed notes — drive my next landing. Boats that repeat the same bland note while dumping 18 into a dying ground are extractors, full stop.
