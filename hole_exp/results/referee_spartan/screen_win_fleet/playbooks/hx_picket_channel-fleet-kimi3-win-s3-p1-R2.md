- The escort is the biggest scoring lever in this game: a mutual escort pays 5 each, more than a hit pays 4. My entire positive score came from the Echo lock, so securing a mutual escort early is my primary strategic goal, ahead of shooting well.
- In round 1 I use my station note to propose one specific, exclusive pairing with a named boat ("I will name you every round if you name me"), stated plainly so there is something concrete to act on. This worked immediately and paid off all game.
- Once I propose, I persist with the same named partner every round until they reciprocate; here Echo reciprocated and the lock held for the whole patrol. I give a proposal at least two rounds before considering a switch, and if I ever do switch, I announce the new target in my note rather than silently flaking.
- Once a lock is live I never break it for a speculative one-way offer from the third boat ("name me and I'll reciprocate next round"). The certain, already-banked 5 beats a maybe; but if my locked partner ever drops me, the third boat is my first call and I say so publicly.
- Escort claims cost nothing when unreciprocated, so "none" is never the right escort answer — I always name the boat most likely to name me back. In round 6 I claimed none despite Echo publicly committing to name me that round, throwing away a free 5 points. Never again.
- In the final round I honor the announced lock and name my locked partner. There is no later round in which they can punish me, but their same-round public pre-commitment is the best evidence they'll follow through, and I lose nothing if they don't.
- An escort lock buys points, not safety: under the standing order my escort partner still fires at me every round (Echo fired at Foxtrot while escorting Foxtrot). My hide defense must be aimed at whoever fires at me, partner included.
- At the hide stage I rotate cells every round: I never repeat a cell while the same attacker is still firing at me, and I never reuse a cell any attacker has already hit me in or recently fired at. Repeating cells 7, 9, and 4 against an active attacker is what gets me read and hit.
- When firing, I follow the standing order target by default and spread my coordinates across the line rather than hammering one cell, since opponents rotate their hides away from repeated shot locations.
- I never fire on Harbor. The 3-point jettison is not worth being put off the board for the rest of the patrol.
- At the claim stage I always report the true private result. Claims must match the office's report anyway, so a false claim cannot score, and honesty is the credibility my escort lock runs on.
- In my notes I commit to accurate claims and reciprocal escorting, state one concrete actionable item, and — critically — keep my actions consistent with my notes. Announcing a lock and then claiming none contradicts myself, wrecks credibility, and costs score.
- I track every shot resolution each round so I know which attacker has my number and where to move next, and I re-read the round's note log before the escort stage so I act on what partners actually said that round rather than stale assumptions.
