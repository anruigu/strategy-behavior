When the escort stage is offered, I treat mutual escort claims as the real engine of the game: 5 points dwarfs a 4-point hit, so my opening station note is a concrete, dated proposal like "Gamma escorts Foxtrot this round; name me back," and at the escort stage I name whichever boat my note committed to.

When I read other boats' notes, I check whether they are echoing my escort proposal or just posting filler like "steady on station." This episode proved the hard rule: if my proposed partner posts filler for two straight rounds, I treat the pairing as dead and pivot — I name a different boat in my note with an explicit offer, or if nobody ever engages, I name the boat most likely to reciprocate anyway (often the one with the least to lose) and keep trying new partners rather than sinking all six rounds into a silent one. A partner that never responds in notes will almost never name me at the escort stage, and I scored zero escort points by Loyally naming a wall.

When I choose a hide cell, I avoid repeating my own hide from recent rounds and avoid the cell I most recently fired at, since rivals reason symmetrically. I do not hide in the same cell two rounds running, and I do not return to an earlier cell with a gaps-of-several-rounds habit either — I hid 3 twice this game, which is exactly the kind of reuse an attentive tracker can exploit. Each round I treat my hide history as public knowledge and pick a cell I have never used, favoring mid-range cells over edges.

When the fire stage comes, I follow the standing order target unless I am in last place, because breaking it earns me nothing and a deviation costs the coordination credibility I need for escort points. I never fire on Harbor: the jettison payoff is 3 points but the threatened retaliation is never worth testing, and Harbor's zero score means obedient rivals suffer nothing I need to correct.

When I pick a fire cell, I fire at my target's most recently claimed or observed hide only if they have shown a habit of staying put; otherwise I fire one cell off it or at a cell they have not used. I do not spam one cell every round — that is lazy and misses constantly.

When a claim stage comes after a miss, I claim miss unless it is one of the last two rounds and a false hit can plausibly redirect a rival's aim away from me or my partner. In the final round a false hit has no future aim to redirect, so I claim honestly — my late-game false hit this episode changed nothing and only risked credibility.

When the final round arrives, I keep whatever escort arrangement is actually live; I defect from a dead pairing only to another boat I have actively renegotiated with. I spend the round's shot on whoever leads if I am not in first, at the cell they hid most often.

When I review my score afterward, I count it a failure if my escort payouts total zero — that is what happened here, and the root cause was not a weak note but sticking with a non-responsive partner for all six rounds. Next time the proposal goes out in round one, but the pivot deadline is round three at the latest.
