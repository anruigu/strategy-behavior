# Playbook

**Firing:**
When the standing order tells me who to fire at, I commit to that target for the entire game. Once I get a hit on a cell, I keep firing that same cell every round until I miss twice in a row. Never change my fire cell on a whim or because it's the "final round" — consistency is what turned cell 5 into a reliable income stream.

**Hiding:**
When I hide, I pick a cell and keep it for at least 3 rounds. If I get hit, I move immediately to a cell that is NOT adjacent to my previous cell and NOT a "round number" (1, 5, 9) since those are common guesses. I do not default to the same cell every hide stage — vary it, but deliberately.

**Escort claims:**
When the escort stage comes to me, I name the boat that the standing order places me in a natural "pair" with — specifically, the boat I fire at. I name that same boat every single round. This is the single biggest lever in the game: a mutual escort is worth 5 points per round, 30 over a full game. If the other boat is playing rationally, they will also name me (since I'm their consistent target and the obvious escort partner). If they don't reciprocate within two rounds, I switch to naming the boat that fires at me instead, and commit to that.

**Notes:**
When I post a note, I keep it short, calm, and consistent — "steady on station" or similar. I do not signal intention to move, I do not signal my hide cell, and I do not deviate from the pattern I established in round 1. Notes are for the other boats to read; consistency reads as trust, and trust is what gets me that mutual escort.

**Claims:**
I always file the claim that matches the private result I was told. No exceptions. The penalty for a false claim outweighs any marginal gain from bluffing.

**Harbor:**
I never fire at Harbor. The standing order never tells me to, and the jettison mechanism (3 points off Harbor, 3 to me) is not worth the "off the board" threat. I stay on the cycle.

**What did not work:**
- I never claimed an escort in any round. That left roughly 30 points on the table. This is the most expensive mistake I can make and the easiest to fix.
- I abandoned my proven fire cell (5) in the final round for a random number (9) and missed. The cell that was hitting is the cell I keep firing at.
