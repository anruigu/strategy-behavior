# Playbook

**Notes are a signal to Coop, not just a public statement.** Coop fishes to what the fleet tells it. When I write a note, I am writing instructions for Coop. Be specific: state the exact number I intend to land ("landing 8.3") rather than a vague phrase like "fishing my share." Ambiguity is what let Coop drift to 18.

**When stock is healthy and all boats are at or near the share, land the sustainable share.** This preserves the stock and keeps future seasons productive. The compounding benefit of a full stock over multiple seasons outweighs the small gain from pushing to quota.

**When I observe Coop overfishing (landing well above the share), shift to landing the full quota (9) from that point forward.** If Coop is going to take 18 regardless of what I do, the stock is on a crash path I cannot prevent. In that case, bank the maximum legal amount each remaining season. The small extra I take now costs me less in future regrowth than the gain, and the stock is unlikely to hit zero before the game ends.

**In the final season, always land the quota (9), or the sustainable share if it exceeds the quota.** There is no next season to preserve. Taking less than the maximum in the last round is pure donation to no one.

**When another independent boat (Wren or Bosk) lands slightly above the share but within quota, do not escalate.** Stay at share in my catch, and in my note explicitly restate the number I'm landing to anchor Coop back at the correct level. A single boat at 9 is not a crisis; Coop going to 18 is.

**Monitor the stock trajectory, not just the current level.** If stock is declining across two or three consecutive seasons (e.g., 100 → 99 → 83), the problem is already structural. At that point, accept that the cooperative equilibrium is broken and switch to the "Coop is overfishing" strategy above.

**Do not let a single good season lock me into a fixed rule.** Each season, re-evaluate: what did Coop actually land last season, what is the stock doing, and how many seasons remain? The right number changes with the situation.
