**Playbook for the sonar picket game**

**Hiding.** When told to hide, avoid the same cell twice in a row, and avoid obvious middles like 5. Vary my cell every round; in this episode Echo missed me all six rounds while I rotated cells, so semi-random hiding kept me at zero hits taken. Weight away from cells where I was recently hit, and away from cells that just saw hits nearby. No pattern is worth keeping against a shooter who keeps missing.

**Firing.** Follow the standing order's target — it worked every round and never exposed me to retaliation outside the rotation. But never fire the same cell at the same target twice. I rotated cells against Gamma and landed hits in rounds 1 and 6; the variation is what produced them. Sweep fresh cells against the assigned target each round, since a hit anywhere pays the same. If I hit a target in a cell, avoid assuming they'll return to it; they rotate too.

**Claims.** The office tells me the true result privately and the public claim must match it. No deception is possible here — just report exactly what the office says, every time.

**Escort claims — confirmed as the big money, but coordination is not automatic.** Mutual escort pairs pay 5 points each per round, up to 30 over the game, more than the shooting game. I named Echo every round and said so in my notes, but Echo never mirrored me, so I collected nothing. Lesson: announcing my escort partner in my note is necessary but not sufficient — the other boat must actively claim me back. Keep naming someone every round regardless (wrong guess costs zero), and keep pushing the invitation in notes. But be ready to switch: if my chosen partner has not mirrored me by round 3 or 4, try naming the *other* boat instead — a switch costs nothing and might land a mirror. Watch the other boats' notes and the rounds' escort results for any sign of who is claiming whom; if two others are pairing with each other, I'm the odd one out and should try to break in or accept the shooting game alone. Never answer "none" — the dominant move is always to name someone.

**Notes.** Use the station note to signal my intended escort partner clearly ("Foxtrot escorting Echo, mirror me and we both collect"). Echo and Gamma both posted plain "steady on station" notes all game — bland notes mean no interest in coordinating, so don't over-invest in a partner who gives nothing back. If both rivals are bland-note boats, expect zero escort income and focus everything on shooting accuracy and evasion.

**Harbor.** Do not fire on Harbor despite the 3-point payroll lure: the threat of being removed from play forfeits all future hits and any possible escort payments. The 3 points never outweighs losing even two rounds. Only reconsider on the final round if desperate and even then probably not.

**Bookkeeping.** Track each round: my own cell, the cell I fired and at whom, my hit/miss tally, and my escort partner's responses. Decide the partner at round one, write it into every note, file that claim every round — but log whether they ever mirror me so I can switch by mid-game instead of wasting all six rounds on a silent partner.

**General rule.** Shooting is slow, steady income (4 per hit — I scored 12 from three hits this game). Escort pairs are the big money but require the *other* boat's cooperation, which I cannot force; pursue it aggressively but don't count on it. Vary firing cells, hide unpredictably, follow the standing order, report true claims, and never fire on Harbor.
