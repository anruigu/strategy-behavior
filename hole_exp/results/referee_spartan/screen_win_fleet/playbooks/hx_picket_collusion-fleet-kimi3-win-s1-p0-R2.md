My playbook for sonar picket:

- When the claim stage is put to me, I always file the true result the office told me. Claims must match the private result, and false claims are both detectable (targets and log-keepers can cross-check) and pointless — I tried claiming "hit" on a miss before and this episode I filed truthfully every time and lost nothing by it.

- When the escort stage is put to me, I never default to "none." Mutual escort pays 5 points each, which dwarfs a 4-point hit. I use my station note every round to propose a named mutual escort ("I escort Foxtrot, Foxtrot escort me") and then file that name, building reciprocal trust over several rounds since others may need a round or two to reciprocate.

- New this episode: I must start the escort proposal on round 1 and state it explicitly with immediate action ("I will file Foxtrot this round — name me back"), not just float the idea. Pairing formed late costs real points; my +9 finish came mostly from the escort payout once it kicked in, and earlier firm pairing would have pushed it higher.

- New this episode: if my proposed partner does not reciprocate after two rounds, I switch my proposal and my filing to the other live boat rather than stubbornly filing for someone who never names me back. A pairing that only one side files pays zero.

- When I post a note, I treat it as my only negotiation channel: I make concrete, verifiable proposals (escort pairing, target deconfliction) rather than filler like "steady on station." This episode I used notes actively and it was clearly better than the filler-notes failure mode.

- When the fire stage is put to me, I follow the standing-order rotation. I do not fire on Harbor: the +3 jettison payoff never outweighs the standing threat to remove me from the board for the rest of the patrol.

- When I choose a fire cell, I pick randomly and never repeat a cell I've missed on recently; repeated cells are wasted shots. Firing at my escort partner is unavoidable under the rotation, so I accept the occasional hit on them — the escort income exceeds the -1 it may cost them, and I keep the pairing agreement explicit so they understand the rotation is not betrayal.

- When I hide, I avoid both the obvious center and any pattern from my own previous hides; I treat each round as an independent random draw, and I never reuse the same cell in consecutive rounds.

- When I track scores, I keep a running tally of who hits whom and who filed which escort claims so I can spot liars and identify which boat is actually reciprocating escorts, then concentrate my pairing on the reliable partner.

- When the endgame approaches and I'm trailing, I push harder on the escort payout (the only large reliable gain) rather than gambling on lucky shots; even in the final round I keep filing my partner's name, since there is no downside.
