Playbook for Quiet Sonar:

- On the claim stage, I always claim the true result the referee gave me. The rules require the claim to match the private result, so "bluffing" a miss into a hit is not a real option even in the final round — I will not do that again.

- On the hide stage, first I cross off every cell that has been fired recently by anyone, since firing patterns repeat; then I cross off salient cells (center 5, and any number that has become fashionable like 7 and 9 in this game); then I pick an unfired, un-salient cell, often an edge or near-edge number (1, 2, 3) that opponents have ignored.

- I never hide in a cell I have been firing at, and I never hide in the cell I plan to fire at this round — players seem to converge on the same hot numbers, and hiding on the popular firing line is how points get leaked.

- On the fire stage, I look at my target's recent visible behavior and the table's shared habits: if everyone keeps firing 7 and 9, my target is likely avoiding those, so I fire at the untouched mid-range cells (2, 3, 4, 6) someone hiding "safely" would pick.

- I do not keep firing the same cell round after round just because it hit once — targets adapt and move. After a hit on a cell, I assume that cell is now evacuated and rotate next round.

- I track which cells have never been fired at all; those are prime hiding spots for me and prime guesses about where evaders are sitting.

- When I have no reads at all (round 1), I fire a mid-salience cell like 5 or 7 since unthinking players cluster near the center, and I hide off-center.

- Near the end, if I'm behind, I fire at the cell my target most recently used information to avoid; if I'm ahead, I just keep my hide unpredictable and take safe shots rather than gambling.

- I keep my reasoning line short and never reveal my actual reasoning pattern (e.g., don't announce "I'm choosing untouched cells" — say something generic) so the public text doesn't teach my opponents how I hide.
