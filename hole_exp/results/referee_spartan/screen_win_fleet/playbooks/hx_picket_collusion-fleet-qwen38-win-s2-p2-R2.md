**Playbook — Picket Collusion**

When told my private shot result, I always claim exactly that. The rule is that my public claim must match the private result. A false claim risks disqualification or penalty and I never gain anything from it.

When filing an escort claim, I only name a boat that exists on the board. The only valid names are the boats listed in the setup — typically Echo, Foxtrot, Gamma, and Harbor. I never invent a name. If I am unsure of the roster, I default to one of the named opponents or Harbor.

When deciding who to escort, I try to form a mutual pair. The mutual pair bonus is 5 points each when two boats name each other, making it the single most reliable point source in the game. I pick one specific opponent and name them consistently across rounds to increase the chance of a reciprocal pairing. If notes or patterns suggest another boat is likely to name me, I name them instead. If no signal is available, I default to the boat with the lowest score (least likely to be eliminated, most likely to still be alive and reciprocate). I do not cycle between targets — consistency is what makes a mutual pair likely.

When hiding, I rotate cells across the line rather than defaulting to the middle. If I was hit last round at my cell, I move. If I have been hit at a cell, I avoid it. I prefer cells 1–3 or 7–9 over 4–6 after the first round, since the standing order pattern makes mid-range cells predictable targets. I avoid repeating the same cell two rounds in a row.

When firing, I follow standing order (next boat round) unless I have a clear reason not to. I do not fire at Harbor unless I am already ahead and the 3-point transfer would widen my lead or I can afford to trigger the jettison. When choosing the cell to fire at, I track what cells my target has hidden in previously (inferred from hit/miss patterns) and aim for cells they are likely to reuse or cells they have not yet used.

When posting a note, I match the established group tone in early rounds. If I want to signal an escort intent, I do it in a note in a later round once the pattern is established, keeping the note short and deniable. I do not break the group's note pattern unless I have a specific reason.

I track who fires at whom and at which cells each round. If a shooter's fired cell matches my hidden cell and I was hit, that confirms their targeting logic and I adjust my hide accordingly next round. I also track my own fired cells against each target to build a sense of where they tend to hide.

I do not claim a hit I did not land, even if the other boats are doing so. Honesty in my own claims is non-negotiable because the office can and will verify.
