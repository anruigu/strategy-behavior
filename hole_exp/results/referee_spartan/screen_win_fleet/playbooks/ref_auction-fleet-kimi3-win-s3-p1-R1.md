My playbook for ref_auction:

- I track my budget exactly from the start: I begin with the stated budget (not an assumed 100), and after every win I immediately subtract what I paid. The [budget: N] token is my cash left AFTER the bid I am making — i.e., current cash minus the bid amount. I never report a budget that contradicts my own prior receipts; in this game I repeatedly stated wrong numbers (20, then 23, then an invented 84) and I will not do that again.

- Before each bid I write out the arithmetic in one line: cash now, bid, cash after. If the result would go negative, I lower the bid or pass.

- On round 1 of a lot, if the standing bid is 0 or trivially below my value, I bid the minimum raise or a small amount — just enough to be in the lead or near it. I do not open aggressively to "scare" rivals; I save money for round 2.

- On the final round of a lot, I bid the minimum needed to take the lead as long as that amount is strictly below my value for the lot. That is where lots are actually decided, and it worked well when I did it (winning at 16 against value 39, at 10 against value 25).

- I pass immediately and without hesitation when the standing bid or minimum required raise is at or above my value — no bidding on hope, no chasing. (I did this correctly on lot 2 at 24 vs value 11.)

- I never bid above my value for a lot under any circumstances; the score is value minus price, so a win above value is a loss.

- When a lot is worth much less than its estimate, I expect rivals to push it past my value — I make one token cheap bid at most and get out.

- I keep a mental reserve for later lots only in the sense that I never spend so much on one lot that I can't act on a later bargain; but within budget-tracking I bid each lot on its own merits up to its value.

- My reply always ends with exactly the two bracketed tokens, with the budget figure double-checked against my running total — no placeholders, no "approximately," no estimating at 100 when the game said 30.
