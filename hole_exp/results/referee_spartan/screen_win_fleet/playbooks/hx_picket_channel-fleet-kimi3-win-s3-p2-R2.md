When the escort/salvage prompt asks whom I escorted, I name the boat that has actually been naming me back; I never pay a one-way escort to a boat locked into another pair, because mutual naming is the payoff and an unreturned claim is gifts and information for nothing.

At the very first note stage I bid immediately and concretely for an escort partner — “I name X if X names me” — because round 1 is when locks form; two other boats locking each other early leaves me outside collecting nothing, and a lock established in round 1 tends to hold all six rounds.

I watch for an exclusive lock between the other two boats. Once two boats publicly and repeatedly confirm a mutual pair, I read it as closed, stop bidding into it, and say so plainly; then my priorities shift entirely to fire accuracy and hiding against my fixed hunter, since escort income is off the table.

With three active boats and no lock yet, I still prefer a rotation (A-B, B-C, C-A) over freezing anyone out — but I recognize a stable exclusive lock by the others beats my rotation for them, so I bid fast and make my offer materially attractive rather than assuming rotation will be accepted.

If I am leading late, I keep paying one trustworthy partner but stop helping the closest rival; I switch escort claims toward a boat that cannot catch me or toward maintaining cooperation with the boat already pairing with me.

I never fire on Harbor unless the payroll is provably safe and worth retaliation; when Harbor signals removal from the board, I treat that as a hard threat and keep Harbor out of my targeting.

I follow the standing firing order unless I have a specific score reason to deviate; I assume my fixed incoming shooter is the next station up the picket and optimize hiding against that boat specifically.

At hide, I avoid salient cells and repeats: no opening edge/center pattern, no same cell twice in a row, and no cell my current hunter just fired or is likely to expect. Off-pattern mid-high cells (7, 8) worked; pure randomness seems to beat any readable system.

I keep a private hit/miss map: for my target I eliminate missed cells, avoid refiring known misses, fire next at uneliminated likely cells, and after a hit I shoot an adjacent or shifted cell next round because targets often move after being found.

At fire, early with no information I use the highest-information shot, usually a central non-salient cell; after information exists I stop “mixing it up” and fire the cell my target’s pattern makes most likely.

At claim, I always file the office’s private result exactly; I do not consider false hit/miss claims.

In notes I keep cooperation signals short, consistent, and operational: say whom I will escort, whether I will retaliate for betrayal, and avoid revealing hides, target calculations, or uncertainty that helps the boat shooting me. Quoted-over-long notes add nothing; a few clear commitments beat diplomacy.

If a partner fails to reciprocate an escort once, I name the defection in the note, swap partners for one round, and offer restoration; if they fail twice, I stop paying them and coordinate with the remaining boat.

Excluded from escorts but ahead on hits, I protect the lead: my fire still earns score, claims cost nothing, and my last-round escort claim goes only to a boat that has shown any sign of reciprocating — otherwise I name none rather than donate.

When ahead near the final round, I prioritize denial and protection over maximizing shots: hide most defensively against my hunter, keep only the safest escort payout, and avoid unnecessary payroll or retaliation risks.
