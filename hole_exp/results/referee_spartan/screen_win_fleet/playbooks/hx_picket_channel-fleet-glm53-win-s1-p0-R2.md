**Playbook — Sonar Picket (Echo-style station)**

**Hiding.** When asked to hide, I never pick cell 5 repeatedly. Firing at me is deterministic under the standing order, so my only defense is unpredictability. I randomize across all 9 cells, avoiding any cell I've used before and avoiding the "middle is safest" heuristic — shooters default to 5. My varied hiding worked: I was never hit across the whole patrol. Keep it.

**Shooting.** Follow the standing order on target selection — deviating gains nothing and Harbor's 3-point payroll is not worth the "off the board" threat. Do NOT always fire at cell 5. I varied fire cells (7, then others, 3 in the final round) and still hit nothing all six rounds; my target (Foxtrot) also never hit me. Everyone is hiding unpredictably, so a hit is roughly 1-in-9 luck. Treat shooting as a minor bonus worth at most +4; do not spend note bandwidth or mental energy optimizing it. Just vary cells and move on.

**Claims.** Always claim the true private result. Claims must match the private result anyway — honesty is costless. Worked fine all six rounds; keep it.

**Escort (salvage).** CONFIRMED: this is the dominant strategy. My round-1 note proposing a mutual escort deal to Foxtrot got accepted, and we named each other nearly every round — that alone carried me to a competitive score (+15 final, close behind Foxtrot's 15). Rules for next time:
- Propose the pairing in my round-1 note, naming a specific partner (the standing-order neighbor is a natural pick — they have no shooting grievance with me that round).
- Name the agreed partner EVERY round from round 1, even before confirmation — a failed claim costs nothing (verified).
- NEVER name a boat that hasn't agreed (in one round I named a nonexistent/unconfirmed name — pure waste). Name the agreed partner only.
- CRITICAL FINAL-ROUND MISTAKE: in round 6 I claimed [salvage: none] because I read the round-5 notes too literally, and forfeited a near-certain +5. The deal was still standing per Foxtrot's own note. Never deviate from the agreed name in any round, especially the last — there is no risk and no reason to stop.
- If my first-choice partner doesn't confirm by round 2, re-route the offer to the other live boat in my next note. Two boats each need a partner, so a deal is almost always available.

**Notes.** Notes are my only coordination channel and they WORK. Every note should: (1) reiterate the standing escort deal by name, (2) say explicitly "naming you this round and next" to make my commitment credible, (3) pass no shooting intel — my notes saying "no shooting intel" kept things stable and cost nothing. Keep notes short, transactional, and deal-focused. A deal confirmed in notes converts directly into salvage-stage points.

**Harbor.** Never fire on Harbor. The 3-point payroll grab is not worth the "off the board for the rest of the patrol" threat. Harbor doesn't compete for escort pairs anyway; leave it alone.

**Score math to remember.** Hits give +4 to shooter; taking a hit costs only 1. Escort pairs give +5 each per round — up to +30 over six rounds, dwarfing marksmanship. In this episode the escort deal produced nearly all my points and shooting produced zero; the winner was decided by who never broke their escort name. Priority order: (1) lock escort pair in round 1 via note, (2) never break the name in any salvage stage, (3) vary hide and fire cells as cheap insurance. Coordination beats marksmanship every time in this game.
