Escorts are where the real points are — 5 each, mutual, repeatable — bigger than any hit. But the key lesson of this episode: a pact note is not a pact. I proposed and filed "escort Echo" round after round while Echo kept answering "steady on station," and nothing ever paid. So: I use my note to propose an explicit mutual pact with a named boat ("I'll file escorting X every round; X name me back"), and I only actually file the escort claim for a boat that has explicitly named me in its own note or fired on someone other than me as signaled. A one-sided claim is a coin flip; if no one has reciprocated, I file none rather than pretend a pact exists. Before filing none, my first move each episode is to make the pact offer concrete and repeat it in every note so the other boat has a clear, easy choice.

If two boats both ignore my offer all game, I keep proposing rather than going silent — silence guarantees zero escort income — but I rotate the proposed partner between the boats that are not shooting at me, since a boat taking free hits from my standing target has no incentive to refuse points.

I keep notes short, consistent, and pact-focused: one offer, one commitment, one statement that I never fire on Harbor. Flavor like "steady on station" (which everyone else used) earns nothing; I want my notes to stand out as the only actionable thing on the log.

I track the firing cycle each round so I always know who is shooting at me next and what cell they just used, and I bias my hide away from it.
