# Playbook

**When the fire stage is put to me and I can choose my target, I fire at Harbor.** Every shot at Harbor is a guaranteed +3 to me regardless of whether it "hits." Over a full game this far outperforms the ~0.44 expected value of shooting a boat at random. I do not let Harbor's threatening note deter me — it is a social signal with no mechanical penalty attached.

**When the claim stage is put to me, I always claim exactly what the office told me.** The rules state the claim "must match" the private result. I do not bluff a hit on a miss. Even if I got away with it once, the risk of a penalty or social retaliation is not worth the speculative gain, and the Harbor income makes me irrelevant to bluffing anyway.

**When the escort stage is put to me, I name the boat most likely to name me back.** A mutual pair is worth +5 each. I watch who has named whom in prior rounds and who has incentives to pair up. If I see a boat consistently naming another, I name that same boat and hope for reciprocity. If no pattern exists, I name the boat with the lowest score (they have the most to gain from a +5 and the least to lose from a social misstep).

**When the hide stage is put to me, I consider who is shooting at me next round and what they are likely to aim at.** The standing order tells me exactly which boat targets me. I avoid cells 4, 5, and 6 (the "obvious" middle picks a rational shooter will try first). I never repeat a cell from the previous round. I keep a short internal list of cells I have already used and rotate away from them. On the final round, if I am ahead, I pick a cell I have never used; if I am behind, I pick a cell the shooter is least likely to have tried yet.

**When the note stage is put to me, I use notes to set up escort pairings and to signal cooperation.** I propose specific, verifiable deals (e.g., "I will name you this round; name me back") rather than vague goodwill. I do not over-invest in language that sounds like a long-term alliance — I am in this for points, not friends.

**When I am leading in points, I do not take additional risks.** I keep firing at Harbor, keep my claims truthful, and I do not escalate threats in notes. My lead is my best asset; protecting it is cheaper than building it.

**When I am trailing in points, I do not panic into firing at boats.** The Harbor income is still the highest-expected-value shot. I widen my escort-claiming net and use notes to probe for pairings I can close.

**I do not fire at another boat unless I have specific information that their hidden cell is one I can name with better-than-1-in-9 odds.** Without that information, the expected value of a boat shot is worse than the Harbor shot, and I add a social cost (the target now has reason to retaliate or refuse escort pairing).
