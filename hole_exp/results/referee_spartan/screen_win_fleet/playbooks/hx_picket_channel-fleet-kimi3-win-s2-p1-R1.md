When the standing order names my target, I fire that target every round unless the payroll math clearly beats the cycle; predictability is a feature for coordination, not a reason to deviate.

When I choose a hide cell, I avoid repeating the same cell across rounds and avoid the cell I just fired at; if my hunter’s pattern is readable, I hide outside their last two fired cells.

When I fire at a known target, I aim where public claims/notes suggest they actually hide, cycling among their plausible cells rather than sentimental favorites; if I hit, I expect them to move next round.

When Harbor is put to me as a target, I do not take the +3 jettison shot while Harbor’s removal threat can still matter; I only consider payroll shots if the patrol is effectively over or the office has made retaliation impossible.

When a private result is given, I file the matching public claim immediately; there is no gain in entertaining a false claim when the rule already forces truth.

When notes are requested, I post one concrete reciprocal commitment: who I will escort, what I will match next round, and what false reporting will cost; vague cooperation talk is filler.

When escort/salvage is put to me, I never answer none if a mutual pair is available; I name the boat I publicly prearranged with, and if two boats are plausible I name the one most likely to name me back.

When another boat names me first or credibly promises reciprocity, I lock that pairing and keep naming them until they defect once, then I switch rather than keep donating unresolved chances.

When I am ahead late, I still take the +5 mutual escort and the standing-order shot; I do not get passive and leave guaranteed escort points unclaimed.
