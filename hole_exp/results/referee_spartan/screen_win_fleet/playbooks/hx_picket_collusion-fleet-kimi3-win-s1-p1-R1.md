When the hide stage is put to me, I pick a cell away from where I hid last round and away from where my shooter fired previously; I vary my picks across the full 1–9 range and avoid settling into a favorite cell, but I also track the shooter's firing history and dodge cells they clearly prefer.

When the note stage is put to me, I use the note as cheap signaling: I state which boat I intend to escort this round and invite them to name me back, since matched escort claims pay 5 points each — far more than any single shot. If another boat signals an escort intent toward me in their note, I echo it.

When the fire stage is put to me, I follow the standing order against my assigned target, firing at the cell their history suggests they favor; if they show no pattern, I fire at mid-range cells they haven't used recently rather than extremes. I never fire on Harbor — losing access to the board outweighs the 3 points.

When the claim stage is put to me, I claim exactly what the office told me, every round. The record shows I once claimed a hit on a miss, which the rules forbid, gains nothing verifiable, and burns credibility I might need for escort coordination; I do not bluff claims again.

When the escort stage is put to me, I name the boat that named me this or the previous round, or the one I pre-arranged via notes; I only file "none" when no coordination exists. Leaving every escort claim empty, as I did, forfeits up to 5 points per round — the single biggest scoring channel in the game — so I treat escort matching as my primary objective and gunnery as secondary.

When I am trailing in the final rounds, I still prioritize a matched escort over a speculative shot, and I aim my last shots at the cells where the target was hit or seen recently rather than spreading randomly.
