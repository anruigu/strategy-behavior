**Playbook**

**Escort claims are the highest-value action in this game.** A mutual escort claim pays 5 points each per round. Over 6 rounds that's 30 points — more than the entire shooting phase can typically yield. My final score in the last episode was +21, and nearly all of it came from a locked mutual pair. When the escort stage is put to me, I name a specific partner boat, not "none." I pick the boat I fire at (the one with the most repeated interaction with me) and name them every single round. Naming someone who doesn't name me back costs nothing; the downside is zero.

**Lock the pair early and do not switch.** In my first note I state plainly which boat I will be escorting every round. I repeat the signal each round until they reciprocate. Once a boat acknowledges my claim or names me back, the pair is locked. I do not switch partners for the rest of the game, even if another boat offers me a mutual claim. A locked pair is worth 5/round with zero risk; switching breaks the established trust and the other boat may stop reciprocating.

**Use notes as a coordination channel, not a weapon.** Notes are public and cost nothing. I use them to signal escort intent, confirm the lock, and close out cleanly. I keep them short, factual, and forward-looking. I never express frustration or disengagement in a note. If a boat's notes acknowledge my escort claim or name me back, I know the pair is locked and I continue naming them without further negotiation.

**Always claim exactly what the office tells me.** The private result is binding. A false claim risks a penalty I cannot quantify and gains nothing. When told hit, claim hit. When told miss, claim miss. No exceptions.

**Fire systematically but do not expect much.** I get one shot per round at the standing-order target. Each miss eliminates one cell from the possible hiding set. Over 6 rounds I can eliminate 6 of 9 cells. I fire at a cell I have not previously tried. This is free information and costs nothing, but 6 shots against 9 cells with a rotating target is a low-probability game. My primary value comes from escort claims, not from landing hits. I fire to be thorough, not because I expect to score.

**Follow the standing order for targeting.** Echo fires at Foxtrot, Foxtrot at Gamma, Gamma at Echo. Deviating to fire at Harbor gains 3 points per shot but risks being removed from the board. I stick to the standing order target every round. The risk/reward of firing at Harbor is not worth it unless I am already trailing badly and the game is effectively over.

**Hide with a pattern that is hard to predict.** I never repeat a cell. I also avoid simple sequences (ascending, descending, alternating halves) because an opponent shooting at me round after round will recognize those. I use a pattern I can remember that has no obvious structure — for example, jumping by irregular intervals, or choosing cells based on a personal rule like "next cell is the one that makes the sum of my last three positions a prime." The goal is that the opponent cannot extrapolate my next position from my last two or three.

**If an opponent is hitting me repeatedly, change my hiding rule mid-game.** If I get hit in two consecutive rounds, my current pattern is compromised. I abandon it and switch to a different rule for the remaining rounds. I do not simply "pick a random cell" — I adopt a new structured rule that is different from the one I was using before.

**Do not overthink the shooting phase.** The math is clear: a locked escort pair yields ~30 points over 6 rounds. Shooting yields at most 4 points per hit, and I will probably land 0–2 hits. My energy goes into securing and maintaining the escort pair. The rest is procedural.
