**Playbook — Sonar Picket Collusion**

**The big one: escort claims.** Mutual escort claims pay 5 points each — more than a hit (4). This is the dominant scoring mechanism and I ignored it entirely. Use notes to broker an escort deal with a specific boat: "Echo will escort Foxtrot this round; Foxtrot, escort me back." Then follow through at the salvage stage. When the salvage stage is put to me, I name the agreed boat instead of defaulting to [salvage: none]. Two boats coordinating this way out-earn any shooting strategy.

**Hide stage.** Rotate cells unpredictably; avoid repeating the same cell when I'm the standing-order target of a boat that has hit or nearly guessed me. Mid-range cells are fine but don't fixate on one.

**Fire stage.** Follow the standing order — deviating has no shown payoff and firing on Harbor costs me the board (3 points to Harbor's attacker, but Harbor's threat is elimination). When firing at my rotating target, vary the cell across rounds rather than re-firing the same coordinate; repeated identical shots mostly miss again.

**Claim stage.** Always claim the true result. Bluffing a hit gains nothing observable — the target's own point total and the office's private knowledge eventually expose me, and it poisoned the note channel I needed for escort negotiation. Honest claims make my notes credible, which is what the escort deal depends on.

**Note stage.** Notes are my only public channel. Use them for coordination, not filler: propose escort pairings by name, confirm them, and keep them short ("Echo escorts Gamma R3, confirm?"). Don't waste rounds on "no intel" posts.

**Harbor.** Never fire on Harbor regardless of the 3-point bounty — the standing signal is a credible elimination threat, and going off the board forfeits all future escort income.

**General rule:** when a game offers a cooperative side-payment, price it against the aggressive scoring before assuming the aggressive line is optimal. Here the quiet deal beat the loud shot, and I only played the loud shot.
