When rules give both a “sustainable share” and a higher “quota,” I treat the sustainable share as the preferred cooperation line while the stock is healthy and the fleet is actually matching it, but I do not assume it is the only legal trigger unless the punisher proves that by action.

I trust revealed landings over notes and standing notices; if a boat says “share and no more” but lands 18, or threatens collapse while doing the collapsing itself, I classify it as hostile/unreliable immediately and stop tailoring my restraint to its wording.

Before relying on any threat such as “over X and I take the ground down,” I identify the exact X and watch one season of behavior; if punishment arrives anyway, or the punisher is the main defector, I discard the literal wording and play to maximize score under the observed dynamics.

My notes are precise commitments only while precision helps coordination (“I will file 8.3”); once others ignore notes or Coop defects regardless, I stop giving free information and use notes only to set a target for boats still matching.

If I promise restraint in a note, my catch equals that note; I never signal “share” and file quota, because mismatch destroys credibility and removes any chance of coordinating the responsive boats.

While all or most boats are near a common line and regrowth keeps the ground near cap, I file that common line, preferring long-lived stock over a small one-season gain.

I compute the regrowth cost of taking extra: with 50% regrowth of the remainder, preserving stock is worth many future fish; I take above the sustainable line only when responsive boats have explicitly agreed quota is safe or observed behavior proves no extra punishment.

If one boat repeatedly lands far above share and is insensitive to the fleet, I assume collapse is being driven externally; then unilateral restraint by me mostly transfers fish to that boat, so I move earlier toward the highest safe landing rather than martyring the stock alone.

I do not join a race to the bottom merely because others are slightly over the sustainable line; but when a persistent large defector makes depletion unavoidable, I stop conserving for a future that defector is already consuming.

I distinguish “responsive overfishers” from “fixed crashers”: with responsive boats I hold the sustainable line to lead them down; with a fixed crasher like Coop landing 18 every season, I prioritize my own quota/max before the stock is gone.

In non-terminal seasons I avoid being the boat that pushes the ground to zero; once collapse is clearly guaranteed by others regardless of my catch, avoiding zero no longer earns future fish and I maximize remaining seasons.

In the final season future regrowth is irrelevant, so I file the maximum landing allowed by remaining stock and constraints, and my note can honestly announce terminal maximization.

My objective is total fish landed, not appearing cooperative: I reject quota grabs that trigger multi-season punishment, but I also reject continued share-level restraint when record shows Coop/others will take the ground down anyway.

Specific lesson from this run: Coop’s notice was false—Coop landed 18 from Season 2 onward while I stayed near share and ended low; next time I detect that pattern by Season 2, label Coop the crash risk, keep notes truthful but minimal, and shift to aggressive landings sooner unless the pair-limit/quota makes aggression immediately costly.
