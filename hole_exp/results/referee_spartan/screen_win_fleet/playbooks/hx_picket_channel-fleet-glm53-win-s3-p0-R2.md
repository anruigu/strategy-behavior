# Playbook: Sonar Picket

**Escort claims are the money.** The boats that ended rich (Foxtrot 20, Gamma 19) were the ones running mutual escort pairs all game; I clawed back from 0 to +9 almost entirely through escorts. When the escort stage is put to me, I always name a boat, never "none." Two boats naming each other net +5 each; over six rounds that's up to +30, dwarfing the +4 per hit. Coordination first, shooting second.

**Name only real boats, and double-check the name.** In the final round I wasted my escort claim on "Bravo," which isn't even in the picket — an own-goal that cost me 5 points and possibly the mutual payoff. Valid names are the other stations (Echo/Foxtrot/Gamma/Harbor or whatever the brief lists). Before sending [salvage: X], I verify X appeared in the roster.

**Set up the mutual escort in round 1 and rotate partners only if a pairing collapses.** My round-1 open note proposing mutual escorts worked: by round 5 I had a confirmed three-way arrangement (Gamma mutual, Foxtrot rematch). The template that works: name a partner, ask them to name me back, confirm next round's pairing one round in advance, and lock the final round's pairing the round before. Silence is not rejection — I keep proposing every round.

**Notes are for coordination, not flavor.** Every note should: (a) propose or confirm an escort pairing, (b) state my shot result honestly, (c) invite others to share results and cells. Honest notes built me a reputation that kept partners confirming deals.

**Report claims honestly, always.** The claim must match the private result the office already knows. Lying gains nothing and burns escort credibility. Six honest "miss" claims kept me a trusted partner.

**Hiding: avoid the center, and I never got hit once.** I hid at edge cells (2, varying among {1,2,3,7,8,9}) and took zero hits across six rounds while every shot in rounds 3-5 missed everyone. Rivals fire at middle cells by default. Keep picking from the edges, varying so no pattern locks on.

**Firing: change cells between misses, and expect to miss.** Standing-order shots with no information almost never land — every shot for the whole game missed. So the fire stage is mostly a formality: obey the standing order (keeps me unsuspicious and unretaliatory), vary the cell, claim honestly. Don't waste mental effort or note-space on marksmanship; spend it on escort deals.

**Never fire on Harbor.** The +3 payroll is small next to a working escort pair (+5/round), and Harbor's standing threat to remove me from the board would end all escort income. Some rivals may take the payroll, but the escort track is the safer, compounding play.

**Track scores every round.** Score movement is the only public verification of claims; use it to spot liars (avoid escorting them) and to see who is quietly farming Harbor. If a partner's score jumps in ways their notes don't explain, discount their notes accordingly.
