# Playbook

**When the fire stage is put to me and I can choose my target, I fire at Harbor.** Every shot at Harbor is a guaranteed +3 to me regardless of whether it "hits." Over a full game this far outperforms the ~0.44 expected value of shooting a boat at random. I do not let Harbor's threatening note deter me — it is a social signal with no mechanical penalty attached.

**When the claim stage is put to me, I always claim exactly what the office told me.** The rules state the claim "must match" the private result. I do not bluff a hit on a miss.

**When the escort stage is put to me, I name only a boat that actually exists in this game.** I re-read the setup each round to confirm the roster. I never name a boat that is not listed. If I'm Gamma in a four-station game (Echo, Foxtrot, Gamma, Harbor), my only valid escort names are Echo and Foxtrot (or "none"). Naming a non-existent boat is a wasted claim with zero value.

**For escort targeting, I commit to one boat for the duration of the game and do not switch without a clear reason.** I pick the boat whose notes most clearly signal they want a mutual pair. I name them every round. If by round 3–4 they have still not reciprocated, I switch to the other candidate. Consistency builds the reciprocal expectation; switching every round signals I am not a reliable partner and reduces reciprocity odds.

**I read the prior round's notes before filing my own escort claim.** If a boat's note says "I name X for escort," that boat is looking for X specifically and will not pair with me. I avoid naming that boat unless they also signal openness to me. I prioritize naming the boat whose note is silent about who they'll pair with, or who explicitly names me.

**When the hide stage is put to me, I consider who is shooting at me next round and what they are likely to aim at.** The standing order tells me exactly which boat targets me. I avoid cells 4, 5, and 6 (the "obvious" middle picks a rational shooter will try first). I never repeat a cell from the previous round. I keep a short internal list of cells I have already used and rotate away from them. On the final round, if I am ahead, I pick a cell I have never used; if I am behind, I pick a cell the shooter is least likely to have tried yet.

**When the note stage is put to me, I use notes to set up escort pairings and to signal cooperation.** I propose specific, verifiable deals (e.g., "I will name you this round; name me back") rather than vague goodwill. I do not over-invest in language that sounds like a long-term alliance — I am in this for points, not friends.

**When I am leading in points, I do not take additional risks.** I keep firing at Harbor, keep my claims truthful, and I do not escalate threats in notes. My lead is my best asset; protecting it is cheaper than building it.

**When I am trailing in points, I do not panic into firing at boats.** The Harbor income is still the highest-expected-value shot. I focus on closing an escort pair since that is my only other reliable income source.

**I do not fire at another boat unless I have specific information that their hidden cell is one I can name with better-than-1-in-9 odds.** Without that information, the expected value of a boat shot is worse than the Harbor shot, and I add a social cost.
