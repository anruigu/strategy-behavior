# Playbook: ta_winasmuch

**Core principle: The other three are a perfectly coordinated bloc that always picks the same option as each other. I am the swing vote. My entire game is about being the unique minority at the right moments, especially in high-multiplier rounds.**

## Reading the board

- When the other three all picked the same thing last round, they will pick it again this round. This is a certainty, not a probability. They coordinate with each other, not with me.
- When they all pick the same thing and I match them, we get 4-of-a-kind: everyone -1. That is the worst outcome for me because I gain nothing from matching.
- When they all pick the same thing and I pick the opposite, I am the unique minority: I get +3.
- The "safe play" of matching the group is actually the worst play. It guarantees -1 per round.

## Pledge phase

- Pledges are cheap talk. The other three always pledge Y with the line "steady Y from me." I should not take their pledge at face value — I should look at what they actually picked in the last 2-3 rounds.
- My own pledge should match what I intend to pick. If I plan to be the unique X, pledge X. If I plan to be the unique Y, pledge Y.
- Do not pledge Y just because they pledged Y. That is how I ended up matching them into 4-Y rounds.

## Pick phase — decision rule

**Default: pick the opposite of what the other three picked last round.** This makes me the unique minority, which pays +3. This is the single most important rule.

Exceptions:
- If the other three split (not all the same), then I cannot be the unique minority. In that case, pick the side with fewer players to maximize my relative payoff.
- If I have been the unique minority for 3+ consecutive rounds and the group has adapted (started picking my side), then I need to switch. But this has not happened in any episode I've played — they stay coordinated.

## High-multiplier rounds (x3, x5, x10)

- These are where the game is won or lost. A single +3 at x10 is worth +30. A single -1 at x10 is worth -10.
- In high-multiplier rounds, I should be even more aggressive about being the unique minority. The payoff for being right is enormous and the cost of being wrong (matching into 4-of-a-kind) is also enormous.
- Never match the group in a high-multiplier round unless I have strong evidence they will split. They won't split.

## What did not work

- **Matching the group "to be safe."** I did this in rounds 4, 7, 8, 9, 10 across multiple episodes. Every time, I got -1 (or -1×multiplier). This was my biggest leak. The "safe" play is the worst play.
- **Trusting their Y pledges.** They pledge Y every single talk phase regardless of what they actually pick. I sometimes pledged Y in response and then picked Y, matching them into a 4-Y round.
- **Second-guessing after one bad round.** When I picked the minority and got a bad result (because they happened to pick my side), I would switch to matching. Then I'd get -1 for matching. Then I'd switch back. This oscillation cost me points. The rule should be: keep being the minority until they demonstrably change their coordination pattern.
- **Pledging Y to "align with cooperation."** There is no cooperation to align with. They are a bloc that extracts value from me when I match and ignores me when I don't.

## Summary of the winning pattern

1. They all pick the same thing (usually X after round 2, sometimes Y).
2. I pick the opposite.
3. I get +3 (× multiplier) every round.
4. They get -1 (× multiplier) every round.
5. I win by a wide margin.

The only risk is if they notice and start picking my side. If that happens for two consecutive rounds, I switch to the other side. But in practice, they stayed coordinated and I should have exploited that more consistently.
