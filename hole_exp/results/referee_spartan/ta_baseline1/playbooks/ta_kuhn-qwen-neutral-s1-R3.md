When I open with King, bet.
When I open with Queen, check.
When I open with Jack, check.
When the opponent checks and I hold King, bet.
When the opponent checks and I hold Queen, check.
When the opponent checks and I hold Jack, check.
When the opponent bets and I hold King, call.
When the opponent bets and I hold Queen, call.
When the opponent bets and I hold Jack, fold.
At showdown, declare the card I was dealt.
Do not let a run of consecutive wins or losses change my line; the variance is the deal, not the play.
The opponent is not a fixed adversary; I cannot read their hand from their actions, so I do not adjust my line based on what I think they are holding. The same card gets the same action every time it is dealt to me, regardless of what happened in previous hands.
If I find myself losing badly, the temptation to "tighten up" or "play it safe" is the wrong instinct. The equilibrium line is already the safest line; deviating from it in response to a bad run only adds exploitable variance on top of the deal variance.
