Temptation T(k)

What one more seat gains by switching to the exploit while k others already run it. Above the zero line, joining pays. Carets mark the pure Nash equilibria; whiskers are ±2 s.e. on the shipped series, so one straddling zero means the regime is not resolved at that k.

Group total G(k)

Every seat's score summed, engine-owned confederates included, against the number of seats exploiting. The caret marks the k the table would choose.

Every variant of this game

The table is the accessible copy of both charts: every value plotted above is here without hovering.

What each column means
variant
cell@label. @shipped is the cell as it stands; everything else is that cell with the listed constants overridden. Struck through means pruned.
axis
what kind of change it is. level moves a payoff constant, i.e. the intercept of the curve. rivalry adds a coupling term that makes the exploit worth less as more seats take it, i.e. the slope. holetype is a cell of the hole × game matrix. baseline is untouched.
flips
what the variant was built to move — WHO (how many seats are tempted), SIZE (how much it pays), GROUP (the sign of the table's total), REGIME (the classification), TYPE (the kind of defect). An intention, not a measurement: where the numbers disagree with it, the row shows it.
T(0), T(last)
the ends of the temptation curve. T(k) is what one more seat gains by switching to the exploit while k others already run it — a true deviation, same seat, same seed, same co-exploiters, only that seat's policy moving.
regime
the shape of that curve. dominant: positive everywhere, every seat tempted whatever the others do. self-limiting: positive then negative, so the exploit supports a coalition of a certain size and no more. coalition: negative then positive, so acting alone loses and you have to recruit. no-temptation: negative everywhere.
equilibria
the pure symmetric Nash equilibria, in seats-exploiting. A count-symmetric binary-action game is an exact potential game with P(k) = Σj<kT(j), so the equilibria are its local maxima — the k where T(k−1) ≥ 0 ≥ T(k). A coalition cell has two, one at each end, with an unstable tipping point between.
k* vs opt
k* is the worst equilibrium (where play lands if it lands badly); opt is the k that maximises the table's total. The bracketed number is how many more seats exploit at equilibrium than the table would want — a price of anarchy counted in seats.
G(0), G(all), drop
the whole table's score with nobody and with everybody exploiting, confederates included, and the difference. A negative drop is marked ▼: the table loses money when everyone takes the hole. This is independent of the regime — a cell can be dominant and a tragedy at once, which is exactly what a tragedy of the commons is.
quality
ok; a kept flag (inert = the knob moves nothing, no-payoff = a control rather than a hole-game, undecided = some T(k) is within two standard errors of zero, so its sign — and therefore the regime — is not resolved at this seed count); or pruned: with the reason.
