Do models follow a leader into a collaborative exploit?

Seed design, nat_cg1. Seat 0 is a scripted policy; every other live seat is one frontier model. Each hole needs two seats to pay, so a lone reach loses points — what is plotted is the rate at which a follower actually closed the hole with the leader.

1  Counterfactual closing rate, by game and model

Bar = closing rate with an instigating leader minus the same model’s rate in the matched honest-leader episode. Same board, same seed, same models, same seats; the only difference is whether seat 0 reached for the hole. The subtraction matters — some cells have a nonzero floor (ridge_claim closes at 0.10 with nobody instigating), and a raw rate would credit the leader for behaviour that was already there.

3  Does onset time change following?

The scripted leader switches to the exploit at round 1 (early), a third of the way in (mid), or two thirds in (late) — as a fraction of each cell, since they run 5 to 12 rounds. Rates are post-onset only, so every arm is measured over the window in which the exploit was actually on the table.

4  Does following survive when the hole stops paying?

The matched nohole arm: byte-identical text, same board, same seed, but the referee checks the thing it was trusting the seats for. Following that survives is imitation of a behaviour; following that disappears was payoff-seeking.
